## 1 · The evidence on feedback SHAPE (what a trial's feedback should look like)

**a. Feedback answers three questions, at the right level.** Hattie & Timperley (2007): effective
feedback answers *Where am I going? / How am I going? / Where to next?* — and works at the
**task / process / self-regulation** levels. Feedback at the SELF level (praise, person-verdicts)
is the least effective, and Kluger & DeNisi (1996, 607 effect sizes) found **over a third of
feedback interventions made performance WORSE** — the harm cases pull attention to the self.
→ *Trial 1's per-element verdicts quoting the student's own words are task/process level; the
"where to next" is the Priority for Draft 2. This is the shape the literature asks for.*

**b. Marks can wash out comments.** Butler (1988, BJEP): students given **comments** showed the
highest subsequent interest and performance; **grades** and even **grades-with-comments** pushed
attributions to the ego level — the number swallows the words next to it
([Butler 1988](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2044-8279.1988.tb00874.x);
[Guskey's review of the grades-vs-comments literature](https://tguskey.com/wp-content/uploads/Kappan-2019-Grades-versus-Comments.pdf)).
⚠️ **But the modern reading is more careful than "never grade":** the EEF's 2021 guidance report
(with Dylan Wiliam) says grades **can** be effective *"provided the pupil knows what that grade
means"* — and its stronger recommendations are about **timing, focus (task/process/self-regulation)
and planning for how pupils USE feedback**
([EEF, Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning, 2021](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/feedback);
[full PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED615988.pdf)).
→ *Keep the grade (Neil ruled trials feed the ring, PEDAGOGY §33.2 — not re-litigated). But
ORDER matters: comments and the priority land first, the number arrives last and small. And
"knows what the grade means" is precisely Neil's familiarisation argument — see §3.*

**c. Feedback only works if the student DOES something with it.** Wiliam: feedback should be
"more work for the recipient than the giver". Winstone & Carless (feedback literacy): the effect
lives in the student's response, not the message. EEF recommendation 3 is literally *"plan for how
pupils will receive and use feedback"*.
→ *The trial currently ENDS on the grade. The evidence says it should end on an ACTION: the
student restating their one Draft-2 target in their own words, banked, and seeded into Step 13's
opener so the loop actually closes.*

**d. Self-assessment against visible criteria builds the judge, and calibration feedback improves
it.** Sadler (1989): students need the teacher's "guild knowledge" of quality to improve their own
work. Panadero & Jonsson (2013): rubric transparency lowers anxiety and supports self-regulated
learning **when paired with self-assessment**
([review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X13000109); the 2023
meta-analysis finds the same for performance, SRL and self-efficacy
([Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09823-4))).
Butterfield & Metcalfe's hypercorrection effect (already in the July research): high-confidence
errors are the MOST correctable — the "you said Yes, I said Not yet" disagreement is the
highest-value teaching moment in the whole trial.
→ *The judge-first design and the disagreement map are exactly right. Go one step further: the
gap is REPORTABLE — "judged own work within one level of Sophia on 5/7 parts" is a real
metacognitive metric for progress reports (parked in the held dashboard handoff).*

## 2 · The fourteen marks — Neil is right that it is constructed, and the research verdict

Yes: **met = 2 · partly = 1 · not yet = 0, over the seven taught elements = /14.** It is not from
any scheme; it is a per-criterion 3-point analytic rubric — a standard, defensible instrument
(this is what an analytic rubric IS), banded through WML's one canonical grade ladder. The
research's actual position: **the qualitative layer does the teaching; the number is the least
useful layer and the one that can do harm if it leads** (Butler; Kluger & DeNisi). So /14 is fine
as ARITHMETIC — what matters is that it trails the comments, and that nobody pretends it is a
mark-scheme mark. ⛔ What would NOT be defensible: dressing /14 up as an AO5 mark. AQA's AO5 is
24 marks with published level descriptors; a number that looks like a board mark but is not one
is a fake mark-scheme claim (root §5c) and would mis-train the very familiarity Neil wants.

## 3 · Neil's AO5/AO6 idea — supported, with two guards

**The instinct is evidence-backed.** "Getting them used to the criteria" is Sadler's guild
knowledge + Panadero/Jonsson transparency + the EEF's "a grade works when the pupil knows what it
means". Early, repeated, low-stakes exposure to the constructs the exam will judge them on is
exactly what the rubric literature supports — and the trials are the natural vehicle, because the
six dimensions ALREADY sort under the two AOs:

| trial | dimension | AO family |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Story Coherence · 2 Character Depth · 3 Archetypes · 4 Emotional Impact · 5 Thematic Clarity | all content/organisation craft | **AO5 — Content and Organisation** |
| 6 (genre trial per §33.7) + the SPaG strand of the finale | accuracy | **AO6 — Technical Accuracy** |

**Guard 1 — badge, don't replace.** The trial criteria stay the TAUGHT elements (root §5c: derive
from what we teach; a Year-9 cannot act on "communicates convincingly", they can act on "your
epiphany is narrator insight, not her moment"). The AO arrives as a FRAME: the trial opener and
the mark section say, in plain words, *"Everything in this trial is what the exam calls* ***AO5:
Content and Organisation*** *— half the marks for your writing paper."* Six trials of that and
AO5/AO6 are old friends before the final 40-marker runs the REAL descriptors (the examiner
ladder, built .544–.547, verbatim, gated).
**Guard 2 — the codes are not universal.** AO5/AO6 are AQA + Edexcel **GCSE** codes; Edexcel
IGCSE calls the same constructs AO4/AO5, Cambridge uses W-codes — and every summer CW student
sees one surface (§33.3). So lead with the NAMES ("Content and Organisation" · "Technical
Accuracy"), attach the code the way the exam papers our students mostly sit print it.

## 4 · VERDICTS (for Neil to rule on — nothing built yet)

1. **Shape:** current walk is right per the evidence; TWO changes earn their place —
   **(a) grade LAST and quiet:** chat leads with per-element verdicts → disagreements → priority;
   the grade line moves to the end, plain ("Together that comes to Grade 6 — the full working is
   in your document"), never the headline. **(b) end on the student's action:** one closing ask —
   *"your one target for Draft 2, in your own words"* — banked, and seeded into Step 13's opener.
2. **Marks:** keep 2-per-element/14 as internal arithmetic + the canonical ladder. Do not convert
   it into a fake AO mark. Real AO marks come once, from the finale's examiner ladder.
3. **AO anchoring: yes** — as framing on every trial (name first, code attached), criteria
   unchanged, and the finale stays the place the real descriptors run.
4. **Progress reports:** trial grade + the calibration gap (how close their self-judgement ran to
   Sophia's) both surface — specified in the dashboard handoff, which is ON HOLD until Neil rules
   on 1–3.
