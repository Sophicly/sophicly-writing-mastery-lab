# Mastery Learning — Time vs Achievement — Research Basis for the Blog Article

**Question:** Can we argue, in public and in Sophicly's name, that a time-based school system
structurally cannot deliver mastery — and that this is why we teach the way we do?

**Verdict: YES — and more strongly than the first pass concluded.**

> **⚠ REVISION, 29 Jul 2026.** The first version of this doc (28 Jul) said the structural argument
> was sound but the effect evidence was "contested". **Neil pushed back — that the evidence is
> growing — and he was substantially right.** A wider pass changed the picture. What is actually
> weak is a *narrow* thing: **group-based mastery learning, in 1980s classrooms, measured on
> standardised tests**. What is strong is **technology-enabled mastery/tutoring** and
> **mastery learning in medical education**, neither of which existed in the form we now mean when
> Slavin wrote his 1987 critique. The correction matters, so it is recorded rather than quietly
> patched.

**Method:** every citation resolved via Crossref/OpenAlex DOI lookup (28–29 Jul 2026); quoted
abstracts are the publishers' own. Items not independently verified are flagged as such.

## THE HEADLINE FOR THE ARTICLE
The intuition is old and correct; the objection to it was always **logistical**, not theoretical;
and the logistics changed. That is a genuinely strong, honest, defensible thesis — and it is
Khan's argument too (see `2026-07-29-sal-khan-ted-mastery-SOURCE.md`).

## VERIFIED FINDINGS

### 1. Aptitude is a measure of TIME NEEDED, not capacity — Carroll (1963)
- Carroll, J. B. "A Model of School Learning." *Teachers College Record* 64(8), 1963.
  doi:10.1177/016146816306400801 — **1,359 citations.**
- Degree of learning is a function of time actually spent against time needed. Hold time constant
  and achievement must vary; hold achievement constant and time must vary. There is no third option.
  *(Standard account of the model, not a quotation from the paper.)*

### 2. Mastery learning helps MOST the students who are behind — Kulik ×2 & Bangert-Drowns (1990)
- *Effectiveness of Mastery Learning Programs: A Meta-Analysis*, **Review of Educational Research**,
  doi:10.3102/00346543060002265 — **329 citations.**
- Verbatim: "**108 controlled evaluations** showed that mastery learning programs have **positive
  effects on the examination performance** of students… **The effects appear to be stronger on the
  weaker students in a class**." Also, honestly: mastery "**may increase student time on
  instructional tasks**" — it costs time. That IS the trade, and it is the argument.

### 3. ⭐ Technology-enabled tutoring gets close to human tutoring — VanLehn (2011)
- *The Relative Effectiveness of Human Tutoring, Intelligent Tutoring Systems, and Other Tutoring
  Systems*, **Educational Psychologist**, doi:10.1080/00461520.2011.611369 — **~2,021 citations.**
- Verbatim abstract: "It is widely believed… when compared to No tutoring, the effect sizes of
  answer-based tutoring systems, intelligent tutoring systems, and adult human tutors are believed
  to be d = 0.3, 1.0, and 2.0 respectively. **This review did not confirm these beliefs. Instead,
  it found that the effect size of human tutoring was much lower: d = 0.79.** Moreover, **the effect
  size of intelligent tutoring systems was 0.76, so they are nearly as effective as human
  tutoring.**"
- **Two things at once, and both matter to us:** it *refutes* the 2-sigma folklore — including for
  human tutors — AND it establishes that a well-designed system performs at roughly human-tutor
  level. d ≈ 0.8 is a large effect in education.

### 4. ⭐ Intelligent tutoring: median +0.66 SD — Kulik & Fletcher (2016)
- *Effectiveness of Intelligent Tutoring Systems*, **Review of Educational Research**,
  doi:10.3102/0034654315581420 — **~800 citations.**
- Verbatim: "**The median effect… was to raise test scores 0.66 standard deviations over
  conventional levels, or from the 50th to the 75th percentile.** However, the amount of improvement
  … depended to a great extent on **whether improvement was measured on locally developed or
  standardized tests**, suggesting that **alignment of test and instructional objectives is a
  critical determinant** of evaluation results."
- ⚠ **Read the moderator carefully — it is the most useful sentence in this whole doc for
  Sophicly.** The recurring weakness is *misalignment* between what was taught and what was tested.
  Our entire product is built to the exam's own mark scheme, so the alignment condition is one we
  deliberately satisfy. This is also the honest reason the 1980s school studies looked weak on
  standardised tests, and why it does not automatically transfer to us.

### 5. ⭐ The strongest modern evidence is in MEDICAL education — and it reaches patient outcomes
- Cohen/Barsuk/Wayne/McGaghie line of work, e.g. *Simulation-based education with mastery learning
  improves residents' lumbar puncture skills*, **Neurology** (2012), doi:10.1212/wnl.0b013e31825dd39d
  — 301 citations; *Simulation-based Mastery Learning Improves Cardiac Auscultation Skills in
  Medical Students*, doi:10.1007/s11606-010-1309-x — 143 citations; *Patient Outcomes in
  Simulation-Based Medical Education: A Systematic Review*, doi:10.1007/s11606-012-2264-5 — 383
  citations; McGaghie, Barsuk & Wayne, *The promise and challenge of mastery learning* (2017),
  doi:10.2147/amep.s141073.
- Why this is the trump card: in medicine, mastery learning is held to a standard no school study
  faces — **does the trained clinician actually perform better on real patients?** — and it clears
  it. Nobody in that field regards mastery learning as unproven.
- ⚠ Different domain (procedural skills, adult professionals). Cite as *"where mastery learning has
  been tested most rigorously"*, never as evidence about GCSE English.

## REFUTED / DO NOT CITE

### ⛔ "2 sigma" — dead, and now doubly so
- Bloom's *The 2 Sigma Problem* (1984, doi:10.3102/0013189x013006004) is real, but:
  - Slavin (1987), *Mastery Learning Reconsidered*, **Review of Educational Research**,
    doi:10.3102/00346543057002175 — verbatim: "**essentially no evidence** to support the
    effectiveness of group-based mastery learning **on standardized achievement measures**. On
    experimenter-made measures, effects were generally positive but **moderate**… with **little
    evidence that effects maintained over time**."
  - VanLehn (2011) then found the **2.0 figure for human tutoring itself was not confirmed**
    (d = 0.79).
- **So: never print "2 sigma", "two standard deviations", or "as effective as one-to-one tutoring"
  as an expectation.** Use d ≈ 0.66–0.79 from the ITS literature if a number is needed at all.

### ⛔ Growth mindset as a co-equal pillar (Khan's second idea)
- Sisk, Burgoyne, Sun et al. (2018), *To What Extent and Under Which Circumstances Are Growth
  Mind-Sets Important to Academic Achievement? Two Meta-Analyses*, **Psychological Science**,
  doi:10.1177/0956797617739704 — ~987 citations. Verbatim: "**Overall effects were weak for both
  meta-analyses.** However, some results supported specific tenets… **students with low
  socioeconomic status or who are academically at risk might benefit**."
- Yeager, Hanselman, Walton et al. (2019), **Nature**, doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y — ~1,483
  citations. A short online growth-mindset intervention "**improved grades among lower-achieving
  students**" and increased advanced-maths enrolment — but effects held where "**peer norms aligned
  with the messages of the intervention**".
- **Consequence:** do not present mindset as equally evidenced. Lead with mastery; treat mindset as
  something mastery *produces* (a student who repeatedly experiences "not yet → mastered" acquires
  the belief) rather than as a standalone intervention we sell.

### ⛔ Still not available
No defensible claim that mastery learning produces a specific GCSE grade uplift. Any number we quote
must be measured on our own students.

## WHAT CHANGED — the argument that makes the old critiques non-binding
This is the spine of the article, and it is honest:
1. Carroll/Bloom's structural insight was never really disputed.
2. The **implementation** was the problem. Winnetka (Washburne, 1919–1943 — verified as a real
   programme) did mastery-based learning and it was judged logistically unscalable: one teacher
   cannot run 30 different tracks, produce per-student worksheets and assess on demand.
3. Slavin's 1987 critique is a critique of *that* constrained thing — group-based mastery inside a
   fixed calendar, where "coverage vs mastery" is decided by the calendar. Slavin's own phrase, the
   **coverage-versus-mastery dilemma**, names the constraint rather than refuting the principle.
4. What changed is the logistics: on-demand explanation, adaptive practice, immediate per-element
   feedback. And when mastery/tutoring is delivered that way, the measured effects are large
   (VanLehn d = 0.76; Kulik & Fletcher +0.66 SD) and, in medicine, reach real-world outcomes.
5. So the honest claim is not "mastery learning is proven to give you a grade 9". It is:
   **the objection to mastery learning was always practical, and the practical objection no longer
   holds.**

## WHAT THE ARTICLE MAY CLAIM
- A time-based system holds time constant, so achievement must vary. **(Carroll)**
- Schools are not villains for this — a cohort must be moved through a calendar. **(Neil's
  concession; Slavin's coverage-vs-mastery dilemma)**
- We identify the gaps, then build on top of them anyway. **(Khan's home-building analogy —
  attribute it)**
- Benefits concentrate in the students who are behind. **(Kulik et al. 1990; and mindset effects
  concentrate the same way — Yeager et al. 2019)**
- Mastery costs time; that is the trade Sophicly absorbs. **(Kulik et al. 1990)**
- Well-designed tutoring systems now perform near human-tutor level. **(VanLehn 2011;
  Kulik & Fletcher 2016)** — ⚠ but see the brand rule: **do not lead with "AI"** in customer copy.
- Alignment between what is taught and what is tested is decisive — and we teach to the mark scheme.
  **(Kulik & Fletcher 2016)**
- Where mastery learning has been tested most rigorously — medical education — it improves real
  performance. **(McGaghie/Barsuk/Wayne line)**

## WHAT THE ARTICLE MUST NOT CLAIM
- "2 sigma" / "two standard deviations" / "as good as one-to-one tutoring" as an expectation.
- That mastery learning is proven to raise standardised results in schools (Slavin says otherwise).
- Growth mindset as a large, general effect.
- Any grade-uplift percentage not measured on Sophicly students.
