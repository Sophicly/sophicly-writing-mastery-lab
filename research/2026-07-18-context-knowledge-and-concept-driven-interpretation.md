# Context Knowledge & Concept-Driven Interpretation — Research Basis
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **For:** the WML context/concept design (applied in `PLANNING-PROTOCOL-AUDIT-AND-PLAN-2026-07-18.md` §8).
**Claim being grounded:** students must build interpretations OUT OF understanding the text's context; most arrive knowing almost nothing; we ASK-FIRST, then build knowledge (expert insight + Library reading), then have them derive the concept themselves (ownership preserved).

---

## 1. Ask-first — activate prior knowledge before instruction
- **Ausubel (1968):** *"The most important single factor influencing learning is what the learner already knows. Ascertain this and teach him accordingly."* Advance organizers (Ausubel 1960): new knowledge anchors onto activated prior structures; ask-first outperforms lecture-first.
- **Schema theory — Anderson & Pearson (1984); Bransford & Johnson (1972):** comprehension is a text×schema interaction. Bransford & Johnson's "washing clothes" passage is unintelligible until the schema is named → **eliciting the relevant frame first is what makes later input usable.** Lecturing into an un-activated schema is low-retention by construction.

## 2. Background knowledge drives comprehension & inference
- **Willingham (2009):** "reading comprehension" is largely domain/background knowledge, not a drillable transferable skill.
- **Recht & Leslie (1988)** — *the baseball study*: weak general readers with high baseball knowledge out-comprehended strong general readers lacking it. Prior domain knowledge beat generic reading skill.
- **Hirsch (1987; 2006):** the "knowledge deficit" — comprehension/inference stall where assumed background knowledge is absent.
- **Kintsch (1988, 1998) — Construction–Integration / the *situation model*:** a reader builds a *textbase* (what the words say) and a *situation model* (what it means in the world). **The situation model cannot form without the knowledge the text assumes.** A student with no Jacobean/medieval knowledge can decode *Macbeth*'s words but cannot build the situation model that yields a valid interpretation. **McNamara et al. (1996):** high-knowledge readers generate knowledge-based inferences low-knowledge readers cannot.

## 3. Why ungrounded interpretation fails — AND the one judgement call
Valid inference IS the situation model doing work; without domain knowledge there is no situation model, so an "interpretation" is a guess unconstrained by the text's world. **BUT:** the stance "a random interpretation earns *no marks* and *shouldn't*" is an **assessment-validity / pedagogical decision** (keyed to GCSE examiner reward), **not** a lab finding. Reader-response (Rosenblatt 1978) legitimises reader-generated meaning. Research supports "grounded readings are higher-quality/higher-inference" — it does NOT prove "ungrounded = zero." Frame the marks claim as a design decision, honestly.

## 4. Build knowledge without just telling
- **Generative learning — Fiorella & Mayer (2015); Wittrock (1974):** learners must actively construct meaning from supplied material; passive facts don't transfer. Supplying the *knowledge* while the student forms the *interpretation* IS the generative prescription (and the ownership line).
- **Elaborative interrogation — Pressley et al. (1987); Dunlosky et al. (2013):** "why would that be true?" → durable learning (rated moderate-high utility). This is the "derive the CONCEPT" step.
- **Self-explanation — Chi et al. (1994). Retrieval + elaboration — Roediger & Karpicke (2006); Karpicke & Blunt (2011):** read-to-learn then retrieve/use beats re-study. Maps to **Library READING (encode) → derive concept (retrieve + elaborate).**

## 5. Cognitive-load / timing caveat
- **Sweller (1988); Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas (1998):** a novice mid-planning is near working-memory capacity; dumping context-building INTO the interpretation step adds extraneous load → overwhelm.
- **Mayer's pre-training principle (2009):** build component knowledge BEFORE the main task, freeing its load for reasoning → **build context before the interpretation rung, not during** (and cap the flood — the 3-insight budget).
- **Expertise-reversal (Kalyuga et al. 2003):** context-building that helps a novice HARMS a strong student → don't force a grade-7 who holds the context down the knowledge-building rungs (ties to §2 ability calibration).

## 6. Synthesis
Interpretation quality is a function of the situation model (Kintsch), which cannot form without domain knowledge (Willingham; Recht & Leslie; Hirsch) — so a reading built on no context is a guess. The remedy is not to lecture (un-activated schema doesn't stick — Ausubel; Bransford & Johnson) but to **activate first (ask), supply knowledge generatively (Fiorella & Mayer; read-to-learn), and have the student derive the concept via elaborative interrogation** (Pressley; Chi) — sequenced as **pre-training, before the interpretation step** to protect working memory (Sweller; Mayer). The lone judgement call is the "ungrounded = no marks" stance.
