# UK Exam System — Verification of Neil's Claims (for the mastery article)

Neil made a set of claims on 29 Jul 2026 and asked for them to be checked before publication.
Checked 29 Jul 2026. **Two are refuted or unsupported — do not print those.**

## ✅ VERIFIED — safe to publish

### 1. Grade 9 is a higher standard than the old A*, and deliberately rarer
Ofqual blog, *Setting grade 9 in new GCSEs* (Cath Jadhav, Associate Director, Standards and
Comparability, 5 April 2017) — https://ofqual.blog.gov.uk/2017/04/05/setting-grade-9-in-new-gcses/
Verbatim:
> "**Grade 9 is not the same as A\*; it's a new grade, designed to recognise the very highest
> performing students.**"
> "**So there will be fewer grade 9s than there are currently A\*s.**"

### 2. The grade 9 formula — about 1 in 5 of the top students
Same source, verbatim:
> "This approach will mean that across all subjects (when all GCSEs are graded 9 to 1) **about 20%
> of those students achieving grade 7 or above will achieve a grade 9**."
> "The formula is: **Percentage of those achieving at least a grade 7 who will be awarded a grade 9
> = 7% + 0.5 × (percentage of students awarded grade 7 and above)**"

### 3. The 2025 numbers
Ofqual blog, *GCSE and level 1 and 2 results 2025 at a glance* (21 Aug 2025):
> "Outcomes at **grade 7 and above are 21.8%** compared with 21.6% in 2024, and outcomes at
> **grade 4 and above are 67.1%** compared with 67.4% in 2024."
- **Grade 9 = 5.1% of GCSE entries in England, 2025** (reported figure).
- Applying Ofqual's formula to 21.8% gives 7 + (0.5 × 21.8) = 17.9% of that 21.8% ≈ **3.9%**.
- ⚠ **The two figures differ** (5.1% actual vs ~3.9% formula-implied) because the formula is applied
  per subject to a matched 16-year-old cohort, and entry profiles vary by subject. **Safest public
  phrasing: "around one in twenty entries earns a grade 9, and only about one in five of the
  students who reach grade 7 go on to reach grade 9."** Both halves are directly sourced.
- English Language specific (FFT Education Datalab, 21 Aug 2025): "**54.2% of 16-year-olds achieved
  grade 5 or above in English language** compared to 55.4% last year"; English Language grades
  became "slightly more polarised" in 2025, with grades 4–6 down 1.1 points and both 7–9 and 1–3 up.
### 3b. ⭐ ENGLISH-SPECIFIC FIGURES — obtained from the primary source (29 Jul 2026)
**Source: JCQ, "Table 9 — GCSE Full Course results for main grade set and subjects, 16 year olds,
England, Northern Ireland and Wales, 2025", sheet Table 9a (England), published 21 August 2025.**
Both sexes combined, 16-year-olds, England:

| Subject | Entries | Grade 9 | 9–8 | 9–7 | 9–4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **English Language** | 623,963 | **2.9%** | 9.0% | **19.5%** | 70.6% |
| **English Literature** | 594,229 | **3.6%** | 9.7% | **20.3%** | 74.2% |
| English Language (2024) | 630,837 | 2.9% | 8.8% | 19.0% | 71.2% |
| English Literature (2024) | 601,317 | 3.6% | 9.8% | 20.2% | 73.9% |

Derived (subtraction, English Language 2025): **grade 8 alone ≈ 6.1%**, **grade 7 alone ≈ 10.5%**.

⚠ **Note the gap:** grade 9 in English Language (2.9%) is well BELOW the all-subject figure (5.1% of
entries). **Neil's recollection of "3% to 4.5%" was right for English** — better than the headline
number. Always use the subject-specific figure in an English article; the all-subject number
overstates how common a grade 9 in English is.

### 4. The 2017 reform made GCSEs harder and exam-focused
gov.uk, *Get the facts: GCSE reform* (updated 26 Jan 2018):
> "New GCSEs in English language, English literature and maths will be taught in schools in England
> from September 2015, with the first results issued in August 2017."
> "There will be **new, more demanding content**, which has been developed by government and the
> exam boards."
> "Resit opportunities will only be available each November in English language and maths."

### 5. What the exam actually rewards in writing — from our own protocols
Source of truth: `protocols/aqa/language2/modules/knowledge-mark-scheme-lang2.md` (AQA 8700).
- **AO5 (Content and Organisation, 24 marks):** "Communicate clearly, effectively and imaginatively,
  selecting and adapting tone, style and register for different forms, purposes and audiences.
  Organise information and ideas, using structural and grammatical features to support coherence and
  cohesion of texts."
- **AO6 (Technical Accuracy, 16 marks):** "Students must use a range of vocabulary and sentence
  structures for clarity, purpose and effect, with accurate spelling and punctuation."
- Top band (AO5 Level 4, 19–24): "Communication is **convincing and compelling**…"
  Top band (AO6 Level 4, 13–16): "Sentence demarcation is **consistently secure and consistently
  accurate**… **Wide range of punctuation** used with a high level of accuracy… **full range of
  appropriate sentence forms** for effect… **Standard English consistently**… with **secure control
  of complex grammatical structures**."
- **This is Neil's point, evidenced:** every one of those is a *skill demonstrated on the day* —
  none of it is recall of content. 40 marks of Question 5 are awarded for how well the student
  writes, not for what they remember.

### 6. Skills are built by practice with feedback and correction — Ericsson
- Ericsson, K. A., *Deliberate Practice and the Acquisition and Maintenance of Expert Performance in
  Medicine and Related Domains* (2004), doi:10.1097/00001888-200410001-00022 — ~2,969 citations.
- Verbatim from the abstract: "Nobody becomes an outstanding professional without experience, but
  **extensive experience does not invariably lead people to become experts.**"
- That single sentence is the whole argument for why "more essays" is not the same as improvement,
  and why targeted correction of specific weaknesses is what moves a grade.

## ⛔ REFUTED — do NOT publish

### 7. "Japan, Finland and China are far ahead of the UK"
**Not supported by PISA 2022.** The OECD's own reporting places the **United Kingdom above the OECD
average in mathematics, reading and science — in the same statement as Finland and Japan**. The UK
also appears in the list of ten systems combining a large share of basic proficiency with high
socio-economic fairness (with Canada, Denmark, Finland, Hong Kong, Ireland, Japan, Korea, Latvia,
Macao). Singapore led reading on 543 against an OECD average of 476. **Mainland China did not
participate as a single entity in PISA 2022.**
→ **Drop this argument entirely.** It is the one claim in the brief a teacher could refute in a
single search, and the article does not need it. The argument stands on the time/mastery conflict
alone.
Source: OECD, *PISA 2022 Results (Volume I)*, "How did countries perform in PISA?"

## ⚠ UNVERIFIED — do not state as fact

### 8. "Coursework was removed because students were getting too much help / unrealistic marks"
Plausible and widely believed, and controlled assessment reliability was certainly part of the
public debate — **but not verified in this pass.** gov.uk documents the *what* ("more demanding
content", exam-focused structure) without stating this *why*. If the article wants this point, it
needs a primary source (an Ofqual or DfE consultation response). Otherwise, state only what is
sourced: the reformed GCSE is assessed by terminal examination with more demanding content.

### 9. "Grade 9 is effectively an A**"
A reasonable lay gloss, and consistent with Ofqual's "not the same as A*… fewer grade 9s than A*s" —
**but Ofqual never says "A\*\*".** Write it as Ofqual does: a new, higher grade above the old A*.

### 10. "Grade 9 was introduced to close the gap between GCSE and A level"
Not verified. Ofqual's stated purpose is to "recognise the very highest performing students". Do not
attribute the A-level-gap motive without a source.

## THE ARGUMENT THAT SURVIVES (and it is strong)
1. Schools work to a fixed calendar — they must cover the syllabus in the time available.
   Neither hero nor villain: it is the job.
2. The exam does not mainly test coverage. In AQA English Language Q5, **40 marks** go to how
   clearly, accurately and effectively the student *writes* (AO5 + AO6) — a skill, performed on the day.
3. Skills are built by practice with specific feedback and correction, not by exposure alone
   (Ericsson).
4. Grade 9 is deliberately scarce — around 1 in 20 entries, about 1 in 5 of those who reach grade 7.
5. Therefore a family aiming at the top grades needs something the calendar cannot provide:
   time that varies with the student, and correction targeted at that student's specific weaknesses.
