# Help-Seeking & Corrective Feedback — Research Basis
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **For:** WML planning §9 (student-callable help budget + corrections). Applied in `PLANNING-PROTOCOL-AUDIT-AND-PLAN-2026-07-18.md` §9.

---

## PART 1 — Student-callable help with a spendable budget
1. **Help-seeking is a metacognitive skill; the TYPE matters.** Nelson-Le Gall (1981, 1985): **instrumental** help-seeking (the minimum needed to then succeed alone — a hint, a principle) = adaptive; **executive** (asking for the answer to dodge the work) = maladaptive. Goal: shape toward instrumental. Karabenick & Newman: adaptive help-seeking is a self-regulatory competence to *encourage*, not suppress.
2. **Free help is abused; scarcity is the corrective.** Aleven & Koedinger: ~72% of help-seeking actions were unproductive (hint-abuse + help-avoidance). Roll et al. (*Help Tutor*; "Help helps, but only so much"): behaviour can be improved but the learning effect is bounded. → an *unlimited* insight button reproduces the abuse; a **finite spendable** insight converts reflex → decision (the instrumental/executive discriminator).
3. **Novices choose help BADLY → SHARED control.** Corbalan, Kester & Kirschner (2008): shared control (system constrains the option set, learner picks within it) beat both pure program- and pure learner-control; pure learner control is worst for low-prior-knowledge / low-SRL students (our struggling cohort). → the student picks *within* a code-bounded set; must NOT override the contingent-shift rung.
4. **A CHOSEN resource engages more than a PUSHED one.** Deci & Ryan (SDT — autonomy is a basic need). Patall, Cooper & Robinson (2008) meta-analysis (41 studies): choice reliably raised intrinsic motivation, effort, performance; effect strongest at **2–4 successive choices** (more dilutes). → supports both the callable insight and a ~3-option help-type menu.

**Recommendations (→ §9.1/9.2):** default stays system-PUSH; student-PULL is a bounded overlay that FEEDS the code's rung logic (never overrides — the menu picks *which unlocked move* is delivered; code still governs escalation eligibility). ONE shared code-counted "near-give" budget (not two — a separate student budget stacks to over-help). Cap = per-question sub-cap of 1, under a per-paper ceiling (~3–4). The "expert insight" menu option = L3/L4 (draws from the budget); "explain further" (re-hint) + "ask more questions" (Socratic) are FREE. Menu on detected struggle only. Frame the spend as agency.

## PART 2 — Corrections done right (the WRONG regime)
1. **Target TASK/PROCESS, never SELF.** Hattie & Timperley (2007): feedback works at task/process/self-regulation/self levels; **self-level (praise/person criticism) is least effective**. Kluger & DeNisi (1996) meta-analysis (607 effect sizes): mean effect positive (d≈.41) but **>⅓ of interventions DECREASED performance** — the harm cases direct attention to the self, not the task.
2. **Never a bare "wrong" — elaborated + response-specific.** Shute (2008): verification ("correct/incorrect") < **elaborated** feedback (why it's wrong + cues + worked correction); nonevaluative, supportive, timely, specific — state what/how/why. Immediate for factual/procedural errors.
3. **Errors-as-learning protect motivation.** Yeager et al. (2014) "wise feedback" (high standards + assurance the student can meet them → more revision, better drafts). Dweck growth-mindset. Metcalfe (2016) errorful learning: generating an error THEN getting feedback aids retention; **hypercorrection (Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001): high-confidence errors are corrected MOST readily** — the surprise recruits attention.
4. **The ownership line = correct FACTS, question the GROUNDING of interpretations.** Safe corrective feedback is task/process-directed. A literary *conclusion* is often not falsifiable, but its *grounding* is → correcting a conclusion imposes the tutor's reading (injection); correcting the grounding stays on the process level (legitimate). Maps to §8.

**Recommendations (→ §9.3):** add **WRONG** as a third regime (alongside weak-but-owned → one push then accept; failure → the ladder). WRONG fires ONLY on a VERIFIABLE error (misread of the text · false context/biographical fact · misidentified technique) — discriminator: falsifiable against text/fact? FORM (3 parts): name the error specifically · why · the fix. FRAMING: correct the error not the student; wise-feedback pair; exploit the hypercorrection window (don't soften a confident wrong answer). INTERPRETATION CAVEAT: correct the GROUNDING not the reading. Grep-able B-check: WRONG keys on a falsifiable-error detector, never interpretive content.
