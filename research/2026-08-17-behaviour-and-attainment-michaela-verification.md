# Behaviour and attainment — what survives checking, and what does not

**Date:** 2026-08-17 (**revised same day** after Neil corrected the framing — see §0)
**Commissioned by:** Neil, for a blog article on behaviour (blog FIXLIST **B29**)
**Question:** Michaela Community School gets extraordinary GCSE results from a disadvantaged intake
and names behaviour as a foundation. Does the evidence support that, and what is the honest version
of the claim?

**Method:** School's own published results page; DfE performance tables; Ofsted; DfE National
Behaviour Survey; EEF Toolkit; the instructional-time and classroom-management literature; the
phone-ban literature and its replications; the media-multitasking literature; published criticism of
the school.

---

## §0 ⭐ THE CONSTRUCT ERROR IN THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS DOC — read this first

The first version of this document answered Neil's question with the **EEF Toolkit's "behaviour
interventions" strand (+3 months)** and concluded that the evidence for behaviour was "real but
modest". **That was the wrong evidence base, and the conclusion it produced was wrong.**

**EEF "behaviour interventions" means targeted programmes aimed at pupils with challenging
behaviour** — mentoring, anger management, individual support plans, whole-school approaches to
*reducing disruption in pupils who are disruptive*. Neil is not describing that. His words:

> *"When I say behaviour interventions, I don't mean shouting at a student or being angry. What I
> mean is teaching them the behaviour that produces the optimal result."*

That is a different construct with a different and **substantially stronger** literature:
**instructional time, engaged time, and the explicit teaching of classroom routines.** Applying the
remediation evidence to a routines claim understated it by a wide margin.

⚠ **Lesson for the next model: "behaviour" is at least three separate literatures** — (1)
remediating disruptive pupils, (2) sanctions/discipline policy, (3) taught routines that protect and
raise engaged learning time. Michaela's silent transitions are (3). Most public argument about the
school is about (2). **Do not answer a question about one with evidence from another.**

## VERDICT (revised)

**Neil's principle survives checking, and the mechanism he named is the one the research points to.**

The claim that holds is not "strict is better" — Neil explicitly is not making it, and the evidence
would not support it. The claim that holds is: **the behaviours a school teaches determine how much
of the day is actually spent learning, and time actually spent learning is one of the strongest
predictors of achievement once prior attainment is accounted for.**

⭐ **And this is the same pillar as article 1, approached from the other side.** Article 1 is built
on **Carroll's 1963 model — learning is a function of time spent divided by time needed.** Article 1
attacks the *denominator* (mastery: give each student the time they need). **Michaela's silent
transitions attack the numerator: manufacture more time actually spent.** Article 6 is therefore the
sibling of article 1, not a new pillar — which is editorially much stronger than a standalone piece
about a famous school.

## CONFIRMED — safe to print

### A. The results (source: the school's own results page, 2025)
- **Over 80% of all GCSE grades were 7–9.** ⚠ *Grades*, not pupils. Neil's recollection was right;
  the precision matters, because "80% of pupils got grade 7+" is a different, unverified claim.
- **Over 40% of pupils achieved five or more grade 9s.**
- **100% of pupils passed both English and Maths.** 99% of grades 4–9; 97% 5–9.
- ⚠ **Progress 8 = 2.55 — the highest in the country for the third consecutive year — but this is
  the 2024 figure, NOT 2025.** ⭐ **Progress 8 cannot be calculated for 2024/25 or 2025/26 at ANY
  school in England**, because those cohorts never sat Key Stage 2 tests (cancelled in the
  pandemic), so no prior-attainment baseline exists. **Do not print 2.55 next to the 2025 grades
  without saying which year each belongs to** — the first cut of the article did exactly that and
  it read as one year's result. The selection rebuttal stands on the 2024 data.

### B. Why "they must select the brightest" fails — the load-bearing point
- ✅ **Non-selective — confirmed from the government's own record**, not the school's page: DfE Get
  Information about Schools, URN 140862, *"Admissions policy: Non-selective"*, 747 pupils, free
  school, Brent. This is the fact the whole argument rests on, so it is worth having from source.
- **32%–39.7% eligible for free school meals** (varies by census date); around half attract pupil
  premium.
- **Progress 8 is measured from each pupil's own Key Stage 2 starting point.** It is
  prior-attainment-adjusted by construction, so a school cannot post 2.55 by admitting able
  children — able children are already expected to do well and the measure accounts for it.
- **Ofsted Outstanding in 2017 and again in 2023**; behaviour and attitudes **"exemplary"**.

### C. ⭐ Time is the mechanism — and this is the strongest evidence in the file
- **David Berliner's work on Academic Learning Time.** The finding, stated in the literature almost
  exactly as Neil states it: *the variability in allocated time, engaged time and academic learning
  time is **the most potent explanatory variable** for variability in student achievement, **after
  initial aptitude has been removed as a predictor**.* The "after aptitude removed" clause is what
  makes it the same logic as Progress 8.
- **The critical distinction: allocated time bears only a small relationship to achievement;
  ENGAGED time bears a much stronger one.** Hours in the building are nearly worthless as a
  predictor. Minutes actually working are not. **This is the whole article in one sentence**, and it
  is why "more lessons" and "longer school day" are weak levers while transitions and attention are
  strong ones.
- **Jacob Kounin (1970)** — the founding classroom-management study, 49 classrooms videotaped for a
  full day with pupil work-involvement coded **every 12 seconds**. His discriminators between
  effective and ineffective teachers were not sanctions but **momentum** (starting promptly, keeping
  activities moving, closing them efficiently), **smoothness** (no jarring interruptions to the flow
  of the lesson) and **withitness**. ⭐ **Transitions are named in the founding research as a
  primary variable.** Michaela's line-up is a Kounin intervention, and it long predates the school.
- **Marzano's meta-analysis puts improved classroom management at an effect size of ≈0.52** — enough
  to move an average pupil from roughly the 50th to the 70th percentile. Compare the EEF's
  behaviour-*interventions* figure of +3 months (≈0.2). **The routines construct is roughly twice
  the size of the remediation construct.** Cite both and name which is which.
- **AERO (Australian Education Research Organisation)** publishes an explainer specifically on
  teaching routines: well-established routines let pupils follow them with little teacher
  involvement, **reducing interruptions and increasing teaching time**, and explicitly teaching and
  modelling them **frees working memory for learning**. ⭐ This is the citation for Neil's exact
  formulation — you *teach* the behaviour, you do not merely punish its absence.
- **DfE National Behaviour Survey:** teachers *affected by misbehaviour* reported losing about
  **7 minutes of every 30** of lesson time; **25% reported more than 10 minutes lost per 30**.
  ⚠ Quote as *teachers affected*, never "every classroom in England".

### D. ⭐ The attention half — this is Neil's YouTube student, and it is well evidenced
- **Sana, Weston & Cepeda (2013), *Computers & Education*.** Multitasking on a laptop during a
  lecture **lowered test performance by 11%**. Sitting **in view of a multitasking peer lowered it
  by 17%** — *worse than doing it yourself*. ⭐ The distraction is contagious, which is the
  strongest single argument for a whole-class norm rather than an individual rule.
- **Ravizza, Uitvlugt & Fenn (2017), *Psychological Science* ("Logged In and Zoned Out").** 507
  students; **non-academic internet use predicted final exam performance even after controlling for
  intelligence and motivation.** Using the internet *for class purposes* did not help.
- This is engaged time, not allocated time. A student scrolling YouTube during a lesson is present,
  counted, and learning nothing — the exact gap Berliner's distinction describes.

## REFUTED / NOT SUPPORTED — do not print

1. ⛔ **"Strict discipline causes high grades."** Neil is not claiming this and neither should the
   article. Michaela changes many variables at once and no study isolates sanctions as the cause.
   The defensible claim is about **taught routines and protected time**, not severity.
2. ⛔ **"Ban phones and grades go up."** ⭐ **But state the reconciliation, because it is genuinely
   interesting and it is the honest version.** Beland & Murphy (2015, 91 English schools) found
   bans raised scores ~6.4% of a standard deviation; **Kessel et al. (2020) found no effect in
   Sweden**; the **2025 SMART Schools study (Lancet Reg. Health – Europe, 1,200+ adolescents, 30
   schools) found no difference in English or maths attainment** between banning and non-banning
   schools. **The school-POLICY evidence is weak. The individual-DEVICE-during-learning evidence
   (§D) is strong.** Those are different questions, and conflating them is why the public argument
   goes in circles. Say so.
3. ⛔ **"Self-discipline beats IQ."** Duckworth & Seligman (2005) is quoted everywhere for this, but
   **later work found intelligence the stronger predictor**, and only intelligence predicted
   *change* in attainment over time. Add to the banned list.
4. ⛔ **Any instruction to run a household like a no-excuses school.** We have no standing for that.
5. ⛔ **Naming or praising the headmistress** (Neil's constraint, B29). Cite the school, its
   published results, its Ofsted report.

## CAVEATS THE ARTICLE MUST CARRY (our house "two things must be said honestly" beat)

- **Cohort size and curriculum breadth.** ~120 pupils per year group; analysis of similar-sized
  schools finds **the more exam subjects offered, the higher the average Progress 8**, and Michaela
  is an outlier on a narrow offer. A real choice with real costs.
- **Sixth-form entry is selective** (grade 8 required in the chosen subjects), and post-16 progress
  has been reported as around average — so the sixth form is not the same population.
- **One school is one data point.** The Michaela numbers are descriptive. The causal weight in this
  article is carried by Kounin, Berliner, Marzano, Sana and Ravizza — not by Michaela.

## APPLICATION — the article (blog #6)

**Working title:** *The lesson that starts four minutes late, every time*
**Alternatives:** *Nobody is judged on hours in the classroom* · *What the country's top-performing
school actually changed*

**Angle.** Not discipline. **Time.** Open with the arithmetic Neil gave: one student arrives three
minutes late, the teacher cannot start, another minute or two goes on settling, and five minutes are
gone. Repeat across five or six transitions a day and the loss is structural, not incidental. Then
the reveal: this is the *numerator* of the same equation article 1 was about. Article 1 said some
students need more time than the timetable gives them. This one says the timetable is not even
delivering the time it claims to.

**Shape:**
1. The four-minute lesson. Concrete, arithmetical, no adjectives.
2. Michaela's numbers and the Brent context — then kill the objection with Progress 8.
3. What the school actually does, framed as Neil frames it: **they teach the behaviours, they do not
   just punish their absence** (AERO on routines; Kounin on momentum and transitions).
4. The evidence that this is about time, not temperament: Berliner (allocated vs engaged), Marzano
   ≈0.52 — and be explicit that this is a bigger effect than the behaviour-remediation programmes
   people usually mean by "behaviour".
5. The attention half — Sana's 11% / 17%, Ravizza's 507 students. Neil's YouTube observation,
   anonymised and generalised, never as a student anecdote.
6. Honest limits: the phone-ban reconciliation, the narrow curriculum, one data point.
7. What it means at home — the same variable, supplied domestically: work starts at a fixed time,
   in a fixed place, without negotiation, with the phone in another room. **Described as what the
   evidence supports, never as a parenting instruction.**
8. Sophicly bridge: a fixed weekly rhythm, work marked every week, no negotiation about what comes
   next — engaged time by design.
