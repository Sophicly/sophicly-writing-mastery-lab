## Truby — The Anatomy of Story (22 Steps)

### Protagonist
- One clear goal early; reader "rides the train" toward it. Bond via desire + moral problem, not surface traits. Let the hero seem to hide something. No sidekick more interesting than the hero. Keep slight distance so we can see them change.
- Term: hero; "desire and need".

### Flaw / Weakness ("Weakness and Need")
- Hero held back by a serious flaw ("need") they must overcome. TWO layers: psychological need (hurts only the hero) AND moral need (makes the hero hurt others). Show the moral flaw in action early. Build moral from psychological. Or take a virtue pushed too far. Hero must NOT know their flaw at the start.
- Examples: The Verdict (Frank: alcoholism + using people); The Godfather (Michael: overconfident → ruthless).
- Term: psychological need vs moral need.

### Wound / Ghost
- Past event that still haunts and paralyses the hero — the SOURCE of the weakness. Ghost and weakness must line up (two faces of one wound). Withhold it — reveal slowly, never dump up front. Rare variation: "paradise" hero with no ghost, shattered by outside attack.
- Examples: It's a Wonderful Life (George's fear of Potter harming family); Hamlet (murdered father).
- Term: the ghost.

### Inciting Incident
- The small moment connecting need to desire; jolts the paralysed hero into action. Best shape: "out of the frying pan, into the fire" — feels like escape, is actually the worst trouble.
- Examples: Sunset Boulevard (blown tyre → the trap); Casablanca (Ilsa & Laszlo walk in).
- Term: inciting event.

### Goal / Desire
- ONE specific concrete goal, the spine. Desire (outer) ≠ need (inner). Chasing the outer goal forces the inner need.
- Examples: The Godfather (revenge + protect family); It's a Wonderful Life (see the world, build things).
- Term: desire / desire line.

### Obstacle / Opponent
- The NECESSARY opponent: best able to attack the hero's greatest weakness. Competes for the SAME goal. Often a hidden "true" opponent behind an obvious front. Dark mirror. Four-corner opposition for depth.
- Examples: The Godfather (visible Sollozzo, true opponent Barzini); A Streetcar Named Desire (Blanche vs Stanley + Mitch/Stella).
- Term: true opponent; necessary opponent; four-corner opposition.

### Stakes
- Raise by a stronger, harder-to-identify opponent. Trap the hero (world closes in — "slavery to greater slavery"). Tie stakes to the moral question (defeat costs identity, not just the goal). Strongest = moral jeopardy held open.
- Example: The Verdict weakened by moral turnaround coming too early.
- Term: moral jeopardy.

### Structure / Spine
- Work BACKWARDS from the endpoint (self-revelation). Frame = self-revelation ↔ weakness/need ↔ desire. Self-revelation late, psychological + moral, proven by a new moral action. Moral argument played through action, not preaching. Everything converges at the battle.
- 22 steps (order): self-revelation/need/desire · ghost & story world · weakness & need · inciting event · desire · ally · opponent/mystery · fake-ally opponent · changed desire & motive · plan · opponent's plan & counterattack · drive · attack by ally · apparent defeat · second revelation & decision · audience revelation · third revelation & decision · gate/gauntlet/visit to death · battle · self-revelation · moral decision · new equilibrium.
- Checkable beats: apparent defeat → gauntlet/visit to death → battle → self-revelation → moral decision → new equilibrium.

### Plot / Genre
- Test: "someone did something to get what they wanted, and why." Build an ORGANIC plot from these characters, not a borrowed template. Genres = familiar bones, fresh skin. Detective/thriller: hide the opponent, plant a mystery. Multistrand: every strand a variation on one theme.

## McKee — Story

### Protagonist
- Strong WILL — single-minded desire + drive. EMPATHY not sympathy (recognise ourselves; likeability optional). Reveal true self through CHOICE UNDER PRESSURE, not description. Only as gripping as the opposition.
- Examples: Casablanca (Rick's "I stick my neck out for no man" vs his actions); Ordinary People (quiet Calvin).
- Terms: empathy vs sympathy, willpower, choice under pressure.

### Flaw
- Build on a CONTRADICTION (inner conflict) not one trait. Gap between outer mask (characterisation) and true character. Prove the flaw in action under pressure.
- Example: Macbeth (ambition warring with guilt).
- Terms: character vs characterization; dimension = contradiction.

### Wound / Backstory
- McKee treats lightly — "backstory", not "wound". Past hurt surfaces as a hidden inner need beneath the stated goal; sensed through behaviour, never explained.
- Example: The Crying Game (Fergus's need to love beneath the politics).

### Inciting Incident (PRIORITY)
- Event that radically UPSETS THE BALANCE of the protagonist's life. Arouses desire to restore balance → conceives an Object of Desire. Forces a reaction. Raises the Major Dramatic Question. Must be germane; central-plot one must happen ON THE PAGE. Causally locked to the Climax ("because of the II, the Climax had to happen").
- Examples: Ordinary People (mother scraping food into the disposal); Jaws (shark kills → sheriff must destroy it).
- Terms: Inciting Incident, upsets the balance, Object of Desire, the Spine, Major Dramatic Question.

### Goal / Desire
- Clear conscious desire (Object of Desire), stated as an action. Layer an unconscious desire that contradicts it. It is the Spine, unbroken II→Climax.
- Terms: Object of Desire, conscious vs unconscious desire, the Spine.

### Obstacle / Antagonist (PRIORITY)
- THE PRINCIPLE OF ANTAGONISM: a protagonist can only be as compelling as the forces of antagonism make them. Forces = ALL forces opposing the will (inner, personal, social) — not just a villain. Make opposition more powerful & complex. Directly opposed, head-on. Push to the "negation of the negation" (worse than the mere opposite).
- Terms: Principle of Antagonism, forces of antagonism, negation of the negation.

### Stakes / Values (PRIORITY)
- Every scene/story turns on a STORY VALUE that swings charge (life/death, love/hate, freedom/slavery, truth/lie). A scene "turns" only when the value FLIPS. Escalate through Points of No Return; the GAP between expectation and result. Raise stakes each beat. Drive to absolute, irreversible change at the Climax.
- Example: Casablanca (love placed in ever greater risk; the "married all along" reveal).
- Terms: story values, positive/negative charge, the Gap, Points of No Return, raising the stakes.

### Structure
- beats → scenes → sequences → acts → story. Each unit causes change. Scene = small value turn; act climax = major reversal; story climax = absolute irreversible change. Every turn powered by the gap + a Turning Point. Anchor on the Spine → inevitable ending. Unify causally, vary tone.

## Truby — The Anatomy of Genres (Step 5 archetypes/genres)

Universal shape beneath all: weakness/need → desire → opponent (attacks the weakness) → plan → battle → self-revelation → new equilibrium.

- **Horror** — vulnerable hero hunted by an overpowering monster (externalised dark side); plan = flee/survive; every gain has a terrible cost. Meaning: our flaws can enslave us. (Dracula, Alien, Halloween)
- **Action** — hero oppressed from outside wins freedom by fighting back; HOW they fight can corrupt them; escalating physical challenges → one-on-one battle. Meaning: freedom fought for, means can cost your soul. (Die Hard, Gladiator)
- **Myth / Hero's Journey** — leave ordinary world → trials/opponents → return transformed; outer journey mirrors inner. Often a "Chosen One". Meaning: life is a journey to understand yourself. (Odyssey, Star Wars, The Lion King)
- **Fantasy** — mundane hero crosses into a magical second world, returns able to live more fully; build a rich world (make-or-break); one "super magical moment". Meaning: realise your full potential. (Harry Potter, Wizard of Oz, LOTR)
- **Science Fiction / Dystopia** — hero struggles to stay a free individual inside an enslaving future society; define the world by its social stage; individual vs system; tech drives the plot. Dystopia = SF + Horror. Meaning: whether the individual survives the systems we build. (1984, Star Wars, Avatar)
- **Crime** — told from the criminal's side; establish the moral code early; the net tightens; justice via self-revelation. Meaning: morality & justice. (In Bruges, Breaking Bad)
- **Thriller** — ordinary hero pulled into a deadly web, must become a detective to survive; two tracks (solve the crime + overcome the personal weakness); true enemy is intimate. Meaning: uncover who your true enemies are. (North by Northwest, Silence of the Lambs)
- **Detective** — opponent HIDDEN till the end; reader/detective solve together; most reveals of any genre; a blind spot. Meaning: how we know truth. (Sherlock Holmes)
- **Love** — two people of mutual value overcome a private fear of love + obstacles; open with the GAZE; "yes/no" every beat; end on communion OR tragic separation. Meaning: the self grows by joining another. (When Harry Met Sally, Pride & Prejudice)
- **Coming-of-Age** — young hero grows up via psychological/moral development + hard education in how society works. Meaning: creating the self. (Jane Eyre, David Copperfield)

Combine genres: pick ONE primary (its beat-sequence = backbone), layer 1–2 secondary. Star Wars = Myth + SF + Action. Genre "ladder" = each genre's underlying life-question; pick the one matching your intended meaning.

## Hauge — Storytelling Made Easy (Step 4 spine + goal/inner journey)

### Six-step story spine (his "spine of every story")
1. **Setup ("Before Picture")** — the hero's ordinary, stuck life before; builds empathy, sets the "before".
2. **Crisis ("Wakeup Call" + the Goal)** — one event breaks inertia → hero forms a clear GOAL; launches the story.
3. **Pursuit / Taking Action** — hero commits, first concrete actions toward the goal.
4. **Conflict / Obstacles** — real obstacles, setbacks, doubts; struggle makes success meaningful.
5. **Climax ("Finish Line")** — overcome the final biggest obstacle, cross the line; the payoff.
6. **Aftermath ("After Picture")** — the transformed new life; proves the change was real.

### Outer Goal ("visible goal / finish line")
- A single specific finish line the reader can PICTURE — not "get rich" but "a bank balance of £100,000". Tangible/measurable. State it early (in the Crisis). Cut anything that doesn't serve it.
- Examples: £100,000 statement; Ironman finish line; a beach in Fiji.

### Inner Journey ("Identity → Essence")
- Identity = the mask/false self (protects from past pain); Essence = the true self. Driven by a WOUND → false belief + FEAR. The fear/flaw is the real inner obstacle; growth = the COURAGE to drop the mask. Fears must be universal ones the reader shares. (Optional layer — a strong outer goal can carry a story alone.)
- Examples: Good Will Hunting (beaten as a child → believes he's worthless); Shrek (scares people off to hide fear of rejection).

### Empathy (the "unbreakable bond") — four methods, applied when the hero is introduced
1. Sympathy — undeserved misfortune ("we empathise with people we feel sorry for").
2. Jeopardy — something vital at risk ("we identify with people we worry about").
3. Likeability — kind, generous, good-hearted.
4. Power/competence — genuinely good at something admirable (we want to be them).

### Stakes
- Through jeopardy: the hero must stand to lose something of vital importance. Deepest stake = losing HOPE itself ("if this fails, I'm condemned to the life I have now"). A deadline/ticking clock raises stakes.

### Logline — not a formula in this book. Nearest = the vivid Goal/finish line: name the hero, their specific finish line, and what's at risk.

## Russin & Downs — Screenplay: Writing the Picture (scene craft, show-don't-tell — Steps 6+)

### Three-act structure
- Act 1: world + Disturbance (inciting incident) → ends when the hero takes action toward a goal.
- Act 2: RISING ACTION — each obstacle bigger ("path of most resistance"); apparent wins → worse trouble; ends on the DARK MOMENT (total failure).
- Act 3: ENLIGHTENMENT → CLIMAX (opposition defeated at peak tension) → CATHARSIS (short release). Ending must not linger + feel inevitable in hindsight.
- Terms: Disturbance, Rising Action, Dark Moment, Enlightenment, Catharsis.

### Conflict engine
- Character needs an incomplete life (a real desire). The desire must be blocked (barrier/obstacle/opponent). NO COMPROMISE (can't back down). Emotion ≠ conflict. Best conflict = motivated opposing desires (both believe they're right).

### Scene craft (PRIORITY for Steps 6+)
- A scene has a beginning/middle/end + ONE dramatic question + rising conflict → a climax leading to the next scene. ENTER LATE, GET OUT EARLY. The scene must TURN (value charged +/- at top, check at end; unchanged = nothing happened, cut it). Built from BEATS (each beat causes the next). Contrast between scenes raises tension.

### Show don't tell (PRIORITY)
- Write ONLY what we can see or hear. Turn inner states into concrete action ("haunted eyes drift to his manacled hands; clutches the tattered photo"). Strong visual needs no thought-hint. Context carries meaning. One small revealing detail > broad explanation.

### Stakes & tension
- Something to lose in every power struggle — name it. Stakes needn't be galactic (a small personal loss implying larger repercussions). #1 beginner failure = stakes too low. Rising action + ticking clock; outcome genuinely in doubt.

## Edson — The Story Solution (23 Actions): empathy + wound specialist

### Protagonist / Empathy ("character sympathy" — the BASIC NINE, stacked in the first ~10 min)
Courage (always — cowardice never allowed) · Unfair Injury (something unjust, NOT their fault — ~75% of films open this way) · Skill (good at something) · Funny · Just Plain Nice (a small kindness to someone weaker) · In Danger · Loved by friends/family · Hard Working · Obsessed (one driving goal). Stack several early; imperfect heroes bond MORE, but every flaw must be outweighed by sympathy tools.
- Examples: Erin Brockovich (unemployed single mum, honest, snubbed & injured); Men in Black (Agent K reassures frightened immigrants — nice + skill).
- Terms: character sympathy, the basic nine, Unfair Injury.

### Flaw
- Real weaknesses make the hero human. NEVER cowardice; never let everything bad be the hero's own fault. Always outweigh with sympathy tools. Deepest flaw = inner defensiveness (the shield).
- Example: Melvin in As Good As It Gets (cruel/OCD but "not his fault").

### Wound (Edson's signature — TRAUMA → SHIELD)
- ONE specific past TRAUMA — a single wound to the heart. A concrete past event so painful it left them isolated with a blind spot in how they treat others.
- The trauma creates a SHIELD — a self-protective attitude they hide behind to avoid being hurt again; the shield BLOCKS them from connecting. (This is the "ironic shield".)
- Never named in Act One — we only SEE it in behaviour. Secret rule: the hero can't win the outer goal until they drop the shield.
- Heal across Act Two: EXPRESS (say the problem out loud) → BATTLE (a bold action at the midpoint that FAILS, shield goes back up) → OVERCOME (a braver, honest action, drop the shield for good, prove they've changed). Each step a VISIBLE action, ideally risking self for others.
- Examples: Signs (wife killed → loses faith); Pretty Woman (told she was worthless → "I have all this money and nobody will help me").
- Terms: TRAUMA, SHIELD, Character Growth Arc, EXPRESS/BATTLE/OVERCOME; cites Hauge's identity→essence.

### Inciting Incident
- A specific problem "plops into the hero's lap" early, thrust on them by someone/something else (often an unfair injury), forcing a response. Then the Call to Adventure (opportunity/threat) they often first resist.
- Example: Erin Brockovich (boss lets her poke into one file).

### Goal / Desire
- A clear specific OUTER goal (concrete real-world thing) + an INNER need (drop the shield/heal the trauma) the hero doesn't know yet. The two must CONNECT: can't achieve the outer goal until the inner need is met. Both active and visible through action.
- Example: Sideways (wine trip outer / let go of ex-wife inner).

### Obstacle / Adversary
- A genuinely POWERFUL adversary (not a weak side-character). "Get mean" — make the worst happen. ESCALATE steadily. Make it PERSONAL (tangled with the wound). Hero must defeat them THEMSELVES.
- Example: Up (Muntz becomes a murderous personal threat).

### Stakes
- "What does the hero lose if they fail?" must be HUGE. Push to metaphorical life-or-death even in comedy/romance. Make us fear for the hero; threaten what we already care about. Raise as the story climbs.
- Example: Working Girl (losing = losing everything she's fought for).

### Structure (23 Hero Goal Sequences — load-bearing beats)
Act 1: establish hero + stack sympathy, Inciting Incident (early), Call to Adventure, plant trauma/shield → STUNNING SURPRISE #1 (throws hero into the special world, locks the Act-2 goal). Act 2: obsessive pursuit, escalating obstacles, EXPRESS → BATTLE (midpoint, fails) → OVERCOME → STUNNING SURPRISE #2 (destroys the plan). Act 3: the hero (not a rescuer) delivers the Obligatory Scene + Denouement.
- Terms: Hero Goal Sequence, Fresh News, Stunning Surprise #1/#2, Midpoint, Obligatory Scene, Denouement.
