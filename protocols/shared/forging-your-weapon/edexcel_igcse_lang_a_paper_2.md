#### **THE POEM (An Unknown Girl — Moniza Alvi)**

> In the evening bazaar  
> studded with neon  
> an unknown girl  
> is hennaing¹ my hand.  
> She squeezes a wet brown line  
> from a nozzle.  
> She is icing my hand,  
> which she steadies with hers  
> on her satin-peach knee.  
> In the evening bazaar  
> for a few rupees  
> an unknown girl  
> is hennaing my hand.  
> As a little air catches  
> my shadow-stitched kameez²  
> a peacock spreads its lines  
> across my palm.  
> Colours leave the street  
> float up in balloons.  
> Dummies in shop-fronts  
> tilt and stare  
> with their Western perms.  
> Banners for Miss India 1993,  
> for curtain cloth  
> and sofa cloth  
> canopy me.  
> I have new brown veins.  
> In the evening bazaar  
> very deftly  
> an unknown girl  
> is hennaing my hand.  
> I am clinging  
> to these firm peacock lines  
> like people who cling  
> to the sides of a train.  
> Now the furious streets  
> are hushed.  
> I'll scrape off  
> the dry brown lines  
> before I sleep,  
> reveal soft as a snail trail  
> the amber bird beneath.  
> It will fade in a week.  
> When India appears and reappears  
> I'll lean across a country  
> with my hands outstretched  
> longing for the unknown girl  
> in the neon bazaar.
>
> ¹ hennaing: the art of body decoration using a plant dye  
> ² kameez: loose fitting tunic

The Task: "How does the writer present the importance of traditional Indian culture in *An Unknown Girl*?

In your answer, you should write about:
• how the writer presents the cultural experience with the unknown girl
• how the writer presents feelings about the place
• the use of language and structure.

You should support your answer with close reference to the poem, including brief quotations." (30 marks)

PARAGRAPH 1:  
"The writer shows that traditional Indian culture is important by writing about henna. She says 'an unknown girl / is hennaing my hand', which shows the girl is putting henna on her hand. This is repeated three times to show it is important. She uses a metaphor when she says 'She is icing my hand', which makes it sound like a cake. There is also a peacock, which is a symbol of India. The 'Dummies in shop-fronts' have 'Western perms', which shows the West is in India too. At the end she says 'It will fade in a week', which is sad. This shows that she likes Indian culture and wants to keep it."

PARAGRAPH 2:  
"Alvi presents traditional Indian culture as precious because the speaker cannot keep it: like the henna on her hand, it is beautiful but it will not last. The refrain 'an unknown girl / is hennaing my hand' returns three times, so the ritual becomes the fixed point of the poem while the busy street shifts around it. Alvi even turns the art into a verb, 'hennaing', so the tradition is something still being done, not something remembered. The metaphor 'She is icing my hand' makes the craft sweet and familiar, yet it is a Western kitchen image, a hint that the speaker meets this culture as a visitor. Against the girl's skill, 'very deftly', stand the 'Dummies in shop-fronts' that 'tilt and stare / with their Western perms': the personification makes the manufactured, Westernised India look lifeless and judging, so the handmade tradition feels alive by contrast. The simile 'like people who cling / to the sides of a train' turns her hold on the 'firm peacock lines' into something urgent and dangerous. Then the short, end-stopped sentence 'It will fade in a week.' states the loss without decoration. The fading henna stands for a tradition slipping away from her. The poem therefore ends with the speaker far from the bazaar, 'longing for the unknown girl / in the neon bazaar'."

### **PHASE 4: THE ANATOMY OF THE WEAPON**

**"Why is Paragraph 2 a Weapon? (The TTECEA Structure)"**

Break down the winning paragraph to show the student the hidden structure:

1. **T (Topic Sentence):** Establishes a **concept/argument** — the culture is precious *because* the speaker cannot keep it — not just "the writer shows culture is important."  
2. **T (Technique):** Identifies methods precisely — *refrain*, *a noun turned into a verb* ('hennaing'), *metaphor*, *personification*, *simile*, *short end-stopped sentence*.  
3. **E (Evidence):** Embeds brief quotations tightly inside the argument, as the question asks ("including brief quotations"), not as standalone sentences.  
4. **C (Close Analysis):** Zooms in on single words — 'hennaing', 'icing', 'deftly', 'cling', 'fade', 'longing' — and reads what each one suggests in this poem.  
5. **E (Effect):** Explains what the reader feels and why — the handmade tradition feels *alive* next to the staring dummies; the plain line 'It will fade in a week.' lands as loss.  
6. **A (Author's Purpose):** Explains *why* Alvi shapes the poem this way — to show that a tradition passed on by hand can slip away from a person, and that the longing for it lasts longer than the henna does.

**Note for this question:** Paper 2 Question 1 is not marked for context (AO3), so the paragraph spends no words on history or the poet's life. All of its marks come from understanding (AO1) and analysis of language and structure (AO2).
