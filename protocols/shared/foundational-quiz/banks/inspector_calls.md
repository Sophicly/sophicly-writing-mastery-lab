### Quiz: An Inspector Calls

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The play is set in 1912 but was written in 1945. Why does Priestley choose this gap in time?
   * **Options:** A) Because he could not remember 1945, B) So the audience, knowing what came after 1912, can judge the Birlings' confident claims as badly wrong, C) Because 1912 was the year the play was first performed, D) To avoid mentioning either world war
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Writing after two world wars, Priestley lets the audience see through Birling's smug predictions — the gap fuels the play's dramatic irony.
   * **Why A:** The date choice is deliberate craft, not forgetfulness.
   * **Why C:** The play was first staged in 1945, not 1912; 1912 is only the setting.
   * **Why D:** The gap in fact points the audience straight towards the wars Birling dismisses.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Mr Birling calls the Titanic "absolutely unsinkable" and dismisses talk of war. What is the effect on the audience?
   * **Options:** A) They trust his judgement, B) They see he is confidently wrong, which undermines his views on everything else, C) They believe war will never come, D) They think he is joking
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The audience knows the Titanic sank and war came — so Birling's certainty exposes him as unreliable, including on responsibility.
   * **Why A:** His being so plainly wrong is designed to destroy the audience's trust in him.
   * **Why C:** The point is the opposite: the audience knows war did come.
   * **Why D:** Birling is entirely serious, which is what makes the irony bite.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Priestley was a socialist, and the play argues for social responsibility against a "every man for himself" attitude.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Priestley uses the Inspector to press the idea that people are responsible for one another, challenging Birling's self-interest.
   * **WhyWrong:** Priestley did hold socialist views — the play sets collective responsibility against Birling's belief that a man should look after only himself.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** In the rigid Edwardian class system of 1912, why is a working-class woman like Eva Smith so vulnerable?
   * **Options:** A) She has powerful friends to protect her, B) She has little money, no security and no power against wealthy employers, C) She is related to the Birlings, D) She can easily find another good job
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. With no savings, no vote and no protection, a working-class woman was at the mercy of those with wealth and influence.
   * **Why A:** She is friendless and isolated — that is central to her powerlessness.
   * **Why C:** Eva is a stranger to the Birlings, not family — they simply use their power over her.
   * **Why D:** Once dismissed and blacklisted, she finds it almost impossible to get work.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What is the central message the Inspector delivers about responsibility?
   * **Options:** A) Everyone should look after only themselves, B) People are responsible for one another and their actions affect others, C) Only the government is responsible for the poor, D) Responsibility belongs to the young alone
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Inspector teaches that private choices ripple outwards — each character's treatment of Eva contributed to her death.
   * **Why A:** This is Birling's view, which the play sets out to discredit.
   * **Why C:** The Inspector places responsibility on individuals, not only on the state.
   * **Why D:** He addresses the whole family; responsibility is shown to belong to everyone.

   * **Correct:** B

   * **Why A:** The words are figurative, warning of large-scale suffering, not a domestic fire.
   * **Why C:** It looks forward to future catastrophe, not back at Eva's death alone.
   * **Why D:** It is a warning of consequences, not a reward.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** Birling's confident claims about the Titanic and peace are an example of which technique?
   * **Options:** A) Dramatic irony, because the audience knows he is wrong, B) A metaphor, C) A stage direction, D) A rhyming couplet
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The audience's knowledge of what really happened makes Birling's certainty ironic and exposes his poor judgement.
   * **Why B:** These are literal predictions, not metaphors.
   * **Why C:** A stage direction is an instruction to actors, not a spoken claim.
   * **Why D:** The play is in prose dialogue, not rhymed verse.
