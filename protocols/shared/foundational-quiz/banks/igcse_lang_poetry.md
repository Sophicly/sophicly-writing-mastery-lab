### Quiz: IGCSE Language A Poetry

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** "Out, Out—" by Robert Frost is written in which form?
   * **Options:** A) Blank verse — unrhymed iambic pentameter, B) A villanelle, C) A ballad, D) A sonnet
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Frost writes in blank verse — unrhymed lines of iambic pentameter — whose plain, conversational rhythm suits the deceptively ordinary scene before the sudden tragedy.
   * **Why B:** A villanelle is nineteen lines built on two refrains; this poem is a continuous unrhymed narrative.
   * **Why C:** A ballad tells its story in rhymed song-like stanzas; Frost's lines are unrhymed and unbroken.
   * **Why D:** A sonnet is fourteen rhymed lines; this poem runs far longer without rhyme.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** "The Bright Lights of Sarajevo" by Tony Harrison is built mainly from which form?
   * **Options:** A) Rhyming couplets, B) Free verse, C) A sonnet, D) Blank verse
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Harrison writes in rhyming couplets — paired lines that rhyme, such as "pass"/"gas" and "prams"/"grams" — imposing order on the chaos of war.
   * **Why B:** Free verse avoids fixed rhyme; this poem rhymes its lines in pairs throughout.
   * **Why C:** A sonnet is a single fourteen-line form; this poem is a longer sequence of rhymed pairs.
   * **Why D:** Blank verse is unrhymed; Harrison's lines rhyme in couplets.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form Features]**
   * **Question:** What is blank verse?

   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Blank verse is unrhymed iambic pentameter — a steady ten-syllable line without rhyme, close to natural speech.
   * **Why B:** Nineteen lines with refrains describes the villanelle.

   * **Why D:** Paired rhyming lines describe rhyming couplets, which by definition rhyme.

   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Free verse abandons fixed rhyme and regular metre, letting line length and rhythm follow the poem's feeling.

   * **Why D:** Strict iambic pentameter is a regular metre, which free verse deliberately avoids.
