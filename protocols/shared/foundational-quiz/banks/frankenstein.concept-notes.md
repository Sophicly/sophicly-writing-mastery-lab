### Protagonist
- **Note:** Victor Frankenstein begins an idealistic, gifted young scientist who "ardently desired the acquisition of knowledge" and dreamed of banishing death; his hamartia is his hubristic overreaching and, worse, his refusal of responsibility — abandoning the creature the moment it lives — and this turns him into a guilt-ridden, isolated fugitive who loses everyone he loves and pursues his creation to a barren death on the Arctic ice.

### Plot
- **Note:** Driven to breach the boundary of life and death, Victor animates a creature from dead matter but flees in horror; abandoned and shunned by all, the creature turns to revenge, killing William, Clerval and — after Victor destroys the half-made mate — Elizabeth on her wedding night, until the broken Victor hunts his creation across the ice, dies, and the grieving creature vanishes to end itself.

### Themes
- **Note:** The controlling themes are the danger of overreaching for forbidden knowledge (the "Modern Prometheus"), the creator's responsibility to what he makes ("You are my creator, but I am your master"), nature versus nurture — a being born gentle made "malicious because" it is "miserable" — and the destructive power of isolation and rejection on creator and creature alike.

### Effects
- **Note:** Frankenstein is a Gothic novel, and its emotional effect is dread, horror and moral unease: the Gothic gives a society's buried fears a body, so the monstrous "birth" on "a dreary night of November" makes us feel a natural limit has been transgressed, and the creature — a sympathetic shadow, monstrous yet deeply wronged — leaves us disturbed to pity what should repel us, the horror turning back on its overreaching creator and on the darkness we recognise in ourselves.

### Message
- **Note:** The novel warns that knowledge and power pursued without responsibility or moral restraint bring ruin — we must answer for what we create — and that monstrosity is made, not born: it is rejection and cruelty, not any inborn evil, that turn a gentle creature murderous, so the guilt lies with the creator and society, not the creation.
