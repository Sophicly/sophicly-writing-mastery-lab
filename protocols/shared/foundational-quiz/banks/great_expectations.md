### Quiz: Great Expectations

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** In which year was *Great Expectations* first published in complete form?
   * **Options:** A) 1861, B) 1798, C) 1912, D) 1660
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Dickens wrote the novel in 1860–61, releasing it in weekly parts before it appeared as a complete book in 1861.
   * **Why B:** 1798 belongs to the Romantic poets; Dickens was writing decades later, in the Victorian age.
   * **Why C:** 1912 falls after Dickens's death; the novel had already been in print for fifty years by then.
   * **Why D:** 1660 is the Restoration period, long before the industrial, urban world the novel depicts.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** *Great Expectations* reflects the society and values of which era?
   * **Options:** A) The Victorian era, B) The medieval period, C) The Elizabethan age, D) Ancient Rome
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The novel is steeped in Victorian preoccupations — rigid social class, self-improvement, industry and the ideal of the "gentleman".
   * **Why B:** The medieval world of knights and feudal lords is centuries removed from the novel's towns, forges and railways.
   * **Why C:** The Elizabethan age precedes the novel by roughly two hundred and fifty years.
   * **Why D:** Ancient Rome has no bearing on this study of nineteenth-century English society.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** How was *Great Expectations* first presented to its readers?
   * **Options:** A) As a single finished novel released all at once, B) In weekly instalments in Dickens's magazine *All the Year Round*, C) As a stage play performed in London, D) As a long narrative poem
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Like much of Dickens's work, the story appeared serially, in weekly instalments, which encouraged cliff-hangers to keep readers buying the next number.
   * **Why A:** It reached readers gradually in parts, not as one complete volume.
   * **Why C:** It was written as prose fiction, though its drama later inspired many stage and screen adaptations.
   * **Why D:** It is a prose novel, not a poem.

   * **WhyWrong:** This is true — the novel charts Pip's journey from childhood on the marshes to hard-won maturity.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What does Pip ultimately learn from his rise into wealth and "great expectations"?
   * **Options:** A) That money and status guarantee happiness, B) That wealth and gentility do not bring contentment, and he was wrong to feel ashamed of Joe and the forge, C) That he should have been crueller to those beneath him, D) That ambition is always rewarded
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Stung early by Estella's scorn for his "coarse hands" and "thick boots", Pip chases gentility, only to find his new life leaves him hollow and estranged from the good people who love him.
   * **Why A:** The novel shows the opposite — his fortune brings guilt and loneliness rather than joy.
   * **Why C:** Pip's growth lies in humility and gratitude, not in cruelty.
   * **Why D:** His ambition leads to disillusionment, teaching him that worth is not measured in rank.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** How does the theme of guilt run through Pip's story?
   * **Options:** A) Pip feels no guilt at any point, B) From stealing food and a file for the convict as a boy, Pip carries a burden of guilt and conscience that shadows his life, C) Guilt is felt only by Magwitch, D) Guilt appears only in the final chapter
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The theft he commits for the convict on the marshes marks the start of a lasting sense of guilt, and conscience becomes one of the novel's deepest currents.
   * **Why A:** Pip is troubled by conscience throughout, beginning with the stolen food and file.
   * **Why C:** Several characters wrestle with guilt, but Pip's is central to the narrative.
   * **Why D:** His guilt is present from the opening chapters, not merely at the close.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** *Great Expectations* is narrated by Pip himself, looking back on his life. What is the effect of this technique?
   * **Options:** A) It makes the story impossible to follow, B) The adult Pip's retrospective, first-person voice lets him judge and reflect on his younger self's mistakes, C) It hides Pip's thoughts from the reader, D) It proves Pip is dishonest
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. From the opening, where the narrator explains how he "came to be called Pip", the mature Pip looks back with rueful insight on the boy he once was.
   * **Why A:** The retrospective voice makes his development clearer, not harder to follow.
   * **Why C:** First-person narration gives the reader direct access to Pip's inner life.
   * **Why D:** His honesty about his own faults is part of what makes the narrator sympathetic.
