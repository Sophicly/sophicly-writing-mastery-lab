### Protagonist
- **Note:** Meena Kumar begins as an insecure, story-telling British-Indian child who idolises the older Anita and longs to belong in the white Midlands village of Tollington; her growth turns not on mere ageing but on her own choice to see the prejudice woven through Anita's world, so she falls out of that illusion and rises into a girl secure in both her Indian heritage and her British self.

### Plot
- **Note:** Craving Anita's approval draws Meena deep into her world and its rebellions, but from inside it she witnesses the casual racism it conceals — above all Sam Lowbridge's attack, which Anita condones — and that disillusionment, together with Nanima's warmth and her own reflection, returns Meena to her heritage, matures her, and carries her beyond Tollington as she passes for grammar school.

### Themes
- **Note:** The controlling ideas are the search for identity and belonging between two cultures, the everyday prejudice that runs quietly beneath village life and surfaces even in a friend, the way storytelling lets a child try on selves while she works out who she is, and the strengthening of roots through heritage that Nanima's visit brings.

### Effects
- **Note:** Anita and Me is a bildungsroman (coming-of-age novel), and its emotional effect is empathy, poignancy and hope: we feel with Meena as she grows, ache at the childhood innocence and the idol she must lose to gain clear sight, and close in hope at the wiser, more secure self she becomes — the warmth and humour sit beside that tenderness, so growing up feels both costly and worth it.

### Message
- **Note:** The novel affirms that identity is forged rather than inherited whole — a child caught between cultures grows by learning to see prejudice clearly and to claim her own heritage — insisting that racism hides in the ordinary and must be outgrown, that maturity is bought by unlearning our childhood illusions, and that a British-born child of immigrants can hold both heritages in one confident, self-claimed self.
