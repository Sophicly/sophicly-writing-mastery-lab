### Quiz: The Merchant of Venice

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** In roughly which years did Shakespeare write *The Merchant of Venice*?
   * **Options:** A) Around 1596–1598, B) Around 1470–1475, C) Around 1660–1665, D) Around 1710–1715
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Shakespeare wrote the play in the late 1590s, during the reign of Elizabeth I, and it was first printed as a quarto in 1600.
   * **Why B:** The 1470s fall well before Shakespeare was even born in 1564.
   * **Why C:** The 1660s belong to the Restoration, decades after Shakespeare's death in 1616.
   * **Why D:** The early eighteenth century is far too late; the play is Elizabethan.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** In what dramatic genre did Shakespeare's contemporaries class *The Merchant of Venice*?
   * **Options:** A) A tragedy, B) A comedy that turns on courtship and disguise and ends in marriage, C) A history play about English kings, D) A Roman epic
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Though its treatment of Shylock is dark, the play was grouped with the comedies: it hinges on courtship, disguise and mistaken identity, and closes with the lovers united at Belmont.
   * **Why A:** Despite its painful moments, no central lover dies and the ending restores harmony.
   * **Why C:** The history plays dramatise English monarchs; this play unfolds in Italy among merchants.
   * **Why D:** It is not an epic poem about Rome but a stage comedy set in Renaissance Venice.

   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Venice stands for trade, law and the marketplace, while Belmont is a place of music, love and mercy — the two settings embody the play's contrasting worlds.
   * **WhyWrong:** This is true — the scenes alternate between mercantile Venice and Portia's estate at Belmont.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Why was moneylending at interest, or usury, so bound up with Shylock for an Elizabethan audience?
   * **Options:** A) Because lending money was illegal for everyone, B) Because Christian teaching frowned on charging interest, so the trade fell to Jews like Shylock, who were barred from many other professions, C) Because only nobles were permitted to lend money, D) Because Venice had no banks at all
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Christian doctrine condemned charging interest, so moneylending became one of the few trades open to Jews — a background that shapes the hostility between Shylock and Antonio.
   * **Why A:** Lending itself was not forbidden to all; the objection was specifically to charging interest.
   * **Why C:** The trade was not reserved for nobles; it was left largely to those excluded from other work.
   * **Why D:** Venice was a great banking and trading centre; the tension arose from religious attitudes to interest, not a lack of finance.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What central tension does Portia's courtroom speech dramatise?
   * **Options:** A) Love versus money, B) The conflict between strict justice and mercy, as she urges Shylock to temper the law's letter with compassion, C) Youth versus age, D) Town versus country
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Pleading that "the quality of mercy is not strained", Portia sets mercy against Shylock's demand for the exact letter of the law — the play's great moral debate.
   * **Why A:** Money drives the plot, but the trial's argument is justice set against mercy.
   * **Why C:** Generational conflict is not the focus of the courtroom scene.
   * **Why D:** The contrast of Venice and Belmont exists elsewhere, but the trial turns on justice and mercy.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** Shylock's speech "Hath not a Jew eyes?" is most often read as raising which theme?
   * **Options:** A) The folly of ambition, B) Our shared humanity, as Shylock insists a Jew feels, bleeds and suffers exactly as a Christian does, C) The power of fate, D) The beauty of nature
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. By demanding "If you prick us, do we not bleed?", Shylock claims a common humanity, exposing the cruelty of the prejudice he endures.
   * **Why A:** The speech is about shared feeling and revenge, not ambition.
   * **Why C:** Shylock appeals to human sameness, not to destiny.
   * **Why D:** The natural world is not his subject; his subject is human equality.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** How does Portia use disguise to resolve the trial?
   * **Options:** A) She bribes the Duke, B) She enters the court disguised as a young male lawyer named Balthazar and defeats Shylock's claim through the letter of the law, C) She secretly poisons Shylock, D) She persuades Antonio to flee Venice
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Dressed as the learned advocate Balthazar, Portia turns the bond's own wording against Shylock — a coup made possible by the stage convention of disguise.
   * **Why A:** She wins by legal argument, not by bribing the court.
   * **Why C:** There is no poisoning; her victory is achieved through wit and law.
   * **Why D:** Antonio stands trial rather than fleeing; Portia rescues him in the courtroom itself.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** By what clever legal point does the disguised Portia finally defeat Shylock's bond?
   * **Options:** A) She proves the debt was already repaid, B) She grants the pound of flesh but rules the bond allows "no jot of blood", making it impossible to take without breaking the law, C) She declares the bond a forgery, D) She claims that Antonio is already dead
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Portia concedes the letter of the bond, then insists it names flesh but "no jot of blood" — so any drop spilt would forfeit Shylock's own life and lands.
   * **Why A:** The debt is not shown to be repaid; the loan has indeed fallen due.
   * **Why C:** The bond is genuine; Portia defeats it by interpretation, not by calling it false.
   * **Why D:** Antonio is alive and present, awaiting the knife when Portia intervenes.
