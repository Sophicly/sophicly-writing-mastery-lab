### Protagonist
- **Note:** Phil begins as a silent, detached teenager who idly eats while the others panic; his hamartia is not rage but a cold pragmatism, and his own choice to protect the group at any moral cost hardens that detachment into ruthlessness — he masterminds the framing of an innocent postman and the killing of Adam, remaining unmoved to the end while the group defers to him.

### Plot
- **Note:** The group's bullying of Adam escalates until they stone him and he falls, presumed dead; rather than confess, Phil devises a cover-up, planting DNA evidence to frame an innocent postman — but when Adam is found alive, living rough, his survival threatens the lie, so the group has him killed, one concealment forcing the next crime.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central themes are collective guilt and the diffusion of responsibility — blame shared across a group thins to nothing, so together they sanction cruelty no individual would own — the corrupting logic of the cover-up in which each lie demands a larger one, the paradox that shared guilt binds the group more tightly, and the tension between Leah's conscience and Phil's cold self-interest.

### Effects
- **Note:** *DNA* is a social-realist morality play, and its emotional effect is moral discomfort and complicity: because these are ordinary, recognisable teenagers, their easy slide into concealment implicates us — we ask whether we would have done the same — while pathos for the innocent (Adam and the framed postman) exposes the human cost, and the unresolved ending leaves us uneasy and troubled rather than entertained or satisfied.

### Message
- **Note:** The play warns that when responsibility is shared, ordinary people will commit and excuse cruelty no individual would own alone — evil needs no monster — and that justice is bleakly incomplete: the innocent suffer while the guilty protect themselves, guilt corroding the group from within even as it escapes external blame.
