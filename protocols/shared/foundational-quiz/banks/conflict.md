### Quiz: OCR Conflict Poetry

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** In which form does Lord Byron write "The Destruction of Sennacherib"?
   * **Options:** A) Rhymed quatrains in a galloping anapaestic metre, B) Unrhymed blank verse, C) A fourteen-line sonnet, D) Free verse without metre or rhyme
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Byron builds six four-line stanzas of rhymed couplets, driven by a rolling three-beat anapaestic rhythm that sweeps the Assyrian host from splendour to ruin.
   * **Why B:** The poem is tightly rhymed in couplets, not the unrhymed lines of blank verse.
   * **Why C:** It runs to six stanzas of narrative, far longer than a fourteen-line sonnet.
   * **Why D:** Its insistent metre and rhyme are the opposite of free verse.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** "Boat Stealing (from 1799 Prelude)" by William Wordsworth is written in which form?
   * **Options:** A) Blank verse — unrhymed iambic pentameter, B) Rhyming couplets, C) A villanelle, D) A ballad in song-like stanzas
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Wordsworth recounts the theft of the boat in a single unbroken passage of unrhymed iambic pentameter, whose steady onward movement carries the boy from mastery into dread.
   * **Why B:** The lines do not rhyme in pairs; they run on without a rhyme scheme.
   * **Why C:** It has no refrains or fixed nineteen-line shape, so it is not a villanelle.
   * **Why D:** It is a continuous meditation, not a story told in regular ballad stanzas.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form Features]**
   * **Question:** What is the defining metrical feature of "The Destruction of Sennacherib"?
   * **Options:** A) A galloping three-syllable (anapaestic) beat, B) Unrhymed iambic pentameter, C) No discernible rhythm at all, D) A closing rhyming couplet after twelve lines
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each line rides a rolling anapaestic rhythm — two light beats then a stress — so the verse itself seems to charge like cavalry.
   * **Why B:** Unrhymed iambic pentameter describes the blank verse of Wordsworth's boat passage, not Byron's rhymed gallop.
   * **Why C:** The rhythm is emphatic and regular, the reverse of having none.
   * **Why D:** The rhyme falls in couplets throughout, not as a single closing pair.
