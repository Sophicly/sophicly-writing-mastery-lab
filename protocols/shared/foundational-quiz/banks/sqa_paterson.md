### Quiz: Don Paterson Poetry

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Recognising the Poem]**
   * **Question:** Which poem opens with the speaker addressing his newborn son as "Dear son, I was mezzo del cammin", declaring that the child "lit" his lost path as he ran?
   * **Options:** A) The Thread, B) Waking with Russell, C) The Circle, D) 11:00: Baldovan
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Waking with Russell" opens on this scene of waking face-to-face with a four-day-old son, borrowing Dante's opening line from the *Inferno* ("mezzo del cammin", the middle of life's road) to show the child redirecting his father's lost sense of purpose.
   * **Why A:** The Thread describes a difficult birth and the family held by a fragile connecting line, not this waking scene.
   * **Why C:** The Circle watches an older boy painting outer space, not a newborn.
   * **Why D:** 11:00: Baldovan recalls a childhood bus journey through Dundee, not a father addressing a newborn.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form & Features]**
   * **Question:** What form does Paterson use for "Waking with Russell", and what does this choice suggest?
   * **Options:** A) A fourteen-line sonnet, a form traditionally weighted with declarations of love, fitting a father's declaration to his son, B) A free-verse elegy with no fixed shape, C) A ballad built around a repeating refrain, D) An unbroken run of rhyming couplets
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The poem is shaped as a sonnet — a form long associated with love and momentous declaration — turned here towards a father's declaration of love and renewed purpose for his son.
   * **Why B:** The poem holds to the sonnet's fixed fourteen-line frame rather than the open shape of free verse.
   * **Why C:** There is no repeating refrain; the poem develops as a single continuous statement.
   * **Why D:** It is a sonnet, not simply a run of rhyming couplets.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests Meaning & Effects]**
   * **Question:** The poem states that "the true gift never leaves the giver", so the smile "rolled on" and "poured through us like a river". What does this image suggest about the exchange between father and son?
   * **Options:** A) That the son owes the father a debt he must one day repay, B) That love between them circulates rather than being spent, so a shared smile keeps renewing itself between them, C) That the father regrets having woken his son, D) That the smile was a polite formality with no real feeling behind it
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Paterson presents love as reciprocal and self-renewing: giving does not deplete it, so the smile passed between father and son keeps flowing back and forth like a river.
   * **Why A:** The poem frames the exchange as generous and mutual, not as a debt owed.
   * **Why C:** There is no regret in the poem; the encounter is tender and welcomed.
   * **Why D:** The river image conveys real, flowing feeling, the opposite of an empty formality.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form & Features]**

   * **Options:** A) As a rhyming ballad that stays comic and light throughout, B) In free verse, present-tense narration that moves from concrete, everyday detail towards increasingly strange, unsettling imagery, C) As a formal sonnet addressed directly to the poet's son, D) In strict rhyming couplets whose tone never shifts

   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The poem is written in free verse and narrated in the present tense, beginning with the ordinary detail of a bus stop before the journey curdles into something increasingly disorientating and uncanny.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form & Features]**
   * **Question:** What is distinctive about the form of "The Circle"?
   * **Options:** A) Free verse with no rhyme or pattern at all, B) Rhymed quatrains in a tightly controlled, song-like pattern, whose neatness sits against the poem's subject of an unsteady, shaking hand, C) A single unbroken stanza with no line breaks, D) A dramatic monologue spoken by the son himself
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The poem's rhymed, tightly patterned quatrains give it a controlled, almost musical shape that contrasts pointedly with the shaking hand it describes, which cannot close a perfect circle.
   * **Why A:** The poem is formally patterned and rhymed, not written in free verse.
   * **Why C:** It is built from distinct quatrains rather than one continuous block.
   * **Why D:** The father-speaker narrates the scene; the son does not speak in the poem.

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests Meaning & Effects]**

   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The tremor is linked to the trauma of the boy's difficult birth (as described in "The Thread"), so the imperfect circle comes to stand for human frailty set against the cosmic order he is trying to draw.

   * **Why C:** The tremor belongs to the boy's own body and history, not a judgement on the father's parenting.
   * **Why D:** The boy is absorbed in the painting; the poem is not about a lack of interest.
