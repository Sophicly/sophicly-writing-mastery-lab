### Quiz: Frankenstein

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Mary Shelley wrote at a time of experiments with electricity and life. Which idea most influenced the creature's creation?
   * **Options:** A) The steam engine, B) Galvanism — the belief that electricity could animate dead flesh, C) The theory of gravity, D) The invention of the telescope
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Galvanism — experiments passing electricity through dead tissue to make it twitch — lies behind Victor's spark of life.
   * **Why A:** The steam engine is unrelated to the creature's animation.
   * **Why C:** Gravity is not the science behind giving life to dead matter.
   * **Why D:** The telescope has no bearing on the creation of life.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The novel famously began during a competition among friends. What was the challenge?
   * **Options:** A) To write the longest poem, B) To write a ghost or horror story, during the cold, stormy summer of 1816, C) To paint a landscape, D) To translate a Latin text
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Trapped indoors by the stormy "year without a summer" at the Villa Diodati, the group challenged each other to write ghost stories — and Frankenstein was born.
   * **Why A:** The contest was for a frightening tale, not a poem.
   * **Why C:** It was a storytelling challenge, not a painting one.
   * **Why D:** It involved writing original horror, not translation.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The novel reflects the Romantic period's awe at the sublime power and beauty of wild nature.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Grand, wild landscapes — the Alps, glaciers, the Arctic — evoke the Romantic sublime, mirroring the characters' extreme emotions.
   * **WhyWrong:** The novel is steeped in the Romantic sublime — its vast, wild settings reflect the period's awe at nature's power.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Mary Shelley's parents were famous thinkers, and the novel explores whether people are shaped by their upbringing. What debate does this reflect?
   * **Options:** A) Free trade versus tariffs, B) Nature versus nurture — whether circumstance makes a person good or bad, C) Monarchy versus republic, D) Town versus country living
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The creature is born gentle but turned murderous by rejection — dramatising the nature-versus-nurture question central to the thinking of Shelley's parents.
   * **Why A:** The debate is about human character, not economics.
   * **Why C:** It concerns upbringing and character, not forms of government.
   * **Why D:** The novel weighs nature against nurture, not places to live.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What warning does the novel give about the pursuit of knowledge?
   * **Options:** A) That knowledge should always be pursued at any cost, B) That reaching for forbidden knowledge without responsibility leads to ruin, C) That learning is pointless, D) That only scientists matter
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Victor's obsessive quest to create life brings catastrophe — the novel warns against ambition and knowledge divorced from responsibility.
   * **Why A:** The novel cautions against pursuing knowledge recklessly, not for it.
   * **Why C:** It questions dangerous knowledge, not the value of learning itself.
   * **Why D:** Its warning applies to all overreaching ambition, not scientists alone.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** The novel is built from letters and nested stories — Walton's letters contain Victor's tale, which contains the creature's. What is this technique?

   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The frame narrative — story within story — lets us hear Walton, Victor and the creature in turn, shaping our sympathy for each.
   * **Why B:** It is a narrative structure, not a verse form.
   * **Why C:** It concerns how the story is told, not sound effects.
   * **Why D:** There are several nested narrators, not one diary.

   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The bleak, stormy setting creates Gothic horror and mirrors the wrongness of Victor's act — the weather echoing the mood.
