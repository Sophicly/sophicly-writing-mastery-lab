### Quiz: The Tempest

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** *The Tempest* is believed to have been written around which date, making it one of the last plays Shakespeare wrote alone?
   * **Options:** A) Around 1611, B) Around 1660, C) Around 1750, D) Around 1820
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Composed about 1610–11, the play was performed at court in 1611 and is usually placed among Shakespeare's final works, often read as a farewell to the stage.
   * **Why B:** This falls decades after Shakespeare's death in 1616, so he could not have written the play then.
   * **Why C:** The mid-eighteenth century is far too late; the play belongs to the early Jacobean period.
   * **Why D:** The nineteenth century is two hundred years after the play was actually written.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** *The Tempest* is usually classed as which kind of play?
   * **Options:** A) A history play about English kings, B) A late romance, or tragicomedy, blending danger and loss with wonder, magic and a hopeful ending, C) A revenge tragedy ending in the hero's death, D) A farce with no serious themes
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The play mixes the threat of shipwreck and betrayal with magic, young love and forgiveness, ending in reconciliation — the pattern critics call romance or tragicomedy.
   * **Why A:** It dramatises no English royal history; its story is invented and set on a remote island.
   * **Why C:** Although Prospero has been wronged, the play moves towards mercy and reunion, not the hero's death.
   * **Why D:** Beneath its enchantment it treats power, freedom and forgiveness with real seriousness.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** Prospero's magic gives him almost total control over the island and everyone on it. What does the play ultimately suggest about this power?
   * **Options:** A) That such power should be held onto forever, B) That mastery over others is precarious, and in the end Prospero chooses to give it up, breaking his staff and drowning his book, C) That his power comes from Caliban, D) That power brings him lasting contentment on the island
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Having used his art to bring his enemies to justice, Prospero renounces it — vowing to break his staff and drown his book — suggesting that human power over others must finally be relinquished.
   * **Why A:** The play's movement is towards surrendering power, not clinging to it.
   * **Why C:** Prospero's power flows from his own learning and art; Caliban is his unwilling servant, not its source.
   * **Why D:** The island is a place of exile and hardship for Prospero, who longs to return to Milan.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** Although Prospero has the power to punish the enemies who wronged him, what does he finally choose?
   * **Options:** A) To drown them all in another storm, B) Forgiveness and reconciliation, judging that "the rarer action is in virtue than in vengeance", C) To keep them imprisoned on the island forever, D) To hand his dukedom to Caliban
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Prospero decides that "the rarer action is in virtue than in vengeance", turning from revenge to mercy and freeing the men who once betrayed him.
   * **Why A:** He calms the seas and preserves everyone; no one is drowned in the play.
   * **Why C:** Rather than imprison his enemies, Prospero forgives them and restores order.
   * **Why D:** Prospero reclaims his own dukedom of Milan; he does not give it to Caliban.

   * **WhyWrong:** This is true — the desire for freedom drives both Ariel and Caliban, and the contrast in how Prospero treats them is central to the play.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** The wedding entertainment Prospero conjures for Ferdinand and Miranda in Act 4, performed by spirits appearing as goddesses, is an example of which technique?
   * **Options:** A) A masque — a spectacular performance of song, dance and mythological figures staged inside the play, B) A soliloquy, C) A dramatic aside, D) A rhyming couplet
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The masque is a lavish show-within-the-show; Prospero has his spirits perform it to bless the young lovers before he breaks it off with "Our revels now are ended".
   * **Why B:** A soliloquy is one character's private speech aloud, not a staged spectacle.
   * **Why C:** An aside is a brief remark meant to be unheard by other characters, not an elaborate performance.
   * **Why D:** A rhyming couplet is a pair of rhymed lines, not a dramatic show.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** What is the effect of Ariel's song "Full fathom five thy father lies" within the play?
   * **Options:** A) It gives Prospero a factual report of the shipwreck, B) It is an enchanting piece of music that deceives Ferdinand into believing his father has drowned, while transforming that loss into something "rich and strange", C) It is a comic drinking song for Stephano and Trinculo, D) It is spoken by Prospero in soliloquy
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Ariel's ethereal song works its magic on Ferdinand, making him grieve a death that has not happened and turning the imagined drowning into something "rich and strange".
   * **Why A:** The song is an illusion, not a report — Ferdinand's father is alive and unharmed.
   * **Why C:** The comic songs belong to the drunken Stephano and Trinculo; this is Ariel's spirit-music.
   * **Why D:** It is sung by the spirit Ariel, not delivered by Prospero.
