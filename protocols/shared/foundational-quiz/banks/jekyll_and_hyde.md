### Quiz: Jekyll and Hyde

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Victorian gentlemen prized "respectability". How does this help explain why Jekyll hides Hyde?
   * **Options:** A) Because Hyde is a business rival, B) Because a respectable public reputation had to be protected, so Jekyll conceals his private appetites, C) Because Hyde owes him money, D) Because the law required it
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Victorian society split a proper public face from private desire; Hyde lets Jekyll indulge in secret while his reputation stays clean.
   * **Why A:** Hyde is Jekyll's hidden self, not a commercial rival.
   * **Why C:** No debt drives the concealment — it is about protecting reputation.
   * **Why D:** It is social pressure, not the law, that makes Jekyll hide his other self.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Stevenson wrote the story as a Gothic novella. What does that form allow him to explore?
   * **Options:** A) Comic misunderstandings between friends, B) Hidden fears and the darkness beneath a respectable surface, C) A realistic romance, D) A historical account of real events
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Gothic form uses horror, mystery and the uncanny to probe a society's deepest fears — here, the evil concealed within respectable men.
   * **Why A:** The mode is dread and horror, not comedy.
   * **Why C:** There is no romance plot; the focus is fear and duality.
   * **Why D:** The story is invented fiction, not historical record.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The fog and darkness of late-Victorian London are used to mirror the moral murk and secrecy of the story.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The city's "chocolate-coloured pall" and gas-lit fog reflect the hidden, obscured evil at the heart of the novella.
   * **WhyWrong:** The setting does mirror the theme — London's fog and gloom stand for the moral obscurity and secrecy Stevenson explores.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Hyde is repeatedly described as small, ape-like and barely human. Which Victorian anxiety does this reflect?
   * **Options:** A) A fear of foreign trade, B) A fear, after Darwin, that civilised humans might degenerate to a primitive, animal state, C) A fear of new railways, D) A fear of the monarchy
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Following Darwin, Victorians feared "degeneration" — that respectable people hid a lower, animal nature; Hyde embodies that dread.
   * **Why A:** The unease is about human nature regressing, not commerce.
   * **Why C:** Railways are not the concern; evolutionary regression is.
   * **Why D:** The fear is of the beast within the individual, not of the crown.

   * **Answer:** two
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Man is not truly one, but truly two" is the novella's central claim about the double nature within every person.
   * **WhyWrong:** The word is "two" — Jekyll's discovery is that each person contains two natures, not a single one.

6. **Type: Select All [Tests Context]**

   * **Correct:** A, B, C

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What is the central theme Stevenson explores through Jekyll's transformation?
   * **Options:** A) The joy of scientific discovery, B) The duality of human nature — that good and evil exist together in one person, C) The importance of family, D) The beauty of London
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Jekyll and Hyde are two sides of one man; the novella argues that every person contains both a civilised and a savage self.
   * **Why A:** The science brings horror, not joyful discovery.
   * **Why C:** Family is not the focus; the divided self is.
   * **Why D:** London's setting serves the theme of hidden darkness rather than beauty.

8. **Type: True-False [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** The novella refuses Jekyll any redemption: his final statement ends in despair, with Hyde in control and no salvation offered.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Jekyll's confession closes as he loses himself entirely to Hyde — the story withholds redemption, making his fall a moral warning.
   * **WhyWrong:** There is no redemption — Jekyll ends overwhelmed by Hyde, his statement bringing "the life of that unhappy Henry Jekyll to an end".

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** Through Jekyll's experiment, what does the novella suggest about science?
   * **Options:** A) That it should be pursued without limits, B) That meddling with nature and the self can unleash forces beyond human control, C) That science is always harmless, D) That doctors should never experiment at all
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Jekyll's potion releases a self he cannot contain — a warning about the dangers of overreaching, unchecked science.
   * **Why A:** The story warns against limitless experiment, not for it.
   * **Why C:** The experiment proves disastrous, not harmless.
   * **Why D:** The caution is against reckless overreach, not a total ban on all science.

   * **Answer:** ask
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Utterson's rule — the more suspicious a matter looks, the fewer questions he asks — shows how Victorian gentlemen shielded reputations by looking away.
   * **WhyWrong:** The word is "ask" — Utterson deliberately asks fewer questions the more scandalous something seems, protecting reputation through silence.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** The "chocolate-coloured pall" of fog that lowers over London as the mystery deepens is an example of...
   * **Options:** A) Setting used as atmosphere, reflecting the story's hidden evil, B) Onomatopoeia, C) A rhyming couplet, D) A stage direction
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Stevenson uses the oppressive, murky setting to build dread and mirror the concealed horror of the plot.
   * **Why B:** There is no sound-imitation; the effect is visual and atmospheric.
   * **Why C:** The novella is prose, not rhymed verse.
   * **Why D:** It is descriptive narration, not a stage instruction.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** How is the story mostly told, and why does it matter?
   * **Options:** A) As a single first-person account by Hyde, B) As a mystery followed largely through Utterson, with the full truth withheld until Jekyll's closing statement, C) As a play with stage directions, D) As a series of poems
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Tracking events through the puzzled Utterson and delaying Jekyll's confession to the end builds suspense and mirrors how the truth stays hidden.
   * **Why A:** Hyde never narrates; the perspective is largely Utterson's until the final documents.
   * **Why C:** It is a prose novella, not a play.
   * **Why D:** It is narrative prose, not poetry.
