### Quiz: A Christmas Carol

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Early on, Scrooge asks "Are there no prisons? Are there no workhouses?" Which harsh Victorian system does this reflect?
   * **Options:** A) The free hospitals of London, B) The 1834 New Poor Law, which forced the destitute into grim workhouses, C) A generous welfare state, D) The public school system
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The New Poor Law of 1834 sent the poor to punishing workhouses; Scrooge's words echo the era's cold attitude to poverty.
   * **Why A:** The line refers to the punitive Poor Law system, not to free hospitals.
   * **Why C:** There was no welfare safety net — that lack is exactly Dickens's target.
   * **Why D:** Schooling is not the issue; the workhouse and prison system is.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Scrooge speaks of the poor dying to "decrease the surplus population". Whose ideas is Dickens attacking here?
   * **Options:** A) Thinkers like Malthus who treated the poor as an economic surplus, B) The Church of England, C) Charles Darwin, D) The royal family
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Dickens mocks Malthusian thinking, which coldly regarded the poor as an excess "surplus population" to be reduced.
   * **Why B:** Dickens actually draws on Christian charity to oppose this attitude.
   * **Why C:** Darwin's ideas came later and are not the target of this phrase.
   * **Why D:** The satire is aimed at cold economic thinking, not the monarchy.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Dickens wrote the novella partly to awaken Victorian readers to the suffering of the poor and of child labourers.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Moved by the poverty and child labour of the "Hungry Forties", Dickens used the story to stir his readers' consciences.
   * **WhyWrong:** Dickens did write it to prick consciences — he wanted comfortable Victorians to see and act on the suffering of the poor.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** How does the novella draw on Christian ideas of Christmas?
   * **Options:** A) It rejects religion entirely, B) It presents Christmas as a time for charity, forgiveness and care for others, C) It treats Christmas as merely a holiday from work, D) It argues Christmas should be abolished
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Dickens ties Christmas to Christian charity and the Sermon on the Mount — a season demanding generosity and compassion.
   * **Why A:** The story is rich in Christian moral teaching, not a rejection of it.
   * **Why C:** Christmas here carries deep moral meaning, far beyond a day off.
   * **Why D:** Dickens celebrates and defends Christmas rather than dismissing it.

6. **Type: Select All [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Which features of 1840s Victorian society does the novella respond to?
   * **Options:** A) Workhouses and a harsh Poor Law, B) Widespread poverty and child labour, C) A belief that charity was a Christian duty, D) A government pension for every elderly citizen
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A,B,C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Workhouses, poverty and child labour, and the call to Christian charity all shape the world Dickens is writing about.
   * **Why D:** No state pension existed; the poor relied on charity or faced the workhouse.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What is the central theme of Scrooge's journey through the novella?
   * **Options:** A) That money brings happiness, B) Redemption — a selfish man can change and learn social responsibility, C) That ghosts are real, D) That the past cannot be escaped
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Scrooge's transformation from miser to generous benefactor dramatises redemption and the duty we owe one another.
   * **Why A:** The story shows wealth without compassion leaves Scrooge miserable.
   * **Why C:** The ghosts are a device serving the moral theme, not the theme itself.
   * **Why D:** The point is that Scrooge can change his future by changing now.

8. **Type: True-False [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** Marley's ghost drags heavy chains "forged" in life, symbolising the burden of his greed and selfishness.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "I wear the chain I forged in life" makes the chains a symbol of a life spent on money instead of humanity — a warning to Scrooge.
   * **WhyWrong:** The chains are symbolic — Marley forged them through his own greed, and they warn Scrooge of the fate a selfish life brings.

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** How does the novella present charity and generosity?
   * **Options:** A) As a waste of money, B) As a moral duty that brings joy and redeems the giver, C) As something only the poor should practise, D) As unnecessary in a rich society
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Generosity is shown as its own reward — Scrooge's giving at the end brings him the joy his hoarding never did.
   * **Why A:** Dickens presents charity as enriching, not wasteful.
   * **Why C:** The duty of charity falls especially on those, like Scrooge, who have wealth.
   * **Why D:** The novella argues charity is essential precisely because society neglects the poor.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** The three spirits who visit Scrooge — Past, Present and Yet to Come — mainly serve which purpose?
   * **Options:** A) To provide comic relief, B) As a structural device that guides Scrooge, and the reader, through his transformation, C) To frighten the reader for its own sake, D) To describe the weather
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The spirits structure the novella, walking Scrooge through his past, present and possible future so that change becomes irresistible.
   * **Why A:** The spirits are instruments of moral awakening, not comedy.
   * **Why C:** Their purpose is to reform Scrooge, not merely to scare.
   * **Why D:** They reveal Scrooge's life, not the weather.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** Scrooge is introduced through cold imagery — "piercing, searching, biting cold". What is the effect?
   * **Options:** A) It links his icy, unfeeling nature to the coldness around him, B) It is onomatopoeia imitating wind, C) It is a rhyming couplet, D) It is a stage direction
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The cold imagery mirrors Scrooge's hard, unfeeling heart — outer chill standing for inner coldness.
   * **Why B:** The phrase paints a picture of coldness rather than imitating a sound.
   * **Why C:** It is descriptive prose, not rhymed verse.
   * **Why D:** It is narration describing Scrooge, not a stage instruction.
