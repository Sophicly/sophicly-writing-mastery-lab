### Protagonist
- **Note:** Jane begins as a powerless, unloved orphan whose only security is her own self-respect — "I care for myself" — and forms herself, through the cruelty of Gateshead and Lowood, into a self-possessed, independent woman; her growth is driven not by luck but by her own repeated choice to keep her integrity, refusing love without honour and duty without love until she can meet Rochester as an equal.

### Plot
- **Note:** Orphaned and mistreated at Gateshead, then hardened by Lowood, Jane becomes governess at Thornfield and loves Rochester, only to flee when his living wife Bertha is revealed and marriage would compromise her; her flight, an inheritance and the fire that frees and humbles Rochester bring her back to marry him as an equal — each stage flowing by cause from the character her early trials formed.

### Themes
- **Note:** The controlling themes are the equal worth of every soul across class and gender ("I have as much soul as you"), the primacy of self-respect and moral integrity over comfort or passion, the refusal to be owned or caged ("I am no bird; and no net ensnares me"), and the conviction that true love is possible only between free equals.

### Effects
- **Note:** Jane Eyre is a bildungsroman, and its emotional effect is empathy, hope and poignancy: because we live inside Jane's inner life we share the longing of a mistreated child for worth and love, ache through her losses and are lifted by her hard-won independence, tender sadness shadowing her happiness because of all it cost. The Gothic dread of Thornfield and Bertha serves that arc — it is texture, not the governing feeling — and the equal, honourable union at the close leaves us moved and hopeful rather than merely frightened.

### Message
- **Note:** The novel affirms that every person — however poor, plain or lowly — possesses equal dignity and worth, that keeping faith with one's own conscience is finally vindicated even at great cost, and that true love and independence are not opposed but reconciled only in a union of equals.
