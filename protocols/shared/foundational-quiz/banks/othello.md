### Quiz: Othello

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** When did Shakespeare write *Othello*?
   * **Options:** A) Around 1603, in the Jacobean period, B) In the 1750s, during the Enlightenment, C) In the medieval period, before 1300, D) In the late Victorian era, around 1890
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Shakespeare wrote *Othello* around 1603 and it was first performed in 1604, early in the reign of James I — the Jacobean period.
   * **Why B:** The play predates the Enlightenment by roughly a century and a half.
   * **Why C:** Shakespeare belongs to the Renaissance, not the medieval age; the play is far too late for that.
   * **Why D:** The tragedy was written some three centuries before the Victorian era.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** To which dramatic genre does *Othello* belong?
   * **Options:** A) A comedy that ends in marriage and reconciliation, B) A tragedy, tracing a noble hero's downfall and death, C) A history play about English kings, D) A pastoral romance set among shepherds
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. *Othello* is one of Shakespeare's great tragedies, following the ruin and death of a high-standing hero brought low by manipulated jealousy.
   * **Why A:** It ends in death and grief, not the marriages that close a comedy.
   * **Why C:** It concerns a Venetian general, not the reigns of English monarchs.
   * **Why D:** Its world is military and urban, and its mood is tragic rather than pastoral.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The play opens in Venice and then moves its action to the island of Cyprus.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The first act unfolds in the sophisticated city of Venice; the remainder takes place on the isolated outpost of Cyprus, where Othello's mind is worked upon.
   * **WhyWrong:** This is true — the shift from ordered Venice to remote Cyprus removes Othello from society and leaves him exposed to Iago's scheming.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Why is Othello's identity as a Moor significant in the play's original context?
   * **Options:** A) It made him the wealthiest man in Venice, B) As a Moor he is an outsider in Venetian society, and Jacobean attitudes to race let Iago exploit his sense of not belonging, C) It meant he could not serve in the army, D) It gave him a claim to the Venetian throne
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Othello is a North African Moor commanding a white European state; his outsider status, and the prejudice of the age, become tools Iago turns against him.
   * **Why A:** His standing rests on his military skill, not on inherited wealth.
   * **Why C:** Far from barred, he is Venice's most trusted general.
   * **Why D:** Venice was a republic with no throne, and Othello seeks no crown.

6. **Type: Select All [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Which features of classical, Aristotelian tragedy can be seen in *Othello*?
   * **Options:** A) A hero of high rank and stature, B) A tragic flaw or error that drives his downfall, C) A fall from greatness ending in death and pity, D) A cheerful ending in which order is happily restored
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A,B,C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Othello is a great general (high stature), undone by his susceptibility to jealousy (his fatal error), whose fall ends in death and arouses pity — the shape of Aristotelian tragedy.
   * **Why D:** Tragedy closes in loss and sorrow, not the happy restoration of a comedy.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What does Iago mean when he warns Othello of "the green-eyed monster which doth mock the meat it feeds on"?
   * **Options:** A) That Othello is in physical danger, B) That jealousy torments and consumes the very person who suffers it, C) That Othello should eat better, D) That a real creature stalks Cyprus
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Iago's image of jealousy as a "green-eyed monster" captures how suspicion devours the mind it inhabits — the emotion he deliberately kindles in Othello.
   * **Why A:** The threat is inward and psychological, not a physical peril.
   * **Why C:** The line is a metaphor for jealousy, not literal advice about food.
   * **Why D:** No beast exists; the monster is the corrosive feeling within Othello.

   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Iago's paradox admits that his outward "honesty" is a performance, foregrounding the play's concern with deception and the distance between seeming and being.
   * **Why B:** The line is a confession of deceit, not confusion about his identity.
   * **Why C:** It reveals his duplicity, not any desire to depart.
   * **Why D:** It concerns his hidden malice, not his finances.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** Throughout the play characters call Iago "honest Iago". What technique does this repeated epithet create?
   * **Options:** A) Comic relief, B) Dramatic irony, since the audience knows he is the play's deceiver while the characters trust him, C) A change of setting, D) A rhyming couplet
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Because we have heard Iago's true intentions, every trusting use of "honest Iago" deepens the dramatic irony and our sense of dread.
   * **Why A:** The phrase chills rather than amuses, given what we know.
   * **Why C:** An epithet describes a character; it does not move the location.
   * **Why D:** "Honest Iago" is a repeated label, not a pair of rhyming lines.

   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Alone on stage, Iago reveals his plans in soliloquy, drawing the audience into uneasy complicity and sharpening the irony of the trust others place in him.
   * **Why A:** His soliloquies expose his mind, not the scenery.

   * **Why D:** They advance the plot's cruelty rather than recap it neutrally.
