### Quiz: OCR Love Poetry

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** Keats's 'Bright Star' unfolds across fourteen lines as a single arcing sentence, moving from the star to the beloved's breast. Which form is this?
   * **Options:** A) A sonnet, B) A sestina, C) A ballad, D) Free verse
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. 'Bright Star' is a sonnet — its fourteen lines move in one sustained sentence from the star's 'lone splendour' to the beloved's 'ripening breast'.
   * **Why B:** A sestina runs to thirty-nine lines built on six repeating end-words, far longer than these fourteen.
   * **Why C:** A ballad tells a story in song-like stanzas, whereas this poem holds a single meditative address to the star.
   * **Why D:** Free verse abandons fixed length and metre, but this poem keeps the sonnet's fourteen measured lines.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** In Antrobus's 'The Perseverance', six end-words — perseverance, minute, before, father, disappear, laughter — recur in shifting order through every stanza. Which form is this?
   * **Options:** A) A sestina, B) A sonnet, C) A dramatic monologue, D) A ballad
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The circling of the same six end-words, stanza after stanza, is the defining signature of the sestina, and here the repetition mirrors the boy's endless waiting outside the pub.
   * **Why B:** A sonnet is a single fourteen-line poem, not this long sequence of six-line stanzas turning on repeated end-words.
   * **Why C:** A dramatic monologue is defined by a persona addressing a silent listener, not by a fixed pattern of recurring end-words.
   * **Why D:** A ballad narrates through song-like quatrains with regular rhyme, unlike this poem's rotating end-words.
