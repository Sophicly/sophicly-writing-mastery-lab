### Protagonist
- **Note:** Christopher Boone, a fifteen-year-old who sees the world with mathematical logic and cannot bear the unfamiliar, begins confined by fear and dependent on his father; driven by his own determination to investigate Wellington's death and then to find his mother, he crosses the country alone, survives a bewildering world, passes his maths A-level, and comes to believe he "can do anything" — a coming-of-age from fearful innocence to hard-won, courageous independence.

### Plot
- **Note:** Christopher finds his neighbour's dog Wellington killed with a garden fork and resolves to investigate like Sherlock Holmes; his detective quest leads him to question his father and to discover hidden letters revealing that his mother is alive and that his father lied about her death — and killed Wellington — so his trust collapses and he flees alone to London, an ordeal he survives that leaves him confident enough to sit his A-level and imagine an independent future.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central themes are trust and truth (Christopher, who cannot lie, is devastated by his father's deceptions and must slowly rebuild faith), the value of a mind that perceives the world differently — its clarity, honesty and courage — and order against chaos, as Christopher uses maths, prime numbers and counting to impose safety on a world he finds overwhelming, discovering real courage in pressing through fear rather than escaping it.

### Effects
- **Note:** The novel is a coming-of-age story (bildungsroman), and its emotional effect is empathy, hope and poignancy: because we see the world through Christopher's eyes we feel *with* his fear and root for his courage, moved by growth that is genuine yet bought at real cost — a broken trust and a fractured family — so the ending's "I can do anything" leaves us hopeful and ache-struck together, not amused or filled with dread.

### Message
- **Note:** The novel affirms that a mind which sees the world differently is a source of strength rather than a deficiency to be pitied or corrected, and that courage and perseverance through fear can transform a life; it insists too that truth and trust are the foundation of love — even well-meant lies wound deeply, and broken trust must be rebuilt slowly and honestly.
