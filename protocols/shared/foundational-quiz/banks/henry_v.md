### Quiz: Henry V

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** *Henry V*, written around 1599, belongs to which of Shakespeare's dramatic genres?
   * **Options:** A) A comedy of mistaken identity, B) A history play dramatising the reign of a real English king, C) A Roman tragedy, D) A pastoral romance
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. *Henry V* is a history play, the last of a sequence dramatising real English monarchs; it stages the reign of a king who actually ruled and fought in France.
   * **Why A:** There is no comic confusion of identities at its heart; it chronicles the deeds of a real king in war.
   * **Why C:** It concerns an English monarch and the campaign in France, not the ancient Roman world.
   * **Why D:** It is rooted in real history and warfare, not the idealised countryside of pastoral romance.

   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Henry's invasion of France and his victory at Agincourt in 1415 were real events of the Hundred Years' War, which Shakespeare reshapes for the stage.

   * **Why C:** The English Civil War came more than two centuries later; the play concerns a medieval war with France.
   * **Why D:** The Norman Conquest was in 1066, long before Henry's fifteenth-century campaign.

   * **Question:** How does Henry's St Crispin's Day speech develop the theme of national unity?
   * **Options:** A) By promising the men great riches, B) By binding the outnumbered soldiers together as "we happy few, we band of brothers", so that shared struggle levels the ranks, C) By threatening to punish any who flee, D) By reminding them they are far stronger than the French
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Calling his men "we few, we happy few, we band of brothers" turns a frightened, outnumbered army into a fellowship in which even a common soldier is made kin to the king.
   * **Why A:** Henry offers not wealth but honour and brotherhood; the bond he forges is not bought with money.
   * **Why C:** The speech inspires through pride and belonging, not through threats against those who might flee.
   * **Why D:** The English are heavily outnumbered — the power of the speech lies in turning that weakness into shared glory.
