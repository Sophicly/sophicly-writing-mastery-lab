### Protagonist
- **Note:** Othello begins as a dignified, eloquent and trusted Venetian general, a Moor whose word commands the state; his hamartia is not evil but his fatal choice to believe Iago's lies over Desdemona's fidelity; once he does, he falls from noble commander to a man who murders his innocent wife and then, learning the truth, kills himself.

### Plot
- **Note:** Passed over for promotion, Iago vows "I hate the Moor" and turns his resentment into a plot: he poisons Othello's mind with false suspicion of Desdemona and Cassio, plants the handkerchief as "ocular proof", and hardens Othello's jealousy into deadly certainty — Othello smothers Desdemona, Emilia exposes the deceit too late, and Othello takes his own life.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central themes are jealousy as a devouring "green-eyed monster" that consumes the mind it inhabits, the gap between appearance and reality embodied in "honest Iago" ("I am not what I am"), the governing power of reputation and honour, and Othello's status as an outsider whose sense of not belonging Iago weaponises.

### Effects
- **Note:** Othello is a tragedy, and its emotional effect is pity and fear: we pity a great and loving man who destroys himself and his innocent wife through his own fatal weakness for jealousy — his ruin feels like a terrible waste — and we fear because he is recognisably like us, so his fall warns that jealousy and misplaced trust could undo anyone. The revelation of the truth too late leaves us moved rather than merely satisfied.

### Message
- **Note:** The play warns that jealousy, once admitted, is a devouring force that destroys reason, love and life even in the noblest mind, and that evil can hide behind a mask of loyalty — so that trusting the deceiver and doubting the faithful can bring irreversible, self-inflicted tragedy.
