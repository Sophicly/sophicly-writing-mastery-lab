### Protagonist
- **Note:** Scrooge begins a cold, "solitary" miser — "hard and sharp as flint", greedy and cut off from others — whose flaw is not poverty of means but poverty of heart; forced by the spirits to confront the wound that hardened him, he chooses humanity over isolation and is remade into a generous, warm-hearted benefactor who declares "I am not the man I was".

### Plot
- **Note:** Warned by the chained ghost of his old partner Marley, the miser Scrooge is visited by the Ghosts of Christmas Past, Present and Yet to Come; each vision builds on the last — reopening the wound that hardened him, exposing him to the poor and to Tiny Tim, and finally showing his own lonely, unmourned death — until, terrified and grieving, he chooses to change and wakes transformed on Christmas morning.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central themes are redemption and the human capacity to change (no one is beyond saving), social responsibility and compassion for the poor against cold indifference, the moral duty and joy of charity and generosity, and the way a selfish life forges its own binding consequences — Marley's chain "forged in life".

### Effects
- **Note:** A Christmas Carol is a rebirth-and-redemption tale, and its emotional effect is hope and relief: watching even the coldest, cruellest man be genuinely remade reassures us that no one is beyond change and leaves us warmed rather than merely entertained. The pathos and moral discomfort stirred by Tiny Tim and the suffering poor press the reader, like Scrooge, to feel compassion and act — the supernatural dread serving that hopeful arc, not horror for its own sake.

### Message
- **Note:** The novella affirms that a life of greed without compassion imprisons the soul while generosity and care for others redeem us — "mankind" is our common business — and that no one is beyond redemption: it is never too late to confront ourselves, and society is bound by a shared duty of compassion, especially from those who have wealth toward the poor.
