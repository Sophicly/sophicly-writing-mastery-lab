### Quiz: Pride and Prejudice

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** In Regency England, why was marriage so urgent for the Bennet daughters?
   * **Options:** A) Because they enjoyed large personal fortunes, B) Because women had little way to earn or inherit, so a good marriage was their main route to security, C) Because the law forced them to marry young, D) Because they wished to leave the country
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. With few respectable ways for gentlewomen to earn money, a suitable marriage was the chief means of financial security — which drives the plot.
   * **Why A:** The Bennet girls have little fortune; that is exactly the problem.
   * **Why C:** There was social pressure, not a legal requirement, to marry.
   * **Why D:** Marriage was about security at home, not leaving England.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The Bennet estate is "entailed" away from the daughters. What does this mean?
   * **Options:** A) It must pass to a male heir, Mr Collins, leaving the daughters with little, B) It is divided equally between the daughters, C) It has been sold to Mr Darcy, D) It belongs to the church
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The entail directs the estate to the nearest male heir, Mr Collins, so the Bennet sisters could be left with almost nothing — raising the stakes of marriage.
   * **Why B:** The entail specifically prevents the daughters from inheriting.
   * **Why C:** Darcy does not own Longbourn; the heir is Mr Collins.
   * **Why D:** It passes to a male relative, not the church.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Austen's novel gently satirises the rigid class distinctions and manners of Regency society.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Through characters like Mr Collins and Lady Catherine, Austen mocks snobbery and the obsession with rank and wealth.
   * **WhyWrong:** The novel does satirise its society — Austen's irony repeatedly exposes snobbery and the fixation on class and money.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Elizabeth Bennet insists on marrying for love, not just money or rank. Why is this notable for her time?
   * **Options:** A) Because everyone already married only for love, B) Because women were expected to marry for security, so choosing love and respect was a bold stance, C) Because love matches were illegal, D) Because she was wealthy enough not to care
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a world where marriage meant security, Elizabeth's refusal to marry without love and mutual respect is a quietly radical position.
   * **Why A:** Most matches of the day were shaped by money and rank, not love alone.
   * **Why C:** Love matches were allowed but not the practical norm.
   * **Why D:** Elizabeth is not wealthy — she risks her security by holding out.

   * **Answer:** wife
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The ironic opening line makes marriage-for-money the novel's subject from the very first sentence.
   * **WhyWrong:** The word is "wife" — the ironic opening about a rich single man wanting a wife announces the theme of marriage.

6. **Type: Select All [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Which Regency-era realities shape the novel?
   * **Options:** A) Women's dependence on marriage for security, B) Estates entailed away from daughters, C) A strong concern with class and reputation, D) Equal career opportunities for women
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A,B,C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Female dependence, the entail, and anxiety about class and reputation all drive the plot.
   * **Why D:** Gentlewomen had almost no career options — that lack is exactly why marriage matters so much.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** How do the twin faults of the title work in the novel?
   * **Options:** A) Only Darcy is proud and only Elizabeth is prejudiced, and neither changes, B) Both Darcy and Elizabeth must overcome pride and prejudice through self-knowledge, C) Neither character has any faults, D) The title has nothing to do with the characters
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Darcy's pride and Elizabeth's prejudice both mislead them; each learns humility and truer judgement before they can unite.
   * **Why A:** Both characters share both faults, and both genuinely change.
   * **Why C:** Their flaws are central to the story.
   * **Why D:** The title names the very faults the lovers must overcome.

8. **Type: True-False [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** The novel contrasts marriages based on money or looks with Elizabeth and Darcy's marriage based on love and mutual respect.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Set against Charlotte's practical match and Lydia's reckless one, Elizabeth and Darcy's union models marriage founded on understanding and esteem.
   * **WhyWrong:** The novel does draw this contrast — flawed matches around them highlight the love and respect at the heart of Elizabeth and Darcy's marriage.

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** After reading Darcy's letter, Elizabeth realises she has misjudged him. What theme does this moment develop?
   * **Options:** A) That first impressions can be wrong, and self-knowledge corrects them, B) That money is all that matters, C) That she was right all along, D) That reading is dangerous
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Elizabeth's cry "How despicably have I acted!" marks her growth — the novel shows first impressions must be tested by reflection.
   * **Why B:** The lesson is about judgement and humility, not money.
   * **Why C:** She realises she was wrong, which is the point of her growth.
   * **Why D:** The letter brings truth and self-knowledge, not danger.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** Austen is famous for her irony — for instance in the novel's opening line. What does this irony mainly do?
   * **Options:** A) Gently mocks society's assumptions about marriage and money, B) Confuses the reader, C) Describes the weather, D) Provides stage directions
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Austen's irony lets her expose and mock the values of her society while appearing merely to state them.
   * **Why B:** The irony sharpens the meaning rather than confusing it.
   * **Why C:** It comments on social attitudes, not weather.
   * **Why D:** It is a narrative voice, not stage instruction.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** Austen often lets us share Elizabeth's private thoughts while still using a third-person narrator. What is this technique called?
   * **Options:** A) Free indirect speech, B) Onomatopoeia, C) A soliloquy, D) A rhyming couplet
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Free indirect speech blends the narrator's voice with Elizabeth's thoughts, keeping us close to her judgements and errors.
   * **Why B:** It concerns narrative viewpoint, not sound.
   * **Why C:** A soliloquy is a spoken stage speech; this is prose narration.
   * **Why D:** It is a prose technique, not a verse form.
