### Protagonist
- **Note:** Napoleon begins as one revolutionary pig among equals, helping to overthrow Mr Jones under the vow that "All animals are equal"; his defining error is his own choice to seize and hoard power — rearing the dogs in secret, expelling Snowball, taking the milk and apples — until he becomes a whip-carrying, two-legged tyrant indistinguishable from the humans the revolution replaced.

### Plot
- **Note:** Old Major's dream of equality inspires the animals to overthrow Jones, but the pigs quietly seize the milk and apples, Napoleon uses secretly reared dogs to drive out Snowball and rule by force, Squealer's propaganda rewrites the commandments, terror and purges silence dissent, and loyal Boxer is sold to the knacker — each betrayal following by necessity from the last until the pigs walk on two legs and become the new masters.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central themes are the corruption of a revolution once power goes unchecked (the oppressed becoming the oppressors), the hollowing-out of equality into "some animals are more equal than others", and the control of minds through language and propaganda — the endlessly rewritten commandments and mindless chants that replace thought and erase the truth.

### Effects
- **Note:** Animal Farm is a dystopia, and its emotional effect is fear-as-warning: unease and disquiet rather than triumph, because the betrayal creeps in gradually with the animals' own passive complicity, and the final image of pigs indistinguishable from men holds a mirror to our own capacity to be deceived — leaving us cautioned and on guard, not satisfied that a battle has been won.

### Message
- **Note:** The novel warns that a revolution's ideals are betrayed whenever power is left unaccountable — overthrowing one master achieves nothing lasting if the new rulers face no check — and that tyranny returns not only through force but through complacency, forgetting and manipulated truth, so freedom must be guarded by constant vigilance, not merely won once.
