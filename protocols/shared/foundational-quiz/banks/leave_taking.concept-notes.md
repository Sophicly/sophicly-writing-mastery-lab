### Protagonist
- **Note:** Enid is the Jamaican-born mother at the play's heart — one of the Windrush generation who came to Britain after 1948 — defined at first by relentless sacrifice ("man and woman" to her daughters) and by the dream of one day returning "home" to Jamaica; her flaw is clinging too tightly to that idealised past and to her grip on her British-born daughters, and her arc is the hard-won learning to let go and accept that her home and family are now in Britain.

### Plot
- **Note:** Enid's backward-looking hold on an idealised Jamaica strains her British-born daughters, driving Del to rebel and leave — turning instead to Mai and the Caribbean heritage she had resisted — while the death of Enid's mother back in Jamaica shatters the dream of return; forced to confront her denial, Enid at last begins to let go and reach toward acceptance and a fragile reconciliation with her daughters.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central themes are the sacrifices of the migrant Windrush generation, the second generation's search for identity caught between Caribbean heritage and British upbringing, the reclaiming of roots (obeah, Jamaican dialect, Mai's guidance), and "home" reimagined not as a fixed place of return but as something remembered, mourned and finally remade.

### Effects
- **Note:** Leave Taking is a work of social realism, and its emotional effect is pathos and moral discomfort: we feel poignant sympathy for Enid's loneliness and sacrifice and for the family's divisions, and we are troubled by the racism and hardship of 1980s Black Britain that presses on them — an ache of leave-taking softened, at the close, by cautious hope in the family's fragile reconciliation.

### Message
- **Note:** The play affirms that belonging is not simply inherited but forged — each generation must make its own identity from its roots — and that healing comes not from clinging to an idealised past but from taking leave of it: honouring one's heritage while accepting change, so that "home" becomes the life and family one builds and carries, not a place one returns to.
