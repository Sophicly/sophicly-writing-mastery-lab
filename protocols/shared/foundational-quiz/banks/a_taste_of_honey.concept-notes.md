### Protagonist
- **Note:** Jo begins a neglected, defensively hardened teenager, starved of her mother Helen's love; driven by her own longing for warmth and a home, she reaches for affection — briefly with the young sailor (the Boy), then in the tender, unconventional household she builds with Geof — and grows into an expectant mother, yet the play's pathos is that Helen's return pulls her back under the old neglect, the cycle closing rather than breaking.

### Plot
- **Note:** Helen's self-centred neglect leaves Jo lonely and craving love; a brief romance with a young sailor (the Boy) leaves her pregnant and abandoned, so the gentle art student Geof moves in and cares for her, forming a makeshift family — until Helen returns, Geof quietly leaves, and Jo is left facing motherhood back under her mother's shadow.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central ideas are failed, self-centred motherhood and the maternal love Jo is denied; loneliness and the longing for a home of one's own; the constraints of working-class poverty and class; family and love found in unexpected, chosen bonds rather than blood (Jo and Geof); and — in the ironic title — the fleeting fragility of happiness.

### Effects
- **Note:** A Taste of Honey is social realism (kitchen-sink drama), and its emotional effect is pathos and a recognising moral discomfort: we ache for Jo's life hemmed in by poverty and neglect, warmed by the fragile tenderness she finds with Geof yet unsettled that society offers her no rescue — the ironic, unresolved ending refusing tidy comfort and leaving us moved rather than satisfied.

### Message
- **Note:** The play insists that the lives of the poor and marginalised deserve honest, dignified attention, and that love and family can be found in unexpected, chosen bonds rather than blood — yet it warns, without sentimentality, that poverty and neglect tend to repeat across generations, holding a mirror up to a Britain that preferred to look away.
