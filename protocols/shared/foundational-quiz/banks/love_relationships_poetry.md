### Quiz: Love and Relationships Poetry

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** What form does Browning use for "Porphyria's Lover"?
   * **Options:** A) A dramatic monologue, B) A Petrarchan sonnet, C) A ballad, D) An ode
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. A single speaker reveals his own disturbing psychology as he addresses us — the classic shape of a dramatic monologue.
   * **Why B:** It is a long speech, not a fourteen-line sonnet.
   * **Why C:** A ballad tells a story in song-like stanzas; this is one unbroken speech.
   * **Why D:** An ode is a formal poem of praise, not this chilling confession.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Identifying Form]**
   * **Question:** "Sonnet 29" by Elizabeth Barrett Browning uses which form?
   * **Options:** A) Free verse, B) A Petrarchan sonnet, used in a subverted way, C) A ballad, D) A villanelle
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. It is a Petrarchan sonnet, but subverted — an early volta lets the speaker's passion strain against the form.
   * **Why A:** It keeps sonnet length and rhyme, so it is not free verse.
   * **Why C:** A ballad is a narrative song form, unlike this fourteen-line love poem.
   * **Why D:** A villanelle uses strict repeating refrains, which this poem does not.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form Features]**
   * **Question:** How is a Petrarchan sonnet structured?
   * **Options:** A) An octave posing a problem, then a sestet offering resolution, B) Twelve lines then a couplet, C) Three verses and a chorus, D) No structure at all
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Petrarchan sonnet sets up a problem in its eight-line octave, then turns to answer it in the six-line sestet.
   * **Why B:** Three quatrains and a couplet describe the Shakespearean, not Petrarchan, sonnet.
   * **Why C:** A verse-and-chorus shape belongs to song, not the sonnet.
   * **Why D:** The sonnet is a highly structured form.
