### Quiz: Never Let Me Go

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** *Never Let Me Go*, published in 2005, is best described as which kind of novel?
   * **Options:** A) A light-hearted comic romance, B) A speculative, dystopian literary novel, C) A historical novel about the First World War, D) A fast-paced detective thriller
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Ishiguro takes the tools of speculative fiction — an imagined science of cloning — and turns them to quiet, literary ends, using an unsettling premise to explore what it means to be human.
   * **Why A:** The novel is tender but deeply sombre, shadowed by mortality rather than comedy.
   * **Why C:** It has no connection to the First World War; its concern is an imagined near-future science.
   * **Why D:** There is no crime to solve; the tension comes from slow revelation, not detective plotting.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Where and when is the novel set?
   * **Options:** A) A distant planet in the far future, B) An alternative version of England in the late twentieth century, C) Medieval Scotland, D) Present-day outer space
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The story takes place in an England that looks much like our own late twentieth century, yet where medicine has taken a darker turn — an ordinary world made strange by one terrible difference.
   * **Why A:** The setting is recognisably England, not another planet; that familiarity is what makes it disturbing.
   * **Why C:** The novel belongs to a recent, near-contemporary England, not the medieval past.
   * **Why D:** It is grounded in an everyday English landscape of schools, cottages and country roads, not space.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The novel imagines a society that has used human cloning to create people whose organs are later harvested to cure the illnesses of others.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Ishiguro's premise is that cloned children are raised so their bodies can be used for transplants, raising searching questions about the ethics of cloning and biotechnology.
   * **WhyWrong:** This is true — the clones exist to become organ donors, which is the moral horror at the novel's heart.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**

   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Ishiguro's trademark is understatement: Kathy narrates gently and retrospectively, and her very calm makes the cruelty of her world more haunting.
   * **Why A:** Kathy rarely raises her voice in anger; her quiet acceptance is central to the novel's effect.
   * **Why C:** The story is limited to Kathy's own first-person memory, not a narrator who sees everything.
   * **Why D:** The pace is reflective and measured, drawn from recollection rather than urgent action.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What is most striking about how the students respond to the fate that awaits them?
   * **Options:** A) They stage a violent uprising and escape abroad, B) They largely accept it with quiet resignation rather than rebelling, C) They do not know they will die, D) They take the authorities to court
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The most unsettling feature of the novel is that the students do not fight their destiny; their calm acceptance forces the reader to ask why none of them simply runs away.
   * **Why A:** There is no rebellion or escape; the absence of resistance is exactly what disturbs us.
   * **Why C:** The students come to understand their purpose, yet still submit to it.
   * **Why D:** They never seek justice through law; at most they hope quietly for a little more time.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** The students' best artwork was taken away for a special collection. What did the guardians hope this art would prove?
   * **Options:** A) That the students could sell paintings for profit, B) That the students had souls and rich inner lives, so treating them as spare parts was cruel, C) That the students were talentless, D) That art should be forbidden
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The collected art was meant as evidence that the clones possessed souls and feelings, part of a campaign to persuade society that these children deserved to be treated humanely.
   * **Why A:** The purpose was moral, not commercial — the art was gathered to reveal the students' humanity.
   * **Why C:** The aim was the opposite: to show the depth and sensitivity of what the students created.
   * **Why D:** Far from being banned, art was encouraged precisely because it was thought to reveal the soul.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** Ishiguro fills the novel with mild, institutional words such as "carers", "donors" and "completion". What is the effect of this euphemistic vocabulary?
   * **Options:** A) It makes the story feel comic, B) It normalises and conceals the horror, which makes it all the more disturbing, C) It confuses the timeline of events, D) It proves the characters are lying
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The gentle vocabulary lets characters and reader glide past atrocity as though it were routine, and that quiet acceptance of the monstrous is far more chilling than open horror would be.
   * **Why A:** The soft language is unnerving rather than funny; it hides cruelty behind politeness.
   * **Why C:** The euphemisms shape tone, not chronology; the sequence of events stays clear.
   * **Why D:** The characters are not deceiving anyone; the words are simply how their whole society speaks.

   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Kathy's habit of looping back and forth mimics the way real memory works, and her small hints quietly prepare us for revelations to come, deepening the sense of inevitability.
   * **Why A:** The digressions are patterned and purposeful, echoing how the mind remembers.
   * **Why C:** The tone is wistful rather than comic; the pauses build feeling, not laughter.
   * **Why D:** Kathy remains the single narrator throughout; the voice never changes hands.
