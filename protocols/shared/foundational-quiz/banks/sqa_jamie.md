# Foundational Quiz Bank — SQA Scottish Text: Kathleen Jamie Poetry (Higher)

Deterministic, code-scored foundational recall bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank`).
18 questions on the six Kathleen Jamie poems set for SQA Higher (2026 set) — three per poem
across Neil's three understanding dimensions: Recognising the Poem · Form & Features ·
Meaning & Effects. Unlike the universal forms bank (`poetic_forms.md`, Tier A), these are
poem-specific, testing what the student has actually read. Single stage — all six poems are
studied together for this text, so there is no set-staging token. The picker draws a random 5 per
round, stratified across categories. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before
questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

### Quiz: Kathleen Jamie Poetry

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Recognising the Poem]**
   * **Question:** Which poem opens with a memory of a night-time boat crossing — "Remember how we rowed toward the cottage / on the sickle-shaped bay" — after a night at the pub?
   * **Options:** A) Song of Sunday, B) Crossing the Loch, C) Ospreys, D) Mr and Mrs Scotland are Dead
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Crossing the Loch" recalls two young people rowing back to a cottage across a dark loch late one night, a reckless crossing remembered years later with relief.
   * **Why A:** Song of Sunday recalls a dreich childhood Sunday indoors, not a night-time boat crossing.
   * **Why C:** Ospreys follows migrating birds returning to a riverside nest, not a boat trip.
   * **Why D:** Mr and Mrs Scotland are Dead is set at a landfill site, not on a loch.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form & Features]**
   * **Question:** How does Jamie shape "Crossing the Loch" to hold both the story and its meaning?
   * **Options:** A) A strict rhyming ballad tells the tale as legend, B) Conversational narrative shifts into passages of more formal, lyrical reflection, C) A single unbroken list of images with no narrative, D) A dramatic monologue spoken by an unnamed judge
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The poem moves between plain, conversational storytelling — addressed to a friend who shared the memory — and more lyrical, reflective lines that draw out what the crossing came to mean.
   * **Why A:** There is no strict rhyme scheme or ballad metre; the voice is conversational, not legendary.
   * **Why C:** The poem tells a clear narrative of the crossing, not a plotless list of images.
   * **Why D:** The poem is addressed to a remembered companion, not delivered as a judge's monologue.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests Meaning & Effects]**
   * **Question:** What does "Mr and Mrs Scotland are Dead" suggest by setting personal, intimate objects (postcards, a "Dictionary for Mothers") against the anonymous language of the dump?
   * **Options:** A) That personal history is trivial and not worth remembering, B) That an entire way of life and a whole nation's intimate memory can be reduced to anonymous rubbish, C) That Mr and Mrs Scotland were careless people, D) That landfill sites should be abolished
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Setting tender, personal traces against the flat, anonymous language of the tip suggests how an entire generation's intimate life — and by extension the nation's — can end up as discarded rubbish, prompting the poem's ethical question of what should be rescued.
   * **Why A:** The poem values these objects deeply — its unease comes from how easily they are discarded, not from any claim they are trivial.
   * **Why C:** Nothing in the poem blames the couple; the found objects speak of an ordinary, careful life.
   * **Why D:** The poem is reflective rather than a campaigning argument against landfill sites.

14. **Type: MCQ [Tests Form & Features]**

    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Rhyming couplets give the poem a song-like, ceremonial lift suited to a state occasion, while varied line lengths let the voice move flexibly between warning, blessing and address.
    * **Why A:** The poem does use rhyme, particularly in its couplets, alongside its flexible lines.
    * **Why C:** A sonnet's fixed fourteen lines could not carry the poem's shifting ceremonial address.

    * **Correct:** B

    * **Why C:** The poem insists "all things pass and change" and calls for bold action, not resignation.
    * **Why D:** The bird explicitly asks for wisdom "leavened with music and song" — the arts are part of its vision of good governance.
