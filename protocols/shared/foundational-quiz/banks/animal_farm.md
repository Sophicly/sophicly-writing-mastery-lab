### Quiz: Animal Farm

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Animal Farm is an allegory. Which real historical event does it mainly retell?
   * **Options:** A) The French Revolution, B) The Russian Revolution of 1917 and what followed it, C) The American Civil War, D) The First World War
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The animals' rebellion and its betrayal mirror the Russian Revolution of 1917 and the rise of Soviet tyranny.
   * **Why A:** The parallels are with Russia after 1917, not revolutionary France.
   * **Why C:** The novel allegorises the Soviet story, not the American Civil War.
   * **Why D:** It reworks a revolution and its aftermath, not the First World War.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** In the allegory, the pig Napoleon most closely represents which real figure?
   * **Options:** A) Trotsky, B) Stalin, C) Lenin, D) Tsar Nicholas II
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Napoleon, who seizes total power and builds a cult around himself, stands for Stalin.
   * **Why A:** Trotsky is represented by the rival pig Snowball, whom Napoleon drives out.
   * **Why C:** Lenin's ideas are closer to Old Major, who inspires the rebellion.
   * **Why D:** The Tsar is represented by the human farmer, Mr Jones, not by Napoleon.

3. **Type: True-False [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** Orwell wrote the novel to expose how the ideals of a revolution can be betrayed and twisted into a new tyranny.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Orwell, a democratic socialist, attacked the way Stalin's regime corrupted the revolution's promises of equality.
   * **WhyWrong:** Orwell did write it as a warning — his target is the betrayal of revolutionary ideals by a corrupt new ruling class.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** The animals are forced to confess to crimes and are then executed. Which real events does this reflect?
   * **Options:** A) Stalin's show trials and purges, B) Free elections, C) A peace treaty, D) The building of railways
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The forced confessions and killings mirror Stalin's show trials and the Great Terror of the 1930s.
   * **Why B:** The scene shows terror and repression, the opposite of free elections.
   * **Why C:** It reflects violent purges, not any peace treaty.
   * **Why D:** The parallel is with political terror, not industrial building.

5. **Type: Fill [Tests Context]**
   * **Question:** A story in which animals stand for real people and events to teach a political lesson is called a [BLANK].
   * **Answer:** fable
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Orwell called it "a fairy story", but it works as a political fable — a simple animal tale carrying a serious lesson.
   * **WhyWrong:** The word is "fable" — a short tale, often with animals, that carries a moral or political lesson.

6. **Type: Select All [Tests Context]**

   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A,B,C. 1 mark if mostly correct.

   * **Why D:** The moon landings came decades later and have no place in the allegory.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** What does the novel suggest about power through the pigs' behaviour?
   * **Options:** A) That power always makes leaders kinder, B) That power corrupts — the pigs grow as cruel and greedy as the humans they overthrew, C) That animals cannot govern at all, D) That power should belong to the strongest
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The pigs end up walking, drinking and exploiting like Mr Jones — Orwell's warning that unchecked power corrupts.
   * **Why A:** Power makes the pigs crueller, not kinder.
   * **Why C:** The failure is moral corruption, not an inability to organise.
   * **Why D:** The novel condemns rule by force rather than endorsing it.

8. **Type: True-False [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** The commandment "All animals are equal" is finally changed to add "but some animals are more equal than others", showing how the ideal of equality is corrupted.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The rewritten commandment exposes the pigs' hypocrisy — equality has become a cover for privilege.
   * **WhyWrong:** The commandment is indeed rewritten this way — the cynical addition shows equality twisted to justify the pigs' power.

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests Themes]**
   * **Question:** How does the novel show power being maintained through language?
   * **Options:** A) By banning all speech, B) Through propaganda and lies that rewrite the truth to keep the animals obedient, C) By teaching every animal to read equally, D) Through honest public debate
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Squealer's propaganda constantly rewrites events and commandments, using language to control what the animals believe.
   * **Why A:** Speech is not banned; it is manipulated and twisted instead.
   * **Why C:** The pigs in fact keep real literacy and knowledge for themselves.
   * **Why D:** Honest debate is crushed; the pigs rule through spin and fear.

   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "More equal than others" is a deliberate contradiction that lays bare the pigs' hypocrisy and the death of true equality.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** What is the main technique Orwell uses across the whole novel?
   * **Options:** A) Allegory, where the animal story stands for real political events, B) A first-person diary, C) A collection of unconnected poems, D) A factual history essay
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The entire novel is an allegory: every animal and event maps onto the people and moments of the Russian Revolution.
   * **Why B:** It is a third-person fable, not a diary.
   * **Why C:** It is a continuous narrative, not separate poems.
   * **Why D:** It is fiction that carries meaning through story, not a factual essay.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests Techniques]**
   * **Question:** The slogan "Four legs good, two legs bad" is repeated by the sheep. What is its purpose in the novel?
   * **Options:** A) To show simple, catchy propaganda that stops the animals thinking for themselves, B) To describe the farm's geography, C) To make the reader laugh, D) To teach the animals to count
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The reduced slogan is propaganda — an easy chant that drowns out debate and keeps the animals from questioning the pigs.
   * **Why B:** It is a political chant, not a description of the land.
   * **Why C:** Its real work is control, not comedy.
   * **Why D:** It is about obedience, not arithmetic.

   * **WhyWrong:** The ending does show this — the animals cannot tell pigs from men, symbolising how completely the pigs have become their former oppressors.
