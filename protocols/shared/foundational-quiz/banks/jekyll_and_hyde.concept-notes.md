### Protagonist
- **Note:** Dr Jekyll begins as a respected, benevolent doctor whose hamartia is not curiosity itself but his choice to divide and indulge his hidden self; believing "man is not truly one, but truly two", he brews a potion to free his darker nature as Edward Hyde, but each indulgence strengthens Hyde until the transformations turn involuntary and the double overwhelms him, bringing "the life of that unhappy Henry Jekyll to an end".

### Plot
- **Note:** Driven by his theory of the divided self, Jekyll creates a potion that transforms him into the savage Hyde and indulges his appetites, but the evil self grows — trampling a child, then beating Sir Danvers Carew to death — until Jekyll begins changing involuntarily, cannot reverse it, and is found dead as Hyde, his written confession finally revealing the truth to Utterson.

### Themes
- **Note:** The central themes are the duality of human nature — that every person is "truly two", good and evil bound together in one body — the danger of repression behind a respectable Victorian surface, and the overreach of science that releases forces beyond moral control.

### Effects
- **Note:** Jekyll and Hyde is a Gothic novella, and its emotional effect is dread, horror and moral unease: we are disturbed because Hyde is not an outside monster but the evil concealed within a respectable, ordinary man, so his horror implicates human nature itself — and ourselves. The fog-bound city and Hyde's barely-human deformity feed that dread, and the ending leaves us unsettled rather than reassured, warning that the beast may lurk within anyone.

### Message
- **Note:** The novella warns that every person harbours both good and evil, and that trying to sever, deny or hide the darker self only lets it grow until it takes control — while a society obsessed with respectable appearances drives its desires into a secrecy where they fester, and science pursued without moral restraint destroys its maker.
