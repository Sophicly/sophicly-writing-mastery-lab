# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Frankenstein

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Frankenstein against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ for the 19th-century novel: AQA (8702) = AO1+AO2+AO3 (no AO4 on the novel);
Edexcel GCSE (1ET0) splits Part (a) AO2 / Part (b) AO1 (no AO3 marks on the novel); Edexcel IGCSE 4ET1 = AO1+AO2+AO4 (AO4 = CONTEXT, not SPaG);
Eduqas (C720U) = AO1+AO2+AO3 equally weighted; OCR (J352) marks holistically (AO1/AO2 dominant, AO3 context + AO4 technical accuracy); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = knowledge/understanding/effects/personal response across eight levels; SQA = Understanding/Analysis/Evaluation.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Shelley presents Victor as a scientist who creates a creature, abandons it, and is finally destroyed by it." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, conceptualised argument organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character/plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing what Victor does across the plot is character-level comment — AQA Level 3 "some explained response". To climb, the student must treat Victor as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (e.g. ambition severed from responsibility), not narrate his career.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates a career, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare "simple comment" — it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four Frankenstein openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Shelley constructs ambition as a Promethean transgression that severs Victor from the human bonds that might have restrained him, using the novel to interrogate the cost of knowledge pursued without responsibility.", B) "Victor makes a creature, runs away from it, and it kills his family.", C) "Ambition is a dangerous thing in the novel, and it leads to a lot of death and misery.", D) "Shelley presents ambition as destructive through Victor's creation of the Creature, making the reader judge how far the desire to know can corrupt."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a dangerous thing causing death, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the creation) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear explained response). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, then comment, then explained method-and-effect, then concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote the Creature's "I ought to be thy Adam". One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument that abandonment, not birth, damns him. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Shelley uses the metaphor 'spark of being'. This is a technique. It tells us Victor brings the Creature to life." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us he brings life" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

### **SECTION B: EDEXCEL GCSE (1ET0)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** How does Edexcel GCSE structure its 40-mark 19th-century novel question, and which objectives do the two parts test?
   * **Options:** A) One 40-mark whole-text essay blending all objectives, B) Part (a) tests AO2 only on the printed extract; Part (b) tests AO1 only on the whole text, C) Part (a) tests AO1 and Part (b) tests AO3, D) A single 40-mark AO1 question with no extract
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Edexcel splits the novel question: Part (a), worth 20 marks, tests ONLY analysis of language, form and structure (AO2) on the printed extract; Part (b), worth 20 marks, tests ONLY whole-text knowledge and argument (AO1). Keeping the two tasks distinct is essential.

   * **Why C:** Part (a) is AO2 and Part (b) is AO1; AO3 carries no explicit marks on this question.
   * **Why D:** There is a printed extract, and it anchors the AO2 analysis in Part (a).
