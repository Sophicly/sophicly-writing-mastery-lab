# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — DNA

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about DNA (Dennis Kelly) against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and
forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA Modern Text = AO1+AO2+AO3 (whole-paper AO4 SPaG,
not assessed on this question); Edexcel GCSE Post-1914 = AO1+AO3 (AO2 NOT assessed) + AO4(SPaG); Eduqas Post-1914 =
AO1+AO2 + AO4(SPaG); OCR (J352) = AO1+AO2+AO3; Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = AO1(knowledge)+AO2(understanding)+AO3(analysis of effects)+AO4(personal response, NOT SPaG).

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "In DNA the gang bully Adam, he falls, they cover it up, and then they kill him for real." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, conceptualised argument organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Narrating the sequence of events is plot-level comment — AQA Level 3 "some explained response". To climb, the student must treat the gang as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (collective cruelty dissolving individual conscience), not retell the story.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates events, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is a sequenced retelling rather than a bare undeveloped remark, so it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four DNA openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Kelly constructs the gang as a single organism whose collective will dissolves private conscience, using Adam's death to interrogate how ordinary adolescents author atrocity together.", B) "Adam is bullied by the gang, falls down a grille, and the others cover it up before he is found alive.", C) "Bullying is a bad thing in the play, and it leads to a boy being hurt and a lot of lying.", D) "Kelly presents cruelty as contagious through the gang's shared cover-up, making the audience feel how quickly a group silences its own doubt."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The clear rung that names a method (the shared cover-up) and its effect on the audience is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea about collective conscience, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote Cathy "quite enjoying this." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about cruelty becoming a currency of power for the powerless. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**

   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the stage direction and adding "it tells us he does not speak" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs clear explanation of the effect; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used judiciously.
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** Examination (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how Phil's silence makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
