# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Silas Marner

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Silas Marner against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ on the 19th-century novel: AQA (8702) = AO1+AO2+AO3
(no AO4 on the novel); Edexcel GCSE (1ET0) = AO2 on the extract + AO1 on the whole text (no explicit AO3 marks);
Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) = AO1+AO2+AO4(CONTEXT, not SPaG); Eduqas (C720U) = AO1+AO2+AO3 equally weighted; OCR (J352) =
AO1+AO2 dominant, AO3(context)+AO4(SPaG) credited holistically; Cambridge IGCSE (0475) = knowledge/reference,
critical understanding, response to the writer's effects, personal-and-evaluative engagement; SQA = Understanding/Analysis/Evaluation.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Eliot presents Silas as a lonely weaver who is robbed of his gold and then finds a child on his hearth." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character and plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing what happens to Silas across the plot is character-and-event comment — AQA Level 3 "explained/relevant comments". To climb, the student must treat Silas as a vehicle for a concept (isolation and renewed human bonds), not narrate his story.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates events, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with apt references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the weaver, not a bare simple comment — it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four Silas Marner openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Eliot constructs Silas's hoarded gold as the measure of a soul contracted by betrayal, so the guineas become the novel's index of a life emptied of trust until Eppie restores it.", B) "Silas is a weaver who is robbed of his gold and later takes in a child.", C) "Money is a bad thing in the novel, and it makes Silas lonely and sad.", D) "Eliot presents gold as isolating through Silas's nightly counting of his guineas, making the reader feel how far his life has narrowed."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing loneliness, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear rung that names a method (the nightly counting) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, then comment, then explained method-and-effect, then concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "his gold... his own dry, hard hand." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about Silas's love hardening into metal. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes judicious use of precise references to support interpretation rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Eliot uses the metaphor of gold. This is a technique. It tells us Silas is greedy." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us he is greedy" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 examination; Level 6 "analysis of the writer's methods... exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings" with terminology used judiciously.
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

   * **Correct:** B
