# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — The Old Man and the Sea

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about The Old Man and the Sea against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4(SPaG);
Edexcel GCSE Post-1914 = AO1+AO3+AO4(SPaG), NO AO2; Eduqas Post-1914 = AO1+AO2+AO4(SPaG), NO AO3;
OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3 (AO1 & AO3 equally dominant, AO4 technical accuracy elsewhere); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4(PERSONAL RESPONSE, not SPaG), holistic best-fit.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Hemingway presents Santiago as an old fisherman who goes 84 days without a catch, then catches a huge marlin, and loses it to sharks." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — some explained comment that stays at character and plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Tracing what Santiago does across the novella is character-and-plot narration — AQA "some explained response". To climb, the writer must treat the old man as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (endurance conferring dignity in the face of certain defeat), not recount the fishing trip.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates the voyage rather than arguing an idea.
   * **Why B:** A thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this offers neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare simple comment — it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four openings on The Old Man and the Sea from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Hemingway constructs Santiago's ordeal as an interrogation of what dignity can mean when defeat is certain, using the marlin and the sharks to test whether a man's worth lies in outcome or in endurance.", B) "Santiago is a fisherman who catches a marlin and then loses it to sharks on the way home.", C) "Endurance is an important thing in the novella, and it leads to a lot of suffering and pride.", D) "Hemingway presents endurance as ennobling through Santiago's long struggle with the marlin, making the reader admire how far the old man will go."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about an important thing causing suffering, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the struggle) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear explained response). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder runs plot to comment to explained method-and-effect to concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "a man can be destroyed but not defeated." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about dignity surviving defeat. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. Santiago's maxim, selected to anchor a concept, outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Hemingway uses the metaphor of the marlin as a 'brother'. This is a technique. It tells us Santiago respects the fish." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us he respects the fish" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
