# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Animal Farm

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Animal Farm against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA Modern Texts = AO1+AO2+AO3; Edexcel GCSE
Post-1914 = AO1+AO3 (AO2 not assessed) plus AO4 (SPaG); Eduqas Post-1914 = AO1+AO2 plus AO4 (SPaG, AO3 not
assessed here); OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3 (part (a) comparison, AO1 and AO3 equally dominant; part (b) AO1+AO2
holistic); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = AO1 (knowledge) + AO2 (understanding) + AO3 (analysis of effects) + AO4
(personal response, NOT SPaG), marked holistically by best fit.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Orwell tells the story of the animals overthrowing Mr Jones, renaming Manor Farm as Animal Farm, and the pigs gradually taking control until they become just like the humans." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 4 — clear understanding of character and events rather than a conceptualised argument, D) Level 1 — an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Tracing the sequence of the takeover is narrative-led "clear understanding" (AQA Level 4), not the "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" the top band demands. To climb, the student must treat the novella as a vehicle for a concept — how a revolution founded on equality is captured by those who seize power — rather than retell events.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, exploratory argument the top band requires is absent — this narrates the plot, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed" response needs apt references integrated into interpretation, and this offers neither development nor quotation.
   * **Why D:** It is organised, accurate retelling of the whole text, well above an undeveloped simple comment.

   * **Why A:** The top band is far more than plot knowledge — it is a conceptualised, exploratory argument.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four Animal Farm openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Orwell constructs the Rebellion as an ideal betrayed from within, using Animal Farm to interrogate how the language of equality becomes the instrument of a new tyranny.", B) "Animal Farm is about some animals who rebel against the farmer and are then ruled by the pigs.", C) "The pigs are bad because they take over and treat the other animals unfairly, which is quite sad.", D) "Orwell presents the corruption of the Rebellion through Napoleon's rise to power, making the reader judge how far the pigs betray the founding ideals."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating the takeover only, is the lowest band. The bare value-judgement about the pigs being bad, with no method and no evidence, is a step up. The clear, explained rung that names a method — Napoleon's rise — and its effect on the reader is Level 4 clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Orwell uses the slogan 'Four legs good, two legs bad'. This is a technique. It tells us the sheep are simple." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the slogan and adding "it tells us the sheep are simple" is identification with a general comment — AQA Level 3 AO2. Level 4 needs clear explanation of the effect; Level 5 "examination of the writer's methods"; Level 6 "exploration of effects of the writer's methods to create meanings." To rise, explore how the reductive chant lets the pigs replace thought with reflex.
   * **Why A:** No exploration of layered effect — the reduction of a whole creed to a mindless chant is left unexamined.
   * **Why B:** Examination (Level 5) requires working through how the slogan manufactures obedience, absent here.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Assured" signals intellectual authority — "an assured personal response, showing a high level of engagement with the text."    * **Why A:** Confidence words only assert assurance; without an authoritative reading behind them they read as empty emphasis.
   * **Why C:** A volume of opinion is not authority; assurance is command of a well-supported reading.
   * **Why D:** Contrarianism is not assurance; authority of judgement is, whichever reading is taken.
