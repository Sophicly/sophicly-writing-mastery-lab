# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Edexcel IGCSE Anthology Poetry (Language Spec A 4EA1)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder and more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE writing about the anthology poems against the EXACT Edexcel IGCSE Spec A (4EA1) Paper 2
level descriptors, and use the mark scheme's own words. Keys and feedback live server-side and are
stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

**Rewritten 2026-09-27 against the June 2024 Paper 2 mark scheme (4EA1_02_2406_MS) and question paper.**
The earlier version described Questions 1-3 retrieval, a 20-mark single-text analysis and a 20-mark AO3
comparison — a paper that does not exist. The real paper: Paper 2 "Poetry and Prose Texts and Imaginative
Writing", 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks. The poems are examined in Section A, Question 1 (30 marks): "How
does the writer present … in <poem>?", two bullet prompts plus "the use of language and structure", the
whole poem printed. AO1 = 12 marks (four levels: 1–3 · 4–6 · 7–9 · 10–12). AO2 = 18 marks (five levels:
1–3 · 4–6 · 7–10 · 11–14 · 15–18). No AO3, no comparison, no retrieval questions. Section B (imaginative
writing, AO4 18 + AO5 12) appears only where it links to the poems. Question types: MCQ · Select All ·
Fill · Ranking. AOs are tagged per question for stratified random selection.

Edexcel-IGCSE-exclusive: this bank serves ONLY Edexcel IGCSE Spec A (4EA1) anthology poetry, so it is a
single complete section by design.

The five anthology poems: "Disabled" (Wilfred Owen), "Out, Out—" (Robert Frost), "An Unknown Girl"
(Moniza Alvi), "The Bright Lights of Sarajevo" (Tony Harrison), "Still I Rise" (Maya Angelou). Every
quotation is checked against the Anthology text.

### **SECTION A: EDEXCEL IGCSE SPEC A (4EA1 — Anthology Poetry)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** An essay on "Disabled" says: "Owen writes 'Legless, sewn short at elbow'. This tells us the soldier has lost his legs and an arm." Which AO2 level does this reach?
   * **Options:** A) Level 1 (1–3) — limited identification, with no comment on the effect, B) Level 2 (4–6) — some developing comment on the effect, C) Level 3 (7–10) — clear explanations of the effects, D) Level 4 (11–14) — detailed exploration of the effects
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. It chooses a strong quotation but only explains what it means, which shows understanding (AO1), not analysis. AO2 Level 1 is "Limited identification of language and/or structural devices" and "Basic and simple comment on the effect". The next step is to say what the cold, practical words "sewn short" make the reader feel about the soldier.
   * **Why B:** Level 2 needs "some developing comment on the effect". No effect is named.
   * **Why C:** Level 3 needs a clear explanation of an effect.
   * **Why D:** Level 4 needs detailed exploration. This is a single literal statement.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** An essay on "Out, Out—" says: "The verbs 'snarled and rattled' make the saw sound like a wild animal, and because the phrase is repeated, 'snarled and rattled, snarled and rattled', the threat keeps growing until the saw 'Leaped out at the boy's hand'." Which AO2 level fits best?
   * **Options:** A) Level 2 (4–6) — some developing comment, B) Level 3 (7–10) — clear explanations, C) Level 4 (11–14) — detailed exploration of the effects of language and structure, D) Level 5 (15–18) — perceptive analysis
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. It follows one image through the poem, linking a language choice (the animal verbs) to a structural one (the repetition) and to the moment of the accident. That is Level 4: "Thorough and confident selection", "Confident and detailed use of textual references" and "Detailed exploration of the effects of language and structure". Level 5 would add a perceptive point about what the poem is saying, for example that the saw seems more alive and willing than the adults who fail to stop work.
   * **Why A:** This develops the effect well beyond a "developing comment".
   * **Why B:** It does more than explain one effect: it traces the effect through the poem.
   * **Why D:** It explores in detail but does not yet reach a perceptive reading of the poem's meaning.

3. **Type: Fill [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AO2 Level 5 (15–18) descriptor: "Subtle and [BLANK] selection of language and structural devices."
   * **Answer:** discriminating
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 5 reads "Subtle and discriminating selection of language and structural devices", "Discriminating and assured use of textual references" and "Perceptive analysis of the effects of language and structure." Discriminating means well-judged: the most telling details, not the most details.
   * **WhyWrong:** The word is "discriminating". "Thorough and confident selection" is the Level 4 wording.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Rank these comments on "You may write me down in history / With your bitter, twisted lies" from "Still I Rise" from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AO2 level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Angelou opens by handing her enemy the pen, 'You may write me down in history', so the poem begins as a reply to a false record; each 'You may' concession is answered by 'I'll rise', and the pattern turns the whole poem into a correction of that history.", B) "Angelou talks about history and lies, which makes the poem sound angry.", C) "The phrase 'bitter, twisted lies' clearly shows that the speaker's enemies have told untrue stories about her, and the harsh sounds make the lies seem cruel.", D) "Angelou explores the idea of being written about unfairly: 'write me down' suggests being recorded and also being pushed down, and the same 'You may' structure returns in 'You may shoot me with your words', so the attacks build while her answer stays the same."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The weakest gives a simple comment (Level 2). The next clearly explains the effect of the words (Level 3). The third explores a double meaning and a repeated structure in detail (Level 4). The strongest gives a perceptive analysis of how the opening sets up the whole poem as a reply to a false history (Level 5).
   * **WhyWrong:** Each step does more with the same lines: a simple comment, a clear explanation, detailed exploration across the poem, then a perceptive reading of how the structure carries the poem's meaning.

   * **Options:** A) Thorough and confident selection of language and structural devices, B) Confident and detailed use of textual references, C) Detailed exploration of the effects of language and structure, D) Perceptive analysis of the effects of language and structure
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Those three phrases make up Level 4, summed up as "Thorough, Confident, Exploratory". "Perceptive analysis" belongs to Level 5.
   * **Why D:** "Perceptive analysis" is the Level 5 wording.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A student writes about the couplets of "The Bright Lights of Sarajevo": "After the hours that Sarajevans pass / queuing with empty canisters of gas". Which comment reaches the highest AO2 level?
   * **Options:** A) "Harrison uses rhyming couplets.", B) "The rhyme makes the poem sound nice.", C) "The steady couplets, 'pass' and 'gas', give the long lists of daily hardship a dull, repeating beat, so the reader feels how exhausting and routine the queuing has become, which makes the young people's evening walk seem even more precious.", D) "The rhyme is a structural technique that Harrison uses."
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. It links a structural choice to its effect on the reader and to the poem's wider meaning, which is Level 4 moving towards Level 5. Naming the form, calling it "a technique" or saying it "sounds nice" stays at Level 1 or 2.
   * **Why A:** Naming the form is limited identification (Level 1).
   * **Why B:** "Sounds nice" is a basic, simple comment.
   * **Why D:** Calling it a technique still names it without any effect.

7. **Type: Fill [Tests AO2]**

   * **Answer:** explanations
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 3 is "Clear and relevant selection", "Relevant and generally appropriate use of textual references" and "Clear explanations of the effects of language and structure." Level 4 moves from explaining to "detailed exploration".
   * **WhyWrong:** The word is "explanations". "Exploration" is Level 4 and "analysis" is Level 5.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** An essay on "Disabled" notes that the poem moves between "Now" and "In the old times". What must it do with this to reach Level 5?
   * **Options:** A) Analyse perceptively how the jumps between past and present keep setting the soldier's lively youth against his empty present, so the reader feels the loss again and again, B) List every place the tense changes, C) Say that the poem is sad, D) Count the stanzas
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Structure earns Level 5 when its effect is analysed perceptively. Listing the changes is identification, a general "sad" is a basic comment, and counting stanzas describes the poem without analysing it.
   * **Why B:** A list of changes is limited identification.
   * **Why C:** "Sad" is a basic comment with no analysis of structure.
   * **Why D:** Counting stanzas is not analysis of effect.

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** How is AO1 worded in the Paper 2 mark scheme, and how many marks does it carry on Question 1?
   * **Options:** A) "Read and understand a variety of texts, selecting and interpreting information, ideas and perspectives" — 12 marks, B) "Explore links and connections between writers' ideas" — 10 marks, C) "Understand and analyse how writers use linguistic and structural devices" — 12 marks, D) "Communicate effectively and imaginatively" — 18 marks
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO1 is understanding the poem and choosing evidence, worth 12 of the 30 marks. Analysis of language and structure is AO2 (18 marks).
   * **Why B:** Exploring links between writers is AO3, which is not on this paper.
   * **Why C:** That is AO2, and it is worth 18 marks.
   * **Why D:** That is AO4, the Section B writing objective.

10. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** An essay on "An Unknown Girl" makes valid points with accurate quotations, and each point is developed so that the references are "appropriate and relevant to the points being made", but the understanding is not yet held across the whole essay. Which AO1 level fits?
   * **Options:** A) Level 1 (1–3), B) Level 2 (4–6), C) Level 3 (7–9), D) Level 4 (10–12)
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 3 is "Sound understanding of the text", with selection and references "appropriate and relevant to the points being made". Level 4 needs "Sustained understanding" and references that are "detailed, appropriate and fully supports the points being made".
   * **Why A:** The points are valid and developed, so this is well above "limited".
   * **Why B:** Level 2 is "valid, but not developed". These points are developed.
   * **Why D:** Level 4 needs understanding sustained across the whole essay.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Question 1 in June 2024 asked about "the importance of traditional Indian culture in An Unknown Girl", with the bullets "how the writer presents the cultural experience with the unknown girl" and "how the writer presents feelings about the place". An essay writes only about the henna and never mentions the bazaar or the streets. What is the main AO1 problem?
   * **Options:** A) It covers only part of the question, so its selection of ideas is narrower than the question asks for, B) It uses too many quotations, C) It has not compared the poem with another, D) It has not included facts about Moniza Alvi's life
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO1 rewards "selecting and interpreting information, ideas and perspectives" that answer the question. Leaving out a whole bullet narrows the selection. The mark scheme gives plenty to say about the place, for example "the evening bazaar / studded with neon" and "the furious streets / are hushed".
   * **Why B:** Quotations are not the problem. Missing half the question is.
   * **Why C:** There is no comparison on this paper.
   * **Why D:** Background facts are not assessed.

   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **AO:** AO1

15. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Rank these comments on "I have new brown veins" from "An Unknown Girl" from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AO2 level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "The short sentence 'I have new brown veins' stops the flow of the poem, as if the henna has become part of her body, so the culture now seems to run inside her.", B) "Alvi writes about veins.", C) "The metaphor makes the henna lines sound like veins.", D) "The short sentence and the metaphor make the henna part of her body, yet 'It will fade in a week', so the poem shows a belonging that feels deep but cannot last, which is why the ending leaves her 'longing for the unknown girl'."
   * **Correct:** B, C, A, D
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The weakest only points at a word (Level 1). The next identifies the device with a simple comment (Level 2). The third clearly explains the effect of a structural and a language choice together (Level 3). The strongest links the line to the fading henna and the ending, a perceptive reading of what the poem says about culture (Level 5). The mark scheme itself notes that short sentences "create emphasis on the place and the traditional experience".
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest: a word pointed at, a device named with a simple comment, a clear explanation, then a perceptive link across the whole poem.

18. **Type: MCQ [Tests Vocabulary]**
   * **Question:** What does the word "perceptive" in AO2 Level 5 ask for?
   * **Options:** A) Insight: seeing what a choice means for the whole poem, beyond its surface effect, B) A formal tone, C) Covering every technique, D) Correcting other readers
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Perceptive analysis" sees beneath the surface, for example reading the fading henna in "An Unknown Girl" as a tradition that may be lost, as the mark scheme itself suggests.
   * **Why B:** Tone is not insight.
   * **Why C:** Coverage is breadth. Perception is depth.
   * **Why D:** Perception is about the poem, not about other readers.
