# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — The Sign of the Four

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about *The Sign of the Four* against the EXACT mark-scheme
descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified
paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI
is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA 8702 novel = AO1+AO2+AO3 (NO AO4/SPaG on the novel);
Edexcel GCSE 1ET0 = AO2 on the extract + AO1 on the whole text (context is "invisible ink", 0 explicit marks);
Edexcel IGCSE 4ET1 = AO1+AO2+AO4(CONTEXT, not SPaG); Eduqas C720U = AO1+AO2+AO3 equally weighted;
OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4(SPaG), marked holistically; Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = knowledge / critical understanding
(individuality and insight) / writer's effects / personal-and-evaluative response; SQA = Understanding / Analysis / Evaluation.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Conan Doyle presents Holmes examining the clues, deducing that Jonathan Small and Tonga are the killers, and recovering the story of the Agra treasure." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, conceptualised argument organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character/plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Narrating how the detection unfolds is character/plot comment — AQA Level 3 "some explained response". To climb, the student must treat the novel as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (e.g. rationalism striving to master a threatening, unknowable world), not retell the case.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates the case, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about events, not a bare "simple comment" — it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four openings on *The Sign of the Four* from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Conan Doyle constructs the Agra treasure as tainted colonial plunder, using the novel to interrogate whether empire can morally hold the wealth it seizes.", B) "Mary Morstan asks Holmes for help, the treasure is found, and it is finally lost in the Thames.", C) "The treasure is a bad thing in the novel, and it causes a lot of trouble and death.", D) "Conan Doyle presents the treasure as destructive through the deaths it causes, making the reader question the cost of empire."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote Holmes's maxim "whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about rationalism imposing order on chaos. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**

   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the pathetic fallacy and adding "it tells us the mood is gloomy" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
