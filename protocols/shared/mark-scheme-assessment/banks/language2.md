# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — AQA English Language Paper 2 (8700/2)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing against the EXACT AQA mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to
USE the board's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side
and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across AO1–AO6).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking.

AQA **English Language** is marked on a FOUR-level scale — NOT the six-level Literature scale. Exact AQA
8700/2 (June 2024) descriptors: Level 4 Perceptive/detailed · Level 3 Clear/relevant · Level 2 Some/attempts ·
Level 1 Simple/limited. Paper 2 questions: Q2 AO1 synthesis (8) · Q3 AO2 language (12) · Q4 AO3 comparison (16) ·
Q5 writing — AO5 content + organisation (24) + AO6 technical accuracy (16). There is no AO4 on Paper 2.
Ranking questions use AQA's OWN indicative-standard L1→L4 answers as the rungs (one Level apart).

### **SECTION A: AQA (8700/2 — Paper 2)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** For Paper 2 Question 2 (AO1 synthesis, 8 marks), a student writes: "In the first source the doctor works in a hospital and in the second the nurse works on a wharf." On the AQA four-level scale, what is the CEILING for this response, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 4 — a perceptive, detailed synthesis drawing judicious references from both texts, B) Level 3 — it makes clear inferences and selects clear detail from both texts, C) Level 1 — it paraphrases the two places rather than inferring meaning from them, D) Level 2 — it attempts some inference and shows some difference between the texts
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 1 AO1 = "simple, limited summary… offers paraphrase rather than inference." Naming where each person works, with no inferred meaning, is paraphrase — the bottom band. To climb, the student must INFER (e.g. the hospital's permanence against the wharf's makeshift desperation), which is Level 2 and above.
   * **Why A:** Level 4 needs "perceptive synthesis and interpretation… judicious references" — there is no inference and no selected detail here.
   * **Why B:** Level 3 needs "clear inferences from both texts" — this retells the two places rather than inferring from them.
   * **Why D:** Level 2 needs an attempted inference; bare paraphrase of place sits below it at Level 1.

2. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA wording that Question 2 rewards above paraphrase. A top response "makes perceptive [BLANK] from both texts" — the act of drawing out implied meaning.
   * **Answer:** inferences
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 4 AO1 = "makes perceptive inferences from both texts." Inference — reading the implied meaning behind the detail — is what lifts a summary out of Level 1 paraphrase. Each band re-uses the word: simple → clear → perceptive inferences.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "inferences" — Question 2 rewards inferred meaning, not retold detail.

3. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Question 2 tests AO1's second bullet: "select and [BLANK] evidence from different texts." Complete the verb that names combining material across both sources.
   * **Answer:** synthesise
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA AO1 = "Select and synthesise evidence from different texts." Synthesis means weaving detail from BOTH sources into a combined point of difference — not handling each text in a separate paragraph. It is the skill Question 2 is built to test.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "synthesise" — Question 2 rewards combining evidence across the two texts, not summarising them one at a time.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** These four summaries answer Paper 2 Question 2 on the DIFFERENT PLACES the doctor (Source A) and the nurse (Source B) work. Rank them WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) In Source A the doctor works in a hospital that has a range of modern facilities — "book an MRI scan, refer to rheumatology" — which shows several options at his disposal; this seems much more permanent than the wharf, a temporary place where the nurse treats "a poor artilleryman stretched upon a wooden pallet" with only limited equipment, B) In Source A the doctor is in a hospital because it says "book an MRI scan… arrange an ECG test" which shows there is a lot of things; this is different to Source B because she is on a wharf where there is "a poor artilleryman stretched upon a wooden pallet" which shows there isn't very much there, C) The doctor is in a safe dedicated hospital in peacetime — the variety to "book an MRI scan, refer to rheumatology, arrange an ECG test" emphasising the professional expertise supporting him — whereas the nurse copes in a potentially dangerous warzone, her "wooden pallet" reflecting care given without even basic human needs like shelter, D) In Source A the doctor is part of a busy hospital — "book an MRI scan… arrange an ECG test" — showing lots of options for helping people; however the wharf in Source B is very different as there are not so many options, suggesting a more basic place
   * **Correct:** B, D, A, C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. These are AQA's own indicative-standard summaries, one Level apart. The weakest retells detail as "a lot of things" / "isn't very much there" — paraphrase, Level 1. The next attempts a difference ("not so many options… a more basic place") — Level 2. The third makes clear inferences about permanence and equipment — Level 3. The strongest synthesises perceptively, reading peacetime safety against warzone desperation and the "wooden pallet" for the unmet human needs it implies — Level 4.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs paraphrase (Level 1) → an attempted difference (Level 2) → clear inferences about permanence (Level 3) → perceptive synthesis of safety against warzone (Level 4). Each rung is one AQA band higher; the top band INFERS implied meaning rather than retelling detail.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** For Paper 2 Question 3 (AO2 language, 12 marks), a student writes: "The writer uses a metaphor 'sailing the ship alone'. This is a metaphor. It shows his job is hard." What is the AQA Level, and the single move that would lift it?
   * **Options:** A) Level 4 — it analyses the effects of the writer's choices of language, B) Level 1 — only simple comment on effect; the lift is to EXPLAIN how the image makes meaning, C) Level 3 — it explains clearly the effect of the writer's choice, D) Level 2 — it makes accurate, sophisticated use of subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and asserting "his job is hard" is AQA Level 1 AO2: "simple comment on the effect of language." The lift to Level 2–3 comes from EXPLAINING how the image works — a ship's lone captain carrying total responsibility — not from naming the device again.
   * **Why A:** Level 4 "analyses the effects… selects a range of judicious textual detail" — there is no analysis here, only a label and an assertion.
   * **Why C:** Level 3 "explains clearly the effects" — this states the effect without explaining how the language produces it.
   * **Why D:** Repeating "this is a metaphor" is not the "sophisticated and accurate use of subject terminology" the higher bands reward.

6. **Type: Fill [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 4 AO2 descriptor for Question 3: a top response "analyses the [BLANK] of the writer's choices of language."
   * **Answer:** effects
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 4 AO2 = "analyses the effects of the writer's choices of language." The assessed object is the EFFECT on the reader, not the name of the device. Feature-spotting names techniques; analysis works out what they do.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "effects" — Question 3 rewards analysis of what the language does to the reader, not technique-labelling.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Two Question 3 responses both analyse the night-shift extract. One quotes eight lines; the other selects the single phrase "enormous, and on fire" to pin the danger of the extended metaphor. Which AQA phrase rewards the second, and why?
   * **Options:** A) It selects a range of judicious textual detail, because well-judged selection does more analytical work than volume, B) It selects a wider range of textual detail, because more quotation always widens the evidence base, C) It makes sophisticated use of subject terminology, because naming more devices raises the Level, D) It explains the effect at greater length, because longer responses reach the higher bands
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 4 AO2 = "selects a range of judicious textual detail." "Judicious" means well-judged: "enormous, and on fire" is chosen because it carries the danger of the whole metaphor, not because it is long. Precise selection beats quantity.
   * **Why B:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not breadth — more quotation can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Naming more devices is feature-spotting; the band rewards terminology used accurately in service of analysis.
   * **Why D:** Length is not an AQA Level descriptor.

8. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** These four comments answer Paper 2 Question 3 on how the writer uses language to describe night shifts. Rank them WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO2 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) The writer uses a metaphor to compare the difficulty of working the night shift to "sailing the ship alone"; the use of "ship" suggests he has a big job to do and there is nobody there to help him, B) The writer develops an extended metaphor comparing the night shift to a nightmarish voyage; "sailing the ship alone" juxtaposes the huge scale of a "ship" — his overwhelming responsibility — with the isolation of being "alone", and "enormous, and on fire" makes the danger feel inescapable, C) The writer describes working night shifts when he says "sailing the ship alone"; this is a metaphor he uses to compare working the night shift to sailing a ship by himself, D) The writer uses a metaphor to emphasise the scale of the task; by comparing his role to "sailing the ship alone" it suggests a huge responsibility for all his patients, just as a ship's captain is responsible for the safety of their passengers
   * **Correct:** C, A, D, B
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA's own indicative-standard comments, one Level apart. The weakest only re-describes the metaphor in different words — simple comment, Level 1. The next adds one simple effect ("a big job… nobody to help") — Level 2. The third explains the effect clearly through a developed comparison to a captain's responsibility — Level 3. The strongest analyses an extended metaphor and explores layered effects of scale, isolation and danger — Level 4.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs re-description (Level 1) → one simple stated effect (Level 2) → a clear explained effect (Level 3) → analysis of an extended metaphor's layered effects (Level 4). The top band ANALYSES effects; it does not just name the device.

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**
   * **Question:** For Paper 2 Question 4 (AO3, 16 marks), a student writes two strong but separate paragraphs — one on the doctor's feelings, one on the nurse's — and never sets them against each other. What is the AQA ceiling, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 4 — two developed paragraphs show a perceptive understanding of both texts, B) It stays below Level 3, because AO3 credits comparison of perspectives, not two texts analysed apart, C) Level 3 — clear understanding of each text separately meets the clear band, D) Level 2 — covering both texts at all places the answer in the middle band
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA AO3 = "Compare writers' ideas and perspectives." Two strong but separate paragraphs are parallel analysis, not comparison — the response must set one perspective AGAINST the other ("whereas", "by contrast"). Without that comparative link the work on methods cannot be credited as Level 3–4 comparison.
   * **Why A:** Level 4 needs "perceptive… comparison"; depth on each text in isolation is not comparison.
   * **Why C:** Level 3 is "clear and relevant comparison"; isolated understanding of each text has not yet been compared.
   * **Why D:** AQA places responses by the QUALITY of comparison, not by whether both texts are mentioned.

10. **Type: Fill [Tests AO3]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA AO3 wording for Question 4: "Compare writers' ideas and [BLANK], as well as how these are conveyed."
   * **Answer:** perspectives
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA AO3 = "Compare writers' ideas and perspectives, as well as how these are conveyed, across two or more texts." Question 4 has two halves: compare WHAT the writers think (ideas/perspectives) AND HOW they convey it (methods). Dropping either half caps the mark.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "perspectives" — Question 4 compares the writers' viewpoints, not only their language features.

   * **Question:** Select ALL statements that describe an AQA Level 4 ("perceptive, detailed") response to Question 4 (and none that do not).
   * **Options:** A) It analyses how the writers' methods are used, B) It shows a perceptive understanding of the different ideas and perspectives in both texts, C) It selects a range of judicious supporting detail from both texts, D) It names a technique in each text and states that they are different
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 4 AO3 = "analyses how writers' methods are used… selects a range of judicious supporting detail from both texts… detailed and perceptive understanding of the different ideas and perspectives." Analysis of method, judicious detail and perceptive grasp of perspective are all top-band. Merely naming a technique in each and asserting difference is Level 1 "simple cross reference."
   * **Why D:** Naming a method in each text and stating they differ is "simple identification" — the bottom band, not perceptive comparison.

12. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO3]**
   * **Question:** These four responses answer Paper 2 Question 4, comparing how the writers convey their feelings about medical care. Rank them WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO3 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) The doctor had a tough time training — "Sure it was hard work… but I was a doctor now" — showing some difficult experiences; however the nurse is glad to have helped, saying "brotherly love shown by the strong to the weak", which shows she was impressed by how caring they were, B) The doctor finds the hospital hard — "Sure it was hard work… but I was a doctor now" — which shows it wasn't easy; however the nurse is more positive because she says "the sympathy and brotherly love shown by the strong to the weak", C) The doctor's perspective constantly shifts — the list "inhumane… scarred my retinas" is undercut by the casual "Sure…" and the proud "but I was a doctor now", framing suffering as a badge of professional prestige — whereas the nurse treats medicine as a near-spiritual vocation, "brotherly" implying a self-sacrificial devotion to the weak, D) The doctor has mixed feelings — the list "hard work… inhumane… scarred my retinas" is put in perspective by "but I was a doctor now", showing he accepts it as part of the job — though the nurse focuses on the emotional side, "brotherly" suggesting the staff are as committed as family
   * **Correct:** B, A, D, C
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA's own indicative-standard comparisons, one Level apart. The third explains clearly how each writer's method conveys a different perspective — Level 3. The strongest analyses the doctor's shifting, self-regarding tone against the nurse's selfless vocation, reading "brotherly" perceptively — Level 4.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs simple cross-reference (Level 1) → an attempted comparison (Level 2) → a clear explained comparison of perspectives (Level 3) → perceptive analysis of method and contrasting perspective (Level 4). The top band ANALYSES how methods convey perspective, not just that the two differ.

13. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO5]**
   * **Question:** For Paper 2 Question 5 (AO5, 24 marks for content and organisation), which description matches the Level 4 ("compelling, convincing") band?
   * **Options:** A) Tone, style and register are assuredly matched to purpose and audience, with extensive, ambitious vocabulary and sustained crafting of linguistic devices, B) The vocabulary is as ambitious as possible throughout, even where the words do not fit the audience or purpose, C) The article runs as long as it can, packing in ideas to demonstrate stamina and range, D) Every paragraph names the persuasive device it is using so the examiner can tick each one off
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO5
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 4 AO5 = "Tone, style and register are assuredly matched to purpose and audience… extensive and ambitious vocabulary with sustained crafting of linguistic devices." Top-band writing is controlled and matched to the reader — crafting, not quantity or device-spotting.
   * **Why B:** Ambition that ignores audience breaks the "matched to purpose and audience" requirement and drops the band.
   * **Why C:** Length is not an AO5 descriptor; "compelling" and "convincing" describe control, not word-count.
   * **Why D:** Labelling devices is a reading-paper habit; AO5 rewards crafting devices for effect, not naming them.

   * **Answer:** cohesion

   * **Correct:** A

20. **Type: MCQ [Tests Vocabulary]**
   * **Question:** AQA rewards "synthesis" at Question 2 and "judicious" references throughout. A response that synthesises judiciously is one that...
   * **Options:** A) combines well-chosen detail from BOTH sources into a single comparative point, B) writes one full, self-contained paragraph on each source in turn, C) quotes as much as it can from both sources to demonstrate full coverage, D) summarises each source accurately, but keeps the two entirely separate
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Synthesis = weaving evidence from both texts together; "judicious" = well-judged selection. A synthesising, judicious response fuses a well-chosen detail from each source into ONE point of difference — exactly what Question 2 (AO1) and the comparison at Question 4 (AO3) reward.
   * **Why B:** A paragraph on each in turn is parallel summary, not synthesis.
   * **Why C:** Volume of quotation is the opposite of "judicious" selection.
   * **Why D:** Accurate but separate summaries miss the synthesis — the combining across texts.
