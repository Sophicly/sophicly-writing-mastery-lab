# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — AQA English Language Paper 2 (8700/2)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing against the EXACT AQA mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to
USE the board's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side
and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across AO1–AO6).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking.

AQA **English Language** is marked on a FOUR-level scale — NOT the six-level Literature scale. Exact AQA
8700/2 descriptors from the 2026 SAMPLE mark scheme (first exam June 2026 — `research/sources/aqa-8700-2-sms-2026.txt`):
Level 4 Perceptive/detailed · Level 3 Clear/relevant · Level 2 Some/attempts · Level 1 Simple/limited. Paper 2
questions: Q1 AO1 (4, choose four true statements) · Q2 AO1 synthesis (8) · Q3 AO2 language (12) · Q4 AO3 comparison
(16) · Q5 writing — AO5 content + organisation (24) + AO6 technical accuracy (16). There is no AO4 on Paper 2.
Ranking questions use AQA's OWN indicative-standard L1→L4 answers as the rungs (one Level apart): the 2026 sample
scheme (items 22, 25) and the June 2024 scheme (items 4, 8, 12 — `research/sources/aqa-8700-2-jun24-ms.txt`). Every
quotation in a Feedback or Why line of the AQA section is verbatim (gate: `bin/msa-quote-gate.js`). v7.20.815 (FIXLIST
#866, #815d step 2 — Neil: "40 per bank"): the AQA section holds 40 items.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8700/2 — Paper 2)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** For Paper 2 Question 2 (AO1 synthesis, 8 marks), a student writes: "In the first source the doctor works in a hospital and in the second the nurse works on a wharf." On the AQA four-level scale, what is the CEILING for this response, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 4 — a perceptive, detailed synthesis drawing judicious references from both texts, B) Level 3 — it makes clear inferences and selects clear detail from both texts, C) Level 1 — it paraphrases the two places rather than inferring meaning from them, D) Level 2 — it attempts some inference and shows some difference between the texts
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 1 AO1 = "simple, limited summary… offers paraphrase rather than inference." Naming where each person works, with no inferred meaning, is paraphrase — the bottom band. To climb, the student must INFER (e.g. the hospital's permanence against the wharf's makeshift desperation), which is Level 2 and above.
   * **Why A:** Level 4 needs "perceptive or detailed synthesis and interpretation of both texts" and "judicious references" — there is no inference and no selected detail here.
   * **Why B:** Level 3 needs "clear inferences from both texts" — this retells the two places rather than inferring from them.
   * **Why D:** Level 2 needs an attempted inference; bare paraphrase of place sits below it at Level 1.

2. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA wording that Question 2 rewards above paraphrase. A top response "makes perceptive [BLANK] from both texts" — the act of drawing out implied meaning.
   * **Answer:** inferences
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 4 AO1 = "makes perceptive inferences from both texts." Inference — reading the implied meaning behind the detail — is what lifts a summary out of Level 1 paraphrase. Each band re-uses the word: simple → clear → perceptive inferences.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "inferences" — Question 2 rewards inferred meaning, not retold detail.

3. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**

   * **Answer:** synthesise
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA AO1 = "Select and synthesise evidence from different texts." Synthesis means weaving detail from BOTH sources into a combined point of difference (or of similarity, when the question asks for one) — not handling each text in a separate paragraph. It is the skill Question 2 is built to test.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "synthesise" — Question 2 rewards combining evidence across the two texts, not summarising them one at a time.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** These four summaries answer Paper 2 Question 2 on the DIFFERENT PLACES the doctor (Source A) and the nurse (Source B) work. Rank them WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) In Source A the doctor works in a hospital that has a range of modern facilities — "book an MRI scan, refer to rheumatology" — which shows several options at his disposal; this seems much more permanent than the wharf, a temporary place where the nurse treats "a poor artilleryman stretched upon a wooden pallet" with only limited equipment, B) In Source A the doctor is in a hospital because it says "book an MRI scan… arrange an ECG test" which shows there is a lot of things; this is different to Source B because she is on a wharf where there is "a poor artilleryman stretched upon a wooden pallet" which shows there isn't very much there, C) The doctor is in a safe dedicated hospital in peacetime — the variety to "book an MRI scan, refer to rheumatology, arrange an ECG test" emphasising the professional expertise supporting him — whereas the nurse copes in a potentially dangerous warzone, her "wooden pallet" reflecting care given without even basic human needs like shelter, D) In Source A the doctor is part of a busy hospital — "book an MRI scan… arrange an ECG test" — showing lots of options for helping people; however the wharf in Source B is very different as there are not so many options, suggesting a more basic place
   * **Correct:** B, D, A, C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. These are AQA's own indicative-standard summaries (June 2024), one Level apart. The weakest retells detail as "a lot of things" / "isn't very much there" — paraphrase, Level 1. The next attempts a difference ("not so many options… a more basic place") — Level 2. The third makes clear inferences about permanence and equipment — Level 3. The strongest synthesises perceptively, reading peacetime safety against warzone desperation and the "wooden pallet" for the unmet human needs it implies — Level 4.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs paraphrase (Level 1) → an attempted difference (Level 2) → clear inferences about permanence (Level 3) → perceptive synthesis of safety against warzone (Level 4). Each rung is one AQA band higher; the top band INFERS implied meaning rather than retelling detail.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** For Paper 2 Question 3 (AO2 language, 12 marks), a student writes: "The writer uses a metaphor 'sailing the ship alone'. This is a metaphor. It shows his job is hard." What is the AQA Level, and the single move that would lift it?
   * **Options:** A) Level 4 — it analyses the effects of the writer's choices of language, B) Level 1 — a simple comment on effect; the lift is to explain the image, C) Level 3 — it explains clearly the effects of the writer's choices of language, D) Level 2 — it makes accurate, sophisticated use of subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and asserting "his job is hard" is AQA Level 1 AO2: "offers simple comment on the effect of language." The lift to Level 2–3 comes from EXPLAINING how the image works — a ship's lone captain carrying total responsibility — not from naming the device again.
   * **Why A:** Level 4 "analyses the effects… selects a range of judicious textual detail" — there is no analysis here, only a label and an assertion.
   * **Why C:** Level 3 "explains clearly the effects" — this states the effect without explaining how the language produces it.
   * **Why D:** Repeating "this is a metaphor" is not the "sophisticated and accurate use of subject terminology" the higher bands reward.

6. **Type: Fill [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 4 AO2 descriptor for Question 3: a top response "analyses the [BLANK] of the writer's choices of language."
   * **Answer:** effects
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 4 AO2 = "analyses the effects of the writer's choices of language." The assessed object is the EFFECT on the reader, not the name of the device. Feature-spotting names techniques; analysis works out what they do.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "effects" — Question 3 rewards analysis of what the language does to the reader, not technique-labelling.

   * **Options:** A) It selects a range of judicious textual detail: one well-chosen phrase does the analytical work, B) It selects a wider range of textual detail, because more quotation always widens the evidence base, C) It makes sophisticated use of subject terminology, because naming more devices raises the Level, D) It explains the effect at greater length, because longer responses reach the higher bands

   * **AO:** AO2

   * **Why B:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not breadth — more quotation can dilute precision.

9. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**
   * **Question:** For Paper 2 Question 4 (AO3, 16 marks), a student writes two strong but separate paragraphs — one on the doctor's feelings, one on the nurse's — and never sets them against each other. What is the AQA ceiling, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 4 — two developed paragraphs show a perceptive understanding of both texts, B) Below Level 3 — AO3 credits comparing perspectives, not two separate analyses, C) Level 3 — clear understanding of each text separately meets the clear band, D) Level 2 — covering both texts at all places the answer in the middle band
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA AO3 = "Compare writers' ideas and perspectives, as well as how these are conveyed, across two or more texts." Two strong but separate paragraphs are parallel analysis, not comparison: the answer must set one perspective against the other. AQA's own 2026 Level 4 answer turns on a phrase like "In complete contrast to". Without that link, the work on methods cannot be credited as Level 3 or 4 comparison.
   * **Why A:** Level 4 "Compares ideas and perspectives in a perceptive way"; depth on each text alone is not comparison.
   * **Why C:** Level 3 "Compares ideas and perspectives in a clear and relevant way"; each text understood on its own has not yet been compared.
   * **Why D:** AQA places responses by the QUALITY of comparison, not by whether both texts are mentioned.

### **SECTION B: OCR (J351 — Paper 2)**

OCR marks Paper 2 on a SIX-level scale (finer gradation than the four/five-level boards). Synthesis (Q2, AO1, 10) and comparison (Q5, AO3, 20) each run L1 "Limited/Basic" → L6 "Sophisticated/Perceptive". Q4 (AO2, 15) explains how writers use language and structure; Q6 (AO4, 15) evaluates the effectiveness of the argument; writing (AO5 20 + AO6 10) is transactional in a specified form. Ranking questions use OCR's own six-level indicative standards as rungs, one level apart.

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

### **SECTION E: Eduqas (C700U20 — Component 2)**

Eduqas marks Component 2 on a FIVE-band scale. A1/A2 (AO1) retrieve and synthesise an overview of both texts; A3/A4 (AO2, 10 each) analyse persuasive techniques B1 "Limited" → B5 "Perceptive"; A5/A6 split the comparison (AO3, 10 each) into ideas and methods B1 "Simple" → B5 "Perceptive, analytical"; TWO transactional writing tasks (AO5 20 + AO6 10 each) run B1 "Limited" → B5 "Sophisticated". Ranking questions use Eduqas's own five-band indicative standards as rungs, one band apart.

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** For an AO2 analysis question (A3/A4, 10 marks, five-band), a student writes only: "The writer uses a list of three." What is the Eduqas ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Band 1 — limited, minimal identification of the technique, B) Band 3 — a clear explanation of effects, C) Band 2 — some understanding of the technique, D) Band 4 — detailed analysis of influence
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Eduqas AO2 Band 1 = "Limited" — minimal identification. Naming a list of three with nothing further is minimal identification, the bottom band.
   * **Why B:** Band 3 "Clear" needs a clear explanation of the effect — none is offered.
   * **Why C:** Band 2 "Some" needs some understanding of the technique; a bare name does not reach it.
   * **Why D:** Band 4 "Detailed" analyses influence in detail — far above naming a device.
