# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Anita and Me

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Anita and Me against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA Modern Text = AO1+AO2+AO3 (the level grid);
Edexcel GCSE Post-1914 = AO1+AO3 (no AO2) plus a separate AO4 (SPaG) grid; Eduqas Post-1914 = AO1+AO2 plus AO4 (SPaG, 5 marks);
OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3; Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4, where AO4 is PERSONAL RESPONSE, not SPaG.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Meena tells lies to her parents, becomes friends with Anita, and eventually grows out of the friendship." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some clear, explained comment but stays at character and plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is a simple, undeveloped comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing what Meena does across the story is character-and-plot narration — AQA's "clear understanding" register. To climb, the student must treat Meena as Syal's vehicle for a CONCEPT (identity forged between two cultures), not narrate her year in Tollington.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates events, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this offers neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character rather than a bare simple comment, so it sits above the lowest band.

   * **Answer:** conceptualised

   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 strand is "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as belonging as something Meena must construct rather than inherit, not around the sequence of events.
   * **WhyWrong:** The missing AQA word is "conceptualised" — treating the novel as the vehicle for a concept is what lifts AO1 into Level 6.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student says: "AO1 just means proving you understand the novel." Which AQA Level does "clear understanding" actually describe, and what does Level 6 additionally require?
   * **Options:** A) It describes Level 6 — understanding the novel is already the top-band conceptualised response, B) It describes Level 3 to 4 (some, then clear, explained understanding); Level 6 additionally requires a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response", C) It describes Level 1 — understanding is the bare simple comment of the lowest band, D) It describes technical accuracy — understanding the novel is really a matter of accurate spelling and punctuation
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Clear understanding" is AQA Level 4 language, and "some understanding" is Level 3. It is the commonest way students undersell the top band to themselves. Level 6 AO1 is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response... judicious use of precise references."
   * **Why A:** The top band is far more than understanding — it is a conceptualised, critical argument.
   * **Why C:** Explained understanding sits well above a simple comment.
   * **Why D:** Technical accuracy is the separate AO4 strand, not AO1.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four openings on Anita and Me from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Syal constructs Meena's belonging as something she must actively forge rather than inherit, using the novel to interrogate how a child of immigrants authors a self between two cultures.", B) "Meena is a girl who lies to her parents, follows Anita around, and grows up by the end of the book.", C) "Belonging is an important thing in the novel, and it makes Meena feel confused and left out.", D) "Syal presents belonging as difficult for Meena, using her lies and her friendship with Anita to make the reader feel her divided loyalties."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is the "simple comment" of the lowest band. The bare value-judgement about an important thing that causes confusion, with no method and no evidence, is a "supported comment". The clear, explained rung that names methods (the lies, the friendship) and their effect on the reader is "clear understanding". The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, to comment, to explained method-and-effect, to concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both cite Meena's wish to have fair skin and belong among the white villagers. One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about how racism teaches Meena to reject her own reflection. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the reference with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the reference using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the reference is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Syal uses an image of Meena as an outsider in the village. This is a technique. It tells us she feels different." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the image and adding "it tells us she feels different" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs a clear explanation of the effect; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used judiciously.
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.

10. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**

    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines to widen the range of its supporting evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to analysis with exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, C) It deploys rarer, more technical terminology across the response, D) It develops the point across longer, more sustained paragraphs

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**

    * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about the novel's background, B) It is bolt-on context — AQA Level 6 AO3 needs "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" where context drives the interpretation, C) It is too brief — a contextual point must run to several sentences to score, D) Context is credited only on the Language paper, not on this question
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." A free-standing fact is bolt-on. Context must DRIVE the concept: the post-war wave of South Asian migration to industrial towns like the fictional Tollington makes Meena's search for belonging a lived historical pressure, which drives Syal's choices.
    * **Why A:** The fact is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** Context is assessed throughout the AQA modern-text question.
