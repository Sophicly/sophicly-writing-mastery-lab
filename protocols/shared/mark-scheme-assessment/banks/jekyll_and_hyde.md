# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Jekyll and Hyde

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about *The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde* against the EXACT mark-scheme
descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified
paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI
is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA 8702 novel = AO1+AO2+AO3 (NO AO4/SPaG on the novel);
Edexcel GCSE 1ET0 = AO2 on the extract + AO1 on the whole text (context is "invisible ink", 0 explicit marks);
Edexcel IGCSE 4ET1 = AO1+AO2+AO4(CONTEXT, not SPaG); Eduqas C720U = AO1+AO2+AO3 equally weighted;
OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4(SPaG), marked holistically; Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = knowledge / critical understanding
(individuality and insight) / writer's effects / personal-and-evaluative response; SQA = Understanding / Analysis / Evaluation.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Stevenson presents Jekyll as a respectable doctor who drinks a potion, becomes Hyde, murders Carew and is destroyed." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, conceptualised argument organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character/plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing what Jekyll does across the plot is character-level comment — AQA Level 3 "some explained response". To climb, the student must treat Jekyll and Hyde as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (the divided self, repression breeding its own violence), not narrate the plot.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates events, it does not argue an idea about duality.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare undeveloped remark — it sits above the lowest band.

   * **WhyWrong:** The missing AQA word is "conceptualised" — treating the novel as the vehicle for a concept is what lifts AO1 into Level 6.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student says: "AO1 just means showing you understand the novel." Which AQA Level does "showing understanding" actually describe, and what does Level 6 additionally require?
   * **Options:** A) It describes Level 6 — understanding the novel is already the top-band conceptualised response, B) It describes Level 3–4 (some/clear explained understanding); Level 6 additionally requires a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response", C) It describes Level 1 — understanding is the bare simple comment of the lowest band, D) It describes AO2 — understanding the novel is really a matter of analysing methods
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Showing understanding" is AQA Level 3–4 language ("some explained response" / "clear understanding"). It is the commonest way students undersell the top band to themselves. Level 6 AO1 is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response... judicious use of precise references."
   * **Why A:** The top band is far more than understanding — it is a conceptualised, critical argument about the text.
   * **Why C:** Explained understanding sits well above a simple, explicit comment.
   * **Why D:** Analysis of methods is the AO2 strand, not AO1.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four openings on the divided self from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Stevenson constructs Hyde as the atavistic residue of a civilisation that represses its instincts, using the divided self to interrogate the moral cost of Victorian respectability.", B) "Dr Jekyll drinks a potion, turns into Mr Hyde, murders Carew and then dies.", C) "Duality is an important idea in the novel, and it causes a lot of harm and death.", D) "Stevenson presents the divided self through Hyde's separation from Jekyll, making the reader feel how repression breeds a hidden violence."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about an important idea causing harm, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the separation of the two selves) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "trampled calmly over the child's body." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about Hyde's inhuman detachment from conscience. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretations" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretations." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Stevenson uses the simile 'ape-like fury'. This is a technique. It tells us Hyde is violent." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the simile and adding "it tells us he is violent" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "explained/relevant comments on the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 6 wants "analysis of the writer's methods... exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings" with terminology used judiciously.
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

9. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**

   * **Options:** A) "Stevenson's 'troglodytic' Hyde lets him explore how the repressed self regresses beneath civilisation, so the description becomes an analysis of a man degenerating into an earlier evolutionary form.", B) "Stevenson uses description of Hyde's appearance.", C) "Stevenson's word 'troglodytic' examines how Hyde seems to belong to a more primitive, cave-dwelling stage of humanity.", D) "Stevenson uses the word 'troglodytic' to make Hyde seem strange and unpleasant."

### **SECTION B: EDEXCEL GCSE (1ET0)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** In Edexcel's whole-text part (Part (b), AO1), Level 5 (17–20) rewards "discerning references... an integral part of the response." What does "discerning" mean in practice?
   * **Options:** A) Using quotations only from the major characters, B) Choosing references with excellent judgement so each precisely supports the argument, C) Selecting the largest possible number of examples, D) Quoting from the most difficult passages of the novel
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Discerning" means excellent judgement in selection — each reference precisely fitting the argument and made "with assurance". Quality of choice, not major characters, quantity or difficulty.
   * **Why A:** A minor figure such as Enfield or Poole can supply the sharpest evidence; discernment is fit to the argument, not prominence.
   * **Why C:** Stacking examples demonstrates coverage rather than judgement; the top band rewards discerning selection.
   * **Why D:** Difficult passages look impressive, but the reference must be well-judged and precisely support the point.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** In Edexcel's whole-text part, what makes a response "assured" (Level 5) rather than "thorough" (Level 4)?
   * **Options:** A) Writing with confidence using phrases like "clearly" and "obviously", B) Including more personal opinions about the characters, C) Authoritative command of interpretation — definitive conceptual argument held with a high level of engagement, D) Disagreeing with traditional readings of the novel
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Assured" signals intellectual authority — "an assured personal response, showing a high level of engagement with the text." Level 4 "thorough" is clear and detailed but can be tentative; Level 5 makes definitive conceptual claims with analytical authority.
   * **Why A:** Confidence words only assert assurance; without an authoritative interpretation behind them they read as empty emphasis.
   * **Why B:** A volume of opinion is not authority; assurance is command of a well-supported reading.
   * **Why D:** Contrarianism is not assurance; authority of judgement is, whichever reading is taken.
