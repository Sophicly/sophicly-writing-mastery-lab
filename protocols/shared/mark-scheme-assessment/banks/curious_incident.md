# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Mark Haddon's novel and Simon Stephens's stage adaptation against the
EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than
a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach the
client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the questions across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA Modern Text = AO1+AO2+AO3 (no AO4 on this question);
Edexcel GCSE Post-1914 = AO1+AO3 only (AO2 is NOT assessed) with AO4 (SPaG) on a separate grid;
Eduqas Post-1914 = AO1+AO2+AO4 (5-mark SPaG, no AO3 in this section); OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3 (part (a) AO1/AO3 equally dominant);
Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4 (AO4 = personal response, NOT SPaG).

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Christopher finds Wellington dead, decides to investigate, discovers his father's lie, and travels alone to London to find his mother." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, conceptualised argument organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some clear response but stays at plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Retelling Christopher's journey is plot narration — around AQA Level 3 "clear understanding". To climb, the student must treat the play as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (e.g. a literal mind exposing the deceptions of the adults around it), not narrate the murder-mystery.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates events, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is orderly plot narration, not a bare simple comment — it sits above the lowest band.
