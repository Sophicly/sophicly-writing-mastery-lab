# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Jane Eyre

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Jane Eyre against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ for the 19th-century NOVEL: AQA 8702 = AO1+AO2+AO3 (no AO4 on the novel);
Edexcel GCSE 1ET0 = Part (a) AO2 + Part (b) AO1, context is "invisible ink" (0 explicit AO3 marks); Edexcel IGCSE 4ET1 = AO1+AO2+AO4 (AO4 = CONTEXT, not SPaG);
Eduqas C720U = AO1+AO2+AO3 equally weighted; OCR J352 = AO1+AO2 dominant with AO3 (context) and AO4 (technical accuracy); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = knowledge/critical understanding/effects/personal response; SQA = Understanding/Analysis/Evaluation.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Brontë presents Jane as a poor orphan who becomes a governess and then marries Rochester." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, conceptualised argument organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character/plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing what Jane does across the plot is character-level comment — AQA Level 3 "some explained response". To climb, the student must treat Jane as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (e.g. an individual conscience refusing to surrender moral integrity to either passion or social constraint), not narrate her life.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates a life, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare "simple comment" — it sits above the lowest band.

It is the commonest way students undersell the top band to themselves. Level 6 AO1 is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response... judicious use of precise references."
   * **Why A:** The top band is far more than understanding — it is a conceptualised, critical argument.
   * **Why C:** Explained understanding sits well above a "simple comment".

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four Jane Eyre openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Brontë constructs Jane's integrity as a moral will that refuses to be owned, using the novel to interrogate how a woman without rank preserves an inviolable self.", B) "Jane is an orphan who becomes a governess and marries Mr Rochester at the end.", C) "Independence is a good thing in the novel, and it helps Jane in the end.", D) "Brontë presents Jane's independence as unbreakable through her refusal of Rochester, making the reader admire her self-command."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about independence being good, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the refusal) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear explained response). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "I am no bird; and no net ensnares me." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about Jane's refusal to be possessed. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Brontë uses the metaphor of the caged bird. This is a technique. It tells us Jane wants freedom." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the caged-bird metaphor and adding "it tells us she wants freedom" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

9. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Rank these AO2 comments on the red-room from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Brontë's red-room, at once nursery, prison and deathbed, lets her analyse a childhood in which security and menace have become indistinguishable, so the room reads as an emblem of a self already under siege.", B) "Brontë uses imagery in the red-room.", C) "Brontë's blood-tinted red-room examines how Jane's terror begins to manufacture the ghost she dreads.", D) "Brontë uses the red-room to make Jane seem frightened and alone."

   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA AO2 band higher. The bare identification of a method, with no effect, is Level 3. The analysis with exploration of layered effects, tied to a concept, is Level 6. The ladder is identify, explain one effect, examine how it works, then analyse-and-explore.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from identifying imagery, to explaining one effect, to examining how the method works, to analysing and exploring layered effects. Level 6 explores several effects, not a single stated one.

10. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

   * **Options:** A) One 40-mark whole-text essay assessing all objectives together, B) Part (a) assesses AO2 only on the printed extract; Part (b) assesses AO1 only on the whole text, C) Part (a) assesses AO1 and Part (b) assesses AO3, D) A single multiple-choice comprehension section

   * **Why C:** Part (a) is analysis of methods (AO2), not argument; context (AO3) carries no explicit marks on this question at all.
   * **Why D:** No Literature board assesses the novel through multiple choice.
