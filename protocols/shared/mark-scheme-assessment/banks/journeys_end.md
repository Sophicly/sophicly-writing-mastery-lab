# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Journey's End

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Journey's End against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA Modern Texts = AO1+AO2+AO3;
Edexcel GCSE Post-1914 = AO1+AO3 (AO2 not assessed) + AO4 (SPaG); Eduqas Post-1914 = AO1+AO2 + AO4
(5 SPaG marks, AO3 not assessed in this section); OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3 in part (a) comparison and AO1+AO2
holistic in part (b); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = AO1 knowledge, AO2 understanding, AO3 analysis of effects,
AO4 personal response (no SPaG, best-fit holistic).

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Sherriff presents Stanhope as a brave commander who drinks heavily to cope and grows harsh with his men." For AO1, which AQA Modern Text Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a convincing, critical analysis and exploration organised around a concept, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed consideration with references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 4 — a clear understanding that stays at character level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA's Level 4 heading is "Clear understanding". Accurately tracking what Stanhope does across the play is clear character-level understanding, not the "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" the top band demands. To climb, the student must treat Stanhope as a vehicle for a CONCEPT — endurance under command corroding the self — not narrate his behaviour.
   * **Why A:** The convincing, conceptualised argument the top band demands is absent — this describes a man, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed" consideration needs development and integrated references, and this offers neither.
   * **Why D:** It is clear explained understanding of the character, not a bare simple comment — it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four Journey's End openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Sherriff constructs Stanhope as the point where duty and dread collide, using the dugout to interrogate the price endurance exacts from the men who never allow themselves to break.", B) "Stanhope commands a company, drinks whisky, and is killed when the German barrage brings the dugout down.", C) "War is a bad thing in the play, and it leads to a lot of waste and sadness.", D) "Sherriff presents the strain of command through Stanhope's whisky, making the audience feel how leadership erodes a man."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is the lowest band. The clear rung that names a method (the whisky) and its effect on the audience is "clear understanding". The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, to comment, to explained method-and-effect, to concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus audience effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining behaviour to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

The other selects it to pin an argument that fear masters even the bravest officer. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references" to support the interpretation rather than general reference, C) It explains the effect of the quotation on the audience clearly, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references". "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept about fear outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Sherriff uses a stage direction about the candle-flame. This is a technique. It tells us the dugout is gloomy." For AO2, which AQA level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis of methods with exploration of their effects, B) Level 5 — examination of the writer's methods, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the stage direction and adding "it tells us the dugout is gloomy" is identification of the method with a general comment only. Level 4 needs clear explanation of the effect; Level 5 needs "examination of writer's methods"; Level 6 is "exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings", a "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
