# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Julius Caesar

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Julius Caesar against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA/Edexcel GCSE/OCR = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4(SPaG);
Edexcel IGCSE 4ET1 = AO1+AO2+AO4(CONTEXT, not SPaG); Eduqas Shakespeare = AO1+AO2 only (no AO3); SQA = Understanding/Analysis/Evaluation.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Shakespeare)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Shakespeare presents Brutus as an honourable soldier who joins the conspiracy to kill Caesar and then dies at Philippi." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, conceptualised argument organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character/plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing what Brutus does across the plot is character-level comment — AQA Level 3 "some explained response". To climb, the student must treat Brutus as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (e.g. private honour crippling public judgement), not narrate his career.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates a career, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare "simple comment" — it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four Julius Caesar openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Shakespeare constructs Brutus's honour as the very flaw that betrays the republic he loves, using the play to interrogate whether principle can survive the arena of political violence.", B) "Brutus joins the conspiracy, helps to kill Caesar, loses the war and dies at Philippi.", C) "Ambition is a dangerous thing in the play, and it leads to a lot of death and civil war.", D) "Shakespeare presents honour as destructive through Brutus's decision to kill Caesar, making the audience feel how easily idealism is manipulated."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a dangerous thing causing death, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (Brutus's decision) and its effect on the audience is Level 4 (clear explained response). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "the fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, / But in ourselves." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about Cassius reframing destiny as human agency in order to seduce Brutus into the plot. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Shakespeare uses the metaphor 'lean and hungry look'. This is a technique. It tells us Cassius is dangerous." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us he is dangerous" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

### **SECTION B: EDEXCEL GCSE (1ET0 — Shakespeare)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Edexcel's Level 5 (17-20) rewards "discerning references [that] are an integral part of the response." What does "discerning" mean in practice?
   * **Options:** A) Using quotations only from the major characters, B) Choosing references with excellent judgement so each precisely supports the argument, C) Selecting the largest possible number of examples, D) Quoting from the most complex, difficult speeches
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Discerning" means excellent judgement in selection — each reference precisely fitting the argument, woven in "with assurance and full support from the text". Quality of choice, not major characters, quantity or difficulty.
   * **Why A:** A minor character's line — Casca's, or the soothsayer's warning — can be the sharpest evidence; discernment is fit to the argument, not prominence.
   * **Why C:** Stacking examples demonstrates coverage rather than judgement; Level 5 rewards discerning selection.
   * **Why D:** Difficult speeches look impressive, but the reference must be well-judged and precisely support the point.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

   * **Options:** A) Writing with confidence using phrases like "clearly" and "obviously", B) Including more personal opinions, C) Authoritative command of interpretation — definitive conceptual arguments held with a high level of engagement, D) Disagreeing with traditional interpretations
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Assured" signals intellectual authority — an "assured personal response, showing a high level of engagement with the text." Level 4 "thorough" is clear and detailed but can be tentative; Level 5 makes definitive conceptual claims with analytical authority.
   * **Why A:** Confidence words only assert assurance; without an authoritative interpretation behind them they read as empty emphasis.
   * **Why B:** A volume of opinion is not authority; assurance is command of a well-supported reading.
   * **Why D:** Contrarianism is not assurance; authority of judgement is, whichever reading is taken.
