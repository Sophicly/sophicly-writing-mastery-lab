# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Belonging Poetry (Edexcel)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real comparative writing about the Belonging anthology against the EXACT Edexcel (1ET0)
mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a
simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach the
client. The AI is never the scorekeeper. The skill assessed is mark-scheme literacy, NOT poem trivia —
questions reference the named poems only as anchors, never quiz the student on their content.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the picks across the AO split).
The Edexcel GCSE English Literature (1ET0) Belonging anthology comparison is marked out of 20, split by
section: Introduction = 2 (context-building sentence 1, comparative thesis 1), Body Paragraph 1 Form = 5,
Body Paragraph 2 Structure = 5, Body Paragraph 3 Language = 5, Conclusion = 3. Only AO2 (methods — form,
structure and language choices plus their effects, argued comparatively) and AO3 (context — comparative
and causal, never bolt-on) are separately scored on this component; AO1 does not carry its own criterion
here. Responses are placed on Edexcel's five-level scale (Level 1–5).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking.

**Edexcel-exclusive cluster:** only Edexcel (1ET0) sets the Belonging anthology, so this bank has a SINGLE section by design.

### **SECTION A: Edexcel (1ET0 — Belonging)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**
   * **Question:** A student's introduction opens: "Nichols wrote in the 1980s and Rumens wrote around the same time." For the Introduction's context-building criterion (worth 1 mark), why does this stay low?
   * **Options:** A) It states two isolated dates with no link to how each poem's context shapes its theme or purpose DIFFERENTLY — the criterion needs each poem's context evaluated for its distinct effect, not a shared timeframe noted in passing, B) It is inaccurate about the decade, C) It should have been placed in the conclusion instead, D) Context is not assessed on this criterion at all
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Introduction's AO3 criterion asks the student to evaluate how EACH poem's context shapes its themes or purpose DIFFERENTLY — a bare shared decade names a timeframe but evaluates no difference and drives no thesis. A penalty for context not compared would likely apply here.
   * **Why B:** Rough accuracy of the dates is not the issue — the criterion asks for evaluated difference, not accuracy.
   * **Why C:** The criterion is scored in the introduction; a working context point belongs where it is, not deferred.
   * **Why D:** Context is explicitly worth 1 of the introduction's 2 marks.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** One student's thesis reads: "Both Island Man and The Émigrée explore the idea of home." Another writes: "While Nichols renders home as a sensory pull the body cannot obey, Rumens renders it as a memory sealed off by conflict, so that both poets measure belonging by what displacement costs the mind." For the Introduction's comparative thesis criterion, why does the second earn the mark?
   * **Options:** A) It is longer and uses a denser vocabulary, B) It states a clear, precise COMPARATIVE argument comparing the poets' distinct approaches to the shared idea, rather than naming an idea both poems merely explore, C) It uses the word "belonging", the anthology's name, D) It avoids naming either poet
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The criterion requires a clear, precise COMPARATIVE thesis arguing the poets' approaches against one another — naming a shared topic ("home") is not the same as arguing HOW the two poets' approaches differ.
   * **Why A:** Length is not a criterion; the first sentence could be expanded without becoming comparative.
   * **Why C:** Naming the anthology's theme is not the same as an argued comparative claim.
   * **Why D:** Both poets are in fact named in the strong example; omitting names is not what earns the mark.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A Body Paragraph 1 (Form) opens: "Both poets use interesting techniques to make their poems effective." Which fault does this show, and why?
   * **Options:** A) The technique is missing or vague — naming "interesting techniques" identifies no method and claims no comparative concept about FORM, B) The wrong focus area has been analysed, because this sentence discusses effects rather than form, C) Context is absent, D) This is an acceptable topic sentence
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Body Paragraph 1's topic-sentence criterion (0.5 marks) needs a comparative concept about BOTH poets' FORM choices — "interesting techniques" names nothing, so the technique-missing-or-vague penalty applies.
   * **Why B:** The wrong-focus penalty punishes analysing the WRONG focus area (e.g. language in a Form paragraph); this sentence names no focus area at all, so the fault is vagueness, not misdirection.
   * **Why C:** Context is a separate criterion (0.5 marks) not engaged here; the fault is in the topic sentence, not context.
   * **Why D:** A topic sentence naming no method and no comparative claim cannot meet the 0.5-mark criterion.

    * **Why B:** That penalty concerns quotation evidence, not contextual claims.

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**

    * **Options:** A) It never leaves the FOCUS stage — it fails to name a specific emotion, let alone the thought or judgement that emotion produces, and it is not comparative, B) It uses the word "feel", which is always penalised, C) It should be two full paragraphs long to score any marks, D) Effects are only assessed in Body Paragraph 3, not Body Paragraph 1
    * **Correct:** A
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Effects framework moves from FOCUS (what is noticed) to EMOTIONS (a specific feeling) to THOUGHTS (what that feeling makes the reader judge) to ACTIONS. "Feel something" names no emotion category and climbs no rung — and the criterion also requires TWO sentences showing each poet's technique affecting the reader DIFFERENTLY, which a single vague sentence about one poem cannot do.
    * **Why B:** "Feel" is not itself a banned word; the fault is vagueness, not vocabulary choice.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the criterion; two specific, comparative sentences are required, not a fixed word count.
    * **Why D:** Comparative effects (1.5 marks) are scored in every Body Paragraph — Form, Structure and Language alike.
