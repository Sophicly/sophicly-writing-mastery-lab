# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Power and Conflict Poetry (AQA)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real comparative writing about the Power and Conflict cluster against the EXACT AQA
mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a
simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach the
client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the picks across the AO split).
The AQA anthology comparison is marked out of 30 across six levels: AO1 (argument + comparison of ideas) = 12,
AO2 (language, form, structure + effects, subject terminology) = 12, AO3 (ideas/contexts) = 6. AO4 SPaG does not apply.
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking.

**AQA-exclusive cluster:** only AQA (8702) sets the Power and Conflict anthology, so this bank has a SINGLE section by design.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Power and Conflict)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student compares Ozymandias and London: "Both Shelley and Blake present power as something that damages and decays." For AO1, which AQA level does this comparison sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a critical, exploratory comparison built on a conceptualised approach, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed consideration with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — an explained, structured comment that compares at theme level but names no method or concept, D) Level 1 — simple comments relevant to comparison, narrative in approach
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming a shared theme (power "damages and decays") is an explained, structured comparative comment — AQA Level 3. It compares ideas but names no method and treats neither poem as a construct. To climb, the response must analyse HOW each poet crafts the idea and argue a concept about power.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical comparison the top band demands is absent — this states a shared theme, it does not argue an idea through method.
   * **Why B:** A thoughtful, developed consideration needs apt references integrated into interpretation, and this offers neither quotation nor development.
   * **Why D:** This is an explained comparative comment, well above the narrative, simple comments of the lowest band.

2. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO1 evidence descriptor: top-band comparisons make "judicious use of precise references to support [BLANK]."
   * **Answer:** interpretation
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 strand is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means each reference is chosen to do argumentative work — a precise half-line tied to the concept outweighs many general quotations.
   * **WhyWrong:** The missing AQA word is "interpretation" — precise references earn top marks only when they support an interpretation, never when listed as evidence.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses compare My Last Duchess and Ozymandias. One writes about the first poem in full, then the second in full. The other weaves both poems into every paragraph. For AO1, which AQA habit distinguishes the second?
   * **Options:** A) It quotes a greater number of lines from each poem, B) It sustains integrated, interwoven comparison, weaving both poems through every paragraph, C) It analyses the rhyme scheme of each poem in turn, D) It places one comparative sentence in the conclusion
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Sustained integrated comparison — weaving both poems into every paragraph — is the AQA top-band habit. Block treatment (one poem in full, then the other) caps a response lower because the comparison is not sustained across the argument.
   * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not the distinction; AQA rewards judicious selection, not volume.
   * **Why C:** Handling each rhyme scheme separately is still block treatment, not interwoven comparison.
   * **Why D:** A single comparative sentence at the end is not the sustained, paragraph-by-paragraph comparison the top band requires.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four comparative openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Shelley and Blake both construct power as an illusion its holders cannot sustain, each poem interrogating the vanity of authority so the reader is left to measure its emptiness.", B) "Ozymandias is about a broken statue and London is about an unhappy city.", C) "Both poems present power as a bad thing that causes suffering to people.", D) "Both poets present power as destructive — Shelley through the ruined statue, Blake through the marked faces — making the reader judge its cost."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung climbs roughly one AQA level. Retelling each poem in turn is narrative Level 1. A supported comment on a shared theme, with no method, is Level 2. The clear comparison that names a method in each poem and its effect on the reader is Level 4. The critical, exploratory comparison arguing one conceptualised idea about power is Level 6. The ladder is narrative, to supported theme-comment, to clear method-and-effect, to conceptualised comparison.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from a narrative retelling of each poem, to a supported comment on a shared theme with no method, to a clear comparison naming a method in each poem and its effect, to a conceptualised, exploratory comparison. The top rung treats both poems as constructs, not more plot.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "conceptualised approach to the full task." A conceptualised comparison treats each poem as...
   * **Options:** A) a story to be retold accurately in the correct order, B) a construct or argument exploring the cluster's big idea about power and conflict, C) a source of as many quotations as can be embedded, D) a historical document to be explained through the poet's biography
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means organised around an idea — each poem read as a construct exploring a concept (for instance, power's impermanence), with details as evidence. It is not retelling, quote-stacking or biography.
   * **Why A:** Retelling the poem in order is the narrative, Level 1 approach, not a conceptualised argument.
   * **Why C:** Judicious selection, not quantity, is the top-band evidence habit; stacking quotations does not conceptualise.
   * **Why D:** Biography is bolt-on context; it does not organise the response around a concept.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

A second selects one precise half-line from each to pin an argument about how the poets treat soldiers' suffering. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second?
   * **Options:** A) "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)", B) "clear explanation of the effects of the writer's methods", C) "specific, detailed links between context, text and task", D) "identification of the writer's methods"
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." Judicious means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not appended as evidence. A precise half-line tied to the argument outweighs many general quotations.
   * **Why B:** Explaining the effect of a method is AO2 work, not the AO1 matter of how references are selected.
   * **Why C:** Linking context to text and task is the AO3 strand, not the AO1 evidence descriptor.
   * **Why D:** Identifying the writer's methods is a low AO2 behaviour, not judicious selection of references.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Shelley uses a metaphor. This is a technique. It tells us power does not last." For AO2, which AQA level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis of the writer's methods with terminology used judiciously, B) Level 5 — examination of how the method works, C) Level 2 — identification of the writer's method with only a general comment, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method's effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming "a metaphor" and adding "it tells us power does not last" is identification of the writer's method — AQA Level 2 AO2 — with only a general comment. Level 6 needs "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously," exploring the effect on the reader.
   * **Why A:** There is no analysis and no explored effect, so the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires working through how the method makes meaning, which is absent.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted as a label.

   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve the analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.

11. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** Rank these AO2 comments comparing imagery in Storm on the Island and Exposure from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Both Heaney and Owen make an assaulting nature the aggressor, so that the militarised weather in each poem analyses how conflict reduces human beings to something besieged and powerless.", B) "Both poems use imagery of the weather.", C) "Both poets use weather imagery to make nature seem threatening to the people in each poem.", D) "Heaney's besieging storm and Owen's knife-like winds each examine how an indifferent nature turns on those exposed to it."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung climbs one AQA AO2 level. Bare identification of imagery is Level 2. An explained single effect is Level 3–4. Examination of how the method works in each poem is Level 5. Analysis that compares the two poets' differing effects on the reader is Level 6. The ladder is identify, explain one effect, examine, then analyse-and-compare.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from bare identification of imagery, to an explained single effect, to examination of how the method works in each poem, to analysis comparing the poets' differing effects on the reader. The top rung analyses and compares, it does not merely state one effect.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** One comparison "examines" how Blake's repetition works in London. A second reads the same repetition as a sustained pattern that makes the city's oppression feel inescapable, and compares its effect with a method in the paired poem. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines from each poem to widen the supporting evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to analysis, comparing the differing effects of the two poets' methods, C) It uses rarer, more technical terminology across the response, D) It develops each point across longer paragraphs
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is examination becoming "analysis of the writer's methods" — reading a sustained pattern and comparing its effect with the paired poem's method. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the level.
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terminology without analysed effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a level descriptor.

13. **Type: Select All [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 writer's methods for a Power and Conflict poetry comparison? (Select all that apply.)
    * **Options:** A) Imagery and specific word connotations, B) Form (dramatic monologue, sonnet, free verse), C) Structure (the volta, stanza breaks, enjambment), D) The poet's biography
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Imagery, form and structure are all AO2 methods — the deliberate choices each poet made. Biography is context, not a method on the page, and a method earns AO2 credit only once paired with its effect on the reader.
    * **Why D:** A poet's biography is contextual information about the writer, not a method the poet deploys within the poem.

14. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**
    * **Question:** "Shelley wrote Ozymandias in 1817." A student adds this to the end of a comparative paragraph. For AO3, why does it stay low in the AQA bands?
    * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about the period, B) It is bolt-on context — AQA Level 6 AO3 needs "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" where context drives the reading, C) It is too brief — a contextual point must run to several sentences to score, D) Context is not assessed on the AQA poetry comparison at all
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." A free-standing date is bolt-on. Context must drive the concept — for instance, anxiety about the permanence of empire shaping how the reader judges the ruined statue.
    * **Why A:** The date is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** Context is assessed as AO3, worth 6 of the 30 marks on this question.

    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the poems, not other students.
