# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Power and Conflict Poetry (AQA)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real comparative writing about the Power and Conflict cluster against the EXACT AQA
mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a
simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach the
client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the picks across the AO split).
The AQA anthology comparison is marked out of 30 across six levels: AO1 (argument + comparison of ideas) = 12,
AO2 (language, form, structure + effects, subject terminology) = 12, AO3 (ideas/contexts) = 6. AO4 SPaG does not apply.
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking.

**AQA-exclusive cluster:** only AQA (8702) sets the Power and Conflict anthology, so this bank has a SINGLE section by design.
AQA section (v7.20.822, FIXLIST #881, #815d — Neil: "40 per bank"): 40 items, AO1 16 · AO2 16 · AO3 8. Every quotation in a
Feedback or Why line is verbatim from AQA's June 2024 8702/2 mark scheme (`research/sources/aqa-8702-2-jun24-ms.txt`; gate:
`bin/msa-quote-gate.js`). Paper 2 wording: AO1 is "comparison" at every level, and AO2 says "writer's methods" (no "the").

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Power and Conflict)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student's whole comparison of 'Ozymandias' and 'London' explains, point by point, how both poems show power damaging people, with a quotation for each point. It never compares how the poets write and never argues an idea. For AO1, which AQA Level fits?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a critical, exploratory comparison built on an idea, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed comparison with apt references, C) Level 3 — some explained comparison, at the level of theme, D) Level 1 — simple comments relevant to comparison, nothing more
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Explaining shared themes with a reference for each point is Level 3 AO1: "Some explained comparison" and "References used to support a range of relevant comments". Level 6 is a "Critical, exploratory comparison". To climb, compare HOW each poet presents the idea, and argue a concept about power.
   * **Why A:** Level 6 needs a "Critical, exploratory comparison"; this explains themes and argues no idea.
   * **Why B:** Level 5 is a "Thoughtful, developed comparison" with "Apt references integrated into interpretation(s)"; explaining themes point by point is not yet developed thought.
   * **Why D:** It explains each point and supports it, so it is above Level 1's "Simple comments relevant to comparison".

2. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO1 evidence descriptor: top-band comparisons make "judicious use of precise references to support [BLANK]."
   * **Answer:** interpretation
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 strand is "Judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means each reference is chosen to do argumentative work — a precise half-line tied to the concept outweighs many general quotations.
   * **WhyWrong:** The missing AQA word is "interpretation" — precise references earn top marks only when they support an interpretation, never when listed as evidence.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses compare 'My Last Duchess' and 'Ozymandias'. One writes about the first poem in full, then the second, and compares only in its conclusion. The other compares the poems in every paragraph. Why is the second better placed for AO1?
   * **Options:** A) It quotes a greater number of lines from each poem, B) Every AO1 level is a level of comparison, C) It analyses the rhyme scheme of each poem in turn, D) It saves its comparison for one strong conclusion
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA words every AO1 level as comparison, from "Simple comments relevant to comparison" (Level 1) to "Critical, exploratory comparison" (Level 6). A response that compares only in its conclusion gives the examiner little comparison to reward. Our advice: compare in every paragraph.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "Judicious use of precise references", not the number of quotations.
   * **Why C:** Analysing each poem's rhyme scheme in turn is still one poem at a time.
   * **Why D:** A single comparing paragraph at the end is less comparison, not more.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four comparative openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Shelley and Blake both construct power as an illusion its holders cannot sustain, each poem interrogating the vanity of authority so the reader is left to measure its emptiness.", B) "Ozymandias is about a broken statue and London is about an unhappy city.", C) "Both poems show power as a bad thing, because the tyrant is forgotten and London's people suffer.", D) "Both poets present power as destructive — Shelley through the ruined statue, Blake through the marked faces — making the reader judge its cost."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Retelling each poem in turn is Level 1: at the top of that level a response "is likely to be narrative and/or descriptive in approach". The comment that power is bad in both, supported by a detail from each poem but naming no method, is Level 2: "Supported comparison". The comparison that names a method in each poem and its effect on the reader is Level 4: "Clear comparison". The comparison arguing one idea about power is Level 6: "Critical, exploratory comparison". The ladder is retelling, supported comment, method-and-effect, concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest: the retelling ("a broken statue"), then the comment supported by a detail from each poem ("London's people suffer"), then the method-and-effect comparison ("the marked faces"), then the conceptualised comparison ("an illusion").

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 rewards a "conceptualised approach to the full task". A conceptualised comparison treats each poem as...
   * **Options:** A) a story to be retold accurately, in the correct order, B) an argument about an idea, such as power fading, C) a source of as many quotations as can be embedded, D) a document to be explained through the poet's life
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means organised around an idea — each poem read as a construct exploring a concept (for instance, power's impermanence), with details as evidence. It is not retelling, quote-stacking or biography.
   * **Why A:** Retelling the poem in order is the narrative approach of Level 1, not a conceptualised argument.
   * **Why C:** Judicious selection, not quantity, is the top-band evidence habit; stacking quotations does not conceptualise.
   * **Why D:** Biography is bolt-on context; it does not organise the response around a concept.

6. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 5 (21–25) is headed "Thoughtful, developed [BLANK]".
   * **Answer:** consideration
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 heading is "Thoughtful, developed consideration"; its AO1 bullets are "Thoughtful, developed comparison" and "Apt references integrated into interpretation(s)". Level 6 lifts this to a "Critical, exploratory comparison".
   * **WhyWrong:** Level 5's AO1 bullet does say "Thoughtful, developed comparison", but the band's HEADING is "Thoughtful, developed consideration".

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Comparing 'The Charge of the Light Brigade' and 'Exposure', one student appends a quotation from each poem after a point "to add evidence". A second selects one precise half-line from each to pin an argument about how the poets treat soldiers' suffering. For AO1, what distinguishes the second?
   * **Options:** A) It makes judicious use of precise references, B) It clearly explains the effects of each method, C) It makes detailed links between context and text, D) It identifies the writer's methods in each quotation
   * **Correct:** A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "Judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)". Judicious means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not appended as evidence. A precise half-line tied to the argument outweighs many general quotations.
   * **Why B:** Explaining the effect of a method is AO2 work, not the AO1 matter of how references are selected.
   * **Why C:** Linking context to text and task is the AO3 strand.
   * **Why D:** Identifying the writer's methods is Level 2 AO2, not judicious selection of references.

8. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Shelley uses a metaphor. This is a technique. It tells us power does not last." For AO2, which AQA level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with exploration of the method's effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — the method named, with one obvious effect stated, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming "a metaphor" is Level 2's "Identification of writer's methods". Adding "It tells us power does not last" states one obvious effect: Level 3's "Identification of effects of writer's methods to create meanings", at best. Level 6 needs "Analysis of writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" and "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings".
   * **Why A:** Level 6 needs "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings"; one stated effect is not explored.
   * **Why B:** Level 5 needs "Examination of effects of writer's methods to create meanings"; nothing here is examined.
   * **Why D:** Level 4 needs "Clear explanation of writer's methods"; the effect is stated, not explained.

9. **Type: Fill [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO2 descriptor: "Analysis of writer's methods with subject terminology used [BLANK]."
   * **Answer:** judiciously
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO2 is "Analysis of writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously." "Judiciously" warns against feature-spotting: terminology earns marks only when it is selected to serve the analysis of effect.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve the analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.

10. **Type: Select All [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** Select ALL statements that are true of an AQA Level 6 AO2 comparison of two Power and Conflict poems (and none that are not).
    * **Options:** A) It analyses how each poet's method creates meaning, not just names it, B) It compares the different effects the poets' methods have on the reader, C) It names as many techniques as possible in each poem to demonstrate range, D) It uses subject terminology judiciously, only where it serves the analysis
    * **Correct:** A, B, D
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO2 is "Analysis of writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" and "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". Analysing how methods make meaning, comparing their effects, and judicious terminology are all top-band. Maximising technique-count is feature-spotting, which the mark scheme does not reward.
    * **Why C:** Naming many techniques is feature-spotting — AQA rewards judicious selection and analysis of effect, not quantity.

11. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** Rank these AO2 comments comparing imagery in 'Storm on the Island' and 'Exposure' from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Both Heaney and Owen make an assaulting nature the aggressor, so that the militarised weather in each poem analyses how conflict reduces human beings to something besieged and powerless.", B) "Both poems use imagery of the weather.", C) "Both poets use weather imagery to make nature seem threatening to the people in each poem.", D) "Heaney's besieging storm and Owen's knife-like winds each examine how an indifferent nature turns on those exposed to it."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming weather imagery is Level 2: "Identification of writer's methods". One stated effect (nature seems threatening) is Level 3: "Identification of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". Working through how nature turns on the people in each poem is Level 5: "Examination of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". Reading the weather as warfare, with layered effects, is Level 6: "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings".
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest: the bare identification ("imagery of the weather"), then one stated effect ("seem threatening"), then the examination ("an indifferent nature"), then the exploration ("besieged and powerless").

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** One comparison "examines" how Blake's repetition works in 'London'. A second reads the same repetition as a sustained pattern that makes the city's oppression feel inescapable, and compares its effect with a method in the paired poem. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines from each poem to widen its evidence, B) It moves from examining methods to exploring effects, C) It uses rarer, more technical terminology throughout, D) It develops each of its points across longer paragraphs
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "Examination of effects of writer's methods to create meanings" becoming "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". Reading a sustained pattern and comparing its effect with the paired poem's method is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the level.
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terminology without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a level descriptor.

13. **Type: Select All [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 writer's methods for a Power and Conflict poetry comparison? (Select all that apply.)
    * **Options:** A) Imagery and the connotations of specific words, B) Form, such as dramatic monologue or sonnet, C) Structure, such as stanza breaks and enjambment, D) The poet's own life story and the date it was written
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO2 is "Analyse the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects". Imagery and word choice are language; dramatic monologue is form; stanza breaks are structure. The poet's life and the date are context (AO3), not methods on the page.
    * **Why D:** A poet's life and the date are contextual information (AO3), not a method the poet uses within the poem.

14. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**
    * **Question:** "Shelley wrote Ozymandias in 1817." A student adds this to the end of a comparative paragraph. For AO3, why does it stay low in the AQA bands?
    * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about when the poem appeared, B) It is bolted on: Level 6 links context to text and task, C) It is too brief, as a context point must run to several sentences, D) Context is not assessed on the AQA poetry comparison
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "Exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." A free-standing date is bolt-on. Context must drive the concept — for instance, anxiety about the permanence of empire shaping how the reader judges the ruined statue.
    * **Why A:** The date is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** AQA: "Context is assessed throughout the paper"; it carries 6 of the 30 marks here.

16. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**
    * **Question:** On the AQA Power and Conflict comparison, how are the 30 marks allocated across the assessment objectives?
    * **Options:** A) Argument twelve, methods twelve, context six, B) Methods eighteen, argument six, context six, C) Argument fifteen and context fifteen, split evenly, D) Context twelve, methods twelve, SPaG six
    * **Correct:** A
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA: "30 marks – AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6": twelve for the response and comparison (AO1), twelve for methods (AO2), six for context (AO3). "AO4 will be assessed on Section A only", so SPaG carries no marks on the poetry comparison.
    * **Why B:** Methods carry 12 of the 30, the same as AO1; context carries 6.
    * **Why C:** Argument and context are not weighted equally; context is the smallest strand at six marks.
    * **Why D:** SPaG carries no marks on the poetry comparison, and context is six marks, not twelve.

17. **Type: Select All [Tests AO3]**
    * **Question:** A comparison argues: "Contemporary anxiety about the impermanence of empire (context) drives Shelley's idea that power is an illusion (concept), which drives his choice to let the desert outlast the boastful inscription (method)." Select ALL AQA AO3 strengths this shows.
    * **Options:** A) Context is used to drive a concept rather than bolted on as a fact, B) It makes specific, detailed links between context, text and task, C) It explores the ideas and perspectives the context opens up, D) It lists a standalone historical fact with no link to the poem or task
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. This is the AQA Level 6 AO3 chain — context drives concept drives method — with "specific, detailed links between context/text/task" and "Exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors." Listing a standalone fact is the opposite of what happens here.
    * **Why D:** The response does the reverse of bolt-on — every element is linked to the poem and the task.

18. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

    * **Options:** A) Marshalling as many supporting quotations as possible, B) A well-judged reference, chosen for the work it does, C) Quoting each poem accurately and in full, D) Choosing the longest available quotation from each poem
    * **Correct:** B

    * **Why A:** Judicious is about wise selection, not quantity — more can mean less precise.
    * **Why C:** Accuracy alone is not judiciousness; the choice must serve the argument.

19. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
    * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO1 bullet is "Critical, exploratory comparison". In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the faults in each poem and what the poet did badly, B) Forming an argued judgement and taking a position on meaning, C) Writing in a serious, formal and academic tone throughout, D) Disagreeing with the readings that other students offer
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does not mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AO1 asks students to "maintain a critical style and develop an informed personal response".
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday, negative sense of "critical", not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the poems, not other students.
