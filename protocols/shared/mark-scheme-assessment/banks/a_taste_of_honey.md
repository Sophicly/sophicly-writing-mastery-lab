# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — A Taste of Honey

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about A Taste of Honey against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ on this Modern Text (post-1914 drama):
AQA (8702) = AO1+AO2+AO3; Edexcel GCSE (1ET0 Post-1914) = AO1+AO3 (AO2 NOT assessed) with AO4 (SPaG) on a separate grid;
Eduqas (Post-1914 Prose/Drama) = AO1+AO2+AO4(SPaG) (AO3 NOT explicitly assessed here); OCR (J352) part (a) = AO1+AO2+AO3
(AO1 and AO3 equally dominant), part (b) = AO1+AO2 holistic; Cambridge IGCSE (0475) = AO1 knowledge, AO2 critical
understanding, AO3 analysis of the writer's effects, AO4 personal response (NOT SPaG), marked holistically by best fit.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "A Taste of Honey is about Jo, whose mother Helen leaves her for Peter, so Jo has a baby and Geof moves in to look after her." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 4 — clear understanding, but staying at plot/character level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Tracing what happens to the characters is plot-level work — AQA "Clear understanding". To climb, the student must treat the play as a construct arguing an idea (e.g. that care, not blood, defines a family), not narrate the story.
   * **Why A:** The "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" the top band demands is absent — this retells events, it does not argue a concept.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** This is more than a bare "simple comment" — it accurately understands the plot, which lifts it above the lowest band.

The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea about family from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, to comment, to explained method-and-effect, to concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. The jump to the top is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote Jo's line "I'm not planning big things for the future." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument that Jo braces herself against hope because she has learned abandonment. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references" to support interpretation rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards "judicious use of precise references." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, B) Level 5 — examination of the writer's methods, C) The band where the method is identified with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the stage directions and adding "it tells us the flat is poor" is identification with a general comment — AQA's identification-of-methods band. "Clear explanation" needs the effect explained; higher still is "examination", then Level 6 "exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings."
   * **Why A:** There is no analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" requires a sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
