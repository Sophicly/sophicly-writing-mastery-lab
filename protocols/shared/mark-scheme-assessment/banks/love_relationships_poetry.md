# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Love and Relationships Poetry (AQA)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about the Love and Relationships anthology comparison against the EXACT
AQA mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather
than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach
the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper. The skill assessed is mark-scheme literacy, NOT poem
trivia — questions reference the named poems only as anchors, never quiz the student on their content.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the questions across the AO split).
AQA anthology comparison is one 30-mark question marked across SIX levels: AO1 (response + comparison) = 12,
AO2 (methods + effects + terminology) = 12, AO3 (ideas/contexts) = 6. AO4 SPaG does NOT apply to this question.
AQA-EXCLUSIVE cluster: only AQA (8702) sets the Love and Relationships anthology, so this bank is ONE section (AQA), complete by design.
AQA section (v7.20.822, FIXLIST #881, #815d — Neil: "40 per bank"): 40 items, AO1 16 · AO2 16 · AO3 8. Every quotation in a
Feedback or Why line is verbatim from AQA's June 2024 8702/2 mark scheme (`research/sources/aqa-8702-2-jun24-ms.txt`; gate:
`bin/msa-quote-gate.js`). Paper 2 wording: AO1 is "comparison" at every level, and AO2 says "writer's methods" (no "the").

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Love and Relationships)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Both poems are about love. In one poem the speaker loses someone; in the other the speaker wants to grow close to someone." For AO1, which AQA Level does this comparison sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a critical, exploratory comparison organised around an idea, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed comparison with apt, integrated references, C) Level 4 — a clear comparison that names each method and its effect, D) Level 1 — simple comments relevant to comparison, narrative in approach
   * **Correct:** D
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Retelling what each speaker does, and pairing the poems only by shared subject, is AQA Level 1 AO1: "Simple comments relevant to comparison". At the top of Level 1, AQA says a response "is likely to be narrative and/or descriptive in approach" and may include "awareness of similarity or difference". To climb, compare how the two poets present an IDEA, not what happens in each poem.
   * **Why A:** Level 6 is a "Critical, exploratory comparison"; this retells the poems and argues no idea.
   * **Why B:** Level 5 is a "Thoughtful, developed comparison" with "Apt references integrated into interpretation(s)"; this has neither development nor references.
   * **Why C:** Level 4 is a "Clear comparison" with "Effective use of references to support explanation"; this names only what each poem is about.

2. **Type: Fill [Tests AO1]**

   * **Answer:** exploration
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 band heading is "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration". At the top of the level a response "takes a conceptualised approach to the full task supported by a range of judicious references" — it explores an idea across both poems rather than describing each in turn.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "exploration" — the top band explores an idea across both poems, it does not merely analyse one at a time.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — understanding both poems is already the top-band comparison, B) Level 4 — Level 6 also needs a critical, exploratory comparison, C) Level 1 — understanding is only the simple comment of the lowest band, D) AO3 — understanding the poems is really a matter of context
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 4 is headed "Clear understanding", and its AO1 bullet is "Clear comparison". Level 6 AO1 is a "Critical, exploratory comparison" with "Judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)": the comparison is organised around an idea, not around each poem's events.
   * **Why A:** Level 6 is far more than understanding: it is a critical, exploratory comparison.
   * **Why C:** Clear understanding sits well above Level 1's "Simple comments relevant to comparison".
   * **Why D:** Context is the AO3 strand, not AO1.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four openings to a Love and Relationships comparison from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Both poets construct love as a force that exposes the will behind desire: one interrogates possession as a bid for permanence, the other reframes union as an argument the natural world is made to prove.", B) "Both poems are about love; in one the speaker loses someone and in the other the speaker longs for closeness.", C) "Both poems show love as powerful, since one speaker kills his lover and the other begs for a kiss.", D) "Whereas one poet presents love as destructive through violent imagery, the other presents it as unifying through natural imagery, making the reader judge each speaker's control."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Pairing the poems by subject only is Level 1: at the top of that level a response "is likely to be narrative and/or descriptive in approach". The comment that love is powerful in both, supported by one detail from each poem but naming no method, is Level 2: "Supported comparison". The comparison that names each poet's method and its effect on the reader is Level 4: "Clear comparison". The opening that argues a shared idea about desire from the first line is Level 6: "Critical, exploratory comparison". The ladder is subject, supported comment, method-and-effect, concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest: the subject-only pairing ("Both poems are about love"), then the comment supported by a detail from each poem ("begs for a kiss"), then the method-and-effect comparison ("violent imagery"), then the conceptualised argument ("the will behind desire").

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote the same short phrase from Porphyria's Lover. One lists it after a point about the speaker. The other selects it to pin an argument that possession masquerades as love. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to show the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes judicious use of precise references to support interpretation, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "Judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)". "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to an idea outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "The poet uses natural imagery of mingling rivers. This is a technique. It tells us the speaker wants union." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with exploration of the effects of the method, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — the method named, with one obvious effect stated, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the imagery is Level 2's "Identification of writer's methods". Adding "It tells us the speaker wants union" states one obvious effect: Level 3's "Identification of effects of writer's methods to create meanings", at best. Level 4 needs "Clear explanation of writer's methods"; Level 5 "Examination of writer's methods"; Level 6 "Analysis of writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously".
   * **Why A:** Level 6 needs "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings"; one stated effect is not explored.
   * **Why B:** Level 5 needs "Examination of effects of writer's methods to create meanings"; nothing here is examined.
   * **Why D:** Level 4 needs "Clear explanation of writer's methods"; the effect is stated, not explained.

   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO2 is "Analysis of writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously." "Judiciously" warns against feature-spotting: terminology earns marks only when it is selected to serve the analysis of effect.
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.

10. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** Rank these AO2 comments on a poem's natural imagery from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "The poet's mounting catalogue of natural unions analyses desire as a case being argued, so the imagery itself becomes the rhetoric by which longing disguises itself as nature's decree.", B) "The poet uses natural imagery in the poem.", C) "The poet's insistent pairing of natural elements works to press the reader toward accepting union as an inevitable law rather than a choice.", D) "The poet uses the image of mingling waters to make the reader feel that union is natural."
    * **Correct:** B, D, C, A
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming natural imagery and nothing more is Level 2: "Identification of writer's methods". One stated effect (union feels natural) is Level 3: "Identification of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". Working through how the pairing presses the reader is Level 5: "Examination of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". Reading the imagery as the speaker's rhetoric, with layered effects, is Level 6: "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings".
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest: the bare identification ("uses natural imagery"), then one stated effect ("union is natural"), then the examination ("an inevitable law"), then the exploration ("nature's decree").

11. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**

    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines to widen the range of its supporting evidence, B) It moves from examining the method to exploring its effects, C) It deploys rarer, more technical terminology across the response, D) It develops the point across longer, more sustained paragraphs
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "Examination of effects of writer's methods to create meanings" becoming "Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings." Reading a sustained pattern and exploring several effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.

12. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO3]**
    * **Question:** A student ends a paragraph with: "This poem was written in the nineteenth century, when attitudes to love and marriage were different." For AO3, why does it stay low in the AQA bands?
    * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about attitudes in that period, B) It is bolted on: Level 6 links context to text and task, C) It is too brief, as a context point must run to several sentences, D) Context is credited on the Language papers, not on poetry
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "Exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task". A free-standing fact is bolt-on. Context must DRIVE the reading — for instance, showing how a period's ideas about ownership sharpen a poet's presentation of desire.
    * **Why A:** The statement is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** AQA: "Context is assessed throughout the paper."
