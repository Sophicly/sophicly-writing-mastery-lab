# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Love and Relationships Poetry (AQA)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about the Love and Relationships anthology comparison against the EXACT
AQA mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather
than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach
the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper. The skill assessed is mark-scheme literacy, NOT poem
trivia — questions reference the named poems only as anchors, never quiz the student on their content.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the questions across the AO split).
AQA anthology comparison is one 30-mark question marked across SIX levels: AO1 (response + comparison) = 12,
AO2 (methods + effects + terminology) = 12, AO3 (ideas/contexts) = 6. AO4 SPaG does NOT apply to this question.
AQA-EXCLUSIVE cluster: only AQA (8702) sets the Love and Relationships anthology, so this bank is ONE section (AQA), complete by design.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Love and Relationships)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Both poems are about love. In one poem the speaker loses someone; in the other the speaker wants to grow close to someone." For AO1, which AQA Level does this comparison sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a convincing, critical analysis and exploration organised around a shared idea, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed consideration with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 4 — a clear comparison that names a method and its effect, D) Level 1 — simple comments relevant to comparison, narrative and/or descriptive in approach
   * **Correct:** D
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Retelling what each speaker does, and pairing the poems only by shared subject, is AQA Level 1 AO1: "simple comments relevant to comparison", "narrative and/or descriptive in approach". To climb, the student must compare the poets' treatment of an IDEA, not the events of each poem.
   * **Why A:** The convincing, critical exploration of the top band is absent — this retells the poems, it does not argue a shared idea.
   * **Why B:** A thoughtful, developed consideration needs apt references integrated into interpretation, and this has neither development nor quotation.
   * **Why C:** A clear comparison names a writer's method and its effect; this names only the subject matter of each poem.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote the same short phrase from Porphyria's Lover. One lists it after a point about the speaker. The other selects it to pin an argument that possession masquerades as love. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)". "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to an idea outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

7. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "The poet uses natural imagery of mingling rivers. This is a technique. It tells us the speaker wants union." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis of writer's methods with terminology used judiciously, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 2 — identification of writer's methods with only a general comment, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the imagery and adding "it tells us the speaker wants union" is AQA Level 2 AO2: "identification of writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs a clear explanation of the effect; Level 6 needs "analysis of writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" and exploration of effects.
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, which is absent.
   * **Why D:** A clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

10. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
    * **Question:** Rank these AO2 comments on a poem's natural imagery from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "The poet's mounting catalogue of natural unions analyses desire as a case being argued, so the imagery itself becomes the rhetoric by which longing disguises itself as nature's decree.", B) "The poet uses natural imagery in the poem.", C) "The poet's insistent pairing of natural elements works to press the reader toward accepting union as an inevitable law rather than a choice.", D) "The poet uses the image of mingling waters to make the reader feel that union is natural."
    * **Correct:** B, D, C, A
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA AO2 band higher. The bare identification of a method, with no effect, is Level 2 identification of writer's methods. The fine-grained, insightful analysis that reads the imagery as rhetoric is Level 6. The ladder is identify, explain one effect, examine how it works, then analyse-and-explore.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from identifying imagery, to explaining one effect, to examining how the method works, to fine-grained analysis of layered effect. Level 6 explores how the method makes meaning, not a single stated effect.

    * **Correct:** B

15. **Type: MCQ [Tests Comparison]**
    * **Question:** On the AQA anthology comparison, one response analyses one poem in full and then the other in full; another weaves both poems through every paragraph. Which structure targets the top band, and why?
    * **Options:** A) The block treatment — handling one poem fully then the other keeps each analysis clear and complete, B) The interwoven treatment — integrated comparison across every paragraph enables the convincing, critical comparison the top band rewards, C) They are equivalent — the mark scheme does not credit structure, D) The block treatment — it lets the student write more about each poem in turn
    * **Correct:** B
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Integrated, interwoven comparison — weaving both poems through each paragraph — is the top-band AQA habit; it enables a "critical, exploratory comparison". Block treatment, one poem then the other, prevents sustained comparison and caps the response lower.
    * **Why A:** Handling one poem then the other reads as two mini-essays, not the sustained comparison the top band needs.
    * **Why C:** Structure matters here: the interwoven approach is what makes a critical, exploratory comparison possible.
    * **Why D:** Writing more about each poem in turn is coverage, not comparison.
