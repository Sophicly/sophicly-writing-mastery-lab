# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Pride and Prejudice

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Pride and Prejudice against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording,
and forces them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys +
feedback live server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ on the 19th-century novel: AQA 8702 = AO1+AO2+AO3 (NO AO4 on the novel);
Edexcel GCSE 1ET0 = Part (a) AO2 + Part (b) AO1 (no explicit AO3/AO4 marks); Edexcel IGCSE 4ET1 = AO1+AO2+AO4 (AO4 = CONTEXT, not SPaG);
Eduqas C720U = AO1+AO2+AO3 equally weighted; OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4 (SPaG); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = knowledge/reference, critical understanding, response to effects, personal engagement; SQA = Understanding/Analysis/Evaluation.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Austen presents Elizabeth as a witty girl who dislikes Darcy and then changes her mind and marries him." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes explained, relevant comment but stays at character/plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Tracing what the heroine does across the plot is character/plot-level comment — AQA Level 3 explained, relevant comment. To climb, the student must treat Elizabeth as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (first impressions as a failure of judgement the marriage plot exists to correct), not narrate her courtship.
   * **Why A:** The critical, conceptualised argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates a courtship, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare simple comment — it sits above the lowest band.
