# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Great Expectations

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Great Expectations against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ for the 19th-century novel: AQA (8702) = AO1+AO2+AO3, with no AO4 on the novel;
Edexcel GCSE (1ET0) = Part (a) AO2 on the extract + Part (b) AO1 on the whole text, with no explicit AO3 marks; Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) = AO1+AO2+AO4, where AO4 is CONTEXT not SPaG;
Eduqas (C720U) = AO1+AO2+AO3 equally weighted across five Bands; OCR (J352) = AO1+AO2 dominant with AO3 (context) and AO4 (technical accuracy), marked holistically;
Cambridge IGCSE (0475) = knowledge through reference, critical understanding, response to the writer's effects, and personal/evaluative engagement, out of 25; SQA (National 5 / Higher) = Understanding, Analysis and Evaluation, out of 20.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Dickens presents Pip as a poor boy who comes into a fortune, becomes a snob, and is finally humbled." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character and plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing Pip's rise and fall is character-and-plot narration — AQA Level 3 "some explained response." To climb, the student must treat Pip as a vehicle for a concept (ambition mistaking wealth for worth), not narrate his career.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates an arc, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare simple comment — it sits above the lowest band.

   * **Answer:** conceptualised

   * **WhyWrong:** The missing AQA word is "conceptualised" — treating the novel as the vehicle for a concept is what lifts AO1 into Level 6.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both cite Magwitch's "I've made a gentleman on you!" One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument that Pip's gentility is bought by the very convict he is ashamed of. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four Great Expectations openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Dickens constructs Pip's 'great expectations' as an interrogation of a society that mistakes wealth for worth, using his ascent to expose the hollowness of gentility itself.", B) "Pip is a boy who is given money by a secret benefactor and becomes a gentleman in London.", C) "Ambition is a bad thing in the novel, and it makes Pip unhappy and ashamed.", D) "Dickens presents ambition as corrupting through Pip's snobbery towards Joe, making the reader judge how far social climbing distorts him."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing unhappiness, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (Pip's snobbery towards Joe) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear explained response). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, to comment, to explained method-and-effect, to concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Dickens uses the metaphor of Satis House being 'all in decay'. This is a technique. It tells us Miss Havisham is stuck in the past." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us she is stuck" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" at Level 5 requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" at Level 4 needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.

8. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Rank these AO2 comments on the stopped clocks at Satis House from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Dickens's clocks halted at twenty to nine let him analyse how Miss Havisham has arrested time itself, so the decay of the house becomes an anatomy of a life that refuses to move past its wound.", B) "Dickens uses the stopped clocks at Satis House.", C) "Dickens's image of the clocks stopped at twenty to nine examines how Miss Havisham has tried to freeze the moment of her betrayal.", D) "Dickens uses the stopped clocks to make Miss Havisham seem trapped and sad."
   * **Correct:** B, D, C, A
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA AO2 band higher. The bare identification of a method, with no effect, is Level 3. The clear explanation of one simple effect (trapped and sad) is Level 4. The sustained examination of how the method freezes her betrayal is Level 5. The analysis with exploration of layered effects, tied to a concept, is Level 6. The ladder is identify, explain one effect, examine how it works, then analyse-and-explore.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from identifying the clocks, to explaining one effect, to examining how the method works, to analysing and exploring layered effects. Level 6 explores several effects, not a single stated one.

    * **Why A:** The fact is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** Context is assessed throughout the AQA novel question through AO3.

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**

   * **Options:** A) One 40-mark whole-text question blending all objectives, B) Part (a) tests only Language, form and structure (AO2) on the printed extract; Part (b) tests only whole-text argument (AO1), C) Both parts test AO1, AO2 and AO3 equally, D) The question is answered by multiple choice
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Edexcel splits the novel question in two: Part (a), worth 20 marks, assesses only AO2 (language, form and structure) on the extract; Part (b), worth 20 marks, assesses only AO1 (whole-text knowledge and argument). Keeping the tasks distinct is essential.
   * **Why A:** A single blended essay is how several other boards work, but Edexcel deliberately separates extract analysis from whole-text argument.
   * **Why C:** The objectives are separated, not blended; context carries no explicit marks on either part.
   * **Why D:** No GCSE Literature board assesses the novel through multiple choice.
