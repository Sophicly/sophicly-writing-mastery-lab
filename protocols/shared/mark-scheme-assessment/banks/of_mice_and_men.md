# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Of Mice and Men

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about Of Mice and Men against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA Modern Text = AO1+AO2+AO3 (context assessed);
Edexcel GCSE Post-1914 = AO1+AO3 (AO2 NOT assessed) + AO4(SPaG); Eduqas Post-1914 = AO1+AO2 + AO4(SPaG), AO3 NOT assessed in this section;
OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3 (part (a) comparison, AO1 and AO3 equally dominant); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = knowledge/understanding/analysis-of-effects/personal-response, best-fit, AO4 = personal response NOT SPaG.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Steinbeck presents George as a man who looks after Lennie and dreams of owning a small farm." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes clear comment but stays at character/plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". Reporting what George does and wants is character-level comment — AQA Level 3 clear understanding. To climb, the student must treat George as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (e.g. friendship as a fragile defence against Depression-era loneliness), not narrate his behaviour.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, exploratory argument the top band demands is absent — this reports a relationship, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed" response with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is clear comment about the character, not a bare "simple comment" — it sits above the lowest band.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
