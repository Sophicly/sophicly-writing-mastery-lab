# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — Edexcel IGCSE Poetry Anthology (Literature 4ET1)

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about an anthology poem against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection. On Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) the anthology essay is marked on a single
30-mark, five-level grid whose bands bundle AO1 (informed, critical personal response), AO2 (language, form and structure)
and AO4 — and here AO4 is CONTEXT, not SPaG. There is no separate technical-accuracy strand on this paper.

**This is an Edexcel-IGCSE-exclusive anthology — no other board sets this poetry cluster — so this file is a single section, complete by design.**

### **SECTION A: EDEXCEL IGCSE (4ET1 — Poetry Anthology)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO4]**
   * **Question:** On Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) English Literature, the anthology poetry essay assesses AO4. Unlike the UK GCSE boards, what does AO4 credit here, and how should the top band handle it?
   * **Options:** A) Technical accuracy — spelling, punctuation and grammar across the essay, B) Context — an integrated, convincing understanding of the relationship between the poem and the context in which it was written, C) The sheer number of poetic techniques the candidate can name, D) The length and ambition of the vocabulary used
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO4
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. On this specification AO4 is context, not technical accuracy: candidates must "show understanding of the relationships between texts and the contexts in which they were written", and the top band rewards context integrated into the response so it drives the reading rather than sitting apart as a block of facts.
   * **Why A:** Spelling and punctuation is the AO4 meaning on AQA and Edexcel GCSE, but on this specification AO4 is context and no separate accuracy strand applies.
   * **Why C:** Counting techniques is feature-spotting, which the methods strand penalises; it has nothing to do with the context objective.
   * **Why D:** Ambitious vocabulary is not credited here; AO4 rewards understanding of context, not the reach of the wording.

2. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** What separates a Level 4 (19-24) response from a Level 5 (25-30) response on this anthology essay?
   * **Options:** A) The Level 5 response quotes far more of the poem, B) The Level 5 response covers more of the poet's other works, C) The response moves from "sustained analysis" and "detailed awareness" to "cohesive evaluation" and "excellent understanding" — more integrated and more insightful, D) The Level 5 response is noticeably longer
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The climb is from sustained to cohesive — evaluative judgements that flow and connect across the whole response — and from detailed to excellent understanding. It is a shift in integration and insight, never in quantity.
   * **Why A:** Quotation count is not a level descriptor; the ladder rewards how references are handled, not how many appear.
   * **Why B:** Ranging across a poet's wider output is breadth, not the cohesion and insight the top band names.
   * **Why D:** Length is never a level criterion; a concise response can be cohesive and a long one merely sustained.

4. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** Level 5 (25-30) requires "a cohesive evaluation of the interrelationship of the language, form and structure". Which response best fits that descriptor for Do not go gentle into that good night?
   * **Options:** A) It names the villanelle form, then lists three metaphors, then comments on the rhyme, each in a separate sentence, B) It judges how the villanelle's compulsory refrains, its driving imperative verbs and its tightening structure work together to enact a grief that refuses to release the dying father, C) It sets out a dictionary definition of the villanelle before analysing the poem, D) It retells the poem stanza by stanza in order
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Interrelationship" means the methods are evaluated as they work together, not in isolation: here the refrains, the imperative verbs and the circling structure combine to enact the speaker's refusal to let his father die. Judging that combined effect is the cohesive top-band move.
   * **Why A:** Handling form, imagery and rhyme as a list of separate observations is the opposite of interrelationship; the descriptor rewards methods evaluated together.
   * **Why C:** A definition explains the term, not the poem's effect, and does no evaluative work.
   * **Why D:** Retelling the poem in sequence is narrative, which caps the response low.

5. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four openings to an essay on Do not go gentle into that good night from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by Edexcel IGCSE band (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Thomas constructs the villanelle's refrains as an act of defiance in which the son's rage against his father's death widens into a refusal to accept human mortality itself.", B) "The poem is about a man whose father is dying and he is very sad about it.", C) "Thomas urges his dying father to fight death, and the sadness of this makes the poem moving.", D) "Thomas sustains an analysis of defiance, tracing in detail how the repeated command to 'rage' resists the calm of dying."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one Edexcel IGCSE band higher. The bare plot-and-mood rung is Level 2, largely narrative. The supported comment that adds a general feeling with no method is Level 3. The sustained, detailed rung that names the repeated command and its effect is Level 4. The cohesive, evaluative rung that argues one idea about mortality from the first line is Level 5. The ladder runs narrative, to comment, to sustained detail, to cohesive evaluation.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from a narrative of plot and mood, to a supported comment with no method, to a sustained detailed analysis, to a cohesive evaluation built on one governing idea. The top band is cohesion and insight, not more retelling.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** The IGCSE AO2 strand rewards analysis of "the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects." A candidate writes: "Bhatt uses free verse in Search For My Tongue. This is a structural feature." Why does this stall in the lower bands?
   * **Options:** A) Free verse is not a genuine structural choice in the poem, B) It identifies a method but never reaches its effect on the reader, so it stays descriptive rather than evaluative, C) Search For My Tongue is not part of the anthology, D) The candidate should have named more techniques to climb the bands
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO2 wording ends "to create meanings and effects" — naming a method is only the start. Here the candidate stops at identification; to rise, the analysis must reach how the shifting free verse, breaking into Gujarati and back, enacts the loss and recovery of a mother tongue between two cultures.
   * **Why A:** Free verse is a real structural choice in the poem; the fault is that its effect is never analysed.
   * **Why C:** Search For My Tongue is an anthology poem; the weakness is analytical, not one of selection.
   * **Why D:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, which the strand penalises; depth of effect, not quantity, lifts the band.
