# Mark Scheme Assessment Bank — To Kill a Mockingbird

Deterministic, code-scored MARK-SCHEME-LITERACY assessment bank (parsed by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session_msa`).
Examiner-level — harder + more precise than the Mark Scheme Quiz drill. Every question makes the
student JUDGE real writing about To Kill a Mockingbird against the EXACT mark-scheme descriptor wording, and forces
them to USE the mark scheme's own vocabulary rather than a simplified paraphrase. Keys + feedback live
server-side and are stripped before questions reach the client. The AI is never the scorekeeper.

AOs tagged per question for stratified random selection (the picker spreads the 10 across each board's own AO split).
Question types: MCQ · Select All · Fill · Ranking. Boards differ: AQA Modern Text = AO1+AO2+AO3 (no AO4/SPaG on this question);
Edexcel GCSE Post-1914 = AO1+AO3 (AO2 not assessed) + AO4 (SPaG, separate grid); Eduqas Post-1914 = AO1+AO2 (equal) + AO4 (5 SPaG marks), AO3 not assessed here;
OCR J352 = AO1+AO2+AO3 (part (a) comparison; part (b) AO1+AO2 holistic); Cambridge IGCSE 0475 = AO1+AO2+AO3+AO4 (AO4 = personal response, not SPaG), holistic best-fit.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Modern Texts)**

1. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "In To Kill a Mockingbird, Scout grows up, watches her father defend Tom Robinson, and learns that Maycomb is prejudiced." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it is a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — it is a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — it makes some explained comment but stays at character and plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — it is an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Tracing how Scout grows up and what she witnesses is character and plot comment — AQA Level 3 "some explained response". To climb, the student must treat the novel as a vehicle for a concept (prejudice learned and then unlearned; the innocent destroyed by the town's fear), not narrate Scout's year.
   * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates events, it does not argue an idea.
   * **Why B:** A "thoughtful, developed response" with "apt references integrated into interpretation" needs both development and quotation, and this has neither.
   * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the story, not a bare "simple comment" — it sits above the lowest band.

3. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** A student says: "AO1 just means showing you understand what happens to Scout and Atticus." Which AQA Level does "showing understanding" actually describe, and what does Level 6 additionally require?
   * **Options:** A) It describes Level 6 — understanding the novel is already the top-band conceptualised response, B) It describes Level 3–4 (some or clear explained understanding); Level 6 additionally requires a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response", C) It describes Level 1 — understanding is the bare "simple comment" of the lowest band, D) It describes AO2 — understanding the novel is really a matter of analysing methods
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Showing understanding" is AQA Level 3–4 language ("some explained response" and "clear understanding"). It is the commonest way students undersell the top band to themselves. Level 6 AO1 is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response... judicious use of precise references."
   * **Why A:** The top band is far more than understanding — it is a conceptualised, critical argument.
   * **Why C:** Explained understanding sits well above a "simple comment".
   * **Why D:** Analysing methods is the AO2 strand, not AO1.

4. **Type: Ranking [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Rank these four openings on To Kill a Mockingbird from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
   * **Options:** A) "Lee constructs the mockingbird as the emblem of every harmless life that Maycomb's inherited prejudice destroys, using the novel to interrogate how a community teaches itself to fear.", B) "Scout is a girl whose father defends Tom Robinson, and by the end she meets Boo Radley.", C) "Prejudice is a bad thing in the novel, and it causes a lot of unfairness and sadness.", D) "Lee presents prejudice as destructive through Tom Robinson's conviction, making the reader feel how unjust Maycomb has become."
   * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, naming events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing unfairness, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the conviction) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
   * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. The jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.

5. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO1]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "it's a sin to kill a mockingbird." One lists it after a point. The other selects it to pin an argument about the innocent being destroyed by the town's fear. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second from the first?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its supporting evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method in the quotation using subject terminology
   * **Correct:** B
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.

6. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Lee uses the symbol of the mockingbird. This is a technique. It tells us the characters are innocent." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect
   * **Correct:** C
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the symbol and adding "it tells us they are innocent" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the symbol makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.

   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.

10. **Type: MCQ [Tests AO2]**
