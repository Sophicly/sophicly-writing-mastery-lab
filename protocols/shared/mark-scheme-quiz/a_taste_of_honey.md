# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA A Taste of Honey (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 English Literature Paper 2 Section A modern text
A Taste of Honey** (Shelagh Delaney). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical
slug ladder (`a_taste_of_honey.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for A Taste of
Honey lessons so every worked example comes from Delaney's play (never any other modern text's
characters or quotations).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused VERBATIM from the vetted AQA Modern Texts grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels; verbatim AQA band descriptors) plus AO4 technical accuracy (4 marks on a
separate strand). Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to A Taste of Honey: Jo, Helen, Peter, Geof, the
music-hall direct address, the jazz interludes, the shabby Salford flat, "I'm not planning big
things for the future", and Delaney's themes of motherhood, care and post-war poverty.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — A Taste of Honey)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature modern-text question (Section A, A Taste of Honey) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4 technical accuracy adding 4 marks, B) AO2 methods only, C) AO3 context only, D) AO1 alone out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The A Taste of Honey question is a 30-mark level grid — 12 for conceptual argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3) — with a further 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysing Delaney's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to a play rooted in 1950s working-class Salford, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand, but AO1 is worth 12 of the 30, not the entire grid.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 (26–30) demands a critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text — treating the play as a construct arguing an idea, not a story to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as care, not blood, being what makes a family, not around Jo's or Helen's actions.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating A Taste of Honey as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern-text question — the lightest of its three content strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in an A Taste of Honey essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where post-war poverty and cramped housing genuinely drive the reading — never a bolt-on history paragraph, C) Write a full paragraph of 1950s history before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — post-war austerity and cramped housing making Helen's flight and Jo's abandonment feel like the damage of poverty, not simple wickedness — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A history paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in A Taste of Honey? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) The music-hall direct address, in which Helen speaks straight to the audience, B) The connotations of specific word choices such as "comfortless", C) Structural choices, such as the jazz interludes between scenes and the play's episodic shifts, D) Shelagh Delaney's biography.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Dramatic form, word-level connotation and structural choices are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices Delaney made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the audience (Focus/Feel/Think/Act) — naming "direct address" or "the jazz interludes" alone is feature-spotting. Delaney's biography is context about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biographical facts feel relevant because the author made every choice, but they are background about the writer, not a crafted method the play deploys.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO1 Whole Text\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA's modern-text AO1 descriptors ask for a response to "task and whole text", not analysis confined to a single scene of A Taste of Honey.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — range across the play, from Helen's first careless exits to the makeshift household Jo and Geof build, to construct the argument.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one memorable scene feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6 Heading\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's top band (Level 6, 26–30) for the modern-text question is headed with which phrase?
   * **Options:** A) "Thoughtful, developed consideration", B) "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration", C) "Clear understanding", D) "Explained, structured comments".
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 (26–30) is headed "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration". Level 5 is "Thoughtful, developed consideration"; Level 4 is "Clear understanding".
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** "Thoughtful, developed consideration" is the Level 5 heading, one band below the exploratory top.
   * **Why C:** "Clear understanding" is the Level 4 register — clear explanation, not exploration.
   * **Why D:** This resembles lower-band structured-comment language, not the convincing critical analysis of the top band.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO2 summary asks for a "fine-grained and \[BLANK\] analysis of methods supported by judicious use of subject terminology."
   * **Answer:** Insightful
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 6 AO2 summary is a "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods". The pairing warns against feature-spotting: micro-level precision and insight into effect, such as how Helen's direct address recruits the audience as an accomplice, coaxing us to excuse the neglect we are watching — not a list of devices.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "developed" fit lower bands; the Level 6 word is "insightful" — analysis that perceives effect at a fine-grained level.
8. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 AO1 Features\]**

9. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3\]**

   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 credits "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" — context woven into the argument, such as the insecurity of 1950s working-class Salford driving the reading of Helen's neglect as survival, not cruelty — never a bolt-on history paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3

10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA rewards a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the play's faults and what Delaney did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal, academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.

15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Delaney's jazz interludes work. Another reads the jazz as a sustained pattern whose brief sweetness makes the poverty feel starker, exploring several effects. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines to widen its evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to exploration of the effects of the writer's methods to create meanings, C) It deploys rarer critical terminology, D) It develops the point across longer paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "examination" becoming "exploration of effects of the writer's methods to create meanings". Reading the jazz interludes as a sustained pattern and exploring multiple effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.

21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four A Taste of Honey openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Delaney constructs motherhood as a role Helen abdicates and Geof quietly assumes, using the play to interrogate whether care, not blood, is what makes a family.", B) "Jo lives with Helen, then Helen leaves with Peter, and Jo has a baby while Geof looks after her.", C) "A Taste of Honey is a sad play about a bad mother and a lonely girl, and life was hard back then.", D) "Delaney presents Helen's neglect through her casual exits and self-absorbed talk, making the audience judge how far Jo is left to raise herself."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating the household only, is the lowest band. The bare value-judgement about a bad mother and a hard life, with no method and no evidence, is a step up. The clear, explained rung that names a method — the casual exits — and its effect on the audience is Level 4 clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea about family from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus audience effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" to "exploration of effects" at the top. Reading Helen's music-hall direct address, what does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they work on the audience, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the audience — reading Helen's performed charm as a device that recruits the audience as an accomplice, coaxing us to excuse the neglect we are watching. That shift from a single stated effect to layered meaning is the AQA AO2 climb.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
