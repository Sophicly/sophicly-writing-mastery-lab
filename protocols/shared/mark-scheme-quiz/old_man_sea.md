# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA The Old Man and the Sea (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 English Literature Paper 2 Section A modern text
The Old Man and the Sea** (Ernest Hemingway). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the
canonical slug ladder (`old_man_sea.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for The
Old Man and the Sea lessons so every worked example comes from Hemingway's novella (never any other
modern text's characters or events).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused VERBATIM from the vetted AQA Modern Texts grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; verbatim AQA band descriptors) plus AO4 technical accuracy (4 marks on a separate
strand). Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to The Old Man and the Sea: Santiago, the marlin, the sharks,
the 84 days without a catch, "a man can be destroyed but not defeated", the recurring
lions-on-the-beach dream, and Hemingway's themes of endurance and dignity in defeat.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — The Old Man and the Sea)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature modern-text question (Section A, The Old Man and the Sea) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4 technical accuracy adding 4 marks, B) AO2 methods only, C) AO3 context only, D) AO1 alone out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Old Man and the Sea question is a 30-mark level grid — 12 for conceptual argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3) — with a further 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysing Hemingway's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels weighty for a novella shaped by Hemingway's ideal of grace under pressure, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand, but AO1 is worth 12 of the 30, not the entire grid.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 demands a critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text — treating the novella as a construct arguing an idea, not a fishing trip to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as endurance conferring dignity in the face of certain defeat, not around the order of Santiago's voyage.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating The Old Man and the Sea as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern-text question — the lightest of its three content strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in a The Old Man and the Sea essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where Hemingway's post-war preoccupation with grace under pressure genuinely drives his idea — never a bolt-on biography paragraph, C) Write a full paragraph of Hemingway's life story before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — Hemingway's post-war preoccupation with grace under pressure shaping why Santiago's endurance reads as dignified rather than futile — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A biography paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in The Old Man and the Sea? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) The novella's compressed, single-strand form and its plain, unadorned diction, B) The connotations of specific word choices such as the marlin called a "brother", C) Structural shifts, such as the move from the marlin's capture to the sharks' destruction, D) Hemingway's fishing hobby.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Compressed form, word-level connotation and structural shifts are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices Hemingway made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the reader — naming the plain diction or the shift to the sharks alone is feature-spotting. Hemingway's fishing hobby is background about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** A writer's pastime feels relevant because Hemingway drew on it, but it is biographical information about the author, not a crafted choice of language, form or structure in the text.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO1 Whole Text\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA's modern-text AO1 descriptors ask for a response to "task and whole text", not analysis confined to a single episode of The Old Man and the Sea.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — range across the novella, from the 84 days without a catch to the sharks' destruction of the marlin, to build the argument.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one memorable moment feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level Ladder\]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Hemingway uses the metaphor of the marlin as a 'brother'. This is a technique. It tells us Santiago respects the fish." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us he respects the fish" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO2 descriptor: "Analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used \[BLANK\]."
   * **Answer:** Judiciously
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO2 is "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously". "Judiciously" warns against feature-spotting: terminology earns marks only when it is selected to serve the analysis of effect — how the spare, understated prose works on the reader, not a list of devices.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** "Frequently" or "accurately" chase quantity or correctness; the AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.
8. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 AO1 Features\]**
   * **Question:** Which features push an AQA The Old Man and the Sea response to Level 6 AO1? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) A response organised around a concept, treating Santiago and the marlin as evidence for an idea, B) Judicious, precise references woven into interpretation, C) A sustained critical, exploratory argument across the whole novella, D) A careful chronological retelling of the voyage.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** A concept, judicious references and a sustained exploratory argument are all Level 6 AO1 — a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" with "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation". Careful chronological retelling is narrative, capped well below the top band.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why D:** Chronological retelling feels thorough, but narrating the voyage in order caps the response at the lower bands; the top band argues a concept across the whole text.
9. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3\]**

   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 credits "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" — context woven into the argument, such as Hemingway's post-war preoccupation with grace under pressure shaping why Santiago's endurance reads as dignified rather than futile, not a bolt-on biography paragraph.

10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA rewards a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novella's faults and what Hemingway did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal, academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Shift\]**
    * **Question:** AQA's AO2 strand moves from "examination of writer's methods" to "exploration of effects of writer's methods" between which two levels?
    * **Options:** A) Level 3 to Level 4, B) Level 4 to Level 5, C) Level 5 to Level 6, D) Level 2 to Level 3.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Examination" of methods tops out at Level 5; "exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings" is the Level 6 lift. On the sharks passage, that is the difference between examining the plain style and exploring the several effects its restraint sets working.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** These are lower bands where methods are identified or explained, not examined or explored.
    * **Why B:** Level 4 offers clear explanation; Level 5 reaches examination, so the exploration verb arrives one band higher.
    * **Why D:** These bands sit far below the examine-to-explore distinction.

Context earns top marks only when it is connected to the text and the question — Hemingway's ideal of grace under pressure driving the reading of Santiago's composure in defeat — never when it sits alone as a fact.

13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO1 Level Placement\]**
    * **Question:** A student writes: "Hemingway presents Santiago as an old fisherman who goes 84 days without a catch, then catches a huge marlin, and loses it to sharks." For AO1, which AQA Level does this response sit at, and why?
    * **Options:** A) Level 6 — a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, B) Level 5 — a thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation, C) Level 3 — some explained comment that stays at character and plot level rather than concept, D) Level 1 — an undeveloped, simple comment with no explanation.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Tracing what Santiago does across the novella is character-and-plot narration — AQA "some explained response". To climb, the writer must treat the old man as a vehicle for a CONCEPT (endurance conferring dignity in the face of certain defeat), not recount the fishing trip.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** The conceptualised, critical argument the top band demands is absent — this narrates the voyage rather than arguing an idea.
    * **Why B:** A thoughtful, developed response with apt references integrated into interpretation needs both development and quotation, and this offers neither.
    * **Why D:** It is explained comment about the character, not a bare simple comment — it sits above the lowest band.
14. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**
    * **Question:** Which build "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" for The Old Man and the Sea? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Reading Santiago's composure in defeat through Hemingway's post-war ideal of grace under pressure, so context drives why endurance reads as dignified rather than futile, B) Arguing the chain from Hemingway's ideal of grace under pressure (context) to his idea that a man's worth lies in endurance not outcome (concept) to his choice to let Santiago meet the sharks' destruction without complaint (method), C) Exploring the ideas and perspectives the context opens up, linked specifically to text and task, D) Tacking on that Hemingway published the novella in 1952, after the critical failure of his previous book, with no link to the novella.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Each linked reading makes context drive interpretation — grace under pressure shaping the reading of endurance, and the context-concept-method chain. A free-standing publication fact is bolt-on context that earns little; the band rewards context that drives the concept.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why D:** A standalone publication fact with no link to text or task is the classic bolt-on error the descriptor discourages.
15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Hemingway's recurring lions-on-the-beach dream works. Another reads the lions as a sustained pattern that makes lost innocence feel recoverable, exploring several effects. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines to widen the range of its supporting evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to analysis with exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, C) It deploys rarer, more technical terminology across the response, D) It develops the point across longer, more sustained paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "examination" becoming "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods". Reading the recurring lions as a sustained pattern and exploring its several effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.

21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four The Old Man and the Sea openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Hemingway constructs Santiago's ordeal as an interrogation of what dignity can mean when defeat is certain, using the marlin and the sharks to test whether a man's worth lies in outcome or in endurance.", B) "Santiago is a fisherman who catches a marlin and then loses it to sharks on the way home.", C) "Endurance is an important thing in the novella, and it leads to a lot of suffering and pride.", D) "Hemingway presents endurance as ennobling through Santiago's long struggle with the marlin, making the reader admire how far the old man will go."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is the lowest — a simple comment. The bare value-judgement about an important thing causing suffering, with no method and no evidence, is a supported comment, one step up. The clear, explained rung that names a method — the struggle — and its effect on the reader is clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" (Level 4) to "exploration of effects" (Level 6). Reading the sharks stripping the marlin, what does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the reader, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the reader — reading Hemingway's plain, restrained account of the sharks as making loss feel relentless and matter-of-fact, and the restraint itself as a dignity that refuses to plead. That shift from a single stated effect to layered, opened-up meaning is the AQA AO2 climb.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
