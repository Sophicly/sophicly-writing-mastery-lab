### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Silas Marner)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA 19th-century novel question (Paper 1 Section B) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 for a 30-mark total, with no AO4, B) AO2 only, C) AO3 carries the most marks because context is central to the novel, D) A single 40-mark question with AO4 SPaG.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA novel response is marked out of 30 across six levels of five marks each — AO1 and AO2 weighted equally at 12 marks each and AO3 (context) at 6, half their weight. There is no AO4 on the novel; AQA assesses AO4 on Section A only.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysis of Eliot's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument (AO1) and the 6 for context (AO3).
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to Eliot's study of faith and community, yet AO3 is the lightest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — half the weight of argument or methods.
   * **Why D:** The novel is a single 30-mark essay with no AO4 strand — technical accuracy is not assessed on this question.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO1 descriptor requires a "critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text" — reading Silas Marner as a vehicle for an idea, not a plot to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The exact Level 6 wording is "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Treating the novel as a construct exploring gold displaced by love is what lifts AO1 into the top band.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "thoughtful" describe lower bands — "thoughtful" belongs to the Level 5 "thoughtful, developed response", whereas the Level 6 decisive word is conceptualised.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**
   * **Question:** Which sentence is a Level 6 "integrated" use of context for AQA?
   * **Options:** A) "Eliot wrote Silas Marner in 1861.", B) "Eliot draws on the decline of handloom weaving under industrialisation to present Silas as a displaced, alien outsider, her image of the weaver as a spider spinning at a lonely loom making his isolation the cost of an uprooted trade.", C) "The novel is set in a village called Raveloe.", D) "Victorian people were worried about lots of things."
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 1 is a bolt-on fact; Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." Here the decline of handloom weaving drives the concept (a displaced, alien outsider), which drives Eliot's method (the weaver as a spider spinning at a lonely loom).
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** A composition date feels like solid context, but a publication fact bolted onto nothing explains no authorial choice — the classic bolt-on.
   * **Why C:** Naming the setting gestures at place without connecting any contextual idea to Eliot's method or concept.
   * **Why D:** A general statement about Victorian anxiety describes the era rather than the text — integration means context that drives a specific choice.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO1 Quality\]**
   * **Question:** Which features signal a top-band AQA AO1 response on Silas Marner? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Judicious micro-quotations such as "the gold had turned into the child", B) Treating Silas and Eppie as constructs exploring how human bonds redeem a life narrowed by betrayal, C) Retelling the plot from Lantern Yard to the robbery to Eppie's arrival in sequence, D) A conceptualised thesis in the introduction.
   * **Correct:** A, B, D
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, D. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Judicious micro-quotes, construct-level thinking, and a conceptual thesis all push into Level 6 — "judicious use of precise references to support interpretations." Plot retelling (Lantern Yard → robbery → Eppie) caps you at the lower bands.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why C:** Retelling the plot feels like proving you know the novel, but it only shows knowledge of events — AQA rewards conceptualised argument, not narrative summary.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3 Chain\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: For AQA, context about Eliot's move away from orthodox faith should be integrated into your analysis rather than saved for a standalone history paragraph.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA rewards context as a "driver", not a "bolt-on" — Level 6 AO3 wants "specific, detailed links between context/text/task." Weave why Eliot locates Silas's redemption in human love rather than divine grace into the analysis; never write a separate history paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **WhyWrong:** Choosing False usually comes from a KS3 "context paragraph" habit — but a bolt-on caps your AO3 marks because it never links context to Eliot's choices.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Effects\]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Eliot uses the metaphor of gold. This is a technique. It tells us Silas is greedy." For AO2, which AQA Level does this reach, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the method works, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the effect.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us he is greedy" is AQA Level 3 AO2 — a general comment on the method. Level 6 wants "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" and "exploration of effects", reading the hoarded gold as a soul that has poured its capacity for love into metal.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why A:** There is no analysis and no exploration of layered effects here, so the top band is out of reach.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the metaphor makes meaning, which is absent.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted as "greedy".
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 References\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's AO1 descriptor requires "\[BLANK\] use of precise references" — short, well-judged quotations such as "the gold had turned into the child" embedded in your sentence.
   * **Answer:** Judicious
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and precise. The single phrase "the gold had turned into the child", chosen to pin an argument about Eppie replacing the hoard, outperforms six general references.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Students often guess "frequent" or "accurate" — but the descriptor's keyword is judicious, because choosing the right micro-quotation matters more than quoting often.

   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 5 AO1 is a "thoughtful, developed response" with apt references integrated into interpretation — development and integrated quotation, sitting just below the critical, exploratory, conceptualised response of Level 6.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** "Clear understanding" is the Level 4 wording — clear and explained, one band below the thoughtful development of Level 5.
   * **Why C:** "Critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" is the very top band, Level 6.

11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Descriptor Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** Moving up the AQA levels for AO2, the demand climbs from "identification of the writer's methods". Which phrasing marks the Level 6 top?
    * **Options:** A) Identification of the writer's methods with a general comment on effect, B) Analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously, C) Clear explanation of the effects, D) Examination of how the writer's methods work.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 AO2 asks for "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" plus "exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". The verbs climb: identification, explanation, examination, and finally analysis and exploration.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Identification with only a general comment is Level 3 — the foot of this ladder.
    * **Why C:** "Clear explanation of the effects" is Level 4 — explanation, not the judicious analysis of the top band.
    * **Why D:** Examination is Level 5 — sustained working-through, one rung below analysis and exploration.
12. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Terminology\]**

13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Conceptualised\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "conceptualised" response. A conceptualised reading of Silas Marner is one that...
    * **Options:** A) uses difficult, complicated vocabulary to signal sophistication, B) is organised around a concept — human bonds redeeming a life narrowed by betrayal — treating Silas, Eppie and the plot as evidence for that idea, C) packs in as much Victorian historical context as it can, D) is very long and dense with detail.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means built around a concept — reading the novel as an exploration of how human bonds redeem a life narrowed by betrayal, with the characters and events as evidence. It is about organising ideas, not complexity, length or context-stuffing.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Hard vocabulary is not a concept — conceptualised is about the idea, not the wording.
    * **Why C:** Context supports AO3; it does not make a response conceptualised.
    * **Why D:** Length is not conceptualisation — a short response can be conceptualised.
14. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Judicious\]**
    * **Question:** Two students support the idea that Eppie replaces the gold. One quotes six lines; the other selects the single phrase "the gold had turned into the child". Whose reference is "judicious" — and what does "judicious" mean?
    * **Options:** A) The first — judicious means marshalling as much supporting evidence as possible, B) The second — judicious means a well-judged, selective reference chosen to do the most argumentative work, C) Both equally — judicious just means quoting accurately, D) Neither — judicious means choosing the longest quotation available.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and selective — chosen because it does real work, not because it is long or abundant. The precise phrase "the gold had turned into the child" outperforms six general ones. This is why AQA Level 6 says "judicious use of precise references".
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Judicious is about wise selection, not quantity — more can mean less precise.
    * **Why C:** Accuracy alone is not judiciousness; the CHOICE must serve the argument.
    * **Why D:** Length is irrelevant; aptness to the point is what counts.

16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary Critical\]**
    * **Question:** AQA asks for a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novel's faults and what Eliot did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal tone, D) Disagreeing with other readers' interpretations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.

20. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four openings on gold and isolation from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Eliot constructs Silas's hoarded gold as the measure of a soul contracted by betrayal, so the guineas become the novel's index of a life emptied of trust until Eppie restores it.", B) "Silas is a weaver who is robbed of his gold and later takes in a child.", C) "Money is a bad thing in the novel, and it makes Silas lonely and sad.", D) "Eliot presents gold as isolating through Silas's nightly counting of his guineas, making the reader feel how far his life has narrowed."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing loneliness, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear rung that names a method — the nightly counting of the guineas — and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher.
21. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary Exploration\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "clear explanation of the effects" (Level 4) to "exploration of effects" (Level 6). What does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques in the response, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the reader, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the reader. That shift from a single stated effect to layered, opened-up meaning is the AQA Level 4 to Level 6 climb for AO2.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
22. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
    * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 writer's methods to analyse in Silas Marner? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Specific word choices and their connotations (such as "his gold... his own dry, hard hand"), B) Narrative form (Eliot's controlling narrator), C) Structure (the placement of the robbery and of Eppie's arrival on the hearth), D) The biographical timeline of Mary Ann Evans.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Word-level choice, narrative form and structure are all AO2 methods — the deliberate choices Eliot made on the page. But a method only counts as AO2 once you pair it with its effect on the reader; naming "narrator" or "metaphor" alone is feature-spotting. The author's biography sits outside the text and earns nothing.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why D:** A writer's biography is background about the author, not a method within the text; AO2 analyses language, form and structure.
