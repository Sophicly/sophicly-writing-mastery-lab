# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA Lord of the Flies (Modern Text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 Modern Texts** question on William Golding's *Lord of
the Flies* (Paper 2, Section A). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder
(`lord_of_the_flies.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for Lord of the Flies lessons so
every worked example comes from Golding's novel (Ralph, Jack, Piggy, Simon, the conch, the beast, the
island), never another modern text.

**Mark-scheme facts are identical to the shared AQA modern-text grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 / AO2 12 / AO3 6;
six levels; verbatim AQA band descriptors) — reused unchanged from the vetted `mark-scheme-assessment/banks/lord_of_the_flies.md`
source. AO4 (SPaG) is NOT assessed on the AQA modern text question. Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to *Lord
of the Flies*.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth 2 marks
each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Lord of the Flies)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE Modern Text question (Lord of the Flies) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), B) AO2 only, C) AO3 only, D) AO4 SPaG only.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA modern text question is 30 marks: 12 for argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3). AO4 (SPaG) is not assessed on this question at all.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Method analysis matters greatly here, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** SPaG is assessed on some Literature questions, which makes this tempting, but AO4 does not apply to the AQA modern text question.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 Label\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 (26–30) for the modern text is headed "Convincing, critical analysis and \[BLANK\]".
   * **Answer:** Exploration
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The band heading is "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" — the response treats Lord of the Flies as a construct arguing an idea about human nature, not a story to retell.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** "Evaluation" or "analysis" name the skills, but AQA's exact Level 6 heading pairs "analysis" with "exploration".
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern text question — the lightest of its three assessed strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in a Lord of the Flies essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where it drives Golding's concept — don't dump a history paragraph, C) Write a full paragraph of post-war history before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest of the three strands here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives Golding's concept — his post-war pessimism making the boys' collapse feel inevitable — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A history paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in Lord of the Flies? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Symbolism (the conch, the fire, the pig's head), B) Structural shifts across a chapter, C) Specific word choices and their connotations, D) Golding's biography as a schoolmaster.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Symbolism, structure and word-level choice are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices Golding made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the reader (Focus/Feel/Think/Act) — naming "the conch is a symbol" alone is feature-spotting and earns low marks. Golding's biography sits outside the text; it is context, not method.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biography feels relevant because Golding made every choice, but a fact about his life is background about the author, not a method deployed on the page.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests Whole-Text Focus\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA's Modern Text AO1 descriptors ask for a response to "task and whole text", not analysis confined to a single scene.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — range across the whole novel to build the argument (the fire from hope to blaze, the conch from authority to fragments), not just one memorable moment.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one scene, such as Simon's death alone, feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**
   * **Question:** Across how many levels is the AQA modern text question marked?
   * **Options:** A) Four levels, B) Five levels, C) Six levels, D) Nine levels.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA marks this 30-mark question across six levels — Level 1 (1–5) up to Level 6 (26–30) — each carrying banded AO1, AO2 and AO3 descriptors.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** Four levels matches some shorter mark schemes, but the AQA modern text question is banded into six.
   * **Why B:** Five levels is an Edexcel pattern; AQA's 30-mark grid runs to six.
   * **Why D:** Nine is the GCSE grade scale, not the number of marking levels.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Argument\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 demands a "critical, exploratory and \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text".
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea (Golding uses the island to argue that savagery is innate in all people), treating character and plot as evidence for that idea, not narrating the boys' descent.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Answers like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands — the Level 6 word in the AQA descriptor is "conceptualised".
8. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 Descriptors\]**
   * **Question:** Which phrases are taken directly from AQA's Level 6 modern-text descriptor? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) "Critical, exploratory, conceptualised response", B) "Judicious use of precise references", C) "Exploration of effects of writer's methods", D) "Apt references integrated into interpretation".
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** The conceptualised response, judicious references, and exploration of effects are all Level 6 wording. "Apt references integrated into interpretation" is the Level 5 phrase.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why D:** "Apt references integrated into interpretation(s)" belongs to Level 5; references become "judicious" and "precise" only at Level 6.
9. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Judicious References\]**
   * **Question:** Two responses argue that the conch stands for fragile civilisation. One lists three quotations about the conch after the point. The other selects the single detail of the conch shattering "into a thousand white fragments" as Piggy dies. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to prove the breadth of its evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It identifies the writer's method using subject terminology, D) It integrates a clearly explained comment on the effect.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)". "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work — the conch destroyed at the very moment order dies is a single precise detail that pins the concept, outweighing a list.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — stacked quotations dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Naming the method with terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.
   * **Why D:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support interpretation.
10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO3 Context\]**
    * **Question:** "Golding fought in the Second World War, which made him pessimistic about people." A student adds this at the end of a paragraph. For AO3, why does it stay low in the AQA bands?
    * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about Golding's life, B) It is bolt-on context — AQA Level 6 AO3 needs "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" where context drives the interpretation, C) It is too brief — a contextual point must run to several sentences to score, D) Context is not assessed on the modern text question.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context, text and task". A free-standing biographical fact is bolt-on. Golding's war experience must DRIVE the concept: having seen ordinary people commit atrocities, he constructs the boys' descent to argue that savagery is universal, not exceptional.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why A:** The fact is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** Context (AO3) is assessed on the AQA modern text question, worth 6 marks.
11. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO3 Wording\]**
    * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO3 descriptor: context should be shown by "specific, detailed \[BLANK\] between context, text and task".
    * **Answer:** Links
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "specific, detailed links between context/text/task". Context earns top marks only when it is connected to the text and the question, never when it sits alone as a fact.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "links" — context, text and task must be linked, not listed separately.
12. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Conceptualised Response\]**
    * **Question:** Which opening best fits AQA's Level 6 "conceptualised" descriptor for Lord of the Flies?
    * **Options:** A) "Lord of the Flies is a novel about schoolboys stuck on an island.", B) "There are many techniques in this novel.", C) "Golding constructs the island as a laboratory of human nature, using the boys' collapse into savagery to argue that the darkness they discover was always inside them.", D) "This extract comes from the start of the novel."
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. This treats the novel as a conscious construct exploring a big idea — the conceptualised, exploratory thesis Level 6 rewards. The lift is the argument about innate darkness, not the vocabulary.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This retells the situation; narrative summary without argument caps the response at the lower bands.
    * **Why B:** Announcing that techniques exist is feature-spotting with no conceptual claim.
    * **Why D:** Locating the extract is orientation, not interpretation.
13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Golding uses the metaphor of the 'beast'. This is a technique. It tells us the boys are afraid." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
    * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the "beast" as a metaphor and adding "it tells us the boys are afraid" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination of writer's methods"; Level 6 "exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings" with terminology used "judiciously".
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** There is no analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
    * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the beast makes meaning, absent here.
    * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
14. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 AO2 Summary\]**
    * **Question:** Complete AQA's Level 6 summary: a "fine-grained and \[BLANK\] analysis of methods supported by judicious use of subject terminology".
    * **Answer:** Insightful
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA's Level 6 AO2 summary phrase is "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods" — micro-level precision, not broad comment.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "developed" fit lower bands; the Level 6 pairing is "fine-grained and insightful".
15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA's top band asks for a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novel's faults and the things Golding did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal and academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration vs Explanation\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" (Level 4) to "exploration of effects of writer's methods" (Level 6). What does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques in the response, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the reader, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the reader — reading Golding's fire as hope of rescue the boys let die, then the blaze that finally brings it. That shift from a single stated effect to layered, opened-up meaning is the AQA Level 4 to Level 6 climb for AO2.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
17. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 5 Label\]**
    * **Question:** Which phrase heads the AQA Level 5 (21–25) band for the modern text?
    * **Options:** A) "Thoughtful, developed consideration", B) "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration", C) "Clear understanding", D) "Explained, structured comments".
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 5 (21–25) is headed "Thoughtful, developed consideration"; its AO1 asks for "apt references integrated into interpretation". Level 6 (26–30) lifts to "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration"; Level 4 (16–20) is "Clear understanding".
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why B:** "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" is the Level 6 top band, one above.
    * **Why C:** "Clear understanding" is the Level 4 register, one band below.
    * **Why D:** This resembles lower-band structured-comment language, not the thoughtful, developed consideration of Level 5.
18. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Band Boundary\]**
    * **Question:** Which mark range is the AQA Level 6 top band ("Convincing, critical analysis and exploration") for the modern text?
    * **Options:** A) 16–20, B) 21–25, C) 26–30, D) 11–15.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 spans 26–30 marks; Level 5 sits below at 21–25 and Level 4 at 16–20.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** 16–20 is Level 4 ("Clear understanding"), two bands below.
    * **Why B:** 21–25 is Level 5 ("Thoughtful, developed consideration"), one band below.
    * **Why D:** 11–15 is Level 3 ("Explained, structured comments").
19. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Application\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Golding's fire motif works. Another reads the fire as a sustained pattern — hope of rescue that the boys let die, then the blaze that finally brings it — exploring several effects. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines to widen its supporting evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, C) It deploys rarer, more technical terminology across the response, D) It develops the point across longer, more sustained paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "examination of writer's methods" becoming "exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings". Reading the fire as a sustained pattern and exploring its shifting effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.
20. **Type: True/False \[Tests Integrated Context\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: For AQA AO3, a standalone opening paragraph listing facts about Golding's wartime service scores highly because it proves contextual knowledge.
    * **Answer:** False
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. A bolt-on history paragraph scores poorly. AQA Level 6 AO3 rewards "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" — Golding's disillusion with human progress driving the boys' collapse — not detached facts, however accurate.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering True assumes context earns marks on its own, but the band demands context linked to text and task, not a separate block of facts.
21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four Lord of the Flies openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Golding constructs the island as a microcosm in which the removal of civilisation's restraints exposes the savagery he believes is innate in humanity, so the boys' descent indicts the reader as much as the characters.", B) "Lord of the Flies is about some boys who are stranded on an island and turn wild until a ship rescues them.", C) "Savagery is a bad thing in the novel, and it causes the deaths of Simon and Piggy.", D) "Golding presents savagery as destructive through Jack's hunters, making the reader feel how quickly order collapses without rules."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing deaths, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the hunters) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea about innate savagery from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6\]**
    * **Question:** A Lord of the Flies response weaves quotations through every paragraph but merely retells the boys' descent into savagery in order. What lifts it to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) Adding more quotations to prove wider reading, B) A conceptualised approach to the full text with judicious use of precise references — arguing that Golding uses the descent to claim the darkness was always inside the boys, C) Retelling the plot more thoroughly, chapter by chapter, D) A longer paragraph of post-war historical background.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 demands a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" with "judicious use of precise references" — treating the novel as an argument that savagery is innate, not a sequence of events to narrate.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** More quotations chase quantity, but the band rewards judicious, precisely chosen references, not the sheer number of them.
    * **Why C:** Retelling the plot more thoroughly stays at Level 4 "clear understanding" — narration, however complete, is not a conceptualised argument.
    * **Why D:** A longer context paragraph inflates the smallest strand (AO3, 6 marks) and cannot buy the argument and analysis that Level 6 turns on.
23. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Effect\]**
    * **Question:** In the sow's head fixed on a stick — dripping and grinning — Golding creates a \[BLANK\] for the evil the boys carry within themselves, so a terror they have made appears to face them from outside. (One word.)
    * **Answer:** Symbol
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The sow's head is the AO2 method — a symbol; the mark comes from tying it to its effect: Golding externalises the boys' own savagery so the internal evil takes on a monstrous outward shape. Naming "symbol" alone is feature-spotting.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **WhyWrong:** "Metaphor" or "image" name related devices, but the specific method here is a symbol — the pig's head standing for the evil within the boys.
