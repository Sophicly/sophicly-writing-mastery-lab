# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: OCR Youth and Age (per-cluster) v1.0**

Per-CLUSTER mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **OCR GCSE (J352) Poetry Across Time — Youth and Age**
cluster (from *Towards a World Unknown*). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical
slug ladder (`youth_age.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `poetry_anthology.md` for Youth and Age
lessons so every worked example comes from a Youth and Age poem (never a poem from another cluster).

**Mark-scheme facts follow the OCR (J352 — Poetry Across Time) grid** — Part (a) compares the two
printed poems, marked out of 20 with AO2 dominant and AO1 secondary, across six levels (Level 1, 1–3,
up to Level 6, 18–20) and marked holistically; Part (b) explores ONE other poem from your anthology,
marked out of 20 with AO1 and AO2 weighted equally. AO3 (context) is not assessed on either part, and
AO4 (SPaG) plays no part in this poetry question. This is the EASIER drill — the harder examiner-level
judging lives in the matching mark-scheme-ASSESSMENT bank. Only the poem EXAMPLES are scoped to the
Youth and Age cluster poems: Out, Out– · Happy Birthday Moon · Baby Song · The Bluebell · Theme for
English B · My First Weeks · Tea With Our Grandmothers · Equilibrium · Cold Knap Lake ·
Venus's-flytraps · You're.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth 2
marks each.

### **SECTION A: OCR (J352 — Youth and Age)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Part (a) AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** For OCR Part (a) — the comparison of the two printed Youth and Age poems — how are marks weighted?
   * **Options:** A) AO2 dominant, AO1 secondary (total 20), B) AO1 only, C) AO3 only, D) AO4 SPaG.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. OCR Part (a) is AO2-dominant with AO1 secondary, marked out of 20 — focus on methods analysis of both printed poems with an underlying conceptual argument.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** The comparison feels like pure argument, but AO1 is only the secondary strand in Part (a) — the dominant weighting rewards methods analysis of both poems.
   * **Why C:** Context feels like a natural part of poetry essays, but AO3 is not assessed in OCR Part (a) at all.
   * **Why D:** SPaG tempts because it appears elsewhere in Literature, but AO4 plays no part in this poetry question.
2. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Part (b) AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** For OCR Part (b) — exploring one other single poem from your anthology, such as 'The Bluebell' on its own — how are marks weighted?
   * **Options:** A) AO1 = AO2 equal (total 20), B) AO1 only, C) AO2 only, D) AO3 only.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (b) balances AO1 (argument) and AO2 (methods) equally, out of 20. Both must be strong.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** A single-poem exploration feels like pure argument, but Part (b) credits argument and methods equally — analysis of language, form and structure carries half the marks.
   * **Why C:** Methods dominate Part (a), so carrying that weighting across is tempting, but in Part (b) AO2 shares the marks equally with AO1.
   * **Why D:** Context seems a natural addition to any poetry essay, yet AO3 is not assessed in Part (b) either.
3. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO Distinction\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: OCR Part (a) and Part (b) assess the same AO weightings.
   * **Answer:** False
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (a) = AO2 dominant + AO1 secondary. Part (b) = AO1 + AO2 equal. They are different tasks with different weightings — plan accordingly.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **WhyWrong:** Answering True assumes one rubric covers the whole question, but the parts differ — the Part (a) comparison is methods-dominant while the Part (b) single-poem exploration balances argument and methods equally.
4. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Part (b) Task\]**
   * **Question:** Having compared the two printed poems in Part (a), what does OCR Part (b) ask you to do?
   * **Options:** A) Explore in detail ONE other poem from your anthology — no two-poem comparison is required, B) Compare two further anthology poems, C) Write a context essay on the Youth and Age cluster, D) Analyse the same two printed poems again in more depth.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (b) is a single-poem exploration ("Explore in detail one other poem from your anthology"); only Part (a) requires comparison.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** Comparison lives in Part (a); Part (b) asks for one other poem explored in depth, not another pairing.
   * **Why C:** AO3 (context) is not assessed on this question, so a context essay meets no criterion.
   * **Why D:** Part (b) directs you to ONE OTHER poem from your anthology, not back to the printed pair.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests Context Not Assessed\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: A paragraph on Anne Brontë's life and times, however accurate, earns marks in an OCR Part (a) comparison involving 'The Bluebell'.
   * **Answer:** False
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is not assessed in OCR Part (a) at all — time spent on context paragraphs earns no marks, and AO3 is not assessed in Part (b) either. Biography is neither a method nor an assessed objective on this question.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **WhyWrong:** Answering True assumes context paragraphs are scholarly currency everywhere, but on this question AO3 carries no marks — close analysis of language, form and structure is where the credit lies.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Holistic Marking\]**
   * **Question:** How is OCR J352/02 Section A (Poetry across time) marked?
   * **Options:** A) Holistically, with one overall mark indicated at the end of the response, B) One mark per line, C) Spelling first, then content, D) Purely by word count.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The scheme states "the response is to be marked holistically" with examiners indicating the overall mark at the end.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary

   * **Why C:** Spelling (AO4) is not assessed on this poetry question.
   * **Why D:** Length is never the measure — the level descriptors judge quality.

10. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Sustained Critical Style\]**
    * **Question:** Writing on 'Out, Out–' and 'Equilibrium', what features sustain OCR's Level 6 "sustained critical style in an informed personal response"? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Evaluative adverbs across the response, B) A sustained conceptual thesis from intro to conclusion, C) Precise, pertinent micro-quotations skilfully interwoven, D) Switching register between formal and informal.

11. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Interwoven Comparison\]**
    * **Question:** OCR Level 6 demands "a sustained, \[BLANK\] comparison of texts" — weaving both poems together throughout the response.
    * **Answer:** Interwoven
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. A sustained, interwoven comparison of texts is OCR's top-band comparison requirement. Block treatment — writing about one poem in full, then the other — caps you lower.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** "Developed" or "detailed" belong to lower descriptors — OCR's Level 6 comparison bullet asks for an "interwoven" comparison, the two poems woven together throughout the response.
12. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Block Treatment\]**
    * **Question:** A Part (a) response analyses 'Out, Out–' in full for a page, then turns to the other printed poem. What do OCR's descriptors say about this approach?
    * **Options:** A) It prevents the "sustained, interwoven comparison of texts" Level 6 requires, capping the response lower, B) It is fine, provided both poems receive equal space, C) It is fine, provided the final paragraph compares them, D) It is the recommended structure.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Rigid block treatment — one poem fully, then the other — prevents the sustained, interwoven comparison OCR's top level explicitly requires, however thorough each block is.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why B:** Equal space does not repair the structure; the fault is sequential block treatment, not proportion.
    * **Why C:** A single comparative paragraph at the end cannot make the comparison sustained or interwoven throughout.
    * **Why D:** Rigid blocks feel structured, but separating the poems is exactly what the interwoven requirement rules out.
13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Part (a) Focus\]**

    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (a) is AO2-dominant. Close, word-level methods analysis beats any other strategy.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Retelling the buzz-saw accident or the family's reckoning demonstrates comprehension only and earns very little on a methods-dominant task.
    * **Why B:** Context paragraphs feel scholarly, yet AO3 is not assessed in Part (a) — time spent there earns no marks.
    * **Why D:** The poets' lives seem illuminating, but biography is neither a method nor an assessed objective on this question.
14. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Top-Band Comparison\]**
    * **Question:** For the OCR Part (a) comparison, a top-band response: (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Interweaves both poems in every paragraph, B) Sustains conceptual argument across the comparison, C) Uses precise, pertinent quotations skilfully interwoven from both, D) Treats the two printed poems in rigid blocks.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C

    * **Feedback:** Interwoven comparison, sustained argument, and skilfully interwoven evidence define Level 6. Rigid block treatment caps lower. The interweaving is only the mechanic — Level 6 needs it to carry a comparison of the two poets' METHODS and their DIFFERING effects on the reader (e.g. Frost's bare blank verse makes the horror land harder; Gunn's tidy couplets give the newborn's protest its wry, unsettling edge); weaving and connectors alone are not AO2 comparison.

    * **Why D:** Rigid blocks feel structured, but separating the poems prevents the interwoven comparison OCR's top level explicitly requires.

16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evidence Ladder\]**

20. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Critical Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** Which sentence signals OCR Level 6 critical style?
    * **Options:** A) "The poem is good", B) "Gunn's nursery-rhyme couplets wryly turn the cradle-song against itself — the tidy, childlike rhyming of 'womb' with 'room' sharpening, rather than soothing, the newborn's rage at being born", C) "This is a pantoum", D) "Gunn wrote poems".
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Option B evaluates ("wryly turn... against itself"), conceptualises (the comforting form sharpening the discomfort), and sustains sophisticated analysis. That's Level 6.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** A verdict like "the poem is good" sounds evaluative, but it offers judgement with no method, evidence or reasoning behind it.
    * **Why C:** Naming the form is accurate terminology, yet "this is a pantoum" is identification — Level 6 needs the form's effect analysed and judged.
    * **Why D:** A bare factual statement about the poet describes nothing in the text — it is the flattest possible description, not critical style.

23. **Type: Ranking \[Tests Critical Style Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four sentences from a Part (a) comparison of 'Happy Birthday Moon' and 'Baby Song' from WEAKEST to STRONGEST (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Both 'Happy Birthday Moon' and 'Baby Song' are about babies and their parents.", B) "Antrobus repeats whole lines in a pantoum, while Gunn writes in rhyming couplets.", C) "Antrobus's returning lines suggest the patient practice between father and son, whereas Gunn's neat couplets sound like a nursery rhyme at odds with the baby's complaint.", D) "While Antrobus's returning pantoum lines patiently enact a father and deaf son learning to really hear each other, Gunn's cradle-song couplets are wryly turned against the newborn 'raging, small, and red' — the same childlike patterning consoling in one poem and unsettling in the other."
    * **Correct:** A, B, C, D
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung climbs the OCR grid. A shared subject with no method is "a basic response to both text and task". Naming the forms adds relevant subject terminology but stops at identification. Linking each method to a reading makes "some explicit, relevant comparisons between texts" with some analytical comment. The top rung achieves a sustained, interwoven comparison in a critical style — both poets' methods evaluated for their DIFFERING effects within one argued sentence.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from a bare shared subject, to form-spotting with terminology but no analysis, to methods each linked to a reading in an explicit comparison, to an interwoven, evaluative comparison of both poets' methods and their differing effects — the "sustained critical style" of Level 6.
