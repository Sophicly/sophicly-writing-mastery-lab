# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 English Literature Paper 2 Section A modern text
The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time** (Simon Stephens's stage adaptation of Mark
Haddon's novel). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder
(`curious_incident.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for Curious Incident
lessons so every worked example comes from Stephens's play (never An Inspector Calls / Lord of the
Flies / any other modern text).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused VERBATIM from the vetted AQA Modern Texts grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels; verbatim AQA band descriptors) plus AO4 technical accuracy (4 marks on a
separate strand). Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to the play: Christopher, Wellington, the father's
lie, the concealed letters from his mother, the journey to London, the staged Underground sequence,
the meta-theatrical device of Christopher's book being performed, "cannot tell lies", and the
writers' ideas about truth, difference and a world built for other people.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature modern-text question (Section A, The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4 technical accuracy adding 4 marks, B) AO2 methods only, C) AO3 context only, D) AO1 alone out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Curious Incident question is a 30-mark level grid — 12 for conceptual argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3) — with a further 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysing the writers' staged methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to a play about a mind the world was not built for, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand, but AO1 is worth 12 of the 30, not the entire grid.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 demands a critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text — treating the play as a construct arguing an idea, not a murder-mystery to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as a literal mind exposing the deceptions of the adults around it, not around Christopher's actions or the sequence of the investigation.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating the play as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern-text question — the lightest of its three content strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in a Curious Incident essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where it genuinely drives the idea — growing awareness that institutions are built around neurotypical assumptions making Christopher disabled by his environment rather than by himself — never a bolt-on paragraph of facts, C) Write a full paragraph about attitudes to neurodivergence before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — awareness that public spaces are designed around neurotypical minds making Christopher's collapse a failure of his environment, not of himself — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A facts paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Dramatic form — the meta-theatrical device of Christopher's book being performed, and his direct address to the audience, B) Stage directions, lighting and projection cues, C) Structural shifts across the murder-mystery spine of the play, D) The biography of the actor playing Christopher.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Dramatic form, staging choices and structural shifts are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices the writers made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the audience (Focus/Feel/Think/Act) — naming "direct address" or "projection cues" alone is feature-spotting. An actor's biography sits outside the text and is not a writer's method.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** A performer's biography can feel relevant to a staged text, but AO2 analyses language, form and structure in the text — not the life of whoever plays the role.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO1 Whole Text\]**

   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — range across the play, from the discovery of Wellington to the journey to London, to build the argument.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one memorable moment feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Ladder Ceiling\]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Stephens uses a stage direction. This is a technique. It tells us the station is busy." Where does the AQA AO2 ladder cap this, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — it analyses and explores the effects of the writer's methods, B) Level 5 — it examines how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — it is identification of the writer's methods with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — it clearly explains the method and its effect.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the stage direction and adding "it tells us the station is busy" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO2 summary asks for a "fine-grained and \[BLANK\] analysis of methods supported by judicious use of subject terminology."
   * **Answer:** Insightful
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 6 AO2 summary is a "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods". The pairing warns against feature-spotting: micro-level precision and insight into effect, such as how a single staged choice — the projected station signage, say — makes meaning, not a list of devices.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "developed" fit lower bands; the Level 6 word is "insightful" — analysis that perceives effect at a fine-grained level.

10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA rewards a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the play's faults and what the writers did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal, academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.

13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 5 vs Level 6\]**
    * **Question:** A response on the play is thoughtful and developed, with apt references integrated into interpretation. That is AQA Level 5 AO1. What does Level 6 require beyond it?
    * **Options:** A) More quotations from a wider range of scenes, B) A "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" with "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation", C) Longer paragraphs developing each point more fully, D) A dedicated context paragraph to secure the AO3 marks.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct.     * **AO:** AO1

21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four Curious Incident openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Stephens constructs Christopher's literal mind as the play's moral instrument, using a narrator who cannot lie to interrogate a world of adults whose comfortable deceptions he steadily exposes.", B) "Christopher finds Wellington killed, investigates, and in the end travels to London to find his mother.", C) "The play is about being different, which is a difficult but important thing to explore.", D) "Stephens presents Christopher's difference as isolating, using his direct address to the audience to make us share his bewilderment at an unaccommodating world."

The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus audience effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" to "exploration of effects" at the top. Reading the staged Underground sequence, what does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the audience, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the audience — reading the torrent of projected signage and overlapping sound as flooding the stage with more information than any mind could filter, so the audience physically undergoes the overload Christopher describes. That shift from a single stated effect to layered meaning is the AQA AO2 climb.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
