# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: Eduqas Poetry Anthology (per-cluster) v1.0**

Per-CLUSTER mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **Eduqas GCSE (C720U) Poetry Anthology**. Served by
`SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder (`eduqas_poetry_anthology.md`) — this
bank REPLACES the generic `poetry_anthology.md` for Eduqas anthology lessons so every worked example
comes from an Eduqas anthology poem (never a poem from another board's cluster).

**Mark-scheme facts follow the Eduqas (C720U) poetry grid** — the Component 1 poetry section sets a
printed-poem question (15 marks), then a comparison with a chosen anthology poem (25 marks). On the
25-mark comparison "AO1, AO2 and AO3 are equally weighted in this question"; AO4 (SPaG) is not part of
the poetry comparison. Responses sit on a five-band grid: Band 5 (21–25) is "critical, illuminating and
sustained across AO1, AO2 and AO3" with "a wide ranging discussion of the similarities and/or
differences"; Band 4 (16–20) is "focussed, coherent and sustained". This is the EASIER drill — the
harder examiner-level judging lives in the matching mark-scheme-ASSESSMENT bank. Only the poem
EXAMPLES are scoped to the 15 anthology poems: The Schoolboy · I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud ·
Blackberry-Picking · Catrin · Origin Story · Drummer Hodge · Disabled · Kamikaze · War Photographer ·
Remains · Cousin Kate · Sonnet 29 · Dusting the Phone · I Shall Return · Decomposition.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth 2
marks each.

### **SECTION A: EDUQAS (C720U — Poetry Anthology)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Two-Part Structure\]**
   * **Question:** How is the Eduqas Component 1 poetry section structured?
   * **Options:** A) A single printed-poem question (15 marks), then a comparison with a chosen anthology poem (25 marks), B) One 40-mark essay, C) Two unseen poems only, D) A single 25-mark question with no printed poem.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part one analyses a printed poem for 15 marks; part two compares it with a chosen anthology poem for 25 marks.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** A single 40-mark essay is not the Eduqas poetry structure.
   * **Why C:** The printed poem is named and given, not unseen.
   * **Why D:** The printed-poem question (15 marks) precedes the 25-mark comparison.
2. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** For the Eduqas GCSE Poetry anthology comparison, how are the 25 marks split?
   * **Options:** A) AO1 + AO2 + AO3 approximately equal (Balanced all three), B) AO2 only, C) AO3 only, D) AO4 SPaG.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Eduqas weights AO1, AO2 and AO3 approximately equally across the 25 marks. You must engage all three AOs — none can be neglected.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Methods analysis is vital, but assuming AO2 carries everything ignores Eduqas's roughly equal credit for argument and for context.
   * **Why C:** Context is genuinely assessed here, yet it is only around a third of the marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** SPaG tempts because some Literature questions carry AO4, but it is not part of the Eduqas poetry comparison.
3. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Equal Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** The mark scheme states: "AO1, AO2 and AO3 are \[BLANK\] weighted in this question."
   * **Answer:** equally
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The scheme's exact wording is that the three AOs are "equally weighted in this question" — so neglecting any one forfeits about a third of the 25 marks.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **WhyWrong:** Words such as "evenly" or "fairly" paraphrase the idea, but the mark scheme's word is "equally" — AO1, AO2 and AO3 each carry roughly a third of the credit.
4. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Band 5 Range\]**
   * **Question:** Which mark range is Eduqas Band 5 on the 25-mark comparison?
   * **Options:** A) 16–20, B) 21–25, C) 25–30, D) 11–15.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Band 5 is 21–25; Band 4 sits below at 16–20.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why A:** 16–20 is Band 4 ("focussed, coherent and sustained").
   * **Why C:** 25–30 belongs to boards marking out of 30, not the Eduqas 25-mark question.
   * **Why D:** 11–15 is a lower band on this grid.
5. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Band 4 Heading\]**

   * **Why B:** "Simple" belongs to the lowest band.

7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Illuminating\]**

   * **Answer:** illuminating
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Illuminating" is Eduqas's top-band keyword — insight beyond the obvious, showing the examiner something they hadn't seen.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** "Perceptive" or "insightful" are close cousins from other ladders, but Eduqas's specific Band 5 keyword is "illuminating" — shedding new light on the poems.
8. **Type: True/False \[Tests Illuminating vs Thoughtful\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: The difference between Eduqas Band 4 "thoughtful" and Band 5 "illuminating" is the leap from clear understanding to perceptive, fresh insight.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Thoughtful = clear, competent. Illuminating = perceptive, fresh, showing layered or ambiguous readings.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Choosing False usually means treating the band words as synonyms, but Eduqas distinguishes them — thoughtful is clear competence while illuminating adds fresh, perceptive insight.
9. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Band 4 Approach\]**

10. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Sensitive Evaluation\]**

    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "analytical" name lower-band skills — the Band 5 pairing is "sensitive and evaluative", combining alertness to nuance with judgements about effectiveness.
11. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Band 5 AO1\]**

    * **Correct:** A, B, C

12. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Pertinent References\]**
    * **Question:** Eduqas Band 5 demands "pertinent, direct references to support interpretation". What makes a reference "pertinent"?
    * **Options:** A) Using the longest quotations, B) Using famous lines, C) References precisely targeted to illuminate the specific conceptual point, D) Using different stanzas.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Pertinent = acutely relevant, surgically selected — each reference chosen because it proves the exact point being argued.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Long quotations feel like strong evidence, but length is not relevance — pertinent references are short and precisely targeted.
    * **Why B:** Famous lines feel authoritative, yet a reference is pertinent only if it proves your specific conceptual point, not because it is well known.
    * **Why D:** Ranging across stanzas demonstrates coverage, but breadth alone is not pertinence — each reference must illuminate the exact point being made.
13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Pertinent Selection\]**
    * **Question:** A student comparing War Photographer with Drummer Hodge argues that Duffy's photographer tries to process horror into manageable order. Which use of reference is "pertinent"?
    * **Options:** A) Copying out a whole stanza of War Photographer so no detail is missed, B) Quoting "spools of suffering" precisely because its metaphor proves that exact point — suffering wound into neat, developable reels, C) Quoting the list "Belfast. Beirut. Phnom Penh." because it is the poem's best-known line, D) Quoting one line from every stanza of both poems to display coverage.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Spools of suffering" is surgically selected — the metaphor itself enacts the claim that the photographer winds chaos into ordered, manageable form. That precise targeting is what makes a reference pertinent.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Length is not relevance — a whole stanza buries the one phrase that proves the point.
    * **Why C:** Fame is not pertinence — the place-name list evidences the wars' spread, not the ordering of suffering.
    * **Why D:** Coverage is not pertinence — each reference must illuminate the exact point being made, not demonstrate breadth.
14. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Terminology\]**

15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Meanings and Effects\]**
    * **Question:** Band 5 AO2 requires "assured reference to meanings and effects". Which sentence about Kamikaze meets it?
    * **Options:** A) "Garland uses imagery in Kamikaze", B) "The 'shoals of fish flashing silver' pull the pilot back towards life — the sea's living movement makes the honour-code's demand for death feel unnatural, so the reader registers the pull of home before the pilot acts on it", C) "The sea is described as 'green-blue translucent'", D) "Kamikaze has several stanzas".
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Option B moves from method (the silver-fish imagery) to meaning (life against the honour-code) to effect on the reader — the assured method-to-effect chain Band 5 AO2 rewards.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming a technique with no meaning or effect attached is feature-spotting, the opposite of assured reference.
    * **Why C:** Quoting a description without interpreting what it means or does leaves the reference doing no analytical work.
    * **Why D:** Counting stanzas states a fact of layout, touching neither meanings nor effects.

19. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Integrated Context\]**
    * **Question:** For Eduqas Poetry AO3 (~⅓ of the marks), which sentence about Drummer Hodge best integrates context?
    * **Options:** A) "Drummer Hodge is set during the Boer War", B) "Hardy mourns the waste of an anonymous Boer War burial through the burial itself — 'Uncoffined – just as found' — while his three neat six-line stanzas grant Hodge the elegiac dignity the army denied him", C) "Soldiers in the past wore uniforms", D) "Hardy was English".
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Option B integrates context (the anonymity of a Boer War burial) into analysis of the method (the blunt "Uncoffined" and the controlled six-line stanzas) and a conceptual argument (the poem restoring the dignity the army denied).
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why A:** Naming the war seems contextual, but a bare setting fact does no analytical work and earns minimal credit.
    * **Why C:** Period detail about uniforms has no bearing on the poem's methods or meaning — context must illuminate the text.
    * **Why D:** Nationality is accurate background, but stated alone it explains nothing about how or why the poem works as it does.

21. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Comparison Discipline\]**
    * **Question:** Which features build a Band 5 comparison of Disabled and Remains? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Integrated comparison within each paragraph, B) Comparative connectors ("Whereas…", "In contrast…", "Similarly…"), C) Perceptive linking of shared concepts across both poems — war's damage outlasting the fighting, D) Block analysis of Disabled in full, then Remains.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Integrated comparison, connectors and perceptive concept-linking all build Band 5. Block treatment caps lower. The interwoven structure and connectors are only the frame — Band 5 needs that frame to carry a comparison of the two poets' METHODS and their DIFFERING effects on the reader; "Whereas… Similarly…" with no method+effect is feature-spotting, not comparison.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why D:** Treating each poem in its own block feels methodical, but separating them prevents the integrated comparison Band 5 rewards and caps the response lower.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Illuminating Insight\]**
    * **Question:** Which sentence signals Eduqas Band 5 "illuminating" insight?
    * **Options:** A) "War Photographer and Decomposition are both about photographs", B) "Whereas Duffy's photographer winds 'spools of suffering' into ordered six-line stanzas that mirror his struggle to contain chaos, Ghose's free verse loosens as detached composition gives way to guilt — both poems indict the aestheticising eye, but only Decomposition turns the accusation on the speaker himself", C) "Both poets criticise photography", D) "The photographer develops pictures in a darkroom".
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Option B is illuminating (the turn from shared indictment to self-accusation), integrated-comparative, and perceptive of layered meaning. The Band 5 Eduqas voice.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Naming the shared subject feels like understanding, but it is the obvious surface — illumination requires insight beyond it.
    * **Why C:** Spotting a shared attitude links labels without revealing anything about either poet's treatment.
    * **Why D:** Recalling the darkroom setting is an isolated detail with no interpretation or comparison, so it cannot illuminate meaning.

24. **Type: Ranking \[Tests Comparison Openings\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four openings to an Eduqas comparison of Disabled and Kamikaze from WEAKEST to STRONGEST (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Owen and Garland both write about war.", B) "Both Disabled and Kamikaze explore how war damages those who fight.", C) "Owen's veteran, 'waiting for dark' in his wheeled chair, is ignored on his return, while Garland's pilot is shunned by his own family, so both poems consider what happens after the fighting.", D) "While Owen exposes the romantic illusion that led a boy to enlist — the glint of 'jewelled hilts' — curdling into neglect, Garland traces the opposite journey: a pilot who chooses life over honour and is punished with the same isolation, so together the poems argue that war leaves no honourable way home."
    * **Correct:** A, B, C, D
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. A bare shared subject is weakest. A named shared theme with no argument is still descriptive. Clear, secure detail from both poems is "thoughtful" Band 4 territory — focussed and coherent, but not yet argued as a difference. The top rung is "sensitive and evaluative": a perceptive comparative claim about the poets' differing treatments, promising the "wide ranging discussion" Band 5 rewards.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from a bare shared subject, to a named theme with no argument, to secure detail from both poems without an argued difference, to an illuminating comparative thesis arguing how the two poets' opposite journeys reach the same isolation.
