# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA To Kill a Mockingbird (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 Modern Text** question (Paper 2, Section A) on
Harper Lee's *To Kill a Mockingbird*. Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical
slug ladder (`tkam.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for TKAM lessons so every
worked example is drawn from *To Kill a Mockingbird* (Scout, Atticus, Tom Robinson, Boo Radley,
Maycomb; the mockingbird motif; prejudice, justice and moral growth), never another modern text.

**Mark-scheme facts mirror the vetted MSA bank** (`mark-scheme-assessment/banks/tkam.md`), which is
the source of truth: the AQA Modern Text question is marked on **AO1 + AO2 + AO3 only — no AO4/SPaG
on this question** — across **six levels** (Level 1 up to Level 6), each carrying banded AO1, AO2 and
AO3 descriptors. Band vocabulary ("conceptualised", "judicious", "critical", "exploration") and the
level descriptors are reused verbatim from the MSA. The AO split (AO1 12 / AO2 12 / AO3 6, total 30)
follows the sibling vetted banks (the L&R quiz and the generic `modern_text.md` content grid). Only
the EXAMPLES are scoped to *To Kill a Mockingbird*.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — To Kill a Mockingbird)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature Modern Text question (Paper 2, Section A) on To Kill a Mockingbird marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30, across six levels), B) AO2 methods only, C) AO4 SPaG only, D) AO1 argument alone, out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Modern Text question is 30 marks: 12 for argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3), marked across six levels. AO4 (SPaG) is not assessed on this question.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Methods matter greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** SPaG is assessed on some Literature questions, which makes this tempting, but AO4 does not apply to the Modern Text question.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand at 12 marks, yet it shares the 30 with methods (12) and context (6) — never the whole allocation.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Response\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text".
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 strand is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea (prejudice learned and then unlearned), not around Scout's actions or the sequence of events.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact AQA Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating the novel as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA Modern Text question — the lightest of its three assessed strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives Lee's concept — e.g. the segregation of the 1930s South making Tom Robinson's conviction feel inevitable, C) Write a standalone history paragraph on the 1930s before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it where it genuinely drives the concept — the racial injustice of 1930s Alabama makes Maycomb's verdict feel decided before the trial begins — never as a bolt-on paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A standalone history paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must drive the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in To Kill a Mockingbird? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) The mockingbird symbol and specific word connotations, B) Narrative method (Lee's retrospective child narrator — the grown Jean Louise looking back through the young Scout), C) Structure (the mad-dog episode, the trial, the ordering of events), D) Harper Lee's own biography.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Symbol, narrative method and structure are all AO2 targets — but a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the reader (Focus/Feel/Think/Act). Naming "symbol" or "first-person narrator" alone is feature-spotting and scores low. Biography is context about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biography feels relevant because Lee drew on the Alabama of her childhood, but it is contextual information about the writer, not a crafted method the novel deploys.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests Whole-Text Focus\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA Modern Text AO1 asks for a response to "task and whole text", so an essay that analyses only the trial scene in detail risks missing this requirement.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — the argument should range across the novel (Boo Radley, Mrs Dubose, the mad dog, the trial), not rest on one episode however strong.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one memorable scene feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
   * **Question:** AQA asks for a "critical style". In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
   * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novel's faults and what Lee did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and committing to a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal tone, D) Disagreeing with the readings other students have offered.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does not mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** This is the everyday, negative sense of "critical", not the assessment sense.
   * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
   * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Terminology\]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO2 descriptor: "Analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used \[BLANK\]".
   * **Answer:** Judiciously
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO2 is "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously". "Judiciously" warns against feature-spotting: terminology earns marks only when it is selected to serve the analysis of effect.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.
8. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Judicious References\]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "it's a sin to kill a mockingbird." One lists it after a point; the other selects it to pin an argument about the innocent being destroyed by the town's fear. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate breadth of evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It identifies the writer's method using subject terminology.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)". "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards "judicious" selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.
9. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level Ceiling\]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Lee uses the symbol of the mockingbird. This is a technique. It tells us the characters are innocent." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the method works, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the symbol and adding "it tells us they are innocent" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used judiciously.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the symbol makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
10. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO3 Links\]**
    * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO3 descriptor: context should be shown by "specific, detailed \[BLANK\] between context, text and task".
    * **Answer:** Links
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "specific, detailed links between context/text/task". Context earns top marks only when it is connected to the text and the question — e.g. tying 1930s segregation to Tom Robinson's conviction — never when it sits alone as a fact.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "links" — context, text and task must be linked, not listed separately.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Bolt-on Context\]**
    * **Question:** "To Kill a Mockingbird is set in the 1930s, during the Great Depression in Alabama." A student adds this to the end of a paragraph. For AO3, why does it stay low in the AQA bands?
    * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about the period, B) It is bolt-on context — AQA Level 6 AO3 needs "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" where context drives the interpretation, C) It is too brief — a contextual point must run to several sentences to score, D) Context is credited only on the Language paper, not here.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task". A free-standing fact is bolt-on. Context must drive the concept: the segregation and racial injustice of the 1930s South make Tom Robinson's conviction feel inevitable.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why A:** The fact is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** Context is assessed throughout the AQA Modern Text question.
12. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 AO2\]**
    * **Question:** Which statements are true of an AQA Level 6 AO2 response on To Kill a Mockingbird? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) It moves from identifying a method to analysing how it creates meaning, B) It explores several effects the writer's methods have on the reader, C) It names as many techniques as possible to demonstrate range, D) It uses subject terminology judiciously, only where it serves the analysis.
    * **Correct:** A, B, D
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, D. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** AQA Level 6 AO2 is "analysis of the writer's methods... exploration of effects of the writer's methods... subject terminology used judiciously". Analysis, exploration of effects and judicious terminology are all top-band. Maximising technique-count is feature-spotting, which the mark scheme penalises.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why C:** Naming many techniques is feature-spotting — AQA rewards judicious selection and exploration of effect, not quantity.
13. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four openings on To Kill a Mockingbird from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Lee constructs the mockingbird as the emblem of every harmless life that Maycomb's inherited prejudice destroys, using the novel to interrogate how a community teaches itself to fear.", B) "Scout is a girl whose father defends Tom Robinson, and by the end she meets Boo Radley.", C) "Prejudice is a bad thing in the novel, and it causes a lot of unfairness and sadness.", D) "Lee presents prejudice as destructive through Tom Robinson's conviction, making the reader feel how unjust Maycomb has become."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, naming events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing unfairness, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the conviction) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, comment, explained method-and-effect, concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. The jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
14. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Conceptualised Response\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "conceptualised" response. A conceptualised response is one that...
    * **Options:** A) uses difficult vocabulary to signal sophistication, B) is organised around an idea or concept, treating character and plot as evidence for that idea, C) packs in as much historical context as it can, D) is very long and dense with closely-detailed observation.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means built around a concept — e.g. reading the novel as an exploration of how prejudice is learned and then unlearned, with characters and events as evidence. It is about organising ideas, not complexity, length or context-stuffing.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Hard vocabulary is not a concept — conceptualised is about the idea, not the wording.
    * **Why C:** Context supports AO3; it does not make a response conceptualised.
    * **Why D:** Length is not conceptualisation — a short response can be conceptualised.
15. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3 Links\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: AQA Level 6 AO3 rewards "specific, detailed links between context, text and task", not a standalone paragraph of 1930s history.
    * **Answer:** True
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task". Context must drive the concept — e.g. segregation making Maycomb's verdict feel decided before the trial — never sit alone as a fact.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering False assumes context earns marks on its own, but the descriptor demands links between context, text and task.
16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration vs Explanation\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" (Level 4) to "exploration of effects" (Level 6). What does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the reader, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the reader. That shift from a single stated effect to layered, opened-up meaning is the AQA Level 4 to Level 6 climb for AO2.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
17. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Judicious Selection\]**
    * **Question:** Two students support the idea that Atticus embodies moral courage. One quotes six lines; the other selects the single phrase "real courage" to pin the point. The second student's well-judged, selective reference is described by which AQA word? (One word.)
    * **Answer:** Judicious
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and selective — the reference is chosen because it does real argumentative work, not because it is long or abundant. A precise short phrase outperforms six general ones; this is why AQA Level 6 says "judicious use of precise references".
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "thorough" chase quantity, but the AQA word is "judicious" — a wise, selective choice of reference.
18. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**
    * **Question:** A student wants to lift a contextual point about racial injustice into AQA Level 6 AO3. Which approach fits the descriptor?
    * **Options:** A) Open with a separate paragraph of 1930s history before the analysis begins, B) Link the segregated Maycomb courtroom to Lee's idea that law and justice have come apart, so the context drives the reading of Tom Robinson's conviction, C) List several dates and Depression-era facts across the essay, D) Mention that the novel was published in 1960 and leave the point there.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 rewards "specific, detailed links between context/text/task". Tying the segregated courtroom to Lee's idea that law and justice have parted makes context drive the interpretation, not sit beside it.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why A:** A standalone history paragraph is the bolt-on the descriptor discourages.
    * **Why C:** Volume of facts is not rewarded; unlinked context earns little however much is supplied.
    * **Why D:** A publication date is an isolated fact; it makes no link between context and the task.
19. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO3 Chain\]**
    * **Question:** A response argues: "The entrenched segregation of 1930s Alabama drives Lee's idea that Maycomb's verdict is decided before the trial begins, which drives her choice to let the reader hope through Scout while the adult narrator already knows the outcome." Which AQA AO3 strengths does this response demonstrate? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Context is used to drive a concept rather than bolted on as a fact, B) There are specific, detailed links between context, text and task, C) It explores ideas and perspectives opened up by the context, D) It lists a standalone historical fact with no link to text or task.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** This is the AQA Level 6 AO3 chain: context drives concept drives method, with "specific, detailed links between context/text/task" and "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors". Listing a standalone fact is the opposite of what is happening here.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why D:** The response does the reverse of bolt-on — every element is linked.
20. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Examination to Analysis\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Lee's mad-dog episode works. Another does the same but reads the rabid dog as a sustained image of the prejudice loose in Maycomb that Atticus alone will face down, exploring several effects. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines to widen its supporting evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to analysis with exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, C) It deploys rarer, more technical terminology, D) It develops the point across longer paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "examination" becoming "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods". Reading a sustained pattern and exploring multiple effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.

    * **Options:** A) "Lee constructs Atticus as the novel's moral centre, using his defence of Tom Robinson to argue that true courage is conscience acting when defeat is certain.", B) "Atticus is a lawyer who defends Tom Robinson in the trial.", C) "Atticus is a good man, which is an important point about the novel.", D) "Lee presents Atticus as courageous through his decision to defend Tom Robinson, making the reader admire his principle."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A

    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot statement is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement with no method is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung naming a method (the defence) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea, is Level 6. 22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**
    * **Question:** Across how many levels is the AQA Modern Text question (30 marks) marked?
    * **Options:** A) Four levels, B) Five levels, C) Six levels, D) Nine levels.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA marks this 30-mark question across six levels — Level 1 up to Level 6 — each carrying banded AO1, AO2 and AO3 descriptors.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Four levels matches some shorter mark schemes, but the AQA Modern Text question is banded into six.
    * **Why B:** Five levels is an Edexcel pattern; AQA's 30-mark grid runs to six.
    * **Why D:** Nine is the GCSE grade scale, not the number of marking levels.
