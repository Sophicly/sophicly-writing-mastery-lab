# **IGCSE English Language Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: Edexcel IGCSE Spec A (4EA1) Anthology Poetry (per-anthology) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **Edexcel IGCSE English Language Spec A (4EA1)** poetry
anthology. Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder
(`igcse_lang_poetry.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `poetry_anthology.md` for Edexcel IGCSE
Language A lessons so every worked example comes from a 4EA1 anthology poem (never a Literature-spec
or other-board poem).

**Mark-scheme facts are drawn from the vetted Edexcel IGCSE Spec A LANGUAGE (4EA1) MSA grid.** This is
the LANGUAGE specification, so the assessed reading objectives are AO1 (read and understand —
retrieval and comprehension, Questions 1-3), AO2 (analyse language and structure — the single-text
20-mark analysis) and AO3 (compare texts — the 20-mark comparison). CRITICAL: on Spec A LANGUAGE,
AO4 is imaginative WRITING and AO5 is technical accuracy; CONTEXT is NOT an assessment objective on
this paper (context/AO4 belongs to the Edexcel IGCSE Literature specification, a different exam). Each
reading objective is marked across five Levels: AO2 climbs identifies → comments → explains → explores
→ analyses perceptively; AO3 climbs simple → straightforward → developed → thorough → perceptive,
analytical.

Only the poem EXAMPLES are scoped to the five anthology poems: Disabled (Wilfred Owen) · Out, Out—
(Robert Frost) · An Unknown Girl (Moniza Alvi) · The Bright Lights of Sarajevo (Tony Harrison) ·
Still I Rise (Maya Angelou).

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: EDEXCEL IGCSE (4EA1 — Language Anthology)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Structure\]**
   * **Question:** On the Edexcel IGCSE Spec A LANGUAGE (4EA1) reading section, how are the assessment objectives allocated?
   * **Options:** A) AO1 tests reading and understanding (the retrieval questions), AO2 the single-text 20-mark analysis of language and structure, AO3 the 20-mark comparison of two texts, B) AO2 analysis only, C) AO4 context is the highest-weighted reading objective, D) AO5 SPaG is marked across every reading question.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. On the 4EA1 Language paper the reading objectives are AO1 (read and understand — retrieval and comprehension), AO2 (analyse language and structure — the single-text 20-mark question) and AO3 (compare texts — the 20-mark comparison). AO4 (imaginative writing) and AO5 (technical accuracy) belong to the writing section, and context is not assessed here at all.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** AO2 analysis carries heavy marks, but believing it is marked alone ignores the AO1 retrieval questions and the 20-mark AO3 comparison.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to poetry study, yet on this LANGUAGE specification context is not an assessment objective — AO4 here means imaginative writing, not context.
   * **Why D:** Technical accuracy is AO5 and belongs to the writing section, not the reading questions on the anthology poem.

3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 vs AO3 Questions\]**
   * **Question:** On Spec A Language, which reading question is the single-text analysis and which is the comparison?
   * **Options:** A) The single-text 20-mark question tests AO2 (language and structure); the 20-mark comparison tests AO3, B) Both questions test AO1 retrieval, C) The single-text question is marked for context, D) The comparison is marked for spelling and punctuation.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The single-text 20-mark question is AO2 — analysing how one poem's language and structure make meaning. The 20-mark comparison is AO3 — comparing how two texts present an idea. AO1 is the separate retrieval-and-understanding strand.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why B:** AO1 is retrieval and understanding on the short questions; the 20-mark analysis and comparison are AO2 and AO3, not AO1.
   * **Why C:** Context is not assessed on this Language paper at all; the single-text question rewards analysis of language and structure.
   * **Why D:** Spelling and punctuation is AO5 in the writing section, not a mark on the reading comparison.

8. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**

9. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO1 Retrieval\]**
   * **Question:** A retrieval question asks students what the soldier in "Disabled" has lost. Which answer best meets AO1?
   * **Options:** A) "It is a very moving poem about the sadness of war and how badly soldiers were treated.", B) "The soldier has lost his legs — 'Legless, sewn short at elbow' — and an arm, and with them his independence and the notice of the girls he once enjoyed.", C) "Owen uses the metaphor of throwing away his knees to analyse masculine identity.", D) "The poem is written in irregular stanzas that mirror the soldier's broken life."
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO1 rewards accurate reading and understanding: retrieving precisely what the soldier has lost, anchored to the text. A general emotional reaction, a language analysis, or a structural point (both AO2) do not answer the retrieval-and-understanding demand.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** A broad emotional reaction shows no precise understanding of what the text states.
   * **Why C:** Analysing a metaphor is AO2 work, not the AO1 retrieval asked for.
   * **Why D:** A structural observation is AO2; it does not retrieve what the soldier has lost.
10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level 1 Ceiling\]**
    * **Question:** A student writes: "Owen uses the metaphor 'threw away his knees'. This is a metaphor. It tells us the soldier lost his legs in the war." On the Spec A AO2 ladder, which Level does this reach?
    * **Options:** A) Level 5 — perceptive, detailed analysis of the method's effect, B) Level 3 — a clear explanation of how the method shapes the reader's response, C) Level 1 — it identifies a method but only restates the literal meaning, offering no comment on effect, D) Level 4 — it explores the effect of the method thoroughly.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Spec A AO2 ladder runs identifies → comments → explains → explores → analyses perceptively. Naming the metaphor and paraphrasing its literal sense is Level 1 "identifies": a feature is spotted, but no effect is commented on. To climb, the writing must comment on, then explain, then explore, then analyse perceptively how the method works on the reader.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Perceptive, detailed analysis (Level 5) explores layered effects, and none is offered here.
    * **Why B:** A clear explanation (Level 3) requires the effect explained, not the literal meaning restated.
    * **Why D:** Thorough exploration (Level 4) needs the effect worked through; the effect is not even named.
11. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO2\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four comments on Angelou's simile "like dust, I'll rise" and the refrain in "Still I Rise" from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by Edexcel IGCSE Spec A AO2 band (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Angelou's simile analyses how the poem transmutes an image of the trodden-down and worthless into buoyant inevitability, so each return of 'I rise' re-enacts a rising the oppressor cannot prevent.", B) "Angelou uses the simile 'like dust, I'll rise' to make the speaker sound confident.", C) "Angelou's simile 'like dust, I'll rise' clearly explains that although dust is trodden underfoot it still lifts, showing the speaker's refusal to stay down.", D) "Angelou explores the 'like dust' image and the repeated 'I rise' together, tracing in detail how each return of the refrain rebuilds the speaker's defiance."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one Spec A AO2 band higher. The comment naming one simple effect (confident) is Level 2 "comments". The clear explanation of the trodden-yet-rising image is Level 3 "explains". The thorough exploration built across the poem is Level 4 "explores". The perceptive, detailed analysis reading the refrain as a re-enacted rising is Level 5 "analyses perceptively". The ladder is comment → explain → explore → analyse perceptively.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from a simple comment on one effect, to a clear explanation, to a thorough exploration, to a perceptive analysis of how the refrain enacts the rising. The top band is insight, not more quotation.
