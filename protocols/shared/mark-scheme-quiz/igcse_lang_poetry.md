# **IGCSE English Language Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: Edexcel IGCSE Spec A (4EA1) Anthology Poetry (per-anthology) v2.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **Edexcel IGCSE English Language Spec A (4EA1)** poetry
anthology. Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder
(`igcse_lang_poetry.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `poetry_anthology.md` for Edexcel IGCSE
Language A lessons so every worked example comes from a 4EA1 anthology poem.

**v2.0 (2026-09-27) — rewritten against the June 2024 Paper 2 mark scheme (4EA1_02_2406_MS) and
question paper.** v1.0 described Questions 1-3 retrieval, a 20-mark single-text analysis and a 20-mark
AO3 comparison: that paper does not exist. The real paper: Paper 2 "Poetry and Prose Texts and
Imaginative Writing", 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks. The anthology poems are examined in Section A,
Question 1 (30 marks) — "How does the writer present … in <poem>?", two bullet prompts plus "the use of
language and structure", the whole poem printed on the paper. Question 1 is marked AO1 (12 marks, four
levels: 1–3 · 4–6 · 7–9 · 10–12) and AO2 (18 marks, five levels: 1–3 · 4–6 · 7–10 · 11–14 · 15–18).
There is no AO3, no comparison and no retrieval question on this paper. Section B (imaginative writing,
AO4 18 + AO5 12) is covered only where it touches the poems. Descriptor wording is quoted from the mark
scheme; every quotation is checked against the Anthology text.

Poems: Disabled (Wilfred Owen) · Out, Out— (Robert Frost) · An Unknown Girl (Moniza Alvi) · The Bright
Lights of Sarajevo (Tony Harrison) · Still I Rise (Maya Angelou).

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: EDEXCEL IGCSE (4EA1 — Language Anthology)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Question 1 Structure\]**
   * **Question:** On Edexcel IGCSE Spec A Paper 2, where are the anthology poems examined?
   * **Options:** A) In Question 1, a single 30-mark essay on one poem or prose text, B) In three short retrieval questions, C) In a 20-mark comparison of two poems, D) In the Section B writing task only.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Section A is one question, Question 1, on one text from the Anthology (a poem or a prose text). It is worth 30 marks: AO1 12 and AO2 18. There is no retrieval question and no comparison on this paper.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Short retrieval questions belong to Paper 1, not Paper 2.
   * **Why C:** There is no comparison on Paper 2. Question 1 is about one text.
   * **Why D:** Section B is your own imaginative writing. The poems are analysed in Section A.
2. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Split\]**
   * **Question:** How are the 30 marks for Question 1 split?
   * **Options:** A) AO1 12 marks and AO2 18 marks, B) AO2 20 marks and AO3 10 marks, C) AO1 15 marks and AO2 15 marks, D) AO2 30 marks.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO1 (read and understand the text) is worth 12 marks, and AO2 (analyse how the writer uses language and structure) is worth 18. So close analysis of the poem's methods carries the larger share.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why B:** AO3 is not assessed on this paper.
   * **Why C:** The split is not even: AO2 is worth more.
   * **Why D:** AO1 is also assessed, for 12 of the marks.
3. **Type: True/False \[Tests No Comparison\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: On Paper 2 you may be asked to compare two anthology poems for AO3.
   * **Answer:** False
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Paper 2 has no AO3 and no comparison. Question 1 asks about one text, and the paper prints just that one text. Comparison is tested on Paper 1.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Many exams compare poems, but Spec A Paper 2 does not. Revise each poem so you can write a full essay on it alone.
4. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Question Wording\]**
   * **Question:** Which of these is a real Question 1 from a past Paper 2?
   * **Options:** A) "How does the writer create sympathy for the soldier in Disabled?", B) "Compare how poverty is presented in Disabled and one other poem.", C) "List four things the soldier has lost.", D) "Evaluate how far you agree that Disabled is a war poem."
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. That is Question 1 from January 2022. Every Question 1 starts "How does the writer …", then gives two bullet prompts and "the use of language and structure", and ends "You should support your answer with close reference to the poem, including brief quotations."
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why B:** There is no comparison question on this paper.
   * **Why C:** Listing questions are not set on Paper 2.
   * **Why D:** "How far do you agree" is not the Paper 2 question style.
5. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Question Wording\]**
   * **Question:** The last bullet point of Question 1 is always "the use of language and \[BLANK\]". What is the missing word?
   * **Answer:** structure
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "The use of language and structure" is the AO2 part of the question. Structure in a poem includes repetition, line and sentence length, stanza shape, and shifts in time or tense.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** The word is "structure". Language is only half of the bullet; the other half is how the poem is built.
6. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Top Level\]**
   * **Question:** AO2 Level 5 (15–18 marks) asks for "\[BLANK\] analysis of the effects of language and structure". What is the missing word?
   * **Answer:** Perceptive
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 5 is "Perceptive analysis of the effects of language and structure", with "Subtle and discriminating selection of language and structural devices" and "Discriminating and assured use of textual references".
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** The top word is "Perceptive". "Detailed exploration" is Level 4 and "Clear explanations" is Level 3.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Top Level\]**
   * **Question:** AO1 Level 4 (10–12 marks) describes "\[BLANK\] understanding of the text". What is the missing word?
   * **Answer:** Sustained
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO1 runs "Basic" (Level 1), "Some" (Level 2), "Sound" (Level 3) and "Sustained understanding of the text" (Level 4). Sustained means your understanding of the poem holds up across the whole essay.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Level 4 is "Sustained". "Sound" is Level 3.
8. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Levels\]**
   * **Question:** How many levels does the AO2 grid have, and what are the marks?
   * **Options:** A) Five levels: 1–3, 4–6, 7–10, 11–14, 15–18, B) Four levels: 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12, C) Six levels of 3 marks each, D) Nine levels, one for each grade.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO2 has five levels, out of 18. AO1 is the one with four levels (1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12), out of 12.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why B:** That is the AO1 grid, out of 12.
   * **Why C:** The AO2 levels are not all equal: Levels 3, 4 and 5 are four marks wide.
   * **Why D:** Grades 1–9 are not marking levels.
9. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level 1\]**
   * **Question:** A student writes: "Owen says the soldier is 'Legless, sewn short at elbow'. This means he has lost his legs and an arm." Which AO2 level is this?
   * **Options:** A) Level 1 — it picks a quotation but only explains what it means, with no comment on effect, B) Level 3 — clear explanation of the effect, C) Level 4 — detailed exploration of the effect, D) Level 5 — perceptive analysis.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Saying what a line means shows understanding (AO1), but AO2 needs a comment on the effect of the writer's choices. To climb, say what the blunt, clinical words "sewn short" make the reader feel about how the soldier has been treated.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why B:** No effect is explained.
   * **Why C:** Nothing is explored beyond the literal meaning.
   * **Why D:** There is no analysis of language or structure yet.
10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level 3\]**
    * **Question:** A student writes: "The verbs 'snarled and rattled' make the buzz saw sound like an angry animal, which clearly warns the reader that something violent will happen." Which AO2 level fits best?
    * **Options:** A) Level 2 — some developing comment, B) Level 3 — clear explanation of the effect, C) Level 4 — detailed exploration, D) Level 5 — perceptive analysis.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. It chooses relevant words, quotes them accurately and clearly explains their effect. That is Level 3: "Clear explanations of the effects of language and structure". For Level 4 it could follow the image further: the phrase is repeated, "snarled and rattled, snarled and rattled", so the threat keeps building through the poem.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** This is more than a developing comment: the effect is clearly explained.
    * **Why C:** One effect is explained once; it is not yet explored across the poem.
    * **Why D:** It is clear but not yet perceptive.
11. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO2\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these comments on "But still, like dust, I'll rise" in "Still I Rise" from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AO2 level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Angelou turns 'dust', the lowest thing, trodden 'in the very dirt', into proof that she cannot be kept down, and because 'I rise' keeps returning until the poem ends on it repeated, the structure itself rises, so the reader feels her confidence as unstoppable.", B) "Angelou uses a simile to make the speaker sound confident.", C) "The simile 'like dust' clearly shows that even when she is trodden down she will rise again, like dust lifting from the ground.", D) "Angelou explores the dust image in detail: dust is trodden on and ignored, yet it lifts and spreads, and the same idea returns in 'like air, I'll rise', so her rising seems natural and impossible to stop."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The weakest names a simile with a simple comment (Level 2). The next clearly explains how the simile works (Level 3). The third explores the image in detail and links it to another line (Level 4). The strongest connects the image and the repeated refrain into a perceptive analysis of how the poem's structure rises with the speaker (Level 5).
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **WhyWrong:** Each step does more with the same line: a simple comment, a clear explanation, detailed exploration across the poem, then perceptive analysis of language and structure working together.
12. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Level 5\]**
    * **Question:** Which of these phrases come from the AO2 Level 5 descriptor? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Subtle and discriminating selection of language and structural devices, B) Discriminating and assured use of textual references, C) Perceptive analysis of the effects of language and structure, D) Detailed exploration of the effects of language and structure.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Those three phrases make up Level 5. "Detailed exploration of the effects of language and structure" is Level 4.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why D:** "Detailed exploration" is the Level 4 wording, one level below the top.
13. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Structure\]**
    * **Question:** Which of these are structural features you could analyse in the anthology poems? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) The rhyming couplets of "The Bright Lights of Sarajevo", such as "pass" and "gas", B) The shifts between "Now" and "In the old times" in "Disabled", C) The repeated lines "In the evening bazaar" and "an unknown girl / is hennaing my hand" in "An Unknown Girl", D) The year each poet was born.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Rhyme, time shifts and repeated lines are all structure, and the question always asks for "the use of language and structure". The June 2024 mark scheme credits the repeated refrain in "An Unknown Girl" directly. A poet's birth year is a fact about the writer, not a feature of the poem.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why D:** Facts about the poet's life are not structure, and they are not assessed on this paper.
14. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Not Assessed\]**
    * **Question:** A student spends a paragraph on Wilfred Owen's life and the history of the First World War. How does the Paper 2 mark scheme treat this?
    * **Options:** A) It earns little on its own, because the marks are for understanding the poem (AO1) and analysing its language and structure (AO2), B) It earns a separate context mark, C) It is required for Level 5, D) It is marked under AO3.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Paper 2 assesses AO1 and AO2 in Section A. There is no context objective. Background only helps if it is used briefly to explain what the words on the page are doing.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why B:** There is no context mark on this paper.
    * **Why C:** Level 5 needs perceptive analysis of language and structure, not history.
    * **Why D:** AO3 is not assessed on Paper 2.
15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO1 Levels\]**
    * **Question:** An essay on "The Bright Lights of Sarajevo" makes valid points, each with a valid quotation, but never develops them. Which AO1 level fits?
    * **Options:** A) Level 1 (1–3), B) Level 2 (4–6), C) Level 3 (7–9), D) Level 4 (10–12).
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 2 says selection and interpretation "is valid, but not developed". Developing each point, so that the references are "appropriate and relevant to the points being made", moves the answer to Level 3.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Valid points with valid quotations are more than "limited".
    * **Why C:** Level 3 needs the points developed and the references made relevant to them.
    * **Why D:** Level 4 needs detailed references that "fully support" every point.
16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Quotations\]**
    * **Question:** Question 1 ends: "You should support your answer with close reference to the poem, including brief quotations." Which habit fits this best?
    * **Options:** A) Short, well-chosen quotations, each followed by what the words do, B) Copying out whole stanzas, C) No quotations, because the poem is printed on the paper, D) As many quotations as possible in a list.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The question asks for BRIEF quotations, and the top levels reward references that are "discriminating and assured" (AO2) and "detailed, appropriate and fully supports the points being made" (AO1). A few words, well chosen and then analysed, do more than a long copy.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why B:** Copying stanzas shows no selection.
    * **Why C:** The question asks for close reference and quotations.
    * **Why D:** A list of quotations with no analysis stays at the bottom of the AO2 grid.
17. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level 4 vs 5\]**
    * **Question:** Two essays on "Disabled" both explore the line "Passed from him to the strong men that were whole" in detail. What would lift one to Level 5?
    * **Options:** A) A perceptive reading of how the line connects to the whole poem, for example how "whole" sets the strong men against the soldier's broken body and his lost past, B) Quoting the line twice, C) Adding facts about Owen's life, D) Writing a longer paragraph about the same point.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 4 is "Detailed exploration". Level 5 is "Perceptive analysis", seeing what the choice means for the poem as a whole. Length, repetition and background do not change the level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why B:** Repeating a quotation adds nothing.
    * **Why C:** Background is not part of the AO2 grid.
    * **Why D:** Length is not a level descriptor.
18. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Question 1 Bullets\]**
    * **Question:** Question 1 in June 2024 asked: "How does the writer present the importance of traditional Indian culture in An Unknown Girl?" The two content bullets were "how the writer presents the cultural experience with the unknown girl" and "how the writer presents feelings about the place". How should you use the bullets?
    * **Options:** A) Cover both content bullets, and analyse language and structure as you go, B) Choose only one bullet, C) Write about the bullets in a separate essay each, D) Ignore the bullets and write everything you know about the poem.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The bullets tell you what to write about. Covering both keeps your answer focused on the question (AO1), and the third bullet, "the use of language and structure", should run through every paragraph (AO2).
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why B:** The question asks you to write about all of them.
    * **Why C:** It is one essay. Language and structure belong inside every point, not in a separate section.
    * **Why D:** Writing everything you know loses focus on the question, which is what AO1 rewards.
19. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** The AO2 grid sums up each level with a few words. Which words sum up Level 3 (7–10)?
    * **Options:** A) Clear, Relevant, Explanatory, B) Thorough, Confident, Exploratory, C) Discriminating, Perceptive, Analytical, D) Limited, Basic.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO2 key words climb Limited/Basic (Level 1), Some/Developing (Level 2), Clear/Relevant/Explanatory (Level 3), Thorough/Confident/Exploratory (Level 4) and Discriminating/Perceptive/Analytical (Level 5).
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why B:** Thorough, Confident, Exploratory is Level 4.
    * **Why C:** Discriminating, Perceptive, Analytical is Level 5.
    * **Why D:** Limited, Basic is Level 1.
20. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** The AO2 Level 5 descriptor asks for a "discriminating" selection of devices and references. What does "discriminating" mean here?
    * **Options:** A) Well-judged: choosing the most telling words and devices, not the most, B) Unfair to other readers, C) Using every technique in the poem, D) Using long quotations.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme, "discriminating" is praise. It means you chose carefully, picking the few details that matter most and saying why.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why B:** That is the everyday meaning, not the mark-scheme one.
    * **Why C:** Covering everything is the opposite of careful choice.
    * **Why D:** Length is not the point. The question asks for brief quotations.
21. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Section B Link\]**
    * **Question:** In June 2024, Question 1 was on "An Unknown Girl", and Section B Question 2 was "Write about a time when you, or someone you know, decorated something." What does the mark scheme say about the poem and this task?
    * **Options:** A) Responses may "use the poem as inspiration", B) The student must analyse the poem again, C) The student must copy the poem's form, D) The poem cannot be mentioned.
    * **Correct:** A
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Question 2 often links to the Section A text, and the mark scheme lists "use the poem as inspiration" as one way to respond. Section B is marked for AO4 (18) and AO5 (12), not for analysis.
    * **AO:** AO4
    * **Why B:** Analysis belongs to Question 1. Section B is imaginative writing.
    * **Why C:** The form "may be narrative, descriptive or a monologue".
    * **Why D:** The poem may be used for ideas.
22. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Paper Facts\]**
    * **Question:** Which statements about Edexcel IGCSE Spec A Paper 2 are correct? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Question 1 is worth 30 marks: AO1 12 and AO2 18, B) The set poem is printed in full on the paper, C) Section B is imaginative writing, marked AO4 18 and AO5 12, D) Question 1 compares two poems for AO3.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Question 1 is 30 marks (AO1 12 + AO2 18) on one text; the paper says "Remind yourself of …" and prints the whole poem; Section B is imaginative writing, 30 marks (AO4 18 + AO5 12). There is no comparison and no AO3.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why D:** There is no comparison and no AO3 on Paper 2.
