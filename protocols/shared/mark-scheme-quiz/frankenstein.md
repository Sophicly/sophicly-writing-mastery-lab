# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA Frankenstein (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for **Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein*** as the AQA 8702 Paper 1
Section B 19th-century novel. Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug
ladder (`frankenstein.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `19th_century.md` for Frankenstein
lessons so every worked example comes from *Frankenstein* (Victor, the Creature, Walton's framed
narrative, "the modern Prometheus"), never another 19th-century novel.

**Mark-scheme facts are identical to the shared AQA 19th-century-novel grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels of five marks each; AO4/SPaG NOT assessed on the novel) — reused
unchanged from the vetted Frankenstein assessment bank (`mark-scheme-assessment/banks/frankenstein.md`).
Only the worked EXAMPLES are scoped to *Frankenstein*.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Frankenstein)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE 19th-century novel question (Frankenstein) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4/SPaG NOT assessed, B) AO1, AO2 and AO3 at 10 marks each, C) AO1 alone carries all 30 marks, D) AO3 carries the most marks as a "Novel of Ideas".
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The novel response is marked out of 30 across six levels of five marks each: AO1 and AO2 are weighted equally at 12 marks, AO3 (context) carries 6, and AO4/SPaG is not assessed on the novel (it applies only to Shakespeare and the modern text).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** The split is 12/12/6, not 10/10/10, and there is no AO4 tally on the novel.
   * **Why C:** Analysis and context are assessed too; pure knowledge would be plot summary, which never scores highly.
   * **Why D:** Context is the lightest strand at 6 marks, half the weight of argument and analysis.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor. A top-band Frankenstein response is "critical, exploratory and \[BLANK\] in its response to task and whole text."
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 strand is "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, not around Victor's actions or the sequence of events.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** The missing AQA word is "conceptualised" — treating the novel as the vehicle for a concept is what lifts AO1 into Level 6.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level 3\]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Shelley uses the metaphor 'spark of being'. This is a technique. It tells us Victor brings the Creature to life." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the metaphor and adding "it tells us he brings life" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs "clear explanation of the effects"; Level 5 "examination"; Level 6 "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods" with terminology used "judiciously".
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
   * **Why B:** "Examination" (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
   * **Why D:** "Clear explanation" (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO1 Quality\]**
   * **Question:** Which features signal a top-band AQA AO1 response on Frankenstein? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Judicious single-word quotations, B) Treating Victor and the Creature as constructs, C) Retelling the plot in sequence, D) A conceptualised thesis in the introduction.
   * **Correct:** A, B, D
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, D. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Judicious micro-quotes, construct-level thinking, and a conceptual thesis all push you into Level 6. Plot retelling caps you at the lower bands.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why C:** Retelling the plot feels like proving you know the text, but it only shows knowledge of events — examiners reward argument and analysis, not narrative summary.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: For AQA, context such as Galvanism should be integrated into your analysis rather than saved for a standalone history paragraph.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA rewards context as a "driver", not a "bolt-on". Context must drive the concept: Galvanism makes Victor's usurping of life a violation of natural limits, which drives Shelley's choices — never a bolt-on history paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **WhyWrong:** Choosing False usually comes from being taught a separate "context paragraph" at KS3 — that bolt-on habit caps your context marks because it never explains the writer's choices.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**
   * **Question:** "Frankenstein was published in 1818, when Galvanism was widely discussed." A student adds this to the end of a paragraph. For AO3, why does it stay low in the AQA bands?
   * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about the period, B) It is bolt-on context — AQA Level 6 AO3 needs "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" where context drives the interpretation, C) It is too brief — a contextual point must run to several sentences to score, D) Context is credited only on the Language paper, not on this question.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." A free-standing fact is bolt-on. Context must DRIVE the concept: Galvanism makes Victor's usurping of life a violation of natural limits, which drives Shelley's choices.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** The fact is broadly accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
   * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
   * **Why D:** Context is assessed throughout the AQA novel question.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 References\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's AO1 descriptor requires "\[BLANK\] use of precise references" — meaning short, well-judged quotations embedded in your sentence.
   * **Answer:** Judicious
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and precise. Learn micro-quotations (3-4 word phrases heavy with meaning) — a precise reference like the Creature's "misery made me a fiend" tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Students often guess "frequent" or "accurate" — but the descriptor's keyword is judicious, meaning well-judged and precise, because choosing the right quote matters more than quoting often.
8. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level Names\]**
   * **Question:** In the AQA mark scheme, what is the name of Level 4 (16–20 marks)?
   * **Options:** A) Clear understanding, B) Convincing, critical analysis and exploration, C) Thoughtful, developed response, D) Simple, explicit comments.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA names Level 4 "Clear understanding". The ladder climbs from "Supported, relevant comments" (Level 2), to "Clear understanding" (Level 4), "Thoughtful, developed response" (Level 5) and "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" (Level 6).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** This is the name of the very top band (Level 6, 26–30), two levels higher.
   * **Why C:** "Thoughtful, developed response" is Level 5 (21–25), one band above clear understanding.
   * **Why D:** "Simple, explicit comments" is the bottom band (Level 1, 1–5).
9. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 2 Name\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 2 (6–10 marks) is named "Supported, relevant \[BLANK\]".
   * **Answer:** Comments
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 2 is "Supported, relevant comments" — a response relevant and supported by some explanation, identifying deliberate choices with some subject terminology. The ladder climbs to "Clear understanding" (Level 4), "Thoughtful, developed response" (Level 5) and "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" (Level 6).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "response" or "analysis" borrow from higher bands — Level 2 is pitched at the level of supported comment, not the sustained analysis of the upper levels.
10. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: In the AQA 19th-century novel question, AO1 and AO2 each carry 12 marks and AO3 carries 6, for a 30-mark total.
    * **Answer:** True
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The novel response is marked out of 30 across six levels of five marks each, with AO1 and AO2 weighted equally at 12 marks and AO3 (context) carrying half their weight at 6. AO4/SPaG is not assessed on the novel.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering False often comes from assuming context is worth as much as analysis — but AO3 carries 6 marks against 12 apiece for AO1 and AO2, so it is weighted at half.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**
    * **Question:** Across how many levels is the AQA 19th-century novel question marked, and how wide is each?
    * **Options:** A) Four levels of varying width, B) Five levels of six marks each, C) Six levels of five marks each, D) Nine levels, one per GCSE grade.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The novel response is marked out of 30 across six levels of five marks each — Level 1 (1–5) up to Level 6 (26–30) — each carrying banded AO1, AO2 and AO3 descriptors.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Four levels matches some shorter mark schemes, but the AQA novel grid runs to six.
    * **Why B:** Six-mark bands would not divide 30 into whole levels; AQA uses six levels of five marks.
    * **Why D:** Nine is the GCSE grade scale, not the number of marking levels.
12. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 Label\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 6 (26–30) is named "Convincing, critical analysis and \[BLANK\]".
    * **Answer:** Exploration
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The band heading is "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" — a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text, supported by judicious use of precise references.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** "Evaluation" or "comparison" name the skills, but AQA's exact Level 6 heading pairs "analysis" with "exploration".
13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Descriptor Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** Moving up the AQA levels for AO2, the demand shifts from "identification of the writer's methods". Which phrasing marks the Level 6 top?
    * **Options:** A) Awareness of the writer making choices, B) Analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously, C) Explained/relevant comments on the writer's methods, D) Clear explanation of the writer's methods.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 AO2 asks for "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" plus "exploration of effects". The verbs climb: awareness, identification, comment, explanation, and finally analysis and exploration.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** "Awareness of the writer making choices" is the Level 1 wording — the foot of the ladder.
    * **Why C:** "Explained/relevant comments on the writer's methods" is Level 3; comment is a rung below the analysis Level 6 demands.
    * **Why D:** "Clear explanation of the writer's methods" is Level 4 — explanation, not the judicious analysis that defines the top band.
14. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
    * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in Frankenstein? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Language and specific word connotations (the "dull yellow eye"; the Creature's "watery eyes"), B) Form (first-person narration; Walton's framed letters), C) Structure (the pivot from triumph to horror in the creation scene), D) Mary Shelley's biographical timeline.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Language, form and structure are all AO2 methods — the deliberate choices Shelley made. A method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the reader (Focus/Feel/Think/Act); a biographical timeline is bolt-on context about the author, not a method on the page.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why D:** A biographical timeline is background about the author, not a writer's method within the text.
15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** Two students support the idea that the Creature's cruelty is made, not born. One quotes six lines. The other selects the half-line "misery made me a fiend" to pin the point. Whose reference is "judicious" — and what does "judicious" mean?
    * **Options:** A) The first — judicious means marshalling as much supporting evidence as possible, B) The second — judicious means a well-judged, selective reference chosen to do the most argumentative work, C) Both equally — judicious simply means quoting the text accurately and in full, D) Neither — judicious means choosing the longest, most detailed quotation available.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and selective — the reference is chosen because it does real work, not because it is long or abundant. A precise half-line outperforms six general ones. This is why AQA Level 6 says "judicious use of precise references".
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Judicious is about wise selection, not quantity — more can mean less precise.
    * **Why C:** Accuracy alone is not judiciousness; the CHOICE must serve the argument.
    * **Why D:** Length is irrelevant; aptness to the point is what counts.
16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "conceptualised" response. A conceptualised response is one that...
    * **Options:** A) uses difficult, complicated vocabulary to signal sophistication, B) is organised around an idea or concept, treating character and plot as evidence for that idea, C) packs in as much relevant historical context as it can, D) is very long and dense with closely-detailed observation.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means built around a concept — e.g. reading the novel as an exploration of ambition divorced from responsibility, with characters and events as evidence. It is about organising ideas, not complexity, length or context-stuffing.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Hard vocabulary is not a concept — conceptualised is about the idea, not the wording.
    * **Why C:** Context supports AO3; it does not make a response conceptualised.
    * **Why D:** Length is not conceptualisation — a short response can be conceptualised.
17. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA asks for a "critical style". In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novel's faults and the things Shelley did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal and academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
18. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" (Level 4) to "exploration of effects" (Level 6). What does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques in the response, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the reader, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the reader. That shift from a single stated effect to layered, opened-up meaning is the AQA Level 4 to Level 6 climb for AO2.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
19. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO4\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: AO4 (SPaG / technical accuracy) is NOT assessed on the AQA 19th-century novel question.
    * **Answer:** True
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO4/SPaG is not assessed on the novel — it applies only to the Shakespeare and modern-text questions. The novel is marked on AO1 (12), AO2 (12) and AO3 (6) only, for 30 marks.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering False assumes SPaG is marked everywhere, but on the AQA novel question AO4 carries no marks; effort is better spent on argument, analysis and integrated context.
20. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four Frankenstein openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Shelley constructs ambition as a Promethean transgression that severs Victor from the human bonds that might have restrained him, using the novel to interrogate the cost of knowledge pursued without responsibility.", B) "Victor makes a creature, runs away from it, and it kills his family.", C) "Ambition is a dangerous thing in the novel, and it leads to a lot of death and misery.", D) "Shelley presents ambition as destructive through Victor's creation of the Creature, making the reader judge how far the desire to know can corrupt."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a dangerous thing causing death, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the creation) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear explained response). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot, then comment, then explained method-and-effect, then concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
21. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Level 6 Move\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Shelley's fire-and-light motif works. Another reads it as a sustained pattern that makes forbidden knowledge feel both illuminating and scorching, exploring several effects. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more lines to widen the range of its supporting evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to analysis with exploration of the effects of the writer's methods, C) It deploys rarer, more technical terminology across the response, D) It develops the point across longer, more sustained paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "examination" becoming "analysis... exploration of effects of the writer's methods." Reading a sustained pattern and exploring multiple effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.
22. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO3 Links\]**
    * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO3 descriptor: context should be shown by "specific, detailed \[BLANK\] between context, text and task."
    * **Answer:** Links
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "specific, detailed links between context/text/task." Context earns top marks only when it is connected to the text and the question — for instance Galvanism linked to Victor's transgression — never when it sits alone as a fact.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "links" — context, text and task must be linked, not listed separately.
