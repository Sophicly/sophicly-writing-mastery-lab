# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA Never Let Me Go (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 English Literature Paper 2 Section A modern text
Never Let Me Go** (Kazuo Ishiguro). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug
ladder (`never_let_me_go.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for Never Let Me Go
lessons so every worked example comes from Ishiguro's novel (never any other modern text).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused VERBATIM from the vetted AQA Modern Texts grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels; verbatim AQA band descriptors) plus AO4 technical accuracy (4 marks on a
separate strand). Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to Never Let Me Go: Kathy, Tommy, Ruth, Miss Lucy,
Hailsham, the euphemisms "completion" and "donations", "told and not told", the millennial anxiety
about biotechnology, and Ishiguro's themes of mortality, passivity and complicity.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Never Let Me Go)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature modern-text question (Section A, Never Let Me Go) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4 technical accuracy adding 4 marks, B) AO2 methods only, C) AO3 context only, D) AO1 alone out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Never Let Me Go question is a 30-mark level grid — 12 for conceptual argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3) — with a further 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysing Ishiguro's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to a novel steeped in the cloning debate, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand, but AO1 is worth 12 of the 30, not the entire grid.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 demands a critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text — treating the novel as a construct arguing an idea, not a life to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as Ishiguro's euphemisms normalising exploitation, not around what Kathy remembers next.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating Never Let Me Go as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern-text question — the lightest of its three content strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in a Never Let Me Go essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where millennial anxiety about biotechnology genuinely drives Ishiguro's idea — never a bolt-on history paragraph, C) Write a full paragraph on the Human Genome Project before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — a culture able to clone and harvest people is precisely what Ishiguro dramatises through the donors' passivity — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A history paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in Never Let Me Go? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) The retrospective first-person narration filtered through Kathy's calm, euphemistic voice, B) The connotations of specific word choices such as "completion", C) Structural withholding that delays full revelation of the donation programme, D) Kazuo Ishiguro's biography.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Narrative voice, word-level connotation and structural withholding are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices Ishiguro made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the reader (Focus/Feel/Think/Act) — naming "completion" or "the reveal is delayed" alone is feature-spotting. Ishiguro's biography is context about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biography feels relevant because the novel engages the cloning debate, but it is contextual information about the writer, not a crafted method the novel deploys.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO1 Whole Text\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA's modern-text AO1 descriptors ask for a response to "task and whole text", not analysis confined to a single section of Never Let Me Go.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — range across the novel, from Kathy's Hailsham years to completion, to build the argument.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one memorable moment feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6 Heading\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's top band (Level 6) for the modern-text question is headed with which phrase?
   * **Options:** A) "Thoughtful, developed response", B) "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration", C) "Clear understanding", D) "Explained, structured comments".
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 is headed "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration". One band below sits the thoughtful, developed register with apt references integrated into interpretation; "clear understanding" is the Level 4 register.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** "Thoughtful, developed" is the register one band below the top — a developed response with apt references, not exploratory critical analysis.
   * **Why C:** "Clear understanding" is the Level 4 register — clear explanation, not exploration.
   * **Why D:** This resembles lower-band structured-comment language, not the convincing critical analysis of the top band.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO2 summary asks for a "fine-grained and \[BLANK\] analysis of methods supported by judicious use of subject terminology."
   * **Answer:** Insightful
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 6 AO2 summary is a "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods". The pairing warns against feature-spotting: micro-level precision and insight into effect, such as how Ishiguro's flat, understated narration makes the reader supply the horror Kathy suppresses — not a list of devices.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "developed" fit lower bands; the Level 6 word is "insightful" — analysis that perceives effect at a fine-grained level.
8. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 AO1 Features\]**
   * **Question:** Which features push an AQA Never Let Me Go response to Level 6 AO1? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) A response organised around a concept, treating Kathy and the donors as evidence for an idea, B) Judicious, precise references woven into interpretation, C) A sustained critical, exploratory argument across the whole novel, D) A careful retelling of Kathy's memories in the order she narrates them.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** A concept, judicious references and a sustained exploratory argument are all Level 6 AO1 — a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" with "judicious use of precise references". Retelling Kathy's memories in sequence is narrative, capped at clear understanding.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why D:** Following the order of Kathy's recollection feels thorough, but narrating remembered events caps at clear understanding; the top band argues a concept across the whole text.
9. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3\]**

   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 credits "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" — context woven into the argument, such as the era's anxiety about biotechnology driving the reading of the donors' passivity, not a bolt-on history paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **WhyWrong:** It can feel like context earns marks on its own, but the descriptor demands specific, detailed links between context, text and task.
10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA rewards a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novel's faults and what Ishiguro did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal, academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**

13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 4 Register\]**

14. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**

    * **Feedback:** Each linked reading makes context drive interpretation — rationalised cruelty, the believable dystopia, the harvest-culture anxiety. A bare publication date is bolt-on context that earns little; the band rewards context that drives the concept.

15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Ishiguro's understated narration works. Another reads that restraint as a sustained pattern that makes the reader supply the horror Kathy suppresses, exploring several effects. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more of the novel to widen its evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to exploration of the effects of the writer's methods to create meanings, C) It deploys rarer critical terminology, D) It develops the point across longer paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA move into Level 6 AO2 is "examination" becoming "exploration of effects of writer's methods". Reading the restraint as a sustained pattern and exploring how it makes the reader complete the horror is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.
16. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Judicious Vocabulary\]**

    * **AO:** AO1

21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four Never Let Me Go openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Ishiguro constructs the donors' quiet compliance as a mirror held up to the reader's own tolerance of injustice, so the novel's stillness becomes its sharpest accusation.", B) "Kathy narrates her time at Hailsham, then the Cottages, then her years as a carer and the start of her donations.", C) "The novel is quite sad because the characters do not get to live full lives.", D) "Ishiguro presents the clones' acceptance as disturbing, using the quiet donations to make the reader feel how easily cruelty is normalised."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating Kathy's path from Hailsham to donation, is the lowest band. The bare value-judgement about sadness, with no method and no evidence, is a step up. The clear, explained rung that names a method — the quiet donations — and its effect on the reader is Level 4 clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" to "exploration of effects" at the top. Reading Ishiguro's euphemism "completion", what does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the reader, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the reader — reading "completion" as at once comforting the clones and damning the reader is exploration; "it means they die" is explanation. That shift from a single stated effect to layered meaning is the AQA AO2 climb.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
