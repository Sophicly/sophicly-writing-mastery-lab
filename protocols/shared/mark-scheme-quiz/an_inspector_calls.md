# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA An Inspector Calls (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 Modern Text** question (An Inspector Calls, Paper 2
Section A). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder
(`an_inspector_calls.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for An Inspector Calls
lessons so every worked example comes from the play itself (the Birlings, Eva Smith, the Inspector),
never a different modern text.

**Mark-scheme facts match the AQA Modern Text grid** — 30 content marks (AO1 12 / AO2 12 / AO3 6)
banded across six levels, plus 4 AO4 technical-accuracy marks on a separate strand — reused verbatim
from the vetted AQA sources (the `an_inspector_calls` mark-scheme-assessment bank + the generic
`modern_text` quiz). This drill sits BELOW the MSA in difficulty: it teaches the grid, band headings
and descriptor vocabulary; the MSA makes students judge real writing against them. Only the worked
EXAMPLES are scoped to An Inspector Calls.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — An Inspector Calls)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Mark Allocation\]**
   * **Question:** How are the marks allocated on the AQA An Inspector Calls (Modern Text) question?
   * **Options:** A) 30 content marks — AO1 (12), AO2 (12), AO3 (6) — plus 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy, B) 34 marks shared equally across four objectives, C) AO2 alone, marked out of 30, D) AO3 context, marked out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Levels 1–6 grid marks AO1 (12), AO2 (12) and AO3 (6) out of 30, and AO4 (technical accuracy) adds 4 marks on a separate strand — so proofreading earns real marks on this question.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Four equal objectives sounds tidy, but the level grid covers AO1–AO3 out of 30 and technical accuracy sits apart at 4 marks.
   * **Why C:** Methods matter greatly here, yet AO2 is one 12-mark strand of three, never the whole 30.
   * **Why D:** Context is assessed, but AO3 is the lightest strand at just 6 marks — never the full allocation.
2. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Least-Weighted AO\]**
   * **Question:** Of the three content objectives on the An Inspector Calls question — AO1, AO2 and AO3 — which carries the LEAST weight (though it is still essential)?
   * **Options:** A) AO1 (conceptual argument), worth 12 marks, B) AO2 (methods), worth 12 marks, C) AO3 (context), worth 6 marks, D) All three are weighted equally.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA weightings are AO1 (12), AO2 (12) and AO3 (6). Context is the lightest of the three content strands — the fuel, not the main engine — though a conceptualised response still integrates it.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Conceptual argument carries 12 marks, joint top weight, not the least.
   * **Why B:** Methods also carry 12 marks, equal with the argument strand.
   * **Why D:** The three are not equal — context at 6 marks is half the weight of the other two.
3. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 Label\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's top band, Level 6, is headed "Convincing, critical analysis and \[BLANK\]".
   * **Answer:** Exploration
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 6 heading is "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration". Level 5 is "Thoughtful, developed consideration" and Level 4 is "Clear understanding".
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** "Evaluation" or "comparison" name the skills, but AQA's exact Level 6 heading pairs "analysis" with "exploration".
4. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 AO1\]**
   * **Question:** The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor asks for a "critical, exploratory and \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text".
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 strand is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea about the play, not around the order of the Birlings' confessions.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** The missing AQA word is "conceptualised" — treating the play as the vehicle for a concept is what lifts AO1 into Level 6.
5. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Conceptualised Response\]**
   * **Question:** A "conceptualised" response to An Inspector Calls (the AQA Level 6 AO1 marker) is one that…
   * **Options:** A) uses difficult, complicated vocabulary to signal sophistication, B) is organised around an idea — reading the play as Priestley's argument about collective responsibility, with the Birlings and the plot as evidence, C) packs in as much historical context about 1912 and 1945 as it can, D) is very long and dense with closely-detailed observation.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means built around a concept — reading the play as Priestley's indictment of a capitalism that prices human life, with the Birlings and the plot as evidence for that idea. It is about organising ideas, not complexity, length or context-stuffing.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** Hard vocabulary is not a concept — conceptualised is about the idea, not the wording.
   * **Why C:** Context supports AO3; it does not by itself make a response conceptualised.
   * **Why D:** Length is not conceptualisation — a short response can be conceptualised.
6. **Type: True/False \[Tests Whole Text\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA's An Inspector Calls AO1 descriptors ask for a response to "task and whole text", not analysis confined to a single scene.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 wording is "response to task and whole text". Track your concept — collective responsibility — across the whole play, from Birling's opening complacency to the Inspector's exit, not one interrogation in isolation.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Answering False assumes close focus on one scene is enough, but the band explicitly rewards a response to the whole text.
7. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level Naming\]**
   * **Question:** A response with clear, plot-level understanding of An Inspector Calls — but no conceptualised argument — sits at which AQA band, headed by which phrase?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6, "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration", B) Level 5, "Thoughtful, developed consideration", C) Level 4, "Clear understanding", D) Level 1, "Simple, undeveloped comment".
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA heads Level 4 "Clear understanding" — the commonest way students undersell the top band to themselves. Understanding the play clearly is real, but Level 6 additionally demands a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response… with judicious use of precise references".
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** The top band is far more than understanding — it is a conceptualised, exploratory argument.
   * **Why B:** "Thoughtful, developed" (Level 5) needs development and integrated references beyond clear understanding.
   * **Why D:** Clear understanding sits well above a simple, undeveloped comment.
8. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Judicious References\]**
   * **Question:** Two responses both quote "we are members of one body". One lists it after a point about the Inspector. The other selects it to pin an argument that Priestley sets communal duty against Birling's individualism. Which AQA phrase distinguishes the second?
   * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to demonstrate the breadth of its evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation" rather than general reference, C) It gives a clearer explanation of the quotation's effect on the audience, D) It identifies the writer's method using subject terminology.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretation(s)". "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed — a precise phrase tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
   * **Why C:** Explaining the effect is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is SELECTED to support the interpretation.
   * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.
9. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary: Critical\]**
   * **Question:** AQA asks for a "critical" response to An Inspector Calls. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
   * **Options:** A) Pointing out the play's faults and what Priestley did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical analysis and exploration": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** This is the everyday, negative sense of "critical", not the assessment sense.
   * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
   * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary: Judicious\]**
    * **Question:** Supporting the idea that the Inspector overrides Mr Birling's self-justification, one student quotes six lines; another selects the single phrase "we are members of one body". Whose reference is "judicious" — and what does "judicious" mean?
    * **Options:** A) The first — judicious means marshalling as much evidence as possible, B) The second — judicious means a well-judged, selective reference chosen to do the most argumentative work, C) Both equally — judicious just means quoting accurately, D) Neither — judicious means choosing the longest quotation available.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and selective — the reference is chosen because it does real work, not because it is long or abundant. A precise phrase outperforms six general lines, which is why AQA Level 6 says "judicious use of precise references".
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Judicious is about wise selection, not quantity — more can mean less precise.
    * **Why C:** Accuracy alone is not judiciousness; the CHOICE must serve the argument.
    * **Why D:** Length is irrelevant; aptness to the point is what counts.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Ceiling\]**
    * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Priestley uses a stage direction, 'pink and intimate'. This is a technique. It tells us the Birlings are comfortable." Which AO2 Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
    * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis exploring the effects of the writer's methods, B) Level 5 — examination of how the method works, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the stage direction and adding "it tells us they are comfortable" is AQA Level 3 AO2: "identification of the writer's methods" with only a general comment. Level 4 needs the effect clearly explained; Level 5 is examination; Level 6 is "exploration of effects of the writer's methods to create meanings".
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** There is no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
    * **Why B:** Examination (Level 5) requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
    * **Why D:** Clear explanation (Level 4) needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
12. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 AO2\]**
    * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO2 rewards a "fine-grained and \[BLANK\] analysis of methods supported by judicious use of subject terminology".
    * **Answer:** Insightful
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA's Level 6 AO2 summary is "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods". "Insightful" warns against feature-spotting — micro-level precision that opens up meaning, not a list of dramatic devices.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "insightful" — paired with "fine-grained", it marks the top-band quality of method analysis.
13. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 AO2\]**
    * **Question:** Which statements are true of an AQA Level 6 AO2 response on An Inspector Calls? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) It moves from identifying a method to analysing how it creates meaning, B) It explores several effects the writer's methods have on the audience, C) It names as many dramatic techniques as possible to demonstrate range, D) It uses subject terminology judiciously, only where it serves the analysis.
    * **Correct:** A, B, D
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, D. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** AQA Level 6 AO2 is "exploration of effects of the writer's methods to create meanings… subject terminology used judiciously". Analysis, exploration of several effects and judicious terminology are all top-band. Maximising the technique-count is feature-spotting, which the mark scheme penalises.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why C:** Naming many dramatic techniques is feature-spotting — AQA rewards judicious selection and exploration of effect, not quantity.
14. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** AQA's AO2 moves from "examination of the writer's methods" (Level 5) to "exploration of effects of the writer's methods" (Level 6). What does "exploration" add?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of dramatic techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the audience, rather than working through a single fixed effect, C) It works through the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Examination" works through how one method makes meaning; "exploration" opens up several possible effects and how they work on the audience. That shift from a worked-through effect to layered, opened-up meaning is the Level 5 to Level 6 climb for AO2.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
15. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO2\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these AO2 comments on Priestley's lighting stage directions from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Priestley's shift from 'pink and intimate' to 'brighter and harder' lighting explores how the family's flattering self-image is stripped away, the harsher glare drawing the audience into the same scrutiny.", B) "Priestley uses stage directions about the lighting.", C) "Priestley's 'pink and intimate' lighting examines how the Birlings' comfort is a rose-tinted illusion the interrogation will dismantle.", D) "Priestley's 'pink and intimate' lighting makes the Birlings' home seem cosy and comfortable."
    * **Correct:** B, D, C, A
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA AO2 band higher. The bare identification of a method, with no effect, is Level 3. The clear explanation of one simple effect (cosy and comfortable) is Level 4. The sustained examination of the comfort as illusion is Level 5. The analysis exploring layered effects, tying the lighting shift to the audience's implication, is Level 6.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from identifying the lighting, to explaining one effect, to examining how the method works, to exploring several effects. Level 6 explores effects, not a single stated one.
16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO3 Bolt-on\]**
    * **Question:** "Priestley wrote the play in 1945 but set it in 1912." A student tacks this on to the end of a paragraph. For AO3, why does it stay low in the AQA bands?
    * **Options:** A) It is factually inaccurate about the play's dates, B) It is bolt-on context — Level 6 AO3 needs "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" where context drives the interpretation, C) It is too brief — a contextual point must run to several sentences to score, D) Context is not assessed on the Modern Text question.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 rewards "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task". A free-standing date is bolt-on; context must DRIVE the concept — the 1912-for-1945 gap lets the audience feel the dramatic irony that fuels Priestley's warning.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why A:** The dates are accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the issue; a short linked point can be top-band.
    * **Why D:** Context is assessed on this question, worth 6 marks.
17. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 AO3\]**
    * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO3 asks for context shown by "specific, detailed \[BLANK\] between context, text and task".
    * **Answer:** Links
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "specific, detailed links between context/text/task". Context earns top marks only when it is connected to the text and the question, never when it sits alone as a fact.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "links" — context, text and task must be linked, not listed separately.
18. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO3 Chain\]**
    * **Question:** A response argues: "The 1912 setting, staged for a 1945 audience who had endured two wars, drives Priestley's idea that capitalist complacency makes catastrophe inevitable, which drives his choice to have the Inspector foretell 'fire and blood and anguish'." Which AQA AO3 strengths does this show? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Context is used to drive a concept rather than bolted on as a fact, B) There are specific, detailed links between context, text and task, C) It explores ideas and perspectives opened up by the context, D) It lists a standalone historical date with no link to text or task.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** This is the AQA Level 6 AO3 chain: context drives concept drives method, with "specific, detailed links between context/text/task" and "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors". Listing a standalone date is the opposite of what happens here.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why D:** The response does the reverse of bolt-on — every element is linked to text and task.
19. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO4 Separate Strand\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: On the AQA An Inspector Calls question, technical accuracy (AO4/SPaG) is marked on a separate strand, not folded into the AO1, AO2 and AO3 content bands.
    * **Answer:** True
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Levels 1–6 grid marks AO1, AO2 and AO3 out of 30; AO4 technical accuracy sits apart, adding 4 marks for accurate spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures. Proofreading earns real marks.
    * **AO:** AO4
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering False assumes SPaG is baked into the content bands, but AQA marks technical accuracy on its own strand.
20. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four openings on An Inspector Calls from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Priestley constructs the Inspector as the dramatised voice of collective conscience, using the play to interrogate the moral bankruptcy of a capitalism that prices human life.", B) "The Inspector arrives at the Birlings' house and questions each of them about a girl who has died.", C) "Responsibility is an important idea in the play, and it causes a lot of arguments in the family.", D) "Priestley presents the Inspector as a moral force, using his methodical interrogation to make the audience question their own conscience."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. Narrating the Inspector's arrival and questions is Level 1 simple comment. The bare value-judgement that responsibility matters and causes arguments, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 supported comment. The clear rung naming a method (the methodical interrogation) and its effect on the audience is Level 4 clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. The jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
21. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6 Lift\]**
    * **Question:** A response retells how Sheila, Eric and their parents each confess their treatment of Eva Smith, showing clear understanding of the plot. What single move would lift it toward Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) Adding more quotations to prove wider reading, B) A critical, conceptualised argument — reading the confessions as Priestley's case for collective responsibility — with judicious use of precise references, C) Retelling the confessions in more detail, D) A longer paragraph on 1912 as a historical period.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 AO1 demands a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response… judicious use of precise references". Reading the chain of confessions as Priestley's argument that everyone is implicated — not narrating who did what — is the move from clear understanding to the top band.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** More quotations chase quantity; the band rewards judicious, precisely chosen references.
    * **Why C:** More plot detail stays at the "clear understanding" register — it does not add a concept.
    * **Why D:** A longer context paragraph inflates the lightest strand and cannot buy the argument Level 6 turns on.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**
    * **Question:** Across how many levels is the AQA An Inspector Calls (Modern Text) response marked?
    * **Options:** A) Four levels, B) Five levels, C) Six levels, D) Nine levels.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA marks this question across six levels — Level 1 (simple comment) up to Level 6 ("Convincing, critical analysis and exploration") — each carrying banded AO1, AO2 and AO3 descriptors.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Four levels matches some shorter mark schemes, but the AQA modern-text grid runs to six.
    * **Why B:** Five levels is another board's pattern; AQA's grid runs to six.
    * **Why D:** Nine is the GCSE grade scale, not the number of marking levels.
23. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Band Boundary\]**
    * **Question:** On the AQA Modern Text 30-mark grid, which mark range is Level 6, the top band?
    * **Options:** A) 16–20, B) 21–25, C) 26–30, D) 11–15.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 ("Convincing, critical analysis and exploration") spans 26–30 of the 30 content marks; Level 5 ("Thoughtful, developed consideration") sits below at 21–25 and Level 4 ("Clear understanding") at 16–20.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** 16–20 is Level 4 ("Clear understanding"), two bands below.
    * **Why B:** 21–25 is Level 5 ("Thoughtful, developed consideration").
    * **Why D:** 11–15 is Level 3, lower still.
