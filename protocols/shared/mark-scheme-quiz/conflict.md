# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: OCR Conflict (per-cluster) v1.0**

Per-CLUSTER mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **OCR GCSE (J352) Poetry Across Time — Conflict** cluster.
Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder (`conflict.md`) — this bank
REPLACES the generic `poetry_anthology.md` for Conflict lessons so every worked example comes from a
Conflict-cluster poem (never a Love & Relationships / Youth & Age poem).

**Mark-scheme facts follow the OCR (J352) Poetry Across Time grid** — Part (a), the comparison of the
two printed poems, is AO2-dominant with AO1 secondary (total 20); Part (b), "Explore in detail one
other poem from your anthology", balances AO1 and AO2 equally (total 20). The response is marked
holistically, with the overall mark indicated at the end, across six levels — Level 1 (1–3) up to
Level 6 (18–20). AO3 (context) and AO4 (SPaG) are not assessed on this poetry question. This is the
EASIER drill — the harder examiner-level judging lives in the matching mark-scheme-ASSESSMENT bank.
Only the poem EXAMPLES are scoped to the 5 Conflict poems: Papa-T · Songs for the People · We Lived
Happily during the War · Colonization in Reverse · Thirteen.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth 2
marks each.

### **SECTION A: OCR (J352 — Conflict)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Part (a) AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** In OCR Poetry Across Time Part (a) — the comparison of the two printed Conflict poems — how are marks weighted?
   * **Options:** A) AO2 dominant, AO1 secondary (total 20), B) AO1 only, C) AO3 dominant, with context carrying most of the marks, D) AO4 (SPaG) forms one of the scored criteria.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. OCR Part (a) is AO2-dominant with AO1 secondary, out of 20 — the marks reward methods analysis of both poems, carried by an underlying conceptual argument.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** The comparison feels like pure argument, but AO1 is only the secondary strand in Part (a) — the dominant weighting rewards methods analysis of both poems.
   * **Why C:** Context feels like a natural part of poetry essays, but AO3 is not assessed in OCR Part (a) at all.
   * **Why D:** SPaG tempts because it appears elsewhere in Literature, but AO4 plays no part in this poetry question.
2. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Part (b) AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** In OCR Part (b) — exploring one other single poem from your Conflict anthology — how are marks weighted?
   * **Options:** A) AO1 = AO2 equal (total 20), B) AO2 only, C) AO1 only, D) AO3 only.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (b) balances AO1 (argument) and AO2 (methods) equally, out of 20. Both must be strong.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** Methods dominate Part (a), so carrying that weighting across is tempting, but in Part (b) AO2 shares the marks equally with AO1.
   * **Why C:** A single-poem exploration feels like pure argument, but Part (b) credits argument and methods equally — analysis of language, form and structure carries half the marks.
   * **Why D:** Context seems a natural addition to any poetry essay, yet AO3 is not assessed in Part (b) either.
3. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO Distinction\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: OCR Part (a) and Part (b) assess the same AO weightings.
   * **Answer:** False
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (a) = AO2 dominant + AO1 secondary. Part (b) = AO1 + AO2 equal. They are different tasks with different weightings — plan accordingly.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **WhyWrong:** Answering True assumes one rubric covers the whole question, but the parts differ — the Part (a) comparison is methods-dominant while the Part (b) single-poem exploration balances argument and methods equally.
4. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Rule\]**
   * **Question:** Comparing We Lived Happily during the War with the other printed poem in Part (a), a student writes a full paragraph of historical background. What does this earn?
   * **Options:** A) Nothing — AO3 (context) is not assessed in Part (a), so time spent there earns no marks, B) Full AO3 credit, C) Bonus marks for wider reading, D) It substitutes for the AO2 methods analysis.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is not assessed on OCR Poetry Across Time. Historical background, however accurate, earns nothing — the marks live in close analysis of language, form and structure, argued comparatively.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** There is no AO3 strand in this question's grid, so there is no AO3 credit to earn.
   * **Why C:** The level descriptors judge quality of analysis and comparison; no bonus exists for material outside the assessed objectives.
   * **Why D:** Background cannot stand in for methods — AO2 is the dominant objective in Part (a) and demands analysis of language, form and structure.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests SPaG Rule\]**

   * **Answer:** False
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO4 plays no part in this poetry question — spelling is not assessed here, though a sustained critical style still demands controlled, formal writing.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **WhyWrong:** Answering True imports AO4 from elsewhere in Literature, but SPaG is not assessed on this poetry question.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Holistic Marking\]**
   * **Question:** How is OCR J352/02 Section A (Poetry across time) marked?
   * **Options:** A) Holistically, with one overall mark indicated at the end of the response, B) One mark per line, C) A separate sub-mark totalled per paragraph, D) Purely by word count.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The scheme states "the response is to be marked holistically" with examiners indicating the overall mark at the end.
   * **AO:** Vocabulary
   * **Why B:** Line-by-line tallying is not how a holistic level-based grid works.
   * **Why C:** No per-paragraph sub-marks are totted up — the examiner places the whole response on the level grid.
   * **Why D:** Length is never the measure — the level descriptors judge quality.

10. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Interwoven Comparison\]**

16. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Sustained Critical Style\]**

18. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Part (a) Focus\]**
    * **Question:** In Part (a), with Thirteen as one of the printed poems, where should most of your analytical energy go?
    * **Options:** A) A biography of Caleb Femi, B) Paragraphs of social context, C) Close analysis of language, form and structure — how the second-person address ("you") and the extended metaphor of "dying stars / on the verge of becoming black holes" work on the reader, D) Plot summary of the police stop.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (a) is AO2-dominant. Close, word-level methods analysis — the second-person address, the star metaphor — beats any other strategy.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** The poet's life seems illuminating, but biography is neither a method nor an assessed objective on this question.
    * **Why B:** Context paragraphs feel scholarly, yet AO3 is not assessed in Part (a) — time spent there earns no marks.
    * **Why D:** Retelling the poem feels safe, but plot summary demonstrates comprehension only and earns very little on a methods-dominant task.
19. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Top-Band Comparison\]**
    * **Question:** For the OCR Part (a) comparison of two Conflict poems, a top-band response: (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Interweaves both poems in every paragraph, B) Sustains conceptual argument across the comparison, C) Uses precise, pertinent quotations skilfully interwoven from both, D) Treats the first poem in full, then the second in full.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Interwoven comparison, sustained argument, and skilfully interwoven evidence define Level 6. Rigid block treatment caps lower. The interweaving is only the mechanic — Level 6 needs it to carry a comparison of the two poets' METHODS and their DIFFERING effects on the reader (e.g. Bennett's comic patois makes us relish empire "tun history upside dung!"; Femi's second-person address makes us stand where the boy stands).
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why D:** Rigid blocks feel structured, but separating the poems prevents the interwoven comparison OCR's top level explicitly requires.
20. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** Which sentence signals OCR Level 6 critical style?
    * **Options:** A) "Kaminsky uses repetition", B) "Kaminsky's fractured repetition — 'invisible house by invisible house by invisible house' — numbs the line into the very complacency it confesses, so the reader enacts the looking-away the poem indicts", C) "The poem is powerful", D) "Kaminsky wrote about war".
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Option B evaluates, conceptualises (the form enacting the guilt it confesses), and sustains sophisticated analysis of a method's effect. That's Level 6.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming the technique is accurate terminology, yet identification alone earns little — Level 6 needs the method's effect analysed and judged.
    * **Why C:** A verdict like "the poem is powerful" sounds evaluative, but it offers judgement with no method, evidence or reasoning behind it.
    * **Why D:** A bare factual statement about the poet describes nothing in the text — it is the flattest possible description, not critical style.

    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Part (b) is a single-poem exploration ("Explore in detail one other poem from your anthology"); only Part (a) requires comparison.

22. **Type: Ranking \[Tests Comparison Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four comparative sentences on Thirteen and We Lived Happily during the War from WEAKEST to STRONGEST (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Both poems are about conflict.", B) "Both poets show how conflict harms ordinary people, and both use repetition to do it.", C) "Femi's extended metaphor of 'dying stars / on the verge of becoming black holes' presents a child's potential being extinguished, while Kaminsky's repeated 'invisible house' presents disaster kept at a comfortable distance.", D) "Where Femi's second-person address corners the reader alongside a boy 'four minutes from home', Kaminsky's plural 'we (forgive us)' implicates the reader in the very looking-away that lets such harm continue — one poem makes us conflict's target, the other its comfortable bystander."
    * **Correct:** A, B, C, D
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung climbs the OCR grid. A bare shared subject is the weakest, a basic response. Naming a shared idea and technique makes "some explicit, relevant comparisons between texts" but stays descriptive. Comparing named methods with integrated quotation from both poems is stronger. The top rung interweaves both poems in one argued claim, comparing the two poets' methods AND their differing effects on the reader in an evaluative, sustained critical style — the interwoven comparison Level 6 requires.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from a bare shared subject, to a named shared idea with no analysis, to methods compared with integrated quotation, to an interwoven claim arguing how the two poets' different methods produce different effects on the reader.
