# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA Leave Taking (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 English Literature Paper 2 Section A modern text
Leave Taking** (Winsome Pinnock). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug
ladder (`leave_taking.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for Leave Taking lessons
so every worked example comes from Pinnock's play (never any other modern text).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused VERBATIM from the vetted AQA Modern Texts grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels; verbatim AQA band descriptors) plus AO4 technical accuracy (4 marks on a
separate strand). Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to Leave Taking: Enid, Del, Viv, Brod, Mai the obeah
woman and healer, Enid's wish that her daughters "have a better life than me", the Windrush
generation's precarious welcome in Britain, and Pinnock's themes of migration, belonging and
identity.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Leave Taking)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature modern-text question (Section A, Leave Taking) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4 technical accuracy adding 4 marks, B) AO2 methods only, C) AO3 context only, D) AO1 alone out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Leave Taking question is a 30-mark level grid — 12 for conceptual argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3) — with a further 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysing Pinnock's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to a play about the Windrush generation, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand, but AO1 is worth 12 of the 30, not the entire grid.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 (26–30) demands a critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text — treating the play as a construct arguing an idea, not a story to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as what Pinnock argues about home and identity, not around what happens to Enid.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating Leave Taking as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern-text question — the lightest of its three content strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in a Leave Taking essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where the Windrush generation's experience genuinely drives Pinnock's idea — never a bolt-on history paragraph, C) Write a full paragraph of post-war migration history before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — the Windrush generation's promise of belonging, then its betrayal, driving Enid's guarded hope — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A history paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in Leave Taking? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Dramatic form, such as stage directions and the shape of a scene, B) The connotations of specific word choices, C) Structural shifts, such as Brod's recurring stories of Jamaica running through the play, D) Winsome Pinnock's biography.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Dramatic form, word-level connotation and structural shifts are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices Pinnock made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the audience (Focus/Feel/Think/Act) — naming "a stage direction" or "Brod's stories recur" alone is feature-spotting. Pinnock's biography is context about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biography feels relevant because Pinnock drew on the migrant experience, but it is contextual information about the writer, not a crafted method the play deploys.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO1 Whole Text\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA's modern-text AO1 descriptors ask for a response to "task and whole text", not analysis confined to a single scene of Leave Taking.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — range across the play, from Enid's guarded silence about Jamaica to Del's move to Mai the healer, to build the argument. A superb analysis of the single scene where Viv walks out of her exam still caps if it never connects to the rest of the play.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one memorable moment feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6 Heading\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's top band (Level 6, 26–30) for the modern-text question is headed with which phrase?
   * **Options:** A) "Thoughtful, developed consideration", B) "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration", C) "Clear understanding", D) "Explained, structured comments".
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 (26–30) is headed "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration". Level 5 is "Thoughtful, developed consideration"; Level 4 is "Clear understanding".
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** "Thoughtful, developed consideration" is the Level 5 heading, one band below the exploratory top.
   * **Why C:** "Clear understanding" is the Level 4 register — clear explanation, not exploration.
   * **Why D:** This resembles lower-band structured-comment language, not the convincing critical analysis of the top band.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO2 summary asks for a "fine-grained and \[BLANK\] analysis of methods supported by judicious use of subject terminology."
   * **Answer:** Insightful
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 6 AO2 summary is a "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods". The pairing warns against feature-spotting: micro-level precision and insight into effect, such as how a single stage direction makes the audience feel the toll Enid's relentless work takes on her — not a list of devices.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "developed" fit lower bands; the Level 6 word is "insightful" — analysis that perceives effect at a fine-grained level.

10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA rewards a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the play's faults and what Pinnock did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal, academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.

15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Pinnock uses Brod's stories of Jamaica. Another reads them as a sustained motif that makes the ache of the lost homeland feel permanent, exploring several effects. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more of Brod's lines to widen its evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to exploration of the effects of the writer's methods to create meanings, C) It deploys rarer critical terminology, D) It develops the point across longer paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "examination" becoming "exploration of effects of the writer's methods to create meanings". Reading Brod's stories as a sustained motif and exploring multiple effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.
16. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Judicious Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards "\[BLANK\] use of precise references to support interpretation" — a well-judged, selective quotation such as Enid's wish that her daughters "have a better life than me", chosen to do argumentative work. (One word.)
    * **Answer:** Judicious
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and selective — the reference is chosen because it does real work, not because it is long or abundant. A precise phrase like "have a better life than me", pinning an argument that Enid measures her whole worth by her children's advancement, outperforms a whole paragraph of general reference — which is why AQA Level 6 says "judicious use of precise references".
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "frequent" chase quantity, but the AQA word is "judicious" — wise, selective choice of evidence.

21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four Leave Taking openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Pinnock constructs Enid as the embodiment of a migrant generation's bargain — belonging purchased at the price of the self — using her silence about Jamaica to interrogate what assimilation costs.", B) "Enid leaves Jamaica, comes to London, works cleaning jobs and brings up Del and Viv.", C) "Enid works too hard and this is sad and causes problems in the family.", D) "Pinnock presents Enid's relentless work as self-erasing, using her exhaustion to make the audience feel the toll migration takes on her."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating Enid's arrival and jobs only, is the lowest band. The bare value-judgement about her working too hard, with no method and no evidence, is a step up. The clear, explained rung that names a method — her exhaustion — and its effect on the audience is Level 4 clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus audience effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" to "exploration of effects" at the top. Reading Pinnock's use of Mai's readings, what does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the audience, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the audience — reading Mai's readings as a living thread of belonging, the play's argument that identity survives migration only when its roots are carried across the water. That shift from a single stated effect to layered meaning is the AQA AO2 climb.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
