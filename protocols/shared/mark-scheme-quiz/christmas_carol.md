# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA A Christmas Carol (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702** 19th-century novel *A Christmas Carol* (Paper 1
Section B). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder
(`christmas_carol.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `19th_century.md` for A Christmas Carol
lessons so every worked example comes from *A Christmas Carol* (never any other novel or play).

**Mark-scheme facts are identical to the shared AQA 19th-century-novel grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels of five marks; NO AO4 — SPaG is assessed only on the Shakespeare or
modern-text answer) — reused unchanged from the vetted A Christmas Carol assessment bank. Only the
EXAMPLES are scoped to the novella: Scrooge · Marley's ghost · the three spirits · Bob Cratchit ·
Tiny Tim · Fezziwig · Fred · Ignorance and Want · the fog-and-cold imagery · the redemption arc.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — A Christmas Carol)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA 19th-century novel question (A Christmas Carol) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), no AO4, B) AO2 only, C) AO1=15, AO2=15, D) AO1, AO2 and AO3 each carry 10.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The novel response is marked out of 30, with AO1 and AO2 weighted equally at 12 marks each and AO3 (context) carrying half their weight at 6. There is no AO4 on the novel — SPaG is assessed only on the Shakespeare or modern-text answer.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysis of Dickens's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument (AO1) and the 6 for context (AO3).
   * **Why C:** AO1 and AO2 do carry the heavier weight, but they are 12 each — not 15 — and this drops AO3 context entirely.
   * **Why D:** An even 10/10/10 split feels tidy, but AQA weights context at half: the split is 12, 12 and 6.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor. A top-band response to A Christmas Carol is "critical, exploratory and \[BLANK\] in its response to task and whole text."
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 strand is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea (Dickens arguing for social responsibility), not around Scrooge's actions or the sequence of staves.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "thoughtful" describe lower bands — "thoughtful" is the Level 5 keyword; the decisive Level 6 word is conceptualised.
3. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO4 / SPaG\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: On the AQA A Christmas Carol question, marks are awarded for spelling, punctuation and grammar (AO4).
   * **Answer:** False
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. There is no AO4 on the novel — SPaG is assessed only on the Shakespeare or modern-text answer. The Christmas Carol question rewards AO1, AO2 and AO3 alone.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Answering True usually comes from assuming SPaG counts everywhere, but AQA assesses technical accuracy only on the Shakespeare/modern answer, never on the 19th-century novel.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in A Christmas Carol? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Language and specific word choices (the adjective "wolfish"; "decrease the surplus population"), B) Form (the novella's five-stave structure), C) Structure (the fog opening, the shifts between spirits, the reformed ending), D) Dickens's own biography.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Language, form and structure are all AO2 targets. But a method only counts as AO2 once you pair it with its effect on the reader — naming "the five-stave form" alone is feature-spotting; you must say what the method makes the reader focus on, feel, think or do. Dickens's biography is context about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biography feels relevant because Dickens drew on his own childhood poverty, but it is contextual information about the writer, not a method he deploys on the page.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: For AQA, context should be integrated into your analysis rather than saved for a standalone history paragraph.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." A free-standing date is bolt-on; the 1834 Poor Law and Malthusian economics earn marks only when they drive Dickens's choices — his "wolfish" Ignorance and Want, Scrooge's coldness.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **WhyWrong:** Choosing False usually comes from a KS3 habit of a separate "context paragraph" — but bolt-on background caps your AO3 marks because it never explains the writer's choices.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**
   * **Question:** Across how many levels is the AQA 19th-century novel question marked?
   * **Options:** A) Four levels, B) Five levels, C) Six levels, D) Nine levels.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The novel is marked out of 30 across six levels of five marks each, from Level 1 (Simple, explicit comments) up to Level 6 (Convincing, critical analysis and exploration).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** Four levels matches some shorter mark schemes, but the AQA Literature grid runs to six.
   * **Why B:** Five levels is the Edexcel pattern; AQA's 30-mark grid runs to six.
   * **Why D:** Nine is the GCSE grade scale, not the number of marking levels.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 Label\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 (26–30) is labelled "Convincing, critical analysis and \[BLANK\]".
   * **Answer:** Exploration
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The top band is "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" — above "Thoughtful, developed response" (Level 5) and "Clear understanding" (Level 4).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** "Evaluation" or "comparison" name the skills, but AQA's exact Level 6 heading pairs "analysis" with "exploration".
8. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 4 Name\]**
   * **Question:** In the AQA mark scheme, what is the name of Level 4 (16–20 marks)?
   * **Options:** A) Clear understanding, B) Convincing, critical analysis and exploration, C) Thoughtful, developed response, D) Simple, explicit comments.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA names Level 4 "Clear understanding" — clear, explained and sustained, using references effectively to support explanation. Above it sit "Thoughtful, developed response" (Level 5) and "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" (Level 6).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" is the top band (Level 6), two levels higher.
   * **Why C:** "Thoughtful, developed response" is Level 5, one band above clear understanding.
   * **Why D:** "Simple, explicit comments" is the bottom band (Level 1).
9. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the A Christmas Carol question — the lightest of its three assessed strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where it drives Dickens's concept — don't dump a history paragraph, C) Write a full history paragraph before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest of the three strands (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — the 1834 Poor Law making Scrooge's coldness a social argument — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A history paragraph before the analysis feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the argument, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
10. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 AO2\]**
    * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO2 descriptor: "Analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used \[BLANK\]."
    * **Answer:** Judiciously
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO2 is "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously." "Judiciously" warns against feature-spotting: terminology earns marks only when it is selected to serve the analysis of effect.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "judiciously" — terminology used to serve analysis of effect, not scattered to impress.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO1 References\]**
    * **Question:** Two responses both quote "decrease the surplus population." One lists it after a point; the other selects it to pin an argument that Scrooge parrots Malthusian economics before his awakening. For AO1, which AQA phrase distinguishes the second?
    * **Options:** A) It embeds more quotations to show the breadth of its evidence, B) It makes "judicious use of precise references to support interpretations" rather than general reference, C) It integrates the quotation with a clearly explained comment on its effect, D) It names the writer's method using subject terminology.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO1 is "judicious use of precise references to support interpretations." "Judicious" means the reference is chosen to do argumentative work, not listed. A precise reference tied to a concept outweighs many general ones.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** AQA rewards judicious selection, not quantity — more quotations can dilute precision.
    * **Why C:** Explaining the effect of the quotation is AO2 work; the AO1 move is how the reference is selected to support interpretation.
    * **Why D:** Naming the method and terminology is AO2 surface behaviour, not the AO1 distinction of judicious selection.
12. **Type: True/False \[Tests Malthusian Context\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: Scrooge's wish to "decrease the surplus population" reflects the Malthusian economics and 1834 Poor Law that Dickens was attacking.
    * **Answer:** True
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Malthusian economics and the 1834 Poor Law cast the destitute as a "surplus" to be reduced; Dickens puts that doctrine in Scrooge's mouth before his awakening so the reader recognises and rejects it. Context here drives the concept and the method.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering False overlooks that the line is Dickens deliberately voicing the ideology he wrote the novella to indict — context integrated straight into the text.
13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Conceptualised Response\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "conceptualised" response. A conceptualised response to A Christmas Carol is one that...
    * **Options:** A) uses difficult, complicated vocabulary to signal sophistication, B) is organised around an idea, treating Scrooge and the plot as evidence for that idea, C) packs in as much relevant historical context as it can, D) is very long and dense with closely-detailed observation.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means built around a concept — reading the novella as an argument about social responsibility, with Scrooge and events as evidence. It is about organising ideas, not complexity, length or context-stuffing.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Hard vocabulary is not a concept — conceptualised is about the idea, not the wording.
    * **Why C:** Context supports AO3; it does not by itself make a response conceptualised.
    * **Why D:** Length is not conceptualisation — a short response can be conceptualised.
14. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary — Critical\]**
    * **Question:** AQA asks for a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novella's faults and the things Dickens did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal and academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary — Exploration\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" (Level 4) to "exploration of effects" (Level 6). What does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques in the response, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the reader, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the reader. That shift from a single stated effect to layered, opened-up meaning is the AQA Level 4 to Level 6 climb for AO2.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
16. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four A Christmas Carol openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Dickens constructs Scrooge as the embodiment of Malthusian ideology, using the redemption to argue that social responsibility, not charity alone, answers the neglect the 1834 Poor Law entrenched.", B) "Scrooge is a mean man who is visited by three spirits and learns to be kind.", C) "Greed is a bad thing in the novella, and it makes people lonely and unhappy.", D) "Dickens presents greed as destructive through Scrooge's treatment of Bob Cratchit, making the reader judge his coldness."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing unhappiness, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear, explained rung that names a method (the treatment of Cratchit) and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher; the jump to Level 6 is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
17. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 AO2\]**
    * **Question:** Which statements are true of an AQA Level 6 AO2 response to the novella? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) It moves from identifying a method to analysing how it creates meaning, B) It explores several effects the writer's methods have on the reader, C) It names as many techniques as possible to demonstrate range, D) It uses subject terminology judiciously, only where it serves the analysis.
    * **Correct:** A, B, D
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, D. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** AQA Level 6 AO2 is "analysis of the writer's methods... exploration of effects of the writer's methods... subject terminology used judiciously." Analysis, exploration of effects and judicious terminology are all top-band. Maximising technique-count is feature-spotting, which the mark scheme penalises.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why C:** Naming many techniques is feature-spotting — AQA rewards judicious selection and exploration of effect, not quantity.
18. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Dickens uses the adjective 'wolfish' for the boy, Ignorance. This is a technique. It tells us the children are dangerous." For AO2, which AQA Level fits, and what is the ceiling?
    * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with sustained exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the writer's methods work, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the method and its effect.
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the adjective and adding "it tells us they are dangerous" is AQA Level 3 AO2: identification of the writer's methods with only a general comment. Level 4 needs clear explanation of the effect; Level 6 is "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" plus "exploration of effects."
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** No analysis and no exploration of layered effects — the top band is out of reach here.
    * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the method makes meaning, absent here.
    * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted.
19. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 6 AO3\]**
    * **Question:** Complete the AQA Level 6 AO3 descriptor: context should be shown by "specific, detailed \[BLANK\] between context, text and task."
    * **Answer:** Links
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "specific, detailed links between context/text/task." Context earns top marks only when it is connected to the text and the question — the 1834 Poor Law linked to Scrooge's coldness — never when it sits alone as a fact.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "links" — context, text and task must be linked, not listed separately.

    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." A free-standing date is bolt-on.     * **Why A:** The date is accurate — accuracy is not the problem; integration is.

21. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO3 Chain\]**
    * **Question:** A response argues: "Malthusian economics and the 1834 Poor Law (context) drive Dickens's idea that neglect, not poverty, is society's true crime (concept), which drives his choice to make Ignorance and Want 'wolfish' (method)." Select ALL the AQA AO3 strengths this shows. (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Context is used to drive a concept rather than bolted on as a fact, B) There are specific, detailed links between context, text and task, C) It explores ideas and perspectives opened up by the context, D) It lists a standalone historical fact with no link to text or task.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** This is the AQA Level 6 AO3 chain: context drives concept drives method, with "specific, detailed links between context/text/task" and "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors." Listing a standalone fact is the opposite of what is happening here.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **Why D:** The response does the reverse of bolt-on — every element is linked.
22. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO1 Construct\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: To reach AQA Level 6, you should treat Scrooge as a construct Dickens builds to carry an idea, not merely narrate his transformation from miser to generous man.
    * **Answer:** True
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Tracing what Scrooge does across the staves is character-and-plot comment — Level 3. To climb, treat Scrooge as a construct through whom Dickens argues an idea (social responsibility answering Malthusian neglect), the "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" the top band demands.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering False keeps you at the retelling that caps a response at the lower bands — Level 6 argues a concept, it does not narrate the conversion.
