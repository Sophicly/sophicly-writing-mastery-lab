# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA Jekyll and Hyde (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for *The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde* on the **AQA 8702**
19th-century novel question (Paper 1 Section B). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the
canonical slug ladder (`jekyll_and_hyde.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `19th_century.md` for
Jekyll and Hyde lessons so every worked example comes from Stevenson's novel (never any other set
text).

**Mark-scheme facts are identical to the shared AQA 19th-century novel grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels of five marks each; verbatim AQA band descriptors; NO AO4/SPaG on the
novel) — reused unchanged from the vetted Jekyll and Hyde mark-scheme-assessment bank. Only the
EXAMPLES are scoped to Stevenson's novel: Jekyll, Hyde, Utterson, Lanyon, Enfield; the divided self
/ duality; "trampled calmly", "ape-like fury", "troglodytic"; the fog and the potion.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Jekyll and Hyde)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA 19th-century novel question (Paper 1 Section B) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 for a 30-mark total, with no AO4, B) AO2 only, C) AO3 carries the most marks because it is a Novel of Ideas, D) A single 40-mark question with AO4 SPaG.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA novel response is marked out of 30 across six levels of five marks each — AO1 and AO2 weighted equally at 12 marks each and AO3 (context) at 6, half their weight. There is no AO4 on the novel; SPaG is assessed only on the Shakespeare and modern texts.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysis of Stevenson's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument (AO1) and the 6 for context (AO3).
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to a Novel of Ideas, yet AO3 is the lightest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — half the weight of argument or methods.
   * **Why D:** SPaG is assessed on the Shakespeare and modern-text questions, which makes this tempting, but AO4 does not apply to the 19th-century novel.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO1 descriptor requires a "critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text" — reading Jekyll and Hyde as a vehicle for an idea, not a plot to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The exact Level 6 wording is "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text." Treating the novel as a construct exploring the divided self is what lifts AO1 into the top band.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "thoughtful" describe lower bands — "thoughtful" is the Level 5 keyword, whereas the Level 6 decisive word is conceptualised.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO3 Integration\]**
   * **Question:** Which sentence is a Level 6 "integrated" use of context for AQA?
   * **Options:** A) "Stevenson wrote the novel in 1886.", B) "Stevenson draws on late-Victorian fears of atavism and degeneration, sharpened by Darwin's theory of evolution, to make Hyde's 'troglodytic' regression a Victorian nightmare of civilisation collapsing back into instinct.", C) "The novel is set in Victorian London.", D) "Victorian people were worried about lots of things."
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 1 is a bolt-on fact; Level 6 AO3 is "exploration of ideas/perspectives/contextual factors shown by specific, detailed links between context/text/task." Here post-Darwinian fear of atavism drives the concept (a respectable self concealing a primitive one), which drives Stevenson's method (Hyde as 'troglodytic' and ape-like).
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** A composition date feels like solid context, but a publication fact bolted onto nothing explains no authorial choice — the classic Level 1 add-on.
   * **Why C:** Naming the setting gestures at period without connecting any contextual idea to Stevenson's method or concept.
   * **Why D:** A general statement about Victorian anxiety describes the era rather than the text — integration means context that drives a specific choice.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO1 Quality\]**
   * **Question:** Which features signal a top-band AQA AO1 response on Jekyll and Hyde? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Judicious micro-quotations such as "trampled calmly", B) Treating Jekyll and Hyde as constructs exploring the divided self, C) Retelling the plot from potion to death in sequence, D) A conceptualised thesis in the introduction.
   * **Correct:** A, B, D
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, D. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Judicious micro-quotes, construct-level thinking, and a conceptual thesis all push into Level 6 — "judicious use of precise references to support interpretations." Plot retelling (potion → Hyde → Carew → death) caps you at the lower bands.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why C:** Retelling the plot feels like proving you know the novel, but it only shows knowledge of events — AQA rewards conceptualised argument, not narrative summary.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3 Chain\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: For AQA, context about Victorian repression should be integrated into your analysis rather than saved for a standalone history paragraph.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA rewards context as a "driver", not a "bolt-on" — Level 6 AO3 wants "specific, detailed links between context/text/task." Weave why Jekyll hides Hyde (Victorian repression) into the analysis; never write a separate history paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **WhyWrong:** Choosing False usually comes from a KS3 "context paragraph" habit — but a bolt-on caps your AO3 marks because it never links context to Stevenson's choices.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Effects\]**
   * **Question:** A paragraph reads: "Stevenson uses the simile 'ape-like fury'. This is a technique. It tells us Hyde is violent." For AO2, which AQA Level does this reach, and why?
   * **Options:** A) Level 6 — analysis with exploration of the effects, B) Level 5 — examination of how the method works, C) Level 3 — identification of the method with only a general comment on effect, D) Level 4 — clear explanation of the effect.
   * **Correct:** C
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Naming the simile and adding "it tells us he is violent" is AQA Level 3 AO2 — a general comment on the method. Level 6 wants "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" and "exploration of effects", reading the ape-like image as Hyde's regression into a pre-human self.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why A:** There is no analysis and no exploration of layered effects here, so the top band is out of reach.
   * **Why B:** Examination requires sustained working-through of how the simile makes meaning, which is absent.
   * **Why D:** Clear explanation needs the effect explained, not merely asserted as "violent".
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 References\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's AO1 descriptor requires "\[BLANK\] use of precise references" — short, well-judged quotations such as "trampled calmly" embedded in your sentence.
   * **Answer:** Judicious
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and precise. The single phrase "trampled calmly", chosen to pin an argument about Hyde's detachment from conscience, outperforms six general references.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Students often guess "frequent" or "accurate" — but the descriptor's keyword is judicious, because choosing the right micro-quotation matters more than quoting often.
8. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6 Name\]**
   * **Question:** In the AQA mark scheme, what is the name of Level 6 (26–30 marks)?
   * **Options:** A) Clear understanding, B) Convincing, critical analysis and exploration, C) Thoughtful, developed response, D) Simple, explicit comments.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AQA names Level 6 "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration" — a critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text. "Exploration" is the top-band verb, above the "thoughtful, developed response" of Level 5.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** "Clear understanding" is Level 4 (16–20) — clear and explained, two bands below the critical exploration of the top.
   * **Why C:** "Thoughtful, developed response" is Level 5 (21–25), one band below Level 6.
   * **Why D:** "Simple, explicit comments" is the bottom band, Level 1 (1–5) — narrative and descriptive rather than critical.
9. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 Phrasing\]**
   * **Question:** Which phrases appear verbatim in AQA's Level 6 (26–30) descriptors? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Exploration of effects of writer's methods to create meanings, B) Judicious use of precise references to support interpretations, C) Specific, detailed links between context/text/task, D) Awareness of the writer making choices.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Exploration of effects, judicious precise references, and specific detailed contextual links are all Level 6 wording across AO2, AO1 and AO3. Bare "awareness of the writer making choices" is the Level 1 AO2 descriptor.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** "Awareness of the writer making choices" is the very foot of the AO2 ladder — the opposite end of the scale from the exploratory top band.
10. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
    * **Question:** True or False: In the AQA 19th-century novel question, AO1 and AO2 each carry 12 marks and AO3 carries 6, for a 30-mark total.
    * **Answer:** True
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The novel is marked out of 30 across six levels of five marks each, with AO1 and AO2 weighted equally at 12 marks and AO3 (context) carrying half their weight at 6. There is no AO4 on the novel.
    * **AO:** AO3
    * **WhyWrong:** Answering False often comes from assuming context is worth as much as analysis — but AO3 carries 6 marks against 12 apiece for AO1 and AO2.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Descriptor Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** Moving up the AQA levels for AO2, the demand climbs from "identification of the writer's methods". Which phrasing marks the Level 6 top?
    * **Options:** A) Awareness of the writer making choices, B) Analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously, C) Explained/relevant comments on the writer's methods, D) Clear explanation of the writer's methods.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 AO2 asks for "analysis of the writer's methods with subject terminology used judiciously" plus "exploration of effects". The verbs climb: awareness, identification, comment, explanation, and finally analysis and exploration.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** "Awareness of the writer making choices" is the Level 1 wording — the foot of the ladder.
    * **Why C:** "Explained/relevant comments on the writer's methods" is Level 3 — comment is a rung below analysis.
    * **Why D:** "Clear explanation of the writer's methods" is Level 4 — explanation, not the judicious analysis of the top band.
12. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 1 AO2\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 1 AO2 is bare "\[BLANK\] of the writer making choices" — the very foot of the methods ladder, below identification, explanation and analysis.
    * **Answer:** Awareness
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 1 AO2 is "awareness of the writer making choices" — merely noticing that Stevenson made choices, with no analysis of how they work. The verbs climb: awareness, identification, comment, explanation, and finally analysis and exploration at Level 6.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "identification" or "analysis" name higher rungs — the Level 1 word is awareness.
13. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Conceptualised\]**
    * **Question:** AQA Level 6 AO1 rewards a "conceptualised" response. A conceptualised reading of Jekyll and Hyde is one that...
    * **Options:** A) uses difficult, complicated vocabulary to signal sophistication, B) is organised around a concept — the divided self — treating Jekyll, Hyde and the plot as evidence for that idea, C) packs in as much Victorian historical context as it can, D) is very long and dense with detail.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Conceptualised" means built around a concept — reading the novel as an exploration of the divided self, with the characters and events as evidence. It is about organising ideas, not complexity, length or context-stuffing.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Hard vocabulary is not a concept — conceptualised is about the idea, not the wording.
    * **Why C:** Context supports AO3; it does not make a response conceptualised.
    * **Why D:** Length is not conceptualisation — a short response can be conceptualised.
14. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Judicious\]**
    * **Question:** Two students support the idea that Hyde is detached from conscience. One quotes six lines; the other selects the single phrase "trampled calmly". Whose reference is "judicious" — and what does "judicious" mean?
    * **Options:** A) The first — judicious means marshalling as much supporting evidence as possible, B) The second — judicious means a well-judged, selective reference chosen to do the most argumentative work, C) Both equally — judicious just means quoting accurately, D) Neither — judicious means choosing the longest quotation available.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Judicious" means well-judged and selective — chosen because it does real work, not because it is long or abundant. The precise phrase "trampled calmly" outperforms six general ones. This is why AQA Level 6 says "judicious use of precise references".
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Judicious is about wise selection, not quantity — more can mean less precise.
    * **Why C:** Accuracy alone is not judiciousness; the CHOICE must serve the argument.
    * **Why D:** Length is irrelevant; aptness to the point is what counts.

    * **WhyWrong:** The AQA word is "links" — context, text and task must be linked, not listed separately.
16. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary Critical\]**
    * **Question:** AQA asks for a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the novel's faults and what Stevenson did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal tone, D) Disagreeing with other readers' interpretations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.

20. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four openings on the divided self from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Stevenson constructs Hyde as the atavistic residue of a civilisation that represses its instincts, using the divided self to interrogate the moral cost of Victorian respectability.", B) "Dr Jekyll drinks a potion, turns into Mr Hyde, murders Carew and then dies.", C) "Duality is an important idea in the novel, and it causes a lot of harm and death.", D) "Stevenson presents the divided self through Hyde's separation from Jekyll, making the reader feel how repression breeds a hidden violence."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is Level 1 (simple comment). The bare value-judgement about an important idea causing harm, with no method and no evidence, is Level 2 (supported comment). The clear rung that names a method — the separation of the two selves — and its effect on the reader is Level 4 (clear understanding). The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus reader effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is exactly one AQA Level higher.

22. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
    * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 writer's methods to analyse in Jekyll and Hyde? (Select all that apply)
    * **Options:** A) Specific word choices and their connotations (such as "troglodytic"), B) Form (the framing of Enfield's narration), C) Structure (the fog descending, the placement of the Carew murder), D) The biography of Robert Louis Stevenson.
    * **Correct:** A, B, C
    * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
    * **Feedback:** Word-level choice, form and structure are all AO2 methods — the deliberate choices Stevenson made on the page. But a method only counts as AO2 once you pair it with its effect on the reader; naming "framing" or "simile" alone is feature-spotting. The author's biography sits outside the text and earns nothing.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why D:** A writer's biography is background about the author, not a method within the text; AO2 analyses language, form and structure.
