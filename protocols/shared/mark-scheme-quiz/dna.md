# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA DNA (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 English Literature Paper 2 Section A modern text
DNA** (Dennis Kelly). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder
(`dna.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for DNA lessons so every worked
example comes from Kelly's play (never any other modern text's characters or events).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused VERBATIM from the vetted AQA Modern Texts grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels; verbatim AQA band descriptors) plus AO4 technical accuracy (4 marks on a
separate strand). Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to DNA: Phil, Leah, Cathy, Brian, Adam, the
Jan-and-Mark framing, the grille, the cover-up, "quite enjoying this", and Kelly's themes of
collective cruelty and dissolving individual conscience.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — DNA)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature modern-text question (Section A, DNA) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4 technical accuracy adding 4 marks, B) AO2 methods only, C) AO3 context only, D) AO1 alone out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The DNA question is a 30-mark level grid — 12 for conceptual argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3) — with a further 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysing Kelly's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to a play written amid a moral panic about youth cruelty, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand, but AO1 is worth 12 of the 30, not the entire grid.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 (26–30) demands a critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text — treating the play as a construct arguing an idea, not a story to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as collective cruelty dissolving individual conscience, not around Adam's fate or the order of events.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating DNA as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern-text question — the lightest of its three content strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in a DNA essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where it genuinely drives Kelly's idea — the mid-2000s panic about feral youth sharpening his warning that atrocity is authored by ordinary adolescents — never a bolt-on facts paragraph, C) Write a full paragraph about the 2000s before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — the mid-2000s panic about feral youth sharpening Kelly's idea that atrocity is authored by ordinary adolescents, not monsters — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A facts paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in DNA? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Stage directions such as "Silence." and Phil's wordless composure, B) The connotations of specific word choices, such as Cathy "quite enjoying this", C) Structural choices, such as the fractured Jan-and-Mark framing, D) Dennis Kelly's biography.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Stage directions, word-level connotation and structural framing are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices Kelly made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the audience (Focus/Feel/Think/Act) — naming "Silence." or "the Jan-and-Mark framing" alone is feature-spotting. Kelly's biography is context about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biography feels relevant because Kelly wrote the play for young people to perform, but it is contextual information about the writer, not a crafted method the play deploys.

6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6 Heading\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's top band (Level 6, 26–30) for the modern-text question is headed with which phrase?
   * **Options:** A) "Thoughtful, developed consideration", B) "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration", C) "Clear understanding", D) "Explained, structured comments".
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 (26–30) is headed "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration". Level 5 is "Thoughtful, developed consideration"; Level 4 is "Clear understanding".
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** "Thoughtful, developed consideration" is the Level 5 heading, one band below the exploratory top.
   * **Why C:** "Clear understanding" is the Level 4 register — clear explanation, not exploration.
   * **Why D:** This resembles lower-band structured-comment language, not the convincing critical analysis of the top band.

   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 6 AO2 summary is a "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods". 10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA rewards a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the play's faults and what Kelly did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal, academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.

21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four DNA openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Kelly constructs the gang as a single organism whose collective will dissolves private conscience, using Adam's death to interrogate how ordinary adolescents author atrocity together.", B) "Adam is bullied by the gang, falls down a grille, and the others cover it up before he is found alive.", C) "Bullying is a bad thing in the play, and it leads to a boy being hurt and a lot of lying.", D) "Kelly presents cruelty as contagious through the gang's shared cover-up, making the audience feel how quickly a group silences its own doubt."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is the lowest band. The bare value-judgement about a bad thing causing harm, with no method and no evidence, is a step up. The clear, explained rung that names a method — the shared cover-up — and its effect on the audience is Level 4 clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea about collective conscience, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus audience effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" to "exploration of effects" at the top. Reading Leah's restless monologues, what does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the audience, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the audience — reading Leah's restless talk on bonobos and happiness against the gang's silence as a conscience that will not stop speaking. That shift from a single stated effect to layered meaning is the AQA AO2 climb.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
