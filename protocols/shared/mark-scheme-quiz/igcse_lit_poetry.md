# **IGCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: Edexcel IGCSE Poetry Anthology (4ET1, per-anthology) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **Edexcel IGCSE English Literature (4ET1)** poetry anthology.
Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug ladder (`igcse_lit_poetry.md`) — this
bank REPLACES the generic `poetry_anthology.md` for Edexcel IGCSE lessons so every worked example comes
from a 4ET1 anthology poem (never an AQA cluster poem or any other board's set text).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused verbatim from the vetted Edexcel IGCSE MSA bank**
(`mark-scheme-assessment/banks/igcse_lit_poetry.md`): the anthology poetry essay is a SINGLE 30-mark
question marked on ONE five-level grid whose bands bundle AO1 (an informed, critical personal response),
AO2 (language, form and structure) and AO4 — and on this specification **AO4 is CONTEXT, not
spelling/punctuation/grammar**. There is NO separate technical-accuracy strand on this paper. Only the
poem EXAMPLES are scoped to the 4ET1 anthology: Sonnet 116 · La Belle Dame sans Merci · My Last Duchess ·
Remember · Poem at Thirty-Nine · Blessing · Search For My Tongue · Half-caste · Prayer Before Birth · If— ·
Half-past Two · Piano · Hide and Seek · Do not go gentle into that good night · War Photographer · The Tyger.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: EDEXCEL IGCSE (4ET1 — Poetry Anthology)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** On Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) English Literature, which three assessment objectives does the anthology poetry essay reward?
   * **Options:** A) AO1 for an informed, critical personal response, AO2 for language/form/structure, and AO4 for context, B) AO1, AO2 and AO3 for comparison of two poems, C) AO2 and AO4 only, D) AO1 and AO2 only, with no credit for context.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The 30-mark anthology essay rewards AO1 (an informed, critical personal response), AO2 (analysis of language, form and structure) and AO4 (understanding of context) — and on this specification AO4 is context, not spelling and punctuation.
   * **AO:** AO4
   * **Why B:** AO3 comparison is not how this anthology essay is marked; the third objective credited here is AO4 context, and there is no separate comparison strand.
   * **Why C:** Argument is rewarded too — a critical personal response under AO1 — so treating AO1 as unmarked drops a whole third of the grid.
   * **Why D:** Context is credited: it is the AO4 objective on this specification, never omitted.
2. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO4 Meaning\]**
   * **Question:** A top exam trap: on most UK GCSE boards AO4 means spelling, punctuation and grammar. What does AO4 credit on the Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) anthology essay, and how should the top band handle it?
   * **Options:** A) Technical accuracy — spelling, punctuation and grammar across the essay, B) Context — an understanding of the relationship between the poem and the context in which it was written, integrated so it drives the reading rather than sitting apart as a block of facts, C) The sheer number of poetic techniques the candidate can name, D) The length and ambition of the vocabulary used.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. On this specification AO4 is context, not technical accuracy: candidates must "show understanding of the relationships between texts and the contexts in which they were written", and the top band rewards context integrated into the response so it drives the reading.
   * **AO:** AO4
   * **Why A:** Spelling and punctuation is the AO4 meaning on AQA and Edexcel GCSE, but on this specification AO4 is context and no separate accuracy strand applies.
   * **Why C:** Counting techniques is feature-spotting, which the methods strand penalises; it has nothing to do with the context objective.
   * **Why D:** Ambitious vocabulary is not credited here; AO4 rewards understanding of context, not the reach of the wording.

4. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**
   * **Question:** Across how many levels is the Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) anthology poetry essay marked?
   * **Options:** A) Four levels, B) Five levels, C) Six levels, D) Nine levels.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The 30-mark anthology essay is banded across five levels — the top four being Level 2 (7–12), Level 3 (13–18), Level 4 (19–24) and Level 5 (25–30).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** Four undercounts the five-level Edexcel IGCSE grid.
   * **Why C:** Six levels belongs to a different specification's grid; the Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) essay is banded into five.
   * **Why D:** Nine is the GCSE grade scale, not the number of marking levels.
5. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Top-Band Evidence\]**
   * **Question:** Complete the Edexcel IGCSE Level 5 (25–30) evidence descriptor: "\[BLANK\] use of relevant examples to support the response."
   * **Answer:** Discriminating
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 5 evidence word is "discriminating" — examples chosen with sharp judgement so each does real analytical work. The ladder climbs from "clearly relevant" at Level 3 to "fully relevant" at Level 4 to "discriminating" at Level 5.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "relevant" or "detailed" name lower rungs; the specification's top-band evidence word is "discriminating".
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Cohesive vs Sustained\]**
   * **Question:** In an essay on Do not go gentle into that good night, what separates a Level 4 (19–24) response from a Level 5 (25–30) response?
   * **Options:** A) The Level 5 essay simply quotes more of the villanelle, B) The response moves from "sustained analysis" and "detailed awareness" to "cohesive evaluation" and "excellent understanding" — evaluative judgements that connect and build across the whole essay rather than merely being kept up, C) The Level 5 essay ranges across more of Thomas's other poems, D) The Level 5 essay is noticeably longer.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The climb is from sustained to cohesive — evaluative judgements that flow and connect across the whole response — and from detailed to excellent understanding. It is a shift in integration and insight, never in quantity.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** Quotation count is not a level descriptor; the ladder rewards how references are handled, not how many appear.
   * **Why C:** Ranging across a poet's wider output is breadth, not the cohesion and insight the top band names.
   * **Why D:** Length is never a level criterion; a concise response can be cohesive and a long one merely sustained.
7. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO4 Not SPaG\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: On the Edexcel IGCSE (4ET1) anthology essay, AO4 assesses the accuracy of a candidate's spelling, punctuation and grammar.
   * **Answer:** False
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. On this specification AO4 is context — "the relationships between texts and the contexts in which they were written" — not technical accuracy. There is no separate spelling-and-punctuation strand on this paper.
   * **AO:** AO4
   * **WhyWrong:** Answering True imports the AQA / Edexcel GCSE meaning of AO4; on Edexcel IGCSE Spec A, AO4 is the context objective.
8. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Effect\]**
   * **Question:** A candidate writes: "Bhatt uses free verse in Search For My Tongue. This is a structural feature." Why does this stall in the lower bands?
   * **Options:** A) Free verse is not a genuine structural choice in the poem, B) It identifies a method but never reaches its effect on the reader, so it stays descriptive rather than evaluative, C) Search For My Tongue is not part of the anthology, D) The candidate should have named more techniques to climb the bands.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO2 wording ends "to create meanings and effects" — naming a method is only the start. Here the candidate stops at identification; to rise, the analysis must reach how the shifting free verse, breaking into Gujarati and back, enacts the loss and recovery of a mother tongue between two cultures.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why A:** Free verse is a real structural choice in the poem; the fault is that its effect is never analysed.
   * **Why C:** Search For My Tongue is an anthology poem; the weakness is analytical, not one of selection.
   * **Why D:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, which the strand penalises; depth of effect, not quantity, lifts the band.
9. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 writer's methods to analyse in a 4ET1 anthology poem? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Imagery and the connotations of specific word choices, B) Form — such as the sonnet, the dramatic monologue or the villanelle, C) Structure — such as the volta, enjambment or a controlling refrain, D) The poet's biography and the events of the poet's life.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Language, form and structure are the three AO2 methods — the deliberate choices a poet makes on the page to create meanings and effects. But a method only counts once you pair it with its effect on the reader; naming "villanelle" or "dramatic monologue" alone is feature-spotting. The events of a poet's life are contextual background, credited under AO4, not a method within the poem.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** A poet's biography is context about the writer, credited under AO4, not a crafted method the poem deploys.
10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO4 Level 5\]**
    * **Question:** Which sentence best demonstrates Level 5 (25–30) "excellent understanding of context" for My Last Duchess?
    * **Options:** A) "My Last Duchess was written by Robert Browning in the nineteenth century.", B) "The poem is set a very long time ago and is hard to follow now.", C) "Browning sets the monologue in a Renaissance court where a wife's life could be wholly subject to her husband's will, so the Duke's casual confession that he 'gave commands' to end the Duchess's smiles reads as the chilling logic of an honour culture rather than mere personal cruelty.", D) "People in the past believed men were important, which is relevant to the poem."
    * **Correct:** C
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The strong response integrates the Renaissance honour culture into the reading and names its effect on how we judge the Duke — his confession becomes the logic of a whole social order, not an isolated act. Level 5 AO4 rewards context woven into the argument, never a detached fact.
    * **AO:** AO4
    * **Why A:** A composition date is a true fact that does no analytical work on its own.
    * **Why B:** Calling the poem old is a remark about age, not an understanding of context.
    * **Why D:** This gestures vaguely at the past but names no specific belief and makes no link to the poem's effect.
11. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests Level 2 Label\]**

    * **Answer:** narrative
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 2 is "largely narrative" — retelling what the poem describes. The climb to Level 5 is the move from narrative to cohesive evaluation.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "descriptive" or "simple" describe adjacent bands; the Level 2 word here is "narrative".
12. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** The Edexcel IGCSE top band rewards "discriminating" use of examples. One candidate quotes a whole quatrain of Sonnet 116; another selects the single phrase "an ever-fixed mark". Whose use is discriminating, and what does the word mean?
    * **Options:** A) The first — discriminating means supplying as much of the text as possible, B) The second — discriminating means selecting evidence with sharp judgement so the chosen reference does the most analytical work, C) Both equally — discriminating simply means quoting accurately, D) Neither — discriminating means choosing the longest available quotation.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Discriminating" means well-judged and selective: the precise phrase "an ever-fixed mark" pins the idea of love's constancy far more sharply than a whole quatrain. It is this specification's top-band evidence word, distinct from AQA's "judicious" and Edexcel GCSE's "perceptive".
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Discrimination is wise selection, not volume; more text often buries the telling phrase.
    * **Why C:** Accuracy alone is not discrimination; the choice must serve the argument.
    * **Why D:** Length is irrelevant; aptness to the point is what "discriminating" rewards.
13. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four openings to an essay on Do not go gentle into that good night from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by Edexcel IGCSE band (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Thomas constructs the villanelle's refrains as an act of defiance in which the son's rage against his father's death widens into a refusal to accept human mortality itself.", B) "The poem is about a man whose father is dying and he is very sad about it.", C) "Thomas urges his dying father to fight death, and the sadness of this makes the poem moving.", D) "Thomas sustains an analysis of defiance, tracing in detail how the repeated command to 'rage' resists the calm of dying."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one Edexcel IGCSE band higher. The bare plot-and-mood rung is Level 2, largely narrative. The supported comment that adds a general feeling with no method is Level 3. The sustained, detailed rung that names the repeated command and its effect is Level 4. The cohesive, evaluative rung that argues one idea about mortality from the first line is Level 5.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from a narrative of plot and mood, to a supported comment with no method, to a sustained detailed analysis, to a cohesive evaluation built on one governing idea. The top band is cohesion and insight, not more retelling.
14. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Interrelationship\]**
    * **Question:** Level 5 (25–30) requires "a cohesive evaluation of the interrelationship of the language, form and structure". Which response best fits that descriptor for Do not go gentle into that good night?
    * **Options:** A) It names the villanelle form, then lists three metaphors, then comments on the rhyme, each in a separate sentence, B) It judges how the villanelle's compulsory refrains, its driving imperative verbs and its tightening structure work together to enact a grief that refuses to release the dying father, C) It sets out a dictionary definition of the villanelle before analysing the poem, D) It retells the poem stanza by stanza in order.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Interrelationship" means the methods are evaluated as they work together, not in isolation: here the refrains, the imperative verbs and the circling structure combine to enact the speaker's refusal to let his father die. Judging that combined effect is the cohesive top-band move.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Handling form, imagery and rhyme as a list of separate observations is the opposite of interrelationship; the descriptor rewards methods evaluated together.
    * **Why C:** A definition explains the term, not the poem's effect, and does no evaluative work.
    * **Why D:** Retelling the poem in sequence is narrative, which caps the response low.

17. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** The Edexcel IGCSE AO1 strand rewards "an informed personal engagement". What does that require?
    * **Options:** A) Stating whether you personally enjoyed the poem, B) Your own reasoned interpretation, grounded in close knowledge of the poem, C) Recounting an event from your own life that the poem reminds you of, D) Reproducing the interpretation of a well-known critic.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Informed personal engagement" is your own argued reading, built on close textual knowledge and expressed in a critical style — not enjoyment, autobiography or borrowed opinion.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** Registering enjoyment is a reaction, not an informed critical interpretation.
    * **Why C:** "Personal" does not mean autobiographical; it means your own reasoned response to the poem.
    * **Why D:** Repeating a critic is not personal engagement; the reading must be your own.

22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO1 Evaluative Voice\]**
    * **Question:** Which topic sentence best signals the Level 5 (25–30) critical, evaluative voice on Search For My Tongue?
    * **Options:** A) "This poem is about a woman who speaks two languages.", B) "Bhatt writes about her tongue and her mother tongue in the poem.", C) "Bhatt constructs the loss and return of her mother tongue as a struggle in which identity itself is nearly silenced and then, through the poem's shift into Gujarati, unexpectedly reborn.", D) "Search For My Tongue has some Gujarati writing in the middle of it."

    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The evaluative topic sentence treats the poem as an argument about identity and names how the shift into Gujarati enacts its rebirth — a critical style with an informed personal engagement. It earns credit for the reasoned reading, not for grand vocabulary.
    * **AO:** AO1

    * **Why B:** Naming the subject is topic-labelling, not an evaluative interpretation.
