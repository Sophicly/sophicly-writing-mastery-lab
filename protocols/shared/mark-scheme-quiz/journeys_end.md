# **GCSE English Literature Mark Scheme Mastery Quiz: AQA Journey's End (per-text) v1.0**

Per-TEXT mark-scheme-quiz bank for the **AQA 8702 English Literature Paper 2 Section A modern text
Journey's End** (R. C. Sherriff). Served by `SWML_Quiz_Bank::pick_session()` via the canonical slug
ladder (`journeys_end.md`) — this bank REPLACES the generic `modern_text.md` for Journey's End
lessons so every worked example comes from Sherriff's play (never another modern text's characters
or scenes).

**Mark-scheme facts are reused VERBATIM from the vetted AQA Modern Texts grid** (30 marks: AO1 12 /
AO2 12 / AO3 6; six levels; verbatim AQA band descriptors) plus AO4 technical accuracy (4 marks on a
separate strand). Only the EXAMPLES are scoped to Journey's End: Stanhope, Osborne, Raleigh, the
dugout, the raid, "doped with whisky", Osborne reading Alice in Wonderland, and Sherriff's
presentation of the strain of command on the Western Front.

Parsed deterministically (keys + feedback stripped before reaching the client). All questions worth
2 marks each.

### **SECTION A: AQA (8702 — Journey's End)**

1. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** How is the AQA GCSE English Literature modern-text question (Section A, Journey's End) marked?
   * **Options:** A) AO1=12, AO2=12, AO3=6 (total 30), with AO4 technical accuracy adding 4 marks, B) AO2 methods only, C) AO3 context only, D) AO1 alone out of 30.
   * **Correct:** A
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Journey's End question is a 30-mark level grid — 12 for conceptual argument (AO1), 12 for methods (AO2), 6 for context (AO3) — with a further 4 marks for AO4 technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation and a range of vocabulary and sentence structures).
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why B:** Analysing Sherriff's methods matters greatly, but believing AO2 is marked alone ignores the equal 12 marks for argument and the 6 for context.
   * **Why C:** Context feels central to a play about the Western Front, yet AO3 is the smallest strand at just 6 of the 30 marks — never the whole allocation.
   * **Why D:** Argument is the joint-heaviest strand, but AO1 is worth 12 of the 30, not the entire grid.
2. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO1 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA Level 6 (26–30) demands a critical, exploratory, \[BLANK\] response to task and whole text — treating the play as a construct arguing an idea, not a story to retell.
   * **Answer:** Conceptualised
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 6 AO1 descriptor is a "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text". "Conceptualised" separates the top band — the response is organised around an idea, such as endurance under command corroding the self, not around what Stanhope does.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Guesses like "detailed" or "developed" describe lower bands; the exact Level 6 word is "conceptualised" — treating Journey's End as the vehicle for a concept.
3. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Context Weighting\]**
   * **Question:** AO3 is worth 6 marks on the AQA modern-text question — the lightest of its three content strands (AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). How should you treat context in a Journey's End essay?
   * **Options:** A) Skip it entirely, B) Integrate it lightly where the post-war disillusion of Sherriff's 1928 audience genuinely drives his refusal to glorify the trenches — never a bolt-on history paragraph, C) Write a full paragraph of First World War history before the analysis, D) Double the AO3 effort to compensate for its low weight.
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 is the lightest strand here (6 of 30). Integrate it briefly where it genuinely drives the concept — a post-war audience's disillusion driving Sherriff's refusal to glamorise the trenches — not as a standalone paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **Why A:** Because AO3 is the smallest strand it can seem skippable, but ignoring it surrenders 6 marks that light, well-placed context would secure.
   * **Why C:** A history paragraph feels thorough, yet bolt-on background scores poorly — context must serve the analysis, not sit beside it.
   * **Why D:** Doubling the effort misreads weighting — extra context cannot earn more than its 6 marks and steals time from argument and methods.
4. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests AO2 Methods\]**
   * **Question:** Which count as valid AO2 Writer's Methods to analyse in Journey's End? (Select all that apply)
   * **Options:** A) Stage directions and the dugout setting, B) The connotations of specific word choices such as "doped", C) Structural shifts, such as the calm before the raid giving way to the barrage, D) R. C. Sherriff's biography.
   * **Correct:** A, B, C
   * **Scoring:** 2 marks for A, B, C. 1 mark if mostly correct.
   * **Feedback:** Stage directions, word-level connotation and structural shifts are all AO2 targets — the deliberate choices Sherriff made. But a method only counts as AO2 analysis once you pair it with its effect on the audience (Focus/Feel/Think/Act) — naming "stage directions" or "the calm before the raid" alone is feature-spotting. Sherriff's biography is context about the writer, not a method on the page.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **Why D:** Biography feels relevant because Sherriff drew on his own trench service, but it is contextual information about the writer, not a crafted method the play deploys.
5. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO1 Whole Text\]**
   * **Question:** True or False: AQA's modern-text AO1 descriptors ask for a response to "task and whole text", not analysis confined to a single scene of Journey's End.
   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AO1 descriptors name "task and whole text" — range across the play, from the quiet routine of the dugout to the final barrage that brings it down, to build the argument.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **WhyWrong:** Concentrating on one memorable moment feels safe, but the descriptor explicitly rewards engagement with the whole text.
6. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Level 6 Heading\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's top band (Level 6, 26–30) for the modern-text question is headed with which phrase?
   * **Options:** A) "Thoughtful, developed consideration", B) "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration", C) "Clear understanding", D) "Explained, structured comments".
   * **Correct:** B
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Level 6 (26–30) is headed "Convincing, critical analysis and exploration". Level 5 is "Thoughtful, developed consideration"; Level 4 is "Clear understanding".
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why A:** "Thoughtful, developed consideration" is the Level 5 heading, one band below the exploratory top.
   * **Why C:** "Clear understanding" is the Level 4 register — clear explanation, not exploration.
   * **Why D:** This resembles lower-band structured-comment language, not the convincing critical analysis of the top band.
7. **Type: Fill-in-the-Blank \[Tests AO2 Level 6\]**
   * **Question:** AQA's Level 6 AO2 summary asks for a "fine-grained and \[BLANK\] analysis of methods supported by judicious use of subject terminology."
   * **Answer:** Insightful
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The Level 6 AO2 summary is a "fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods". The pairing warns against feature-spotting: micro-level precision and insight into effect, such as how the off-stage guns make the war feel inescapable — not a list of devices.
   * **AO:** AO2
   * **WhyWrong:** "Detailed" or "developed" fit lower bands; the Level 6 word is "insightful" — analysis that perceives effect at a fine-grained level.
8. **Type: Select All That Apply \[Tests Level 6 AO1 Features\]**

   * **Correct:** A, B, C

Careful chronological retelling is narrative, capped at clear understanding.
   * **AO:** AO1
   * **Why D:** Chronological retelling feels thorough, but narrating the raid in order caps at clear understanding; the top band argues a concept across the whole text.
9. **Type: True/False \[Tests AO3\]**

   * **Answer:** True
   * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. AO3 credits "specific, detailed links between context, text and task" — context woven into the argument, such as the post-war disillusion of Sherriff's 1928 audience driving his refusal to glorify the raid, not a bolt-on history paragraph.
   * **AO:** AO3
   * **WhyWrong:** It can feel like context earns marks on its own, but the descriptor demands specific, detailed links between context, text and task.
10. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Evaluative Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA rewards a "critical" style. In a mark scheme, what does "critical" actually mean?
    * **Options:** A) Pointing out the play's faults and what Sherriff did badly, B) Forming an evaluative, argued judgement and taking a position on interpretation, C) Writing in a consistently serious, formal, academic tone, D) Disagreeing with the interpretations other readers have offered.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. In a mark scheme "critical" does NOT mean fault-finding. It means evaluative and argued — you weigh interpretations and commit to a position. AQA pairs it as "critical, exploratory": an argued reading that opens up meaning.
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Why A:** This is the everyday sense of "critical" (negative), not the assessment sense.
    * **Why C:** Tone is not what "critical" rewards — an argued judgement is.
    * **Why D:** Critical means evaluating the text, not other students.
11. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Number of Levels\]**

15. **Type: MCQ \[Tests AO2 Verb Ladder\]**
    * **Question:** One response "examines" how Sherriff uses the off-stage guns. Another reads the barrage as a sustained motif that makes the war feel inescapable, exploring several effects at once. For AO2, what single move takes the second to Level 6?
    * **Options:** A) It quotes more of the play to widen its evidence, B) It moves from examination of the method to exploration of the effects of the writer's methods to create meanings, C) It deploys rarer critical terminology, D) It develops the point across longer paragraphs.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. The AQA Level 5 to Level 6 AO2 shift is "examination" becoming "exploration of effects of the writer's methods to create meanings". Reading the barrage as a sustained motif and exploring multiple effects is the top-band move. Length, rare terms and quote-count do not change the Level.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Quote quantity is not an AO2 criterion; judicious selection is.
    * **Why C:** Rarer terms without explored effect is feature-spotting.
    * **Why D:** Paragraph length is not a Level descriptor.

21. **Type: Ranking \[Tests AO1\]**
    * **Question:** Rank these four Journey's End openings from WEAKEST to STRONGEST by AQA AO1 Level (type the letters in order, weakest first).
    * **Options:** A) "Sherriff constructs Stanhope as the point where duty and dread collide, using the dugout to interrogate the price endurance exacts from the men who never allow themselves to break.", B) "Stanhope commands a company, drinks whisky, and is killed when the German barrage brings the dugout down.", C) "War is a bad thing in the play, and it leads to a lot of waste and sadness.", D) "Sherriff presents the strain of command through Stanhope's whisky, making the audience feel how leadership erodes a man."
    * **Correct:** B, C, D, A
    * **AO:** AO1
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. Each rung is one clear AQA band higher. The plot-summary rung, narrating events only, is the lowest band. The bare value-judgement about war being bad, with no method and no evidence, is a step up. The clear, explained rung that names a method — Stanhope's whisky — and its effect on the audience is Level 4 clear understanding. The critical, conceptualised rung, arguing an idea from the first line, is Level 6. The ladder is plot → comment → explained method-and-effect → concept.
    * **WhyWrong:** Weakest to strongest runs from plot narration, to a value-judgement with no method, to an explained method plus audience effect, to a conceptualised argument. Each rung is one AQA Level higher; the jump to the top band is the move from explaining events to arguing a concept.
22. **Type: MCQ \[Tests Exploration Vocabulary\]**
    * **Question:** AQA AO2 moves from "explanation of effects" to "exploration of effects" at the top. Reading Osborne's recital of Alice in Wonderland to Raleigh before the raid, what does "exploration" add that "explanation" does not?
    * **Options:** A) It names a greater number of techniques, B) It opens up several possible effects or meanings and how they shift the audience, rather than stating a single fixed effect, C) It develops the same single effect at greater length, D) It works without relying on direct quotations.
    * **Correct:** B
    * **Feedback:** ✓ Correct. "Explanation" states one effect; "exploration" opens up several possible meanings and how they work on the audience — reading Osborne's recital of the Walrus and the Carpenter as both shielding the men from terror and sharpening the audience's sense of the slaughter to come. That shift from a single stated effect to layered meaning is the AQA AO2 climb.
    * **AO:** AO2
    * **Why A:** Naming more techniques is feature-spotting, not exploration.
    * **Why C:** Length is not the difference — depth of opened-up meaning is.
    * **Why D:** Quotations are still needed; exploration works through precise references.
