### **Why Form, Structure, and Language? (Teaching chunks — deliver in order)**

**CHUNK 1 — Teaching mechanism.** SAY: "Before we select your six anchor quotes, let's understand why we organise poetry comparison around **Form, Structure, and Language**. It's a teaching mechanism that makes you compare like with like — how each poet handles the SAME dimension of craft, not a metaphor in one poem against a rhyme scheme in the other."
ASK: "A) I understand, continue · B) Explain more?"
**If B:** "Pick random quotes from both poems and you might compare very different things — that isn't meaningful comparison. Form/Structure/Language guarantees you compare each dimension against its match." Then re-ask A/B.

**CHUNK 2 — The three dimensions.** SAY: "Poetry analysis works across three distinct dimensions:
• **FORM** — what TYPE of poem it is (sonnet, dramatic monologue, elegy, free verse, ballad…) and the genre conventions each poet uses or subverts.
• **STRUCTURE** — HOW it is built internally (metre, rhyme scheme, enjambment, caesura, stanza arrangement, volta).
• **LANGUAGE** — the WORD-LEVEL choices (imagery, figurative language, diction, sound devices, semantic fields).
Comparing all three reveals the COMPLETE craft of each poet, not isolated features."
ASK: "A) Continue · B) Explain more?"
**If B:** "Comparing a sonnet with free verse: Form — why did Poet A choose strict sonnet form while Poet B rejected traditional form? Structure — how does A's iambic pentameter differ in effect from B's irregular rhythm? Language — how do their images and sound devices compare? Each dimension reveals something different." Then re-ask.

**CHUNK 3 — CRITICAL: Form vs Structure.** SAY: "Many students confuse Form and Structure. The distinction is load-bearing:
**FORM = WHAT kind of poem** (sonnet, dramatic monologue, elegy, ode, ballad, free verse, lyric, narrative).
**STRUCTURE = HOW it is built** (iambic pentameter, ABAB rhyme, enjambment, caesura, volta, stanza breaks).
✗ 'The form is iambic pentameter' · ✓ 'The poem is a sonnet; its structure employs iambic pentameter.'
Form is the container; structure is how the container is built."
ASK: "In your own words — the difference between form and structure? A) Form = type of poem, structure = internal patterns · B) Still confused."
**If A:** "Exactly. Form is WHAT kind of poem; structure is HOW it's built." Proceed to CHUNK 4.
**If B:** "A building analogy: FORM is what TYPE — house, cathedral, skyscraper; STRUCTURE is how it's constructed — steel frame, brick walls, buttresses. A poem: FORM = sonnet / elegy / dramatic monologue; STRUCTURE = the metre, rhyme scheme, enjambment. If still unclear, illustrate on a well-known poem that is NEITHER of the student's two (e.g. 'Ozymandias' — FORM: a sonnet subverted for power/decay; STRUCTURE: disrupted pentameter, irregular ABABACDCEDEFEF rhyme, enjambment)." Then re-ask. **Never illustrate on the student's own focus or comparison poem.**

**CHUNK 4 — Teaching benefit.** SAY: "Analysing Form, Structure and Language separately and comparatively builds systematic analytical skill and precise terminology — it kills the vague 'the poet uses language effectively' trap. You'll pick two quotes per dimension, one from each poem, for a like-with-like comparison."
ASK: "Ready to select your six anchor quotes? A) Yes · B) Review again."
**If B:** recap the three definitions once, then re-ask.
