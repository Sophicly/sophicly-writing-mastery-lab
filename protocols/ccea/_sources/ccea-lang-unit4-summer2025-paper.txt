  Tasks 3 and 4 are based on two extracts from a newspaper article about
  Artificial Intelligence:

  “The AI doctor will see you now”

  Task 3: Spend 12 minutes on this task. Total 15 marks

  The text below is the beginning of the article.

  Explain how the writer has gained and held the interest of the reader.


                               The AI doctor will see you now

    Pioneer of Artificial Intelligence technology, Geoffrey Hinton, has issued a stark
    warning about the latest developments in AI software. Canadian computer scientist
    Hinton spoke out after it was announced that an AI programme had passed a final
    year medical exam: “Have no doubt about it, chatbots could soon outsmart humans.”

    Medical exams are notoriously difficult to pass because of the level of knowledge and
    expertise required. The fact that a computer programme has been successful marks a
    spine-chilling milestone and propels humanity firmly into the realm of Science Fiction.
    This may suggest that AI could signal the demise of many of society’s most
    established jobs.

    The argument about the potential threat of AI has been in the public domain in recent
    years. In 2023, over 1,000 industry experts (including SpaceX mogul Elon Musk and
    Apple co-founder Steve Wozniak) petitioned for a pause on the dangerous race to
    advance Artificial Intelligence.

    Some believe the time has come to take action before humans lose control. How real
    is the risk from robots?

  Task 4: Spend 12 minutes on this task. Total 15 marks

  The text below is part of the same article.

  Explain how the writer has presented his views on the positives of Artificial
  Intelligence for use in medicine.

    The world’s greatest minds are split over whether Artificial Intelligence will enhance or
    harm our lives. Perhaps this is another example of humanity becoming unnecessarily
    worried about progress.

    History is littered with examples of welcome developments initially being greeted with
    mistrust, for example, organ transplants, antibiotics and vaccinations. Despite the
    controversy, many are still heralding the use of AI as an equally important innovation.

    One breakthrough AI model was shown to determine a person’s likelihood of
    developing a range of medical conditions, such as diabetes, with staggering accuracy.
    The clever computing innovation used both existing resources and self-generating,
    specialised algorithms to diagnose those with the highest risk profile. And it did so in
    a heartbeat.

    The belief that Artificial Intelligence can sensationally transform medical treatment in
    the coming years is anything but artificial. Very soon we could see an AI doctor to
    help with hay fever or to make that verruca vanish.
