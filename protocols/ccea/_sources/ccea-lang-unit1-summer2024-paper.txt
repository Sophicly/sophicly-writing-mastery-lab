                        Four tasks: 50 minutes. Mark allocation: 63 marks

  Reading Non-fiction

  Tasks 2 and 3 are based on a newspaper article about going on holiday alone.

  Task 2: Spend 15 minutes on this task. Total 21 marks

  Read the text below where the writer outlines her thoughts about travelling alone.

  Explain how she has presented this in a way that engages her readers’ interest.
  Support your comments with evidence.


        Search online for ‘holiday’ and you will be bombarded with idyllic photographs. You’ll
        see a picturesque Mediterranean sunset. Such images were my inspiration to go on
        a much-anticipated, well-earned holiday. Despite the fact there’s inevitably a smiling
        couple or a group of laughing friends, here I am, solo in Santorini. And loving it!

        I’ve long turned my nose up at the rising trend in travelling alone. Will I get lost?
        Will I be safe? Will I enjoy it?

        Astonishingly, such concerns put me in the minority. Research revealed that over
        half of us have taken a holiday alone. Meanwhile, online searches for ‘solo travel’
        tripled between 2021 and 2024, with more than 8 million solo travel posts on
        Instagram.

        Travelling alone is no longer unusual. I deserved this trip. I had worked hard. If I was
        to have the holiday I wanted, I had no choice but to take the plunge and go it alone.
        So I did!

  Task 3: Spend 10 minutes on your response. Total 12 marks

  In this part of the text, the writer describes the benefits of travelling alone.

  Read the text below. In your own words, write down two benefits of travelling
  alone: one from each paragraph. For each benefit present two pieces of
  supporting evidence.


        Travelling alone helps you to engage with everyone and everything around you on a
        deeper level. Gone are the distractions of constant company, so you notice more: the
        vast number of people, the atmosphere, the culture all around you. Detractors might
        dismiss solo travel, but for me this trip really was a moment of self-development.

        It was this mindset that took my trip to a higher plane. I’d be surrounded by groups of
        people — but instead of feeling lonely, I’d feel grateful. I’d look around and feel
        empowered by the fact that I’d chosen to come here on my own. I could be perfectly
        content in my own company. I couldn’t recommend a solo trip more highly. It has
        made me feel more liberated than ever. I am proud of myself — and plans for my
        next solo adventure are already under way.

  Reading Media Texts

  Tasks 4 and 5 are based on a book cover for “Where the World Ends” by
  Geraldine McCaughrean.

  Task 4: Spend 17 minutes on this task.                     Total 20 marks

  Read the text below. Explain how language has been used to develop a sense that
  this book would be exciting to read. Present evidence to support your comments.


        Every time a lad came to the St Kilda sea cliffs, he went home less of a boy and
        more of a man. If he went home at all, that is.

        In the summer of 1727, a group of men and boys were left on a remote sea stack to
        harvest birds for food and fuel. But when no one returned to collect them they had to
        endure storms, starvation, isolation and terror, with no way of knowing if they would
        ever be rescued.

        How could they possibly survive in such a challenging place of sea and stone?
        Clinging to life in the grip of a murderous ocean, they were trapped. Trapped in the
        place where the world ends.

        Based on a true event, this is an extraordinary story of human grit, determination and
        survival. Harshly beautiful, and stark with near-despair, this is an unsettling historical
        novel.
