## 1. THE ASSESSMENT OBJECTIVES (verbatim)

> Below are the relevant assessment objectives for English Language Unit 4.
>
> **Reading AO3** Candidates must:
> (i) read and understand texts, selecting material appropriate to purpose, collating from different
> sources and making comparisons and cross-referencing as appropriate;
> (ii) develop and sustain interpretations of writers' ideas and perspectives; and
> (iii) explain and evaluate how writers use linguistic, structural and presentational features to
> achieve effects and engage and influence the reader.
>
> **Writing AO4** Candidates must:
> (i) write to communicate clearly, effectively and imaginatively, using and adapting forms and
> selecting vocabulary appropriate to task and purpose in ways that engage the reader;
> (ii) organise information and ideas into structured and sequenced sentences, paragraphs and whole
> texts, using a variety of linguistic and structural features to support cohesion and overall
> coherence; and
> (iii) use a range of sentence structures for clarity, purpose and effect, with accurate spelling,
> punctuation and grammar.

⭐ **The only AO difference between Unit 1 and Unit 4 is in AO3(i):** Unit 4 adds *"collating from
different sources and making comparisons and cross-referencing as appropriate"*. That clause is
where Task 2's comparison demand comes from. Everything else is worded identically.

> All strands are being assessed in Tasks 2, 3 and 4.

**⚠️ CCEA numbers its objectives AO3 (Reading) and AO4 (Writing)** — not AQA's AO1/AO2/AO5/AO6.

## 3. TASK 1 — PERSONAL OR CREATIVE WRITING. Two separate assessments

The question paper prints the split and the choice:
> Up to 58 marks are available for an organised and engaging piece of writing that matches form and
> purpose with audience.
> Up to 30 marks are available for the use of a range of sentence structures and accuracy in
> spelling, punctuation and grammar.
> Complete only one task. EITHER (a) Personal writing … OR (b) Creative writing …

Summer 2025 set: **(a)** *"Write an essay for the examiner about your favourite teacher."*
**(b)** *"Write a story for entry in a creative writing competition. The audience is young adults.
The picture below is to be the basis for your writing. You may provide your own title."*
Summer 2024 set: **(a)** a personal essay for the examiner about a memorable experience,
**(b)** a story for publication in the school magazine.

⭐ **Both options are marked on the SAME strands and the SAME two grids.** The choice changes the
form, never the tariff and never the criteria.

## 4. TASK 2 — THE COMPARISON, 32 marks

Summer 2025 wording: *"Compare and contrast how the writers have created a sense of excitement in
both extracts. Present supporting evidence from both texts."* Two extracts from two novels arrive on
a separate insert (Texts A and B).

⭐ **The comparison is not a fourth strand — it is folded INTO strands 1 and 3.** That is the single
most important fact about this task: a student who analyses each text beautifully but never connects
them is capped at CL2 on the selection strand, however good the analysis is.

| CL | Read and understand text / select appropriate material | Develop and sustain interpretations of writer's intentions | Explain and evaluate elements of writer's craft |
|---|---|---|---|
| CL0 | No creditworthy response. | No creditworthy response. | No creditworthy response. |
| CL1 | A basic understanding which may or may not reference the text(s) directly. | A basic sense of the overall intention(s). | Basic remarks on the use of language. |
| CL2 | Use of some appropriately selected examples from the text(s) to support understanding and make simple/straightforward connections. | Straightforward attempts to explain the intention(s) of one or both writers. | Straightforward explanation of some elements of the writer's craft, across one or both texts. |
| CL3 | A competent selection of examples from both texts to support understanding and make valid comparisons/contrasts. | Competent explanation of the intentions of both writers which may draw upon explicit and some implicit ideas. | Competent explanation of appropriate elements of the writers' craft, across both texts. |
| CL4 | A purposeful selection of examples from both texts to support understanding and develop relevant comparisons and contrasts. | Increasingly purposeful interpretation of both writers' intentions, including explicit and implicit ideas. | Analysis of relevant elements of the writers' craft across both texts. |
| CL5 | Precise and judicious selection of examples from both texts to support understanding and explore meaningful comparisons and contrasts. | Sustained perceptive interpretation of both writers' intentions. | Evaluation of elements of the writers' craft across both texts and how these elements impact the reader. |

**Mark Grid Task 2 (32 marks)** — 000 = 0 · 100 = 1–2 · 110 = 3–4 · 111 = 5–6 · 112 = 7–8 ·
221 = 9–10 · 222 = 11–12 · 322 = 13–14 · 332 = 15–16 · 333 = 17–18 · 334 = 19–20 · 443 = 21–22 ·
444 = 23–24 · 445 = 25–26 · 554 = 27–29 · 555 = 30–32.

**The ladder of connection, in the board's own words, is worth learning as a sequence:**
*simple/straightforward connections* (CL2) → *valid comparisons/contrasts* (CL3) → *develop relevant
comparisons and contrasts* (CL4) → *explore meaningful comparisons and contrasts* (CL5). And the
craft ladder runs *across one or both texts* (CL2) → *across both texts* (CL3 onwards).

## 5. TASKS 3 and 4 — READING NON-FICTION, 15 marks each

Two extracts from the SAME newspaper article (Summer 2025: *"The AI doctor will see you now"*).
Task 3 takes the beginning of the article; Task 4 takes a later part of it.

Summer 2025 wordings: **Task 3** *"Explain how the writer has gained and held the interest of the
reader."* **Task 4** *"Explain how the writer has presented his views on the positives of Artificial
Intelligence for use in medicine."*

Both tasks use the SAME three strands (Task 4's column headings read "Read and understand text/select
appropriate material" and "Develop and sustain interpretations of the writer's intentions"; the
descriptor text is identical):

| CL | Read and understand text / select material | Develop and sustain interpretations of writer's intentions | Explain and evaluate elements of writer's craft |
|---|---|---|---|
| CL0 | No creditworthy response. | No creditworthy response. | No creditworthy response. |
| CL1 | A basic understanding which may or may not make use of textual evidence. | A basic sense of the writer's overall intention(s). | Basic remarks on the writer's use of language. |
| CL2 | Use of some appropriately selected examples from the text to support understanding. | Straightforward attempts to explain/explanation of the writer's intention(s). | Straightforward explanation of some relevant elements of the writer's craft. |
| CL3 | A competent selection of examples from the text to support understanding. | Competent explanation of the writer's intention(s) which may draw upon explicit and implicit ideas. | Competent explanation of relevant elements of the writer's craft. |
| CL4 | A purposeful selection of examples from the text to support understanding. | Increasingly purposeful interpretation of the writer's intention(s), including explicit and implicit ideas. | Analysis of elements of the writer's craft. |
| CL5 | Precise and judicious selection of examples from the text to support understanding. | Sustained perceptive interpretation of the writer's intention(s). | Evaluation of elements of the writer's craft and how these elements impact the reader. |

**Mark Grid Tasks 3 and 4 (15 marks each — the same grid)** — 000 = 0 · 100 = 1 · 110 = 2 · 111 = 3 ·

⭐ **One mark per grid row.** On a 15-mark task a single strand level is worth a whole mark, so the
difference between *explanation* (CL3) and *analysis* (CL4) on the craft strand is measurable and
immediate.

### 5a. What the board credits — the Summer 2025 Task 4 indicative list, verbatim

*"a range of task-specific material that candidates at all Competence Levels may draw upon … Credit
any other valid suggestions"* — not a required list.

- use of superlative/synecdoche: "The world's greatest minds" to highlight the level and quality of thought associated with this important debate
- use of contrast: "enhance or harm" to convey the polarising nature of the issue
- use of metaphor: "History is littered…" to indicate to the reader that there are many examples where mankind perceived new technology with needless suspicion
- use of triple/anecdote: "organ transplants, antibiotics and vaccinations" to further the argument that other concerning developments in medicine have been amazingly positive innovations
- use of proclamatory verbs: "heralding" to point to and usher in this new world-changing technology and predict its impact
- use of evidence-based research: "One breakthrough AI model was shown to determine a person's likelihood of developing a range of medical conditions... with staggering accuracy" to reinforce the efficacy of this new technology
- use of short sentence: "And it did so in a heartbeat." to highlight the speed and ease with which the AI programme is able to carry out complex processes
- use of pun: "in a heartbeat" to allude to the medical advantages of AI with levity
- use of alliteration: "make that verruca vanish" to humorously reinforce the idea that AI will have a positive impact on medical treatment

⭐ **Every board bullet has the same three parts: technique named + quotation embedded + effect or
purpose.** That is the board showing us what a credited sentence contains.
