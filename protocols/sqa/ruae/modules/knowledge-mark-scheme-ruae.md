## 1. GENERAL MARKING PRINCIPLES (verbatim, page 02)

> This information is provided to help you understand the general principles you must apply when
> marking candidate responses to questions in this paper. These principles must be read in
> conjunction with the detailed marking instructions, which identify the key features required in
> candidate responses.
>
> (a) Marks for each candidate response must always be assigned in line with these general marking
> principles and the detailed marking instructions for this assessment.
>
> (b) Marking should always be positive. This means that, for each candidate response, marks are
> accumulated for the demonstration of relevant skills, knowledge and understanding: they are not
> deducted from a maximum on the basis of errors or omissions.
>
> (c) If a specific candidate response does not seem to be covered by either the principles or
> detailed marking instructions, and you are uncertain how to assess it, you must seek guidance from
> your team leader.
>
> (d) Candidates should gain credit for their understanding of the ideas of the passage, and their
> analysis and evaluation of the writer's use of language.
>
> (e) Unless quoting from the passage, the candidates should use their own words as far as possible.

**[AI_INTERNAL] (b) is the rule that removes every deduction from this paper.** (e) is the rule that
decides every own-words question. (d) is the rule that lets you credit a sound point the example
list does not contain.

## 2. THE MARK FORMULAS, BY QUESTION TYPE (verbatim from the "Expected response(s)" column)

Quote the matching line as the question's **Standard Alignment**.

| Type | SQA's printed formula | How it is earned |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding — own words, N points | `Any two points.` · `Any four points.` · `Any six points.` | 1 mark per key point, each a re-wording of a distinct idea in the stated lines. The question states the number of points. |
| Analysis — one example | `Reference (1)` / `Comment (1)` | A quotation from the stated lines (1) and what that specific word or feature suggests (1). |
| Analysis — two examples | `Reference (1)` / `Comment (1)` / `x2` | The same pair, twice, on DIFFERENT evidence. |
| Linkage | `Any pair OR two selections covering different directions.` | One quotation from the sentence with the earlier idea it refers to (1); one quotation from the sentence with the new idea it introduces (1). |
| Evaluation of the conclusion | `Selection (1)` / `Comment (1)` | An expression quoted from the stated lines (1) and what earlier idea it closes, repeats or answers (1). |

**The 2025 paper's own examples of the marked pair, verbatim, so the shape is unmistakable:**

> ‘taunting’/reference to personification of ‘taunting’ (1) suggests eg his tools are mocking him/he
> is being mocked (1)

> ‘(Frankie) didn’t blink.’/reference to short sentence (1) suggests/emphasises eg she will go
> through with her plan/is confident of success/is determined (1)

**And of a glossed understanding point, verbatim:**

> ‘tap into human feelings’ glossed by eg he can understand people’s emotions (1)

> ‘I’m not as good as the writers I admire’ glossed by eg he compares himself unfavourably with
> others (1)

**[AI_INTERNAL] Note the word SQA itself uses: "glossed by".** An own-words point is a GLOSS — the
same idea in different words. And note "eg" before every example: the list is illustrative, never
exhaustive (principle (c)/(d) above).

## 3. THE ONE EXPLICIT REFUSAL IN THE 2025 INSTRUCTIONS (verbatim, question 9)

> NB do not reward a response which simply says ‘it sums up the main ideas of the passage etc’ unless
> the candidate goes on to explain what the main ideas are.

This is the single most common way the final question is thrown away.

## 4. NO LEVELS, NO BANDS, NO DESCRIPTORS FOR THIS PAPER

The RUAE marking instructions publish **no level descriptors and no band grids** — the paper is
marked point by point. There is therefore **nothing to quote as "Level Alignment"**, and inventing
one breaks PROTOCOL-STANDARD A4. Use §2's formula as the Standard Alignment instead.

## 5. THE COURSE-LEVEL FACTS (verbatim, Course Specification pages 9–10)

> Reading for Understanding, Analysis and Evaluation                          30 marks

> 30 marks (30% of the total mark) are awarded for applying reading skills in understanding,
> analysis and evaluation to questions on one passage.

> Candidates are required to read and understand one unseen non-fiction text. A series of questions
> will focus on their understanding, analysis and evaluation skills.

> The following main command words are generally used in this question paper:
>
>  explain (why): used to assess understanding of a writer's ideas (always followed by 'in your own
> words')
>  explain (how): used to assess the skills of analysis
>  identify: used to assess the skill of summarising (identifying the main points or ideas)

> This question paper is set and marked by SQA, and conducted in centres under conditions specified
> for external examinations by SQA. Candidates complete this question paper in 1 hour.

**[AI_INTERNAL] THE COMMAND WORD IS THE TYPE SWITCH.** *explain why* / *explain … in your own words*
/ *identify* / *summarise* → understanding, one mark per glossed point. *explain how* → analysis,
Reference + Comment pairs. There are no assessment-objective numbers on this course: the three
skills are **understanding, analysis and evaluation**, and those are the only skill words a student
should ever see from us on this paper.

## 6. THE NAMED FAULT LIST (Sophicly's teaching names — NEVER a deduction on this paper)

Each is named in plain words with a worked fix. They cost the student the marks the missing half
would have earned; they never reduce a mark that was earned (§1(b)).

| Plain name | What it looks like | The fix shape |
|---|---|---|
| Reference with no comment | a quotation dropped in with nothing said about it | "Quote it, then finish the sentence: *'…' suggests that …*" |
| Comment with no reference | a judgement with nothing quoted to anchor it | "Name the word first, then say what it suggests." |
| The quotation is too long to zoom into | a whole sentence quoted when one word carries the effect | "Cut to the word doing the work — *'taunting'*, not the whole line." |
| The technique is named but never explained | "he uses personification" and stop | "Naming earns nothing. Say what the personification makes the reader feel or think." |
| A weak analytical verb | *shows · tells us · is about · illustrates the idea that* | "Replace with *suggests · conveys · emphasises · reveals · evokes*, then finish the thought." |
| Lifted wording on an own-words question | the passage's own phrase reused as the "point" | "Re-word it: *'tap into human feelings'* becomes *he understands how people feel*." |
| Evidence from outside the stated lines | a quotation from elsewhere in the passage | "The question names the lines. Only those lines can pay." |
| Two links in the same direction | both halves of a link answer look backwards | "One half of the sentence looks back, the other introduces what comes next — quote both." |
| The conclusion comment stops at "it sums it up" | the explicit SQA refusal in §3 | "Name WHICH idea it closes — say the idea out loud." |
| Fewer points than the question asked for | four points where the paper asked for six | "The paper tells you how many. Count them before you move on." |
