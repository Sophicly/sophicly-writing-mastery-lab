# CAMBRIDGE 0500/0990 — THE TRAPS: every documented way candidates lose marks
### Compiled from Cambridge's June 2024 examiner report (both codes), the published mark schemes, and the measured 40-paper dataset. Every trap carries Cambridge's own words. Nothing invented.

**How to use:** this is the revision checklist and the marker's watch-list. Each trap: who falls
in → Cambridge's words → the fix. The craft volumes teach the methods; this is the anti-method.

---

## THE PAPER-WIDE TRAPS

**T1 — Effort inversely proportional to reward.** The most expensive habit on Paper 1: answers
to 15-mark Q2(d) *"longer and more carefully crafted"* than the 25-mark Q3; summaries polished
while Q3 starves. → Fix: the minute-table in the P1 craft volume; marks-per-minute decides.

**T2 — One house style for everything.** Register is set PER TASK by role + audience + form —
it is not one default. Measured: "formal" appears in **1 of 40** Q3 tasks (June 2024's report);
"persuade" in 5. The examiners' praise-word is *"an appropriate register for their imagined
audience"*. → Fix: teach register-MATCHING (the three-question Voice Decision), never a single
persuasive house style. A journal is private and reflective; a letter to your mother is warm; a
report to a company is formal; a speech to students is direct and encouraging.

**T3 — Ignoring the guidance numbers.** The successful cohort *"paid attention to the guidance
offered to help them focus their answers — writing no more than 120 words in the summary, using
just one example … in 2(c), and selecting three language examples from each paragraph in 2(d)."*
The numbers are the shape of the winning answer even where they are not rules.

**T4 — No planning or review on the two writing-marked tasks (1(f), Q3).** *"It is advisable to
factor in time to plan and review responses to avoid inconsistencies of style, errors that
impede communication and awkward expression."* Bullet-plan, write, read back.

## PAPER 1 — QUESTION BY QUESTION

**T5 — Q1(a)–(e): the extra guess.** *"Less successful responses attempted to include extra
guesses … taking up valuable examination time … and often diluting evidence of understanding."*
Marks are never deducted — but a hedge that "covers all possibilities" earns nothing and a
self-contradiction kills the mark. → One committed answer.

**T6 — Q1(a)–(e): the circular answer.** *"Repeating some or all the language of the question
where own words were specified"* = zero evidence. → Synonym the key terms.

**T7 — Q1(b): the half-explanation.** Explaining one word of a two-word phrase (meaning for
'unusual', repeating 'types') scores 1 of 2. → Both strands, every phrase.

**T8 — Q1(f): the whole-text précis.** *"Own words version of the whole text in the order it was
presented"* — repeats ideas, includes irrelevant detail, argues against the question's focus.
This is the Level 3 shape. → Filter by the question, group into umbrella points, whole-text range.

**T9 — Q1(f): comment creep.** *"Excess often arose from attempts to comment on the writer's
viewpoint and/or … an unnecessary introduction or conclusion."* A summary contains no opinion,
no frame. → Cold open, points only.

**T10 — Q1(f): the 120-word overrun.** Not deducted, not cut off — but *"consistently
well-focused"* (Reading L5) and the Writing /5 concision marks both fall, and the report names
over-length answers as *"missed opportunities to target higher marks"*. → Rehearse the length.

**T11 — Q2(a): the full-sentence habit.** *"It is not necessary to write answers to Question
2(a) in full sentences"* — copying the question out *"added unnecessary time pressure"*. And the
opposite trap: one extra word beyond the underlined meaning ('wafted', 'consciously') loses the
mark. → The exact word or phrase, alone.

**T12 — Q2(b): the scatter-gun.** *"Several suggestions and extra guesses of different meanings
that are contradictory"* dilute to zero. → One precise meaning, in context.

**T13 — Q2(c): the second example.** *"Less effective responses often attempted to discuss more
than one example — time that might have been more profitably spent in Question 2(d)."* The task
says ONE. → Three distinct points about one choice.

**T14 — Q2(d): the wrong paragraphs.** Choices from outside the two named paragraphs *"could not
be credited"* — however brilliant. → Bullet-check the paragraph numbers before writing.

**T15 — Q2(d): the device list.** *"Answers which simply list literary devices … are not likely
to evidence the skills"*; long quotations *"with only the first and last words identified"* earn
*"very thin general comments at best"*. → Short exact quotation → meaning in context → effect.

**T16 — Q2(d): one-paragraph lopsidedness.** Mark scheme Level 3: *"one half of the text may be
better addressed than the other."* Three from EACH, evenly. → 3+3, always.

**T17 — Q2(d): playing it safe with vocabulary.** Writing skills are NOT assessed here —
Cambridge tells candidates to *"work at the very edges of their vocabulary range … rather than
limiting their answer to those words they are sure they can spell correctly."* → Reach.

**T18 — Q3: the form swap.** Real June 2024 examples: *"writing letters back home from Lyn,
presenting the words of speeches … or scripting TV interviews with Mike"* — instead of Mike's
report. Wrong form or wrong perspective = the widest skills window closed. → Name role, audience,
form before the first sentence.

**T19 — Q3: the text-free beauty.** 15 of the 25 marks are reading marks; writing that floats
free of Text C forfeits them. → 3–4 text details per bullet, developed.

**T20 — Q3: the missed bullet.** All three bullets, equal attention (reorder or interleave
freely — coverage is what is checked). → Tick them in the plan.

## PAPER 2 — SECTION BY SECTION

**T21 — Section A: the text-free persuasive essay.** Reading Level 1: *"very limited response
with minimal relation to the text"* = 1–2 of 15. Ceiling ~27/40, realistic 19–25, plus double
the time. → Evaluate both texts; the Evaluator's Frame.

**T22 — Section A: the text-walkthrough.** Writing Level 3: *"relies on the sequence of the
original text"* — caps writing ~10–13/25 however good the prose. Also L2/L1: *"words/phrases
copied from the original text."* → Reorganise both texts' ideas around YOUR argument, own words.

**T23 — Section A: reporting instead of evaluating.** Reading L3 = *"selects and comments on
explicit ideas"*; the top bands demand evaluation of ideas *"both explicit and implicit"* and
(L6) *"assimilates"*. "Says/states" verbs are the tell. → Verdict verbs; judge, don't relay.

**T24 — Section B: the misfitting prepared piece.** *"Narratives which did not comfortably fit
with either title … seemed more suited to titles set in previous examinations or were
pre-prepared"*; *"awkwardly adapted practice pieces."* Preparation is not penalised — MISFIT is
(Table A relevance wording at Levels 1–4). → The adaptation drills in the prepared-story doc.

**T25 — Section B descriptive: the smuggled plot.** Descriptive tasks that drift into narrative
events lose Table A content/structure marks. → Camera, not plot; a character may act but nothing
*happens*.

**T26 — Section B: ambition without control.** Table B's top band is bought by consistency of
quality — "precise" vs "mostly precise", *"consistent well-chosen register"*, accuracy *"almost
always"*. A dazzling first paragraph followed by plain filler reads as "mostly". → Sustained
crafting: every device chosen for effect, precise, register-safe, the whole way through.

## THE POSITIVE TRAPS (things that look like rules but aren't)

**P1 — Word guidance on Q2(d)/Q3/Section A/Section B is advisory** — *"offered to help them
organise their time"* — though it describes the winning shape. Only the summary's 120 is a
stated maximum (enforced through the descriptors, per T10).
**P2 — Marks are NEVER deducted** (Generic Marking Principle 3) — errors are ignored, not
punished; credit is positive-only. The consequence: never waste time erasing; add the better
answer.
**P3 — Alternative answers and unexpected approaches MUST be credited** where valid — the mark
scheme instructs examiners so. Indicative content is *"not a prescription of required content."*
