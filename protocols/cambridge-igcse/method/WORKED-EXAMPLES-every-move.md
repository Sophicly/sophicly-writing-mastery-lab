# PAPER 1

## CRAFT ONE — THE SUMMARY (Q1f)

### Move 1: The Question Filter — five worked filters

The rule: underline only what answers the exact question. Here is the filter applied to five
different real or practice tasks, with the trap that sat next to it.

| the question asks | keep | discard, however interesting |
|---|---|---|
| *supporters' advantages and appeal* (Hyperloop, real s24/11) | speed, directness, low pollution, cheap running, proven tech, comfort, cargo, trade | "remains untested at scale"; construction cost; scepticism of any kind |
| *what attracts someone to turtle conservation AND what they find difficult* (turtles pack) | BOTH strands — presence at something ancient, unfinished science, doable work · night shifts, insects, poor pay, spreadsheets, one-in-a-thousand | the biology of nesting; the description of hatching itself |
| *rewards of leaving office work for a craft* (forge pack) | visible completion, work outlasting you, endless learning, sleep, un-automatable, un-outsourceable | the pay cut, the blisters, four months of hooks, being outperformed by a teenager |
| *what outdoor swimming gives people* (cold-water pack) | the ninety-second reward, mental quiet, company, courage that transfers | learn in summer, never dive, never swim alone, get out early |
| *what Yusuf finds rewarding backstage* (backstage pack) | invisibility as the point, nightly proof, honest team culture, accessibility | missed birthdays, overnight get-outs, poor pay, "something with curtains" |

⭐ **The pattern to teach:** in four of the five, the distractor is the text's *most vivid*
material. Vividness is precisely why weak answers include it. Train the question-first habit and
the vividness stops mattering.

### Move 2: Over-Harvest then Select — the same harvest at two levels

*Task: the rewards of leaving office work for a craft (forge pack, Text B).*

**A Level 3 selection** — nine points, all from the first half of the text, in the text's order:
> pay cut · left logistics · a good job · nice chair · no end to the work · could not describe a
> year's output · a gate eleven miles away · it will outlast him · that is not a small thing

**A Level 5 selection** — the same harvest, chosen for RANGE across the whole text and grouped:
> *completion* (a finished object each day; the gate that outlasts him; being able to describe his
> own work at last) · *the learning* (never stops; a man of sixty-five still improving) ·
> *the security* (cannot be automated, outsourced, or reorganised away) · *the body* (physical
> work, so you sleep) · *the verdict* (never wanted the salary back)

Same reading. The second selects across the WHOLE text and groups, which is what *"points are
skilfully selected to demonstrate an overview"* describes.

### Move 4: The Compression Engine — five compressions

| loose (each ~1 point per clause) | compressed (2–3 points per clause) |
|---|---|
| "It is fast. It is also cheaper to run. And it produces less pollution." | "It is faster, cheaper to run and less polluting than air travel." |
| "You cannot be automated. You cannot be outsourced. You cannot be reorganised out of a job." | "The work resists automation, outsourcing and restructuring alike." |
| "There are night shifts. There are insects. The pay is poor." | "The nights are sleepless, insect-ridden and badly paid." |
| "It quietens the mind. It gives you company. It builds courage." | "It clears the head, supplies company and quietly builds courage." |
| "No degree is needed. Skills work abroad. There is a shortage of workers." | "No degree is required, the skills travel internationally, and technicians are in short supply." |

### Move 5: The Cold Open — five openings, three wrong

| ✗ | why it fails |
|---|---|
| "In this text, the writer discusses the advantages of the Hyperloop." | a frame that earns nothing and costs ~10 of 120 words |
| "There are many reasons why people enjoy cold-water swimming, as the writer explains." | announces instead of answering |
| "I think the most interesting reward is the sense of completion." | opinion — a summary contains none |
| ✓ "Supporters believe the Hyperloop would transform travel between cities: it is extremely fast…" | first point, first clause |
| ✓ "Swimmers gain, within ninety seconds, a feeling the writer calls the best available and free…" | first point, first clause |

## CRAFT TWO — THE LANGUAGE QUESTION (Q2d)

### Move 2: Meaning → Connotation → Effect — five climbs of the ladder

Each below is a single choice taken up all three rungs. Note that rung 1 is *required* by the
mark scheme (Level 4: *"explanations of meanings within the context of the text are secure"*).

| quotation (pack) | meaning | connotation | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| "the sea patiently **grinding** the shore" (turtles) | wearing away by slow friction | machinery, indifference, immense time | the sea is unhurried and impersonal — it dwarfs the volunteers' single night |
| "a silence so deep it felt like a **held breath**" (travel) | air kept in, not released | suspense, deliberateness, the pause before something | the sky becomes expectant rather than empty; the reader waits with her |
| "the envelope **sighing down** … like something falling asleep" (travel) | air escaping slowly | tiredness, breath, a living creature ending its day | after a violent landing the machine is gentled and loved |
| "my lungs simply **shut**" (cold water) | closed; ceased to work | a door, a mechanism, finality | the body acts against him without permission — total loss of control |
| "a **cramped** black corridor" vs "a **warm** room full of people" (backstage) | confined / heated and occupied | confinement, exclusion / comfort, belonging | two worlds six metres apart, and the crew are in the wrong one by choice |

### The same quotation at three levels — three demonstrations

**1. "straight as a pencil line" (travel pack, smoke)**
- *L2:* "This is a simile, which helps you imagine the smoke." — device named, effect asserted.
- *L3:* "It means the smoke is very straight, showing the air is still." — meaning only.
- *L5:* "A pencil line is deliberate, thin and perfectly controlled, so the comparison tells us the
  air is utterly windless — the world below looks *drawn* rather than living, which makes the
  balloon's silent movement through it feel almost like trespass."

**2. "touched, leapt, touched again, dragged" (travel pack, the landing)**
- *L2:* "The writer uses a list of verbs to show the landing."
- *L5:* "Four monosyllabic verbs in a row give the landing its own jolting rhythm — the sentence
  bounces as the basket does — and because each verb is a separate impact, the reader feels the
  landing as a sequence of shocks rather than an event that is over."

**3. "Nobody was on a phone." (cold-water pack, closing line)**
- *L2:* "A short sentence for emphasis."
- *L5:* "The paragraph ends on five plain words defining the group by an absence. Modern life is
  named only by what is missing from the scene, so the claim is made without being argued — and
  the brevity, after a long sentence about laughter and weather, lands it like a verdict."

### Move 3: The Image X-Ray — four full x-rays

| image | compared to what | what it smuggles in |
|---|---|---|
| "the heat met me **like a wall**" (forge) | a solid barrier | heat becomes an obstacle with mass — the forge physically resists entry, so the apprentice is an intruder before she starts |
| "**tractor tracks**" for turtle trails (turtles) | heavy machinery | scale and weight — the animal is industrial in size, and the beach bears the mark of something that does not belong on land |
| "**like something recovered from a shipwreck**" (forge, first hook) | salvage | age, ruin, uselessness — her work is not merely bad but *wrecked*, and comically so |
| "the water — a flat, **oily** black" (cold water) | oil | thickness, unnaturalness, refusal — the sea looks like a substance, not a place |

## CRAFT THREE — THE EXTENDED RESPONSE (Q3)

### Move 2: Development vs Copying — five paired demonstrations

The reading marks reward development. Each pair: the same text detail, copied and then developed.

| copied (Level 1–2) | developed (Level 4–5) |
|---|---|
| "Marta was nervous when she got in the basket." | "She got in the way they all do at that point — one leg, then a pause, then the other — and I said nothing, because a nervous trainee who is hurried does not come back." |
| "The landing was firm and we hit a hedge." | "The wind freshened on the descent, which is when a landing stops being yours and becomes the weather's; we bounced, dragged, and finished against a hedge, exactly as briefed." |
| "There were four hundred and eleven hooks." | "Four hundred and eleven, and I kept nine — not because the other four hundred and two were failures, but because their purpose was in the making, not the keeping." |
| "The turtle went back to the sea without looking." | "She never turned. Nine years ago someone wrote her number on a clipboard on this same beach, and she has crossed an ocean since to leave a hundred eggs and walk away from them — which is the part every new volunteer takes hardest." |
| "The actor missed four pages of the script." | "Four pages went, in front of nine hundred people, and nothing on stage acknowledged it — which is precisely what the audience paid for and precisely what the crew exists to make possible." |

### Move 3: The Voice Decision — the same information in six voices

*(The full six-form treatment, with complete models, is in
`FORM-MASTERY-paper1-Q3-six-forms-with-models.md`. This is the compressed comparison — the same
fact, six times.)*

**Fact:** the trainee held a level altitude for three minutes on her first flight.

| form | how the fact sounds |
|---|---|
| **report** | "The trainee maintained a stable altitude unaided for approximately three minutes. In my assessment this indicates appropriate progress toward solo flight." |
| **journal** | "Three level minutes. Most of them chase the burner for weeks. She found the rhythm on her first morning and thought I was being kind when I said so." |
| **interview** | "— Three minutes doesn't sound like much. — It doesn't, does it? It's everything. It means she stopped arguing with the balloon and started listening to it." |
| **speech** | "One of our trainees held a level altitude for three minutes on her first morning. Three minutes. I have taught people who took a season to do that." |
| **letter** | "She held it level for three whole minutes, Ines, and grinned at the instruments like an idiot, and I have not enjoyed a morning that much in years." |
| **article** | "Osei held a level altitude unaided for three minutes on her first morning — an achievement her instructor acknowledged with a single word." |

# PAPER 2

## SECTION A — THE EVALUATOR'S FRAME

### Move 1: The Two-Voices Map — six worked maps

Each text reduced to a POSITION, not a topic. Six pack pairs:

| pack | Text A's position | Text B's position |
|---|---|---|
| work | the limits round the job, not the job, decide the outcome | it works — but only on your terms |
| habits | design beats willpower; a habit is a tool, not the goal | the apps work until you serve them; a habit only a phone witnesses isn't yours |
| fashion | there is no virtuous purchase, only a less wasteful wardrobe | the problem is disposable clothes, not cheap ones |
| machines | convenience is paid for in decisions; stay the master | the machines are excellent servants and should not be promoted |
| live events | a screen cannot supply the physical or the collective | it is genuinely better and genuinely out of reach |
| homework | type beats quantity, and the same task is not equal for every student | set less of the hard kind, and tell us what it is for |

### Move 2: Verdict Verbs — the same idea at four levels, three times

**1. (work) The claim: part-time jobs teach time management.**
- *L2 identifies:* "Text A says jobs teach time management."
- *L3 comments:* "Text A says jobs teach time management, which is a good point."
- *L4 begins to evaluate:* "Text A claims jobs teach time management, and Amara's experience
  supports this."
- *L6 assimilates:* "The article's strongest card is time management — and Amara's blog
  unknowingly proves it, since her grades rose only once the job forced her to plan. What she adds,
  and the article misses, is the condition: it worked because she capped her hours."

**2. (fashion) The claim: buy less, buy better.**
- *L2:* "Text A says we should buy fewer clothes but better ones."
- *L3:* "Text A says buy less, buy better, and this would reduce waste."
- *L4:* "Text A's advice to buy better assumes a budget, which Leo challenges."
- *L6:* "'Buy better' is advice with a price tag attached, and Leo exposes it: at forty pounds a
  t-shirt the instruction re-creates the private club the article claims fashion escaped. What
  survives the collision is the half both texts share — that lifespan, not price, is the lever."

**3. (machines) The claim: offloading tasks to devices weakens us.**
- *L2:* "Text A says we rely on machines too much."
- *L3:* "Text A says we are losing skills, such as navigation."
- *L4:* "Text A argues we lose skills through lack of practice, and Carmen's month supports this."
- *L6:* "The article's real worry is not laziness but authorship — each convenience is a decision
  taken by a system whose reasons we cannot see. Carmen's experiment tests exactly that and returns
  a more useful answer than the research: she took back the decisions that were forming her, and
  gave the machines the ones that were not."

### Move 3: The Convergence Find — six convergences

| pack | where they disagree | the deeper agreement (this is bullet 2's content) |
|---|---|---|
| work | whether teenage jobs are a risk or a rite | conditions decide: capped hours, a decent employer |
| habits | whether tracking tools help or capture you | tools for starting, never for depending |
| fashion | whether cheap clothing is villain or lifeline | lifespan beats price |
| machines | whether convenience is a gift or a cost | be the master; check periodically that you still can |
| live events | whether live is worth its price | it is worth it *and* rationed — so design the access |
| homework | whether homework is useful or wasteful | type beats quantity; purpose must be stated |

### Move 5: Form conventions — six openings and six closes

| form + audience | opening | close |
|---|---|---|
| letter to a friend | "You asked what I think about the bakery job — so I did some reading, and I'm going to give it to you straight." | "Tell me what you decide." |
| letter to an authority | "I am writing as a student at the school, in response to your invitation for views on funding trips to live performances." | "I hope the committee will consider these points, and I would be glad to help gather student views." |
| speech to peers | "Raise your hand if you've ever downloaded an app to fix your life. Keep it up if it's still on your phone." | "So: one small thing, tonight, badly. That is how all of this starts." |
| speech to parents and teachers | "I want to begin by agreeing with the concern that brought us here tonight." | "Whatever we decide, let us decide it for the students in front of us, not the ones in the research." |
| article for a school magazine | "THE FOUR-POUND JACKET — In a mountain of clothes in a Chilean desert lies the answer to a question nobody asked a teenager." | "The jacket is still going. That, in the end, is the whole argument." |
| article for a teenage magazine | "Somewhere in your house, a machine has already made three decisions for you today. Name one." | "Keep the ones that serve you. Sack the rest." |

## SECTION B — COMPOSITION

### Narrative Move 3: Show the Consequence — six conversions

| claim (weak) | evidence (strong) |
|---|---|
| "I was terrified." | "My knuckles whitened on the rim of the basket." |
| "I was exhausted." | "I could not manage the zip on my own bag." |
| "She was nervous." | "She got in one leg at a time, as if the basket might object." |
| "The room was tense." | "Nine of us stood against a wall in the dark, breathing quietly." |
| "I was ashamed of the work." | "He turned it once and dropped it in the scrap bin without a word." |
| "I felt relieved." | "I said nothing for a mile, and then I noticed I had been smiling at the road." |

### Descriptive Move 2: Varieties of Focus — one path, four depths

*(Task: 'Just before opening time'.)*

1. **Wide:** "The café holds its breath at seven fifty."
2. **Mid:** "Chairs stand upside down on tables like acrobats frozen mid-trick."
3. **Close:** "On the till sits a mug of tea going cold, the only untidy thing in the room."
4. **Out again:** "Outside, the street is already rehearsing — a bus sighs past, a dog tows its
   runner toward the park."

Four moves of the lens in eighty words. That is what the top band means by *"varieties of focus"*.

### Descriptive Move 3: The Five-and-One — five conversions

| three senses blurred | one sense, precise |
|---|---|
| "The kitchen was loud and hot and busy." | "Steam climbed the window and turned the street outside to ghosts." |
| "The beach was cold and dark and windy." | "The shingle sucked and clattered under twenty pairs of feet." |
| "The forge was hot and noisy and dangerous." | "The heat met me like a wall, pressing on my face, drying my eyes." |
| "The theatre was dark and tense and quiet." | "Nine people breathing quietly, six metres behind a farmhouse." |
| "The market was busy and colourful and smelly." | "A crate of split peaches went past at shoulder height, trailing wasps." |
