# ASSESSMENT — PAPER 2, SECTION B, COMPOSITION [40]
## One of four · 350–450 words · 16 content and structure + 24 style and accuracy

Always two descriptive and two narrative titles. The candidate answers ONE.

⭐ **MARK HOLISTICALLY — the whole piece, against both tables.** Do NOT split a composition into
equal per-paragraph marks the way a reading answer is split. Granularity here belongs in the
FEEDBACK, not in the arithmetic.

## TABLE A — content and structure [16]

Every level has a **General** block that applies to both task types, plus a **Specific** column per
type. ⭐ **The General bullets are identical for descriptive and narrative** — *"Content is complex,
engaging and effective"*, *"Structure is secure, well balanced and carefully managed for deliberate
effect"*. The content and structure standard does **not** relax because the student chose description.

**Descriptive, the top band:** *"Many well-defined and developed ideas and images create a
convincing overall picture with **varieties of focus**."*
⭐ *Varieties of focus* means the lens MOVES — wide to close, crowd to one face. A piece that holds
one distance reliably reaches Level 5 and stops there.

**Descriptive drift into narrative** is named at exactly two levels — L4 *"even where there is a
tendency to write in a narrative style"* and L3 *"which may be more typical of a narrative"* — and
is **absent from L5 and L6**. So drift is tolerated at L4 and incompatible with the top two bands.
At L2 the descriptor stops describing description at all: *"The recording of some relevant events."*

**Narrative, the top band:** *"The plot is well-defined and **strongly** developed with features of
fiction writing such as description, characterisation and **effective climax**, and **convincing**
details."* L5 lists the *same four features* without the intensifiers. ⭐ **A story reaches L5 by
HAVING description, characterisation, a climax and details; it reaches L6 by making them LAND.**

## TABLE B — style and accuracy [24]

⭐ **This table contains no reference to content, plot, relevance, task or title.** It is 24 marks of
pure prose quality, identical for all four questions.

⭐ **Every difference between L5 and L6 is a CONSISTENCY difference, not an ambition difference:**
*"precise"* vs *"mostly precise"*, *"consistent"* vs *"mostly consistent"*, SPaG *"almost always
accurate"* vs *"mostly accurate, with occasional minor errors"*. For a student sitting at 17–20,
**making fewer errors and holding the voice steady is worth up to 4 marks; being more ambitious is
worth none** — and risks the errors that cost the 4. Say this plainly.

## RELEVANCE — what it does and does not cost

Relevance language appears **only at Levels 1–4** of Table A and is absent from 5–6, and Cambridge
states descriptors *"should not be interpreted as hurdle statements."* So a loose fit to the title
is **not capped** — it is simply described by the language of L4 and below, costing roughly 5–6
Table A marks. **Table B is untouched by relevance.** Report the cost honestly rather than warning
vaguely about going off-task.

## WHAT TO GIVE BACK, EVERY TIME

1. **Table A /16 and Table B /24, each with the band descriptor quoted**, then the total /40.
2. **Per-section feedback** across the arc — opening, development, climax or turn, ending — so the
   feedback is granular even though the marks are holistic.
3. **The L5→L6 sentence**: for descriptive, where the focus should have varied; for narrative, which
   of the four features did not land.
4. **A gold-standard rewrite of ONE passage** from their own piece, plus one alternative approach.
5. **SPaG counted, not characterised** — how many errors, of what kind, with the corrections shown.
6. **Every penalty carries a fix-example.**
