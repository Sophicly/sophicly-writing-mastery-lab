## Universal rules (every element)

- **Attempt (bottom):** the sentence is present and doing this element's job on this text. 0 only when absent or
  off-task. Cumulative — entailed by any higher rung. (AQA L0 *"Nothing to reward"*; L2 keyword *"Some, attempts"*.)
- **Middle rungs:** independent — never hurdles (PEDAGOGY §35).
- **Perceptive (top):** perceptive + convincing + judicious. Not traceable to the words on the page → not won.
  = the AQA L3 *"Clear, relevant"* → L4 *"Perceptive, detailed"* step.

## The sixteen rungs (top rung first)

### 1. Topic sentence — 0.5
*Relabel: was "Conceptual topic sentence introducing the paragraph's idea" — "conceptual" is an L4 word, now owned by the top rung.*
| rung | earns it | example |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Argues an idea, not a method (no technique words). Goes past the obvious. Genuinely comes out of the paragraph's quotation. | AQA L4: "The writer's description of the hyena criticises its unpleasant appearance and introduces the idea of it being a malicious threat." |
| **Attempt** | A sentence opens the paragraph and says something about this text and this question. | AQA L1: "The writer makes the hyena sound like a really ugly animal." |

### 2. Technique + quotation + inference — 1.0
| rung | earns it | example |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Quotation is judicious; the inference reads beneath the surface AND can be traced to the quoted words. | AQA L4: "…as if the hyena's ugliness is symbolic of a deeper sinister nature that cannot be reversed." |
| **Evidence + inference** | A relevant quotation from the given lines, with an inference that is accurate and clearly explained. | AQA L3: "The addition of the phrase 'beyond redemption' exaggerates its ugliness further by claiming there is nothing that could ever be done to improve its appearance…" |
| **Technique** | Named accurately, with precise terminology, and it is a LANGUAGE technique. | AQA L3: "…the adjective 'ugly' introduces the animal as hideous." |
| **Attempt** | A quotation or a technique is offered, with some comment on it. | AQA L1: "It says, 'it is ugly beyond redemption'. The writer has used an adjective to show the reader that this is not a nice animal to look at…" |

PEDAGOGY §38 falls out: Qamar's "adrift… foreshadowing" keeps Attempt + Evidence/inference (+ Perceptive if earned), loses only Technique → 0.75.

### 3. Word-level close analysis — 0.5
*Relabel: was "Detailed, perceptive word-level close analysis".*
| rung | earns it | example |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Explains why THIS word (a connotation, sound or form it really carries), then bridges the detail back to the wider technique or idea (micro → macro). | AQA L4: "…'redemption' has connotations of being saved from evil…" |
| **Attempt** | Zooms in on a specific word, sound or punctuation mark from the quotation and says something about it. | ours: "The word 'ugly' tells us the hyena is not nice to look at." |

### 4. Effect on the reader, first sentence — 0.5
| rung | earns it | example |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | A precise, named emotion or thought, tied to the technique that causes it, with the reason the reader responds that way. | ours: "The reader recoils: the bluntness of the short sentence leaves no room for pity, so disgust arrives before curiosity can." |
| **Attempt** | Says what the reader feels, thinks or notices. | AQA L1: "…not an animal you would want to be alone with." |

### 5. Effect on the reader, second sentence — 0.5
| rung | earns it | example |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | A genuinely DIFFERENT effect from the first, further along focus → feel → think → act, tied to a technique, traceable. | ours: "Told that the ugliness cannot be redeemed, the reader begins to treat the hyena's body as evidence of its character, and expects cruelty before it has acted." |
| **Attempt** | A second sentence on the reader's response. A repeat of effect 1 in new words stays here. | ours: "It also makes the reader dislike the hyena." |

### 6. Evaluation of the author's purpose — 1.0
*Relabel: was "Perceptive evaluation of the author's purpose".*
| rung | earns it | example |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptive** | Reaches a bigger idea (what the writer suggests about people, nature or society), explored tentatively (perhaps, arguably), traceable to the language this paragraph analysed. | ours: "Perhaps the writer treats ugliness as a moral sign: a body 'beyond redemption' arguably warns the reader that nature in this story will not be kind." |
| **Clear** | Accurate for this text, answers the question's focus, complete in itself and explained. | ours: "The writer presents the hyena's appearance as beyond repair so that the reader sees a threat, never a victim." |
| **Linked** | Tied to THIS technique and THIS idea — not a purpose that would fit any text ("to engage the reader"). | ours: "The blunt short sentence is there to make the hyena's ugliness feel like a settled fact." |
| **Attempt** | Says why the writer did it, about this text. | ours: "The writer does this to show that the hyena is a horrible animal." |

## Two design decisions put to Neil with the table

1. **2-rung elements cannot show "clear but not perceptive" in the number** — a rough attempt and a clear
   sentence both score 0.25 in elements 1, 3, 4, 5. It is what produces the G4/G6/G9 story, so it is built
   as ruled; the prose still separates them.
2. **Rungs judge the thinking; penalty codes keep judging the mechanics** (H1 hanging quote, F1 weak verb
   cost no rung) — preserves ONE FAULT, ONE CHARGE. Sole overlap: inaccurate technique name loses the
   Technique rung and takes no N1.
