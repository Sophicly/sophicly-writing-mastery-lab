## The ruling this implements

PEDAGOGY.md §30: **a plot-update walk iterates over the LENS, not over the plot.** For Step 8 the
lens is values/traits, so the unit is ONE TRAIT AT A TIME. Not per stage (a 23-item menu — root
CLAUDE.md §18: the student answers one and skips the rest), and not per beat (Step 6 emits 98–108
beat rows; seven update steps × 100 beats is not a lesson).

PEDAGOGY.md §29 governs what happens to a tagged beat: per-beat amalgamation, one operation at a
time, with a CDO-shaped ask — *what should this beat show · where doesn't yours show it · rewrite
it* — never "improve this". Then ONE whole-stage continuity pass at the end, for contradiction only.

## Orientation (served as paced chunks, one per tap — §4b)

**Chunk 1.**
Last step you worked out which traits your protagonist has, and whether each one is in balance, in
excess or in deficit. That was about who they ARE. This step asks a harder question: **does your
story actually SHOW it?**

**Chunk 2.**
A trait you named but never dramatised is invisible to a reader. They cannot see your notes — they
only see the beats. So we are going to take the traits you flagged, one at a time, and find where
each one actually appears in the outline you built.

**Chunk 3.**
Two things worth knowing before we start. **One:** a trait usually appears in more than one beat, and
it should — a protagonist in deficit on courage does not become brave in a single moment. **Two:**
"it doesn't show anywhere yet" is a real answer and often the most useful one, because it names
something worth adding. It costs you one tap.

**Chunk 4 (the ask begins).**
Your outline is open beside the chat. I will show you a trait, and you tap the beats where it
shows. Nothing gets deleted at any point — we only ever add.

## The per-trait ask

Shape, per PEDAGOGY §30 and CLAUDE.md §4c (criteria upfront · worked example inline · the concrete
action last):

**A trait shows in a beat when:**
- the protagonist DOES something that could only be done by someone with (or without) it
- a reader who was told nothing could point at the beat and name it
- it is visible in action, not in the narrator explaining the character

**Worked example — courage, in deficit at the start:**
> In *Of Mice and Men*, Lennie's deficit isn't announced. It shows when he hides the dead puppy
> rather than telling anyone. A reader sees a man who cannot face what he has done — nobody had to
> say "Lennie lacked courage".

**Worked example — a trait in excess:**
> Macbeth's ambition in excess shows in the murder of Banquo. Not in the soliloquy where he doubts
> himself; in the point where he acts on it against his own conscience.

**The ask:**
> **{Trait}** — you marked it *{condition}* at the {when}.
> Tap every beat where a reader could actually see this. If it doesn't show anywhere yet, tap that
> instead.

Rung 1 (`💡 More examples`) serves two further worked examples from different texts. Rung 2 points at
the guide and the Table of Techniques. Rung 3, quietest and last, is the only one that costs an API
call.

## The amalgamation ask (only for tagged beats — §29's CDO shape)

> **Stage {N} — {beat label}**
> Your beat says: *{the student's own beat text}*
> You tagged it for **{trait}** ({condition}).
>
> 1. What would a reader have to SEE in this beat for that to land?
> 2. Where does your version not show it yet?
> 3. Rewrite the beat so it does.

The rewrite is appended to the beat, never written over it. If the student is happy as it stands,
that is one tap and the walk moves on — a beat that already works does not need changing.

---

## The continuity pass (once, at the end)

> Read your six stages through once, in order. You are looking for **contradictions only** — a trait
> that is in deficit in Stage V but was already fixed in Stage III, or a change that happens twice.
> You are not revising the writing here.

Then the process-goal close: **"Lens 1 of 7 built in."** Six updates remain (Steps 12, 15, 18, 21,
24, 27), each adding a layer to this same outline.
