## Text A
**Title:** Green shopping won’t save the planet
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** The following text is an article about individuals and the environment.

1  It’s easy being green … too easy. From adorable reusable shopping bags to plastic-free beauty
2  products, the proliferation of green goods makes doing our bit for the planet fun. Indeed, a poll
3  released recently found that most Americans, for example, are shopping for the good of the
4  planet: 76 per cent said they’d bought a product specifically because they thought it was better
5  for the environment.

6  Shopping habits have become one manifestation of how saving the planet has become a
7  matter of personal change rather than of collective action. However, every example of major
8  environmental progress – reducing acid rain, improving air quality, restoring the ozone layer –
9  has been the result of national legislation or a global treaty. We reduced acid rain by restricting
10 industry’s emissions, not by all going out and sprinkling bicarbonate on sensitive forests and
11 lakes. Ozone-chomping CFC chemicals were banned by international laws, not by everyone
12 giving up spray cans and air conditioning.

13 Environmental progress is made by forcing people and industry to stop doing environmentally bad
14 things and start doing environmentally good things, not by relying on individuals’ green goodwill.
15 Recycling figures are up in most countries but acquiring consumer goods, a very environmentally
16 damaging pursuit, is also up. In the ‘reduce, reuse, recycle’ mantra, recycling is the last resort,
17 after all.

18 Just to be clear: recycling, cutting energy use, buying locally and the rest of the ‘what you can do’
19 agenda is better than the opposite. I use public transport (easy in cities, of course), walk almost
20 everywhere else, recycle, sun-dry clothes, keep the house cold in winter and hot in summer
21 – some sacrifice for the sake of the planet, let me tell you! The problem with the emphasis on
22 changing individual behaviour is this: it makes too many of us believe we have done our part.

23 In her new book, ‘Green Gone Wrong’, journalist Heather Rogers calls the emphasis on individual
24 green acts – especially shopping – rather than collective ones, ‘lazy environmentalism’. But it’s
25 nearly ubiquitous. When Rogers gives speeches about garbage, someone always tells her they
26 thought ‘we could cure our environmental ills by consuming the right products,’ she writes.

27 The message that we cannot consume our way out of climate change, or shop our way out
28 of crashing fisheries, or vanishing species or depleted seas isn’t as much fun as, ‘Buy green!’
29 Compared with the scale of the disaster, changing individual behaviour is pathetically inadequate.

## Text B
**Title:** Giving up hope won’t save the planet
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** The following text is an interview given by a well known conservationist.

1  We are destroying the world at a very rapid rate; many people are giving up hope and thinking,
2  ‘Well, there’s nothing I can do on my own.’ The rain forests are disappearing everywhere. Big
3  dams are draining whole countries of their water supply as water becomes more precious than
4  gold; there’s mining, there’s fracking, there’s drilling for oil. We’re in the middle of a great species
5  extinction; we’re losing biodiversity everywhere and we’re burning fossil fuels very, very fast.

6  We are breeding billions of animals just to eat them. This means that whole habitats are being
7  destroyed to grow grain; fossil fuels are being used to take the grain to the animals, the animals
8  to slaughter, and the meat to the table. In addition, the animals are producing masses of methane
9  gas, and that’s a very virulent greenhouse gas which speeds up climate change.

10 We need to start thinking about how everybody can lead more sustainable lives. The wealthy
11 need to start thinking about their environmental footprint – What do I buy? What do I eat? What
12 do I wear? How was it made? Where did it come from? – and thinking whether they need all the
13 stuff that they buy, the meat they eat and the plastics they use.

14 I support an environmental project for young people called Roots and Shoots. It started with 12
15 high school students in Tanzania, and now has members from kindergarten to university in 100
16 countries. The project’s main message is that every individual, every school or college, every
17 community makes a difference every day. Every group chooses itself three projects that are
18 important to the area around them and that can make things better for people, for animals and for
19 the environment – everything from planting trees to recycling, from reducing waste or changing
20 what money is spent on within their organisation, to growing organic food.

21 Young people care about the planet and their future on it. We have a window of opportunity for
22 making some changes, for slowing down climate change. But it’s going to require a lot of effort.
