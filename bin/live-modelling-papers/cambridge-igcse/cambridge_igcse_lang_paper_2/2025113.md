## Text A
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** This text is part of an online podcast discussion between podcast hosts Harpreet and Esther about different attitudes to making mistakes.

1  Harpreet: What I find really annoying are those people who never admit they’re wrong, even
2  when it’s obvious that they have really messed things up.

3  Esther: Yeah.

4  Harpreet: The other day at the sports club, I could see that one of the runners was hobbling
5  around and not really joining in with the warm-ups. She just kept saying she was feeling a bit
6  stiff when it was obvious she’d pulled a muscle because she’d not warmed up properly – even
7  though she said she had.

8  Esther: She might just have been trying really hard, wanting to impress the others? Was it one of
9  the new runners?

10 Harpreet: No, that’s the worst of it. She is one of the older club members who sometimes runs
11 classes and is always telling everyone else how it’s important to do this stretch and that stretch,
12 but then doesn’t even do them herself. It’s not the first time either – she was injured most of last
13 season.

14 Esther: So much for learning from your mistakes then! I mean that’s important, right? Owning
15 your mistake and being better next time? Do you think people are just ashamed to do that? I
16 know a lot of people who get really embarrassed about making a mistake and can’t accept it
17 happened. They usually transfer the blame onto someone else, or even deny it, especially when
18 money is involved.

19 Harpreet: You’re right, no one enjoys being wrong, and what if they’re a trainee brain surgeon or
20 something? Pretty bad if they don’t admit their mistakes and learn from them. Or an artist – if they
21 make a mistake, they wouldn’t just leave it. They’d keep practising, painting over it, or rubbing it
22 out and drawing it again, until they got it perfect. Actually, when you think of it, that’s how most
23 artists work – they see making mistakes as part of being creative. There are loads of times in life
24 when failure is OK. It’s even expected.

25 Esther: Sure, you’d have to read a fiction book to find someone who got everything right first
26 time.

27 Harpreet: Exactly. Unless you’re one of those ‘fake it till you make it’ types. They always seem
28 like they’ve got life sorted, but it’s all about image with them and pretending they know how to do
29 things.

30 Esther: Well, ‘fake it until you make it’ is more than just a catchy saying; researchers have
31 conducted studies on the topic. People say it’s really important to have an optimistic mindset
32 and believe in yourself – you know, the idea that you look confident and so eventually you feel
33 confident.

34 Harpreet: But aren’t they really just deceiving themselves, as well as everyone else?

35 Esther: I suppose so. I mean, I’m not going to advocate hypocrisy, or blatant self-deception,
36 but in some cases, a few psychological ‘lies’ to oneself or others can really improve someone’s
37 mood. It could help them to get through a tricky time until they’re sure of what they’re doing and
38 have got the experience they need. The signals you give on the outside can change how you feel
39 on the inside too.

40 Harpreet: What do you mean?

41 Esther: Well, it’s all about the mind–body connection. You can force a smile, and it relaxes your
42 vocal cords and affects your hormones. Just that loosening of face muscles triggers a happier
43 mood and encourages friendliness.

44 Harpreet: So we should all be going round pretending we know what we’re doing when we don’t,
45 sailing through life not worrying about the impact of our mistakes on others, or covering things
46 up, instead of admitting the mistake and offering to put it right?

47 Esther: I don’t think that’s really what it’s about. Some people probably just fake happiness and
48 confidence in front of other people to keep the perfect image of themselves. When they’re alone,
49 mistakes they’ve made and denied can make them feel like ‘imposters’ who’ll be found out soon.
50 The question for our listeners really is how do we respond when it turns out we were wrong and
51 have made a mistake – when we realise we should have done X rather than Y, or when we find
52 out that the person we thought had stolen something from us was innocent all along?

53 Harpreet: Or, when we realise we used up the last of the milk and there’s none left for coffee?

54 Esther: Ah yes, that too. I’ll get some more later. Sorry.
