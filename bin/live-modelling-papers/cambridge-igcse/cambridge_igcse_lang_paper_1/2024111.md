## Text A
**Title:** Bees
**Author:** Unknown

1  A bee colony is populated by a queen, about 200 male drones and thousands of
2  underdeveloped female workers. The queen is the largest member of the colony due to the
3  workers having fed her a special diet.

4  The queen is the mother of all the workers and also the male drones in the hive. She remains
5  inside, attended by the workers who groom and feed her. The sole purpose of the drone is
6  to mate with her so that the hive continues.

7  Bees have different characteristics that make them suited to pollinating certain plants. For
8  example, the early bumblebee’s small size and agility allow it to enter plants with drooping
9  flowers. The larger garden bumblebee is better at pollinating the deep flowers because its
10 longer tongue can reach deep inside them.

11 Plants can only grow because of pollination. Bees’ transference of pollen between flowering
12 plants enables a plant to reproduce and produce food. The cycle of a plant’s life keeps turning
13 in this way.

14 So thanks to these small creatures, we enjoy a range of foods from orchard fruits to coffee
15 and vanilla. And if you are wearing cotton, that’s because a cotton plant was pollinated.
16 Maybe in your lessons at school you have come across bees too. This would be because
17 many poets, artists, authors and scientists have found them a fascinating subject to study.

18 But bees are in trouble. Nowadays, many of us are aware of how human decisions impact
19 on bee populations. A few organisations try to educate the public on how the pesticides we
20 use on crops to make our food safer and more prolific are toxic to bees, damaging their
21 mental capacities and ability to reproduce. Bee habitats, such as ponds, trees and flowers,
22 are wiped out by our need to urbanise and build houses and by intensive farming methods.
23 We really need to ask ourselves how much our thoughtlessness and selfishness might be
24 costing us all.

## Text B
**Title:** Beekeeping
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** This text is advice given by a practising beekeeper to other people considering keeping bees.

1  Why be a beekeeper? Is it because you like honey? Want to help with local pollination? Aid
2  the global honeybee crisis? Beekeeping is hard work and a considerable responsibility, so
3  needs a lot of thought.

4  Actually, you may have no choice in the matter if beekeeping is forbidden in your area. My
5  town requires hive registrations. You might just live somewhere where there are no restrictions
6  on beekeeping, but that would be lucky.

7  Okay, so you are allowed to keep bees. It took me a while to receive a reply to my request
8  for help from the online beekeeping organisation I contacted, presumably because the
9  beekeepers were all out and about pursuing their hobby. In the end I discovered that there
10 was a lot of advice out there, which I sifted through, finding some of it anecdotal and specific
11 to certain circumstances rather than of use to me.

12 Naturally, I assumed that my spacesuit-like body and head protection would shield me from
13 stings. Choosing clothing that is effective in keeping an angry swarm at bay is important,
14 but bear in mind there is always the occasional unavoidable sting. Yes, it’s happened to me
15 even in a full beekeeping suit with gloves.

16 My bees fly up to eight kilometres when out foraging for nectar and pollen, and that’s fine
17 as I live in a rural area. Your urban neighbours may not be so happy about you keeping
18 beehives in your back garden while they are out enjoying the sun.

19 And that brings me onto another matter. I have a field for my hives. They should be placed
20 on a level site, receive sun during the day and be sheltered from strong winds, as well as
21 being easily accessible for you and their stripy occupants.

22 In the beginning, I bought a heavy hive, intending to give my bees a robust and safe home.
23 By harvest time, when the hives were full of honey, my aching back taught me I needed to
24 purchase a lighter construction. Still now, I need a trusted friend (a fellow beekeeper) to help
25 me shift those hives around.

26 The same friend and I have a reciprocal arrangement to look after our bees when either of
27 us goes away. Bees can’t be left for long periods. Hiring someone else to look after your
28 bees can be costly and you need to be sure that person knows what they’re doing.

29 I spent a lot of money getting started and I made a few expensive purchasing errors, from
30 heavy hives to beekeeping accessories that I never actually used.

## Text C
**Title:** The business venture
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** Damian and Abdul were school friends. As adults, they have gone into business together.

1  Tuesday evening, driving the van home, Damian laughs in pleasure.

2  ‘Sold out in one morning. Brilliant work for our very first day at the market! I’ll try some new
3  ranges and we can take double to sell next week.’

4  Abdul does not share Damian’s optimism. ‘We don’t want to go too quickly, and the van
5  freezer isn’t very big,’ he cautions.

***

6  A few months earlier, Damian and Abdul had been relaxing in Damian’s field, near his
7  beehives. It had been the first mild morning for many months and the two men were sipping
8  Damian’s home-made acacia honey and berry smoothies.

9  The delicate tracery of acacia leaves cast a dancing dappled shade over the beehives. A
10 worker bee, one of the first to venture uncertainly out of the hive that morning, drew dizzy
11 spirals in the air, as it orientated itself, sensing the nectar in the newly budding flowers.
12 Damian could faintly hear the drones inside the hive humming in reverential attendance on
13 their queen. He looked up. Any day now those acacia trees would burst into flower and
14 cascades of clustered white blossom would turn the trees silver. Then this solitary worker
15 would be accompanied by hundreds of other labourers, all intent on drinking their fill of nectar.

16 ‘I agree with that little bee,’ announced Damian, smacking his lips in pleasure at the taste
17 of honey and berries. ‘We should venture out of our own hives and taste the sweet nectar
18 of life. Get ahead of the crowd. Nice big shop in the middle of town.’

19 ‘All the online advice is that small businesses should start slowly. No risk-taking. Trying it
20 out just once a week in the local market is best,’ replied Abdul.

21 ‘There isn’t another smoothie store in town, and no one who makes smoothies like I do!’
22 Damian protested.

23 A few weeks later, Damian conceded defeat when Abdul showed him his painstaking work.
24 ‘My survey suggests that people in the town like smoothies, but they are not prepared to
25 pay too high a price for them. We need to encourage them to try ours.’

26 Facts and figures didn’t interest Damian. He wandered into the house to continue working
27 on his smoothie ranges. All his creations were conventionally sweet: cherries, pineapples
28 and strawberries blended with the bees’ acacia honey, lumps of ice and different milks. He
29 wanted to diversify.

30 A worker bee swung through the kitchen window and settled momentarily by the honey jar.
31 It started to carefully probe droplets of honey that had puddled on the work top, smiling to
32 itself in self-congratulatory glee. ‘What shall I turn my talents to next?’ Damian asked the
33 bee. The bee’s segmented antennae rotated through a full 90 degrees, and then folded
34 outwards, as if in a quizzical hand gesture. Its gossamer wings reclined, and a contemplative
35 silence consumed the kitchen. ‘You’re no help!’ reprimanded Damian playfully.

36 Damian returned to Abdul. ‘How about a savoury range?’ he suggested. ‘I was thinking of
37 pizza ’

38 Abdul didn’t answer. He was looking thoughtful. ‘We could put a few of those cherry ones
39 on special offer to attract buyers. I’ve also been looking at the amount of packaging we plan
40 to use and whether it is eco-friendly. And we need a portable freezer and a big van.’

***

41 Damian smiles, remembering back to that conversation. He parks the van and observes the
42 beehives. A huge swarm of bees flies over the top and up towards the acacia tree.

43 Abdul watches too. ‘I guess we could have rivals if other people realise how successful our
44 business is.’

45 ‘Then we should think bigger and get ahead. A shop that is open every day of the week. I
46 could live there.’

47 ‘But then who would look after your bees?’ asks Abdul, as the two men contemplate the
48 crowds of tiny golden bodies intent on pursuing their own businesses in the white blossom.
