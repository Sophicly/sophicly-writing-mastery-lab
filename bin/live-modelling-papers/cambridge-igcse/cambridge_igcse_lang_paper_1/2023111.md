## Text A
**Title:** The early history of flying
**Author:** Unknown

1  The ability to fly has always fascinated humans. Stories of people attempting to fly can be
2  found in many ancient cultures. However, past human attempts to range freely in the skies
3  have often had bumpy consequences.

4  In Greek mythology there is the legend of Daedalus and Icarus, the father and son who
5  created wings by combining feathers and wax to escape from imprisonment in Crete. In 852
6  CE, inventor Armen Firman was researching what enabled birds to fly. There is a story that
7  he covered his body with feathers and created wing-like garments that he attached to his
8  arms. He then jumped from a tower. Although his attempt was unsuccessful, the garments
9  slowed his descent, allowing him to survive with only minor injuries.

10 Artist and inventor Leonardo da Vinci developed early plans for an aircraft in the fifteenth
11 century. He sketched flying machines that used flapping wings to generate both lift and
12 propulsion. The pilot lay forward, employing arms and legs to operate these wings.

13 Imaginative as da Vinci’s designs were, the fundamental barrier to a human operating his
14 machine was the limited muscle power of humans compared to birds; he could never have
15 overcome this basic fact of human physiology. There was also the issue of how the machine,
16 which had no engine, would be launched by mere wings. A few foolhardy individuals may
17 have been prepared to risk life and limb by trying this out.

18 In the twenty-first century, the emphasis is on passenger comfort and entertainment, especially
19 during long-haul flights where passengers are seated for many hours. Travel by air remains
20 the speediest way of reaching a destination, if perhaps the least interesting for those who
21 enjoy watching the route. Many people will sacrifice environmental concerns and the high
22 costs of flying for speed and convenience.

## Text B
**Title:** Annoying passengers
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** In this text a flight attendant speaks about the things that passengers do that are inconsiderate or unsafe.

1  Some people treat us as if we are quite literally ‘air-heads’. A little politeness costs nothing.

2  If you have an injury or a disability, of course we will happily assist, but some able-bodied
3  passengers expect us to load their heavy bags into overhead stowage. If you can’t lift your
4  bag above your head, please arrange for it to go in the cargo area. Don’t take advantage.

5  The ‘call bell’ is intended to alert crew of an emergency: when a passenger takes ill, for
6  example. Some passengers use this when they want us to bring them drinks or blankets. If
7  the aisle isn’t congested, walk to the staff area to make such requests. Then you can also
8  stretch your legs. It’s actually not good for your circulation to sit too long.

9  You may enjoy loud music. Others don’t. You will find free earphones stored in the back of
10 the seat directly in front of you. They are yours to keep.

11 Some people ignore the seatbelt warning. Your seatbelt ensures that you are safe during
12 take-off and landing. I don’t understand why some people pretend they have their seatbelt
13 fastened when they haven’t. And you can spot these people a mile off by their guilty
14 expressions.

15 You’ve been with us for hours, dying to disembark, but when it’s finally time to leave, you
16 spend ages putting your shoes on and searching under seats. We’re thrilled you like us so
17 much, but we just want to remove our smiles and rest our tired legs. If you are a passenger
18 of limited mobility, of course this is a different story and we will never make you feel rushed
19 to get off the plane.

20 Oh, and talking of seats: we understand that you’re annoyed you haven’t been given a seat
21 next to your friend, but we really can’t do anything now you are on the plane. The issue
22 should have been addressed at check-in hours earlier. On many flights, passengers have
23 paid extra for a particular seat, and they shouldn’t feel pressured to give it up because you
24 are throwing an unbecoming tantrum!

25 Still on seats, you’d be surprised at the state some people leave their area in when they
26 disembark. I remember one family eating a bag of pistachio nuts and then throwing the shells
27 onto the floor. They weren’t even embarrassed.

28 As you’re going to be confined to a small space for hours, any strong food smells will get
29 magnified. You may prefer to enjoy your fast-food on the plane rather than in the terminal,
30 but other passengers will complain. And it’s not like you can just open your window to get
31 rid of the smell!

## Text C
**Title:** The new job
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** This text is about a young person called Jeswin who has just started to work for an airline company called Golden Tours, as part of their airport ‘Meet and Greet’ team. Jeswin is being looked after on his first day by a senior member of the team, Edgar.

1  Edgar laid his large hand on Jeswin’s small shoulder and strode onto the wide Departures
2  concourse, as Jeswin tripped and danced to keep up with him.

3  ‘You’re going to be very busy today,’ Edgar announced, an ironic grimace contorting his
4  round face. Edgar stopped suddenly and twisted Jeswin’s shoulder through a dizzying one
5  hundred and eighty degrees so that Jeswin could see the workplace. The concourse at
6  present was eerily silent. It comprised many empty roped-off labyrinths that passengers
7  would negotiate before they reached the check-in desk, where their luggage would be
8  surrendered into the safe hands of Golden Tours staff and deposited in the cargo area of
9  the plane.

10 Jeswin wriggled to detach himself from Edgar’s large hand. He had been attached (in a more
11 figurative sense) today to Edgar to learn his job: making sure that the passengers had the
12 documents they needed to fly, showing them where to take their luggage and giving helpful
13 and friendly advice.

14 Edgar’s expression began to radiate mild exasperation and Jeswin turned to see its focus:
15 a small knot of passengers wandering uncertainly towards the Golden Tours check-in
16 area. Their luggage trolley was performing a shaky dance, not dissimilar to the movements
17 of Jeswin’s own feet a few minutes earlier. A small child hung onto its side trying to deter
18 three disobedient suitcases from unscheduled disembarkation.

19 Jeswin immediately understood that this family might need support.

20 ‘I’ve got a report to write for the boss tonight!’ huffed Edgar. ‘Apparently she doesn’t know
21 what our job involves. Hah!’

22 He marched towards the group. ‘Good morning. Your documents, please, so I can make
23 sure they are in order before you drop your luggage off.’ Edgar flipped through identity
24 documents with the dexterity of a master player shuffling a deck of cards, before surveying
25 the three suitcases. ‘Take it you packed those suitcases yourselves and they haven’t been
26 out of sight since you got here? Go to the roped area over there. Shouldn’t be too long to
27 wait.’

28 Now a few more groups were drawing closer. Jeswin approached the nearest group and
29 followed the routine. This continued throughout the morning, meeting and greeting passengers
30 bound for numerous destinations. Edgar had also told him to watch out for unattended
31 luggage and report such items to him. This worried Jeswin: it felt like too much to focus on
32 at once, especially as there seemed to be a lot of luggage around. Generally, though, Jeswin
33 was pleased with himself. He was not as fast as Edgar, of course, but he remembered the
34 ‘meet and greet’ procedure, and he felt his courteous manner and diligence paid off, as he
35 often received smiles and thanks in return.

36 Edgar on the other hand was becoming more irritable. ‘Fifteen minutes for lunch, as usual,’
37 he grumbled. ‘I’ve hardly seen you this morning, young man. Has everything been all right?’

38 Suddenly a shout came up from one of the queues. ‘Hey! That man tried to steal my wallet!’
39 An angry-faced accuser stood, finger aimed at an older man behind him.

40 Edgar immediately intervened. ‘Have we got a thief?’ he demanded.

41 ‘No, I’m not a thief. I’m a little unsteady on my feet and I fell over.’ The older man’s chin
42 wobbled as he spoke, and Jeswin saw his eyes blinking rapidly.

43 Surely he was innocent.

44 ‘Let me help,’ Jeswin said. ‘There is probably some mistake here, but you, sir, have had a
45 bit of a shock and need reassuring about your possessions, so I am going to ask Leo over
46 there to let you put your luggage in right now. Meanwhile, you, sir, mustn’t keep standing in
47 this uncomfortable way: I am going to carry your suitcase over there to Maria.’

48 As Jeswin returned from Maria’s desk, he saw Edgar smiling in approval. ‘Well handled,
49 young man. You’ve given me some ideas for this report I’ve got to write.’
