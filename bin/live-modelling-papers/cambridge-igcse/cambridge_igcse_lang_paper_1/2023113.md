## Text A
**Title:** Violin playing
**Author:** Unknown

1  The violin is a musical instrument of high pitch, played with a bow. It has four strings and a
2  round body, narrowed at the middle, and two f-shaped soundholes.

3  An ingenious feat of mechanical design, the violin is also an immensely beautiful instrument.
4  Violin makers often take pride in showcasing their great skill, paying particular attention to
5  the scroll, a decorative piece of woodcarving on the violin’s neck. They intricately carve and
6  even personalise a violin for customers.

7  Just about anyone can learn to play a violin from a relatively young age. However, professional
8  violinists must build up their knowledge and skills if they are going to compete for places in
9  good orchestras. A superior playing technique is an obvious skill: violinists must be able to
10 bow, finger and pluck the strings of their instrument in a wide variety of ways. Knowledge of
11 great works for the violin is another requirement. From historical pieces by Beethoven to
12 more recent compositions by Chen Yi, there is violin music in all genres that players may
13 be expected to be familiar with.

14 Besides playing in orchestras, a trained violinist may find other career opportunities. Many
15 supplement their incomes by giving music lessons. Some may be lucky enough to find work
16 at special events, such as weddings. Others might take their interest into business, for
17 example by opening a music shop, where they can talk knowledgeably to customers about
18 something they love.

19 Violinists usually need to perform minor maintenance on their own instruments. They also
20 need to understand its components in order to have a meaningful dialogue with other
21 musicians. But exceptional violinists will understand their violin in a deeper sense and are
22 not motivated by a need to make a living. Training alone can never enable those musicians
23 who lack great talent to penetrate the heart of their own instrument or to know how to make
24 it sing in that hauntingly beautiful way that makes audiences tremble in the presence of
25 genius. A few violinists live to make music for its own sake.

## Text B
**Title:** A career in music
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** This text is about how a young person first became interested in violin playing and how they made a career of it later on.

1  At 11 years old, I approached my school music department in search of violin lessons. Others
2  were enrolling for the orchestra, so I decided to join. Who wanted to be different? Not me.
3  Realistically, I had no idea what I was getting myself into.

4  But to my surprise and delight, I found it was fun. Soon I was spending my entire break-times
5  in the music room, learning about bowing, fingering and plucking my violin. And I made
6  several lifelong friends.

7  In fact, violin playing began to change my life in a way that was pleasing. Before, I was
8  considered ‘below average’ and my general school grades were poor. Now, suddenly, music
9  was turning everything the other way around. I was considered successful by classmates
10 and teachers, and I rose to that version of me.

11 Hearing my violin create awesomeness in the school orchestra also became life changing.
12 Once, I had no clue what my career was to be. Now I knew I was born to play orchestral
13 music.

14 I wanted to live among those musicians who practised hard to be good at the thing it seemed
15 I was naturally good at: performing. Work would not be work. Work would be satisfying and
16 fulfilling.

17 There was one problem though. According to society, my friends and I were too smart to
18 have careers in music. You see, where I come from, nobody makes a living playing the violin,
19 or with music at all – unless you’re a celebrity or something. Even the teachers told us to
20 become doctors or engineers.

21 My science teacher said, ‘Music is not for you: your average point score is too good.’

22 Little did she realise that her attempt to define my future path only made my desire to play
23 my violin with others like me even stronger.

24 And, yes, after leaving school, my two best friends went to do scientific work, but I joined
25 orchestras. Since then, I have played my violin to audiences who pay me the immediate
26 compliment of – sometimes rapturous – applause, while my friends do their science research
27 behind closed doors, uncertain of when or how their work will be received.

28 They claim to be happy with this, but I would not be able to stand the stress myself.
29 Occasionally we share nostalgic conversations, when they tell me wistfully how they’d love
30 to experience the release, the creative expression and the sheer magic maybe just once
31 again.

## Text C
**Title:** The violinist
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** This text is taken from a story about a violin player called Seth who plays in a travelling orchestra. The orchestra has arrived in a town where Seth will be playing a violin solo in the evening.

1  Seth was roaming the streets and looking in shop windows. A few hours’ idle distraction
2  made for a more focused mind later, he found. More significantly, he really enjoyed getting
3  to see the locality he would perform in while possibly learning a bit about the people who
4  might attend the concert.

5  He stopped in the presence of a small music store and decided to look inside.

6  Partially concealed at the back of the shop was a display of string instruments, at the centre
7  of which sat an absurdly squat violin. To Seth it looked just like a plump monarch holding
8  court amidst other carefully arranged instruments. He stared at it, drawn by its commanding
9  presence. The maker had put considerable effort into carving the decorative top (or scroll)
10 into a magnetically charming face with generous lips pulled sideways and flattened into an
11 expression of permanent merriment. But it was the cheerful eyes, now apparently winking
12 and dancing at Seth, that really captivated him.

13 ‘I dare you ’ the violin’s eyes teased.

14 ‘How quirky yet attractive,’ thought Seth. ‘I wonder if it plays well.’

15 ‘May I try that violin, please?’ he asked a shop attendant.

16 Violin in hand, Seth stopped four strings with his fingers, and drew the bow over them with
17 one rapid sweep. He’d produced a rich chord. He was surprised by how strong the sound
18 was. He raised his bow again and the violin replied enchantingly.

19 Seth felt excitement shooting through him. He turned to the attendant. ‘How much is it?’ he
20 asked.

21 ‘You can have it for a fair price,’ said the attendant. ‘I didn’t pay much for it. A local brought
22 it in last month. Said he’d found it a long time ago. Tried to find out who owned it at the time,
23 but without success. Then he just forgot about it.’

24 Later in the concert hall, Seth opened the violin case and looked contentedly at his purchase.
25 He had his old trusty violin by his side but knew he would play this new one tonight. It felt
26 irrational, but it was almost as if the violin longed to be played and especially in this venue.
27 The last hour of rehearsing had proved what a magnificent instrument it was.

28 The arrival of the audience and the preparations of the other musicians were now taking
29 place. A multitude of subdued murmurs in the audience accompanied the discordant scraping
30 of strings and sharply persistent blowing of reeds. Now and then a loud trombone would
31 assert itself, or an oboe’s plaintive notes would rise, demanding immediate attention to its
32 great misery.

33 It was time for Seth’s solo. His new violin sang out beautifully around the hall. The audience
34 was completely still, apart from a woman who had inclined forward, as if straining to see the
35 instrument he was playing.

36 An hour later, as he left, he saw the same woman waiting outside. ‘Hello, I’m Sylvia,’ she
37 said. ‘Well, that was a surprise! I haven’t heard that violin played for years.’

38 ‘It’s one of the best violins I’ve ever played,’ Seth replied. ‘Do you know the story of this
39 scroll?’

40 In reply, Sylvia showed Seth a photograph on her phone. Seth found himself staring back
41 at a pair of bright, cheerful eyes that winked and danced at him. ‘Father,’ Sylvia explained.
42 ‘My parents played in the orchestra at this very concert hall years ago. Mother had this violin
43 made with father’s face on the scroll. It disappeared after a show about five years ago. The
44 orchestra had to leave straight after to go on tour and mother was devastated not to be able
45 to take that violin. My parents don’t play at all these days, though they sometimes attend
46 concerts here to support the local orchestra.’ She paused. ‘I think mother will be pleased to
47 hear that the violin has been found.’

48 ‘May I contact your parents?’ asked Seth.
