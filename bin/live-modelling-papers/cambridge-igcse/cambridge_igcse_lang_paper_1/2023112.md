## Text A
**Title:** Mountain River Cave
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** This text is about the world’s largest cave, located in Vietnam.

1  Vietnam is home to many spectacular landscapes. From cascading rice terraces to mazes
2  of rivers with rice paddies, there are many sights to see. A little-known fact, however, is that
3  Vietnam is also home to the world’s largest cave, Mountain River Cave, located in the heart
4  of Phong Nha-Ke Bang National Park. The cave was formed between 400 and 450 million
5  years ago, stretches for over 5 kilometres and reaches heights of 200 metres.

6  Mountain River Cave was first discovered in 1990 by a local called Ho Khanh who would
7  trek the jungles of the national park in search of food and timber to sell. He saw a dark
8  opening, and when he peered tentatively inside, he saw clouds and heard the strange gurgle
9  of a river. He was wary about investigating further, so he went home and forgot where he’d
10 found the cave entrance.

11 The cave was re-discovered years later in 2008, by Ho Khanh again, just before August
12 when heavy rains would have made the cave inaccessible. This time, Ho Khanh took note
13 of the path and led two caving experts to the cave for the first expedition. Fortunately, they
14 were physically fit, as exploring the cave fully would have been difficult otherwise. In 2013,
15 the cave was opened to the public, but with a two-year waiting list to visit.

16 The ecosystem created within the cave has its own weather system. A visitor will find a
17 stalagmite standing at 80 metres tall, which is impressive as cave stalagmites tend to grow
18 no more than 10 centimetres per thousand years. Collapsed ceilings have created openings
19 above, allowing foliage to grow inside the cave. There are jungles and rivers enveloped by
20 misty clouds, and microorganisms thriving in the darkness. Fossils date back millions of
21 years.

22 To reach Mountain River Cave, a visitor needs to pass through the only village located inside
23 the national park. This village is only accessible by foot, as it is surrounded by dense jungle.
24 It is sometimes possible to buy some provisions here. It then takes two days of intense jungle
25 trekking from the village to reach the Mountain River Cave entrance.

## Text B
**Title:** Visiting a cave
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** In this text, an experienced cave explorer gives advice to young people wanting to explore caves.

1  I have visited caves all over the world. With their distinct smells, creatures that you rarely
2  see outside and multiple different sounds, these magical grottos tempt you to enter them.
3  It’s easy to act on impulse when out trekking and you see an unexpected cave mouth smiling
4  invitingly at you.

5  But please don’t enter! Caves can be treacherous: no two caves are alike and, exciting
6  though the unknown may seem, safety must come first. It sounds boring, but, wherever
7  possible, on my first visit to a cave, I join a guided tour. After that I am better positioned to
8  decide whether a second visit by myself is viable.

9  Maybe there are no available guides, but you have been reassured that a nearby cave is
10 totally safe and everyone goes, apparently. Some foolhardy thrill-seekers even visit at night,
11 without a torch!

12 That doesn’t mean you should do the same. How well qualified are these people seeking to
13 reassure you? Are they members of the area cave group or simply some local people you
14 met in a café?

15 Okay, you have made sure that your cave visit will be safe. Your responsible guide tells you
16 to enter the cave slowly. This allows your eyes to adjust to the dark, meaning that you don’t
17 miss any interesting rock features early on. Also, you are more likely to notice any potential
18 hazards, such as uneven or wet and slippery floor formations. You are, of course, wearing
19 appropriate footwear.

20 Switch on your torch (you didn’t forget it, I hope) before daylight from the cave mouth
21 disappears. Now comes a fun part: when you can see, what is enhanced by your artificial
22 light? Are there any unusual colours in the rock formations? Did anything move or try to hide
23 when your light came on?

24 Some caves are barren and empty. Others are homes for bats, cave spiders, hibernating
25 animals and many other life forms. Probably none of these creatures is dangerous, but do
26 be careful not to scare them. Just remember where you are and don’t get too distracted by
27 the delights you have suddenly become aware of. If you’re with a group, it could be unsafe
28 to stray. Maybe ask the group leader to slow down if the group is moving too quickly for you,
29 but if this isn’t possible, you must do as you are told.

## Text C
**Title:** The lost cave
**Author:** Unknown
**Context:** Rohaan wants to start up his own building company called RS Building Works. He has decided to build an office in the garden to work in and to meet his clients.

1  Rohaan was tugging at the thick green ivy plant smothering the three-metre rock face at the
2  bottom of the garden.

3  Shaima looked at the ivy cascading down, curtain-like, over the natural wall. ‘It softens that
4  overhanging rock,’ she commented.

5  ‘It’s all in the way, I’m afraid, and I’m not sure what’s behind it,’ said Rohaan. ‘If RS Building
6  Works is going to be successful, I need space to work in. We have to make some sacrifices
7  if we are going to earn money.’

8  ‘I understand,’ said Shaima. She returned to the house. She was working on an important
9  assignment for her college archaeology course. ‘One day, I will go on a real archaeological
10 dig,’ she mused, ‘rather than just writing about other people’s discoveries.’ She smiled at an
11 idea that they had neither the opportunity nor money for now.

12 In the garden, things had taken a more violent turn. Surrendering to one last mighty heave,
13 the roots of the ivy suddenly collapsed away above the rock face with the completeness of
14 a breaking chrysalis shell. Rohaan toppled heavily backwards, as eager green ivy tendrils
15 sprang towards him. Taken by surprise, he writhed around under many stringy choking
16 fingers for several seconds, before ripping his mischievous assailants away.

17 Unaware of events outside, Shaima paused at her laptop, thinking about the bees and the
18 butterflies that the intrusive ivy attracted. She realised that she had mixed feelings about the
19 building project.

20 Rohaan lifted himself to his elbows. He hadn’t broken, twisted or pulled anything important,
21 and he freed himself from the remaining foliage.

22 He looked at what was left of the rock face. A gaping hole stared defiantly back at him, as
23 if inviting a stand-off. Rohaan dropped his eyes to break the gaze and received an immense
24 surprise. Half a metre from the base was the floor of a cave entrance. It was surely inviting
25 him to look inside.

26 After brushing off dead leaf detritus, cobwebs and insects, Rohaan slowly approached the
27 mouth of the cave. He peered inside, his heart pounding. The air was still, dry and cool, the
28 smell, an age-old musty scent. Within a few metres, everything appeared lost in suffocating
29 blackness.

30 ***

31 Armed now with a torch, Rohaan advanced into the cave. At first, it was dry and vaguely
32 warm. As he travelled deeper, Rohaan found himself contorting his shoulders and lowering
33 his head to navigate an increasingly confined space. He walked down a gentle slope with
34 caution, trying to avoid treacherously loose stones underfoot. The walls swallowed the light.
35 He flashed the torch up to the ceiling then right and left ahead of his feet.

36 In front of him, the path suddenly ended. Rohaan could hardly believe what he had found.
37 A huge cavern had appeared, dropping perhaps 10 metres below. The height looked to be
38 the same. In the dim distance was the cavern wall.

39 ‘Hello,’ sang out Rohaan and was greeted with a gratifying echo. He laughed out loud then
40 stood in awe at his find. The bright spotlight shone down on animal bones, though which
41 animals they belonged to was a mystery. It didn’t take a genius to realise that the remains
42 of these creatures had once been animals that had run into the cave to escape a predator,
43 only to fall headlong to their death. He spotted deer antlers, which solved one puzzle. Maybe
44 Shaima could identify the other, old, maybe even extinct species? Would this be their new
45 project? Could they hope to make money?

46 Slowly Rohaan moved the beam around the cavern. He realised he had stopped breathing,
47 and took in a large lungful of air. It was air that must have lingered in this place for thousands
48 of years, and it left a dry ash-like taste on his tongue.

49 Suddenly he heard movement behind him. He turned quickly. ‘Oh, my goodness!’ he heard
50 Shaima call out.
