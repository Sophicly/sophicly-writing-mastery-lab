**Title:** Untitled prose extract
**Author:** Unknown

1  Rachel was doing a research project at Durham University when she first met Peter Kemp.
2  She had taken her degree in Biology in Cambridge, almost as far away from her mother as
3  she could manage, but then she moved back to the north. This was not to be close to her
4  mother but because the birds of the northern hills had become her passion. She started by
5  studying black grouse then transferred her interest to upland birds like curlew and wagtail. Her
6  research project was to find a way to count them accurately and she used a cottage on the
7  moors of Northumberland as her base for her studies.

8  It was a windy day in April and Rachel had come into the town of Kimmerston to have lunch
9  at the White Hart at the request of Bob Hewlett who worked as a Conservation Officer for
10 English Nature, a government agency.

11 The White Hart was a solid, stone-built hotel on Kimmerston’s main street. Once, it had
12 been the only place to eat in the town but it had grown shabby. On Friday nights it was a haunt
13 for underage drinkers. Often it became rowdy, with petty skirmishes and visits by the police.
14 During the rest of the week there was an air of genteel decay. The elderly waitresses, in their
15 black and white uniforms, had few people to serve, even on market day when the town was
16 busy. The food was proudly ‘traditional’ in that the vegetables were overcooked and thick
17 brown gravy was served with everything. When Rachel explained that she was a vegetarian,
18 there was something of a crisis. Eventually a leathery cheese omelette appeared.

19 Rachel had come across Bob before and didn’t like him much. She thought he wanted
20 to use her work for his own advantage and she also felt that he was too close to the local
21 farmers, too desperate to be accepted by them, rather than do his job properly. He lived
22 locally and drove a Land Rover with a couple of labradors in the back, looking very much the
23 country landowner. She had seen him drinking in the pub with the local farmers, all friends
24 together. However, she knew better than to offend him – she might want to work for English
25 Nature one day. When he invited her to lunch at the White Hart to discuss her work, she
26 accepted graciously.

27 ‘I’ve invited Peter Kemp to join us later,’ Bob said suddenly as the food arrived. ‘He’s doing
28 the same sort of work as you for the Wildlife Trust. You might be able to help each other.’
29 As he mentioned Peter Kemp, Bob beamed at her across the table. Despite the Land Rover
30 parked outside, he had had a couple of whiskies while they were waiting to order and then
31 a pint of beer to wash down the meal. Rachel was irritated by Bob’s interference in her work
32 and was certain she would dislike Peter Kemp. She needed no help with her work. Her mother
33 would have dealt sharply with Bob’s patronising attitude but Rachel always found it hard to be
34 assertive without being rude.

35 She first saw Peter Kemp hovering in the doorway of the dining room. A waitress told him
36 he was too late for lunch but he gave her a lovely smile and pointed to their table. As he
37 walked towards them he smiled charmingly but Rachel could sense his confidence. He shook
38 hands with Bob then turned to her.

39 ‘I know of you from colleagues,’ he said. ‘You have an impressive reputation.’ His voice
40 was earnest, trying to please. Rachel knew she was being worked on but since the smile to
41 the waitress, she’d found it impossible to resist him. Even as she submitted to his flattery, she
42 also realised that he wanted something from her. He said he’d like to visit her study area and
43 compare survey methods. By the time they had shared a pot of coffee, she had invited him to
44 the cottage for a couple of days to watch her work.

45 That spring Peter spent more time at the cottage than he did in his office. His excuse was
46 that the Wildlife Trust intended to buy a nature reserve in the hills. Rachel knew this was
47 an excuse, and she was delighted. Her passion for Peter had the intensity of an adolescent
48 crush.

49 However, Rachel made the mistake of inviting him to meet her mother one Sunday. It
50 should have been a relaxed affair but Rachel’s mother took against Peter from the start.
51 She glared into her wine glass as they tried to make conversation and the more hostile she
52 appeared the more Peter tried to charm her. Even Rachel felt that he was trying too hard and
53 coming across as insincere. Later she expected a lecture about her choice in men but her
54 mother was unusually restrained.

55 ‘A bit showy for my taste,’ she whispered as she followed Rachel into the kitchen with a tray
56 of dirty plates. ‘Never trust the showy ones.’

57 But Rachel was captivated by the showiness and she loved the way that Peter would
58 appear at the cottage with flowers and champagne. They would dance on the lawn in the
59 evening. No one had ever made such a fuss of her before. She began to make plans, secret
60 plans which she wouldn’t admit to anyone, about her and Peter getting married, setting up
61 home together and having children.

62 The first betrayal came two months after Peter started his own consultancy firm. Rachel
63 went to work for him and had her own desk in the small office which was all he could afford.
64 She acted as receptionist, secretary and main scientist.
65 Now there were no bottles of champagne but she still dreamed. She understood that money
66 was tight. It was enough that he would occasionally brush his lips over her hair and say, ‘You
67 do know that I couldn’t manage without you, don’t you?’
68 Then she saw an article by him in a magazine. It described a new method for counting
69 upland birds. It was the method she had devised but he took the credit for it. He claimed it as
70 his own work.

71 The method quickly became the recommended system for upland surveys of birds. Peter
72 was suddenly very much in demand and work flooded into the office. Rachel continued to
73 work for him without making a fuss but she could no longer bear for him to touch her. She
74 often wondered why she didn’t confront him but it was a matter of pride. Why indeed did she
75 continue to work for him, supporting the business through its expansion into a smart new
76 office? If she resigned she would have to admit to others and to herself that he had made a
77 fool of her. She would have to accept that his only reason for paying attention to her was to
78 steal her ideas.

79 The second betrayal came in the form of a large envelope which she found propped on her
80 desk one morning. It contained an invitation to Peter’s wedding.
